# FSS — six-month futures for a recipe system

Intended mind change: Replace “more procedures will automatically improve mind change” with a future-dependent strategy that prepares for recipe validity, execution authority, and feedback failure.

Actual starting judgment: The likely useful future was a larger, better-ordered recipe catalog.

Concrete input: a six-month mind-change recipe system; frozen drivers are recipe validity, target-locus diagnosis, feedback, authority, distribution shift, and retrieval cost. Boundary: feedback disappears after deployment.

## Future space

Domain: a system that compiles an intended mind change into an attempt and verification receipt. Horizon: six months. Search goal: futures most important to prepare for, with likelihood and desirability secondary.

## Drivers

1. Recipe validity: high or uneven.
2. Locus diagnosis: correct, ambiguous, or wrong.
3. Feedback: immediate, delayed, or absent.
4. Execution authority: direct, delegated, or unavailable.
5. Distribution shift: stable or novel cases.
6. Retrieval cost: indexed or source-order search.

## Baseline futures

| Future | Driver combination | Internal result |
|---|---|---|
| F1 catalog success | high validity, correct locus, immediate feedback, sufficient authority, stable cases, indexed | recipes are selected and repaired quickly |
| F2 polished misrouting | high local validity, wrong locus, immediate output feedback, authority present | fluent attempts repeatedly act on the wrong state variable |
| F3 gated competence | high validity and diagnosis, authority unavailable | correct plan and handoff exist; target transition remains unperformed |
| F4 silent drift | uneven validity, ambiguous locus, absent feedback, novel cases | apparent KEEPs accumulate without outcome evidence |
| F5 slow but corrigible | uneven validity, correct diagnosis, delayed feedback, delegated authority, indexed | learning is possible but claims must remain partial until delay closes |
| F6 retrieval collapse | high validity but source-order search over a growing catalog | selection time and omission rise despite good procedures |

## Trend extrapolations

Recipe count continuation yields broader coverage but a larger selection surface; acceleration creates duplicate recipes faster than validated distinctions; reversal deletes low-uptake recipes and improves navigation if provenance remains.

Automation continuation speeds compilation; acceleration increases unsupported action if authority gates are weak; reversal makes compilation advisory-only.

Feedback continuation permits repair; acceleration supplies near-real-time transition receipts; reversal removes recipient/outcome observations and converts empirical improvement into simulation-only refinement.

## Wild cards

- A high-consequence misroute causes authority to be revoked: execution moves from direct to mandatory human approval.
- A new model makes source-order reasoning cheap: retrieval cost falls, but recipe validity and locus diagnosis remain necessary.
- A privacy rule forbids retaining recipient state: personalization quality drops and ephemeral local computation becomes required.
- A benchmark rewards visible output change: reindexing and enablement are falsely scored as failures or actions.

Impossible/contradictory futures removed: “the system directly causes a physical relocation without an actuator,” “absent feedback proves benefit,” and “a universally valid recipe remains perfectly calibrated under every distribution shift.”

Selected: F2, F3, and F4 for preparation; F1 as desired target and F5 as the corrigible bridge.

## Selected narratives and indicators

F2: mechanism selection outruns diagnosis. Indicator: repeated stronger messages after recipients already accept the claim. Response: require locus evidence before message selection.

F3: the recipe correctly identifies a physical, permission, or stakeholder operation, but the system cannot execute it. Indicator: plan artifacts without action receipts. Response: emit a typed handoff and preserve `UNRESOLVED`, not a success claim.

F4: internal counts and polished documents increase while human or delayed observations disappear. Indicator: KEEP count rises without distinct findings or later uptake. Response: separate procedure completion, local benefit, distinct finding, later use, and external outcome.

F1 target: an admissibility gate, locus diagnosis, typed transition, executable mechanism, and shape-specific receipt are all present. Path: index the corpus, freeze inputs, route by locus, execute only authorized operations, update on receipts.

## Boundary and action

Changed boundary: feedback disappears after deployment. F1 no longer remains the target as stated; the system must shift to F3/F4 controls: conservative authority, reversible actions, shadow evaluation, and explicit unobserved outcomes. A larger recipe catalog does not repair missing feedback.

## Outcome

Actual mind change: I changed from catalog growth as the main future to feedback and authority as the two dominant failure-preparation drivers, with locus diagnosis preventing polished misrouting.

Benefit or harm: The search produced different preparations for five coherent futures and rejected three impossible success claims. It predicts scenarios; it does not show they occurred.

Verdict: KEEP as a planning application and refinement; no distinct finding counted.

Content assessment: Six drivers, baseline combinations, continuation/acceleration/reversal trends, wild cards, impossibility filters, scoring, narratives, indicators, and implications are explicit.

Organization assessment: Organize future preparation by failure-controlling driver, not by chronology alone. Preserve F1 as goal and F2–F4 as safeguards.

Next attempts: Attach base rates; obtain feedback-frequency data; test an authority-loss incident; simulate distribution shift; compare catalog pruning with better indexing.
