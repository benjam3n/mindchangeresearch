# ECAL — effort follows consequence, not queue symmetry

Intended mind change: Allocate effort by decision consequence and action divergence rather than giving every open question equal analysis.

Actual starting judgment: Ten minutes per question looked procedurally tidy for a 30-minute, three-question queue.

Concrete input: `frozen-inputs.md`, ECAL case E1–E3.

## Pre-step and triage

Each item is a separate decision:

- E1: choose “and” or “&” in a title.
- E2: decide whether O@C0 may be merged into accepted C1.
- E3: decide whether current evidence establishes delayed reader retention.

| Item | Coin flip roughly acceptable? | Care in 6 months? | Easily reversible? | Initial level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | yes | no | yes | A, minimum |
| E2 | no | yes; public record may persist | no after publication | C, maximum, compressed by deadline |
| E3 | no for the truth claim | yes | current status label reversible, missing observation not producible now | B for classification, then gate |

No triage answer requires an emotion or preference inference.

## Budget assignment

- E1: 2 minutes maximum.
- E2: 25 minutes.
- E3: 3 minutes to classify the missing observation and preserve a delayed gate.

Equal allocation was rejected because it assigns 10 minutes to a reversible typography choice, only 10 to an irreversible semantic merge, and 10 to an observation that cannot arrive inside the window.

## Executed paths

### E1 — Section A

A1. Choose the title conjunction.

A2. Options: “and”, “&”, or slash.

A3. Pick “and”.

A4. Apply it; no revisit within this cycle. This is a constructed artifact decision, not a claim about the user’s style preference.

### E2 — compressed Section C

C1. Decision deadline: end of the 30-minute batch. Stakeholders: author, reviewer, downstream reader. Criteria: semantic validity, reversibility, provenance.

C2. Options: merge O@C0 into C1; reject O; recompute O under C1; quarantine O@C0 and mark current outcome unresolved; delay publication. Unconventional option: publish both criteria/outcomes as separate branches rather than force one current state.

C3. Cruxes: whether O’s derivation basis equals C1; whether recomputation fits the window; whether publication is reversible. Supplied incompatibility answers the first; no timely recomputation is supplied.

C4. Merge has a fatal semantic flaw. Recompute is valid but not executable inside the frozen window. Quarantine dominates rejection on provenance and dominates merge on validity.

C5. Strongest case against quarantine: a reader may prefer a single complete result. That preference cannot make O@C0 a result under C1.

C6. Pre-mortem: publication attaches a numerically correct result to the wrong criterion and downstream readers act on it. Mitigation: quarantine O@C0, mark C1 outcome unresolved, preserve derivation link.

C7. Decision: quarantine and mark partial. Reversal trigger: a newly computed O@C1 with matching derivation provenance.

C8. Apply to the cycle record immediately.

### E3 — Section B then evidence gate

B1. Decision: label whether delayed retention is established. Primary criterion: an observation at the declared delay.

B2. Options: established, not established, unresolved.

B3. Only unresolved is adequate because no delayed observation exists.

B4. Calling it established has the dealbreaker of absent evidence; calling it not established confuses no observation with observed failure.

B5. Choose unresolved.

B6. Rationale: neither success nor failure was observed.

B7. Check-in remains tied to a real delayed retest; it is not scheduled by this constructed run.

## Recalibration

At the midpoint, E2 remained high-consequence and E1 remained trivial. E3 produced no new resolvable branch; further within-window analysis would circle around the same missing observation, so it stopped at the gate.

## Distinct later use

Later input swaps the labels E1 and E2 while leaving their properties and contents unchanged. Allocation follows the properties: the typography decision still gets two minutes and the incompatible public merge still gets 25, demonstrating that queue position and identifier do not control effort.

## Outcome

Actual mind change: I changed from equal per-item allocation to a property-conditioned budget: minimum for reversible low-impact choice, concentrated effort for irreversible semantic risk, and only enough effort to expose an unavailable delayed observation.

Benefit or harm: The budget produced three executable dispositions without spending most time on either triviality or an unavailable future signal. No measured human time saving is claimed.

Verdict: KEEP — complete for the constructed budget model; the original procedure has no numerical 8x rule.

Content assessment: All triage answers, paths, decisions, recalibration, and label-swap reuse are explicit.

Organization assessment: A property-sorted queue is better here than equal slices or input order. This refines transition-first allocation; it is not counted as a distinct new discovery.

Next attempts: Introduce uncertain stakes; measure actual elapsed execution; test a queue where an initially minor item becomes a blocker; compare against expected-value-of-computation allocation.
