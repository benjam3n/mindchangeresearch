# Four retained findings

1. **Transition before technique — UNX:** select the smallest supported and authorized state transition at its controlling locus before selecting a reasoning procedure.
2. **Decomposed reversibility — RVA:** refundable or restorable data, elapsed time, and future feasibility are different; one reversible component does not make the whole situation reversible.
3. **Order without inferred motivation — MP:** missing motivational state blocks state-matched motivation heuristics, but stipulated priorities, permission, reversibility, and deadlines may still determine action order.
4. **Semantic-basis compatibility — COL:** individually valid fields cannot be merged when one was produced under a basis invalidated by another accepted field.

No EQI, TD, location, memory, ACR, or trace-matrix result is used as an independent premise here. This prevents a consolidation from quietly importing more than its four eligible findings.

## Organization A: source and domain order

The source view retrieves UNX, RVA, MP, and COL as four application records. Executed literally:

- UNX asks for the state transition but cannot yet choose between restoring C0 and retaining C1 with an unresolved outcome.
- RVA confirms that snapshot rollback is technically reversible in one minute while elapsed time and the lost chance to recompute by 14:00 are not.
- MP withholds any motivation-state heuristic and admits deadline, authority, and reversibility as ordering inputs.
- COL finally rejects attaching O1@C0 to C1.

This route reaches two valid candidates: `(criterion=C0, prior outcome, complete)` after rollback, or `(criterion=C1, outcome=UNRESOLVED, partial)` after quarantining O1. It preserves provenance well. It requires retrieving four method records before its selection condition becomes visible.

## Organization B: transition-gate card

1. **Terminal state and locus:** “current” refers to the accepted C1 criterion; validity forbids an outcome derived under another criterion. The smallest eligible locus is r1's outcome/status relation, not the contributor's mind and not the entire ledger.
2. **Authority and human-state gate:** the coordinator may change r1. Motivation and emotion are unknown and irrelevant to the mechanical validity condition; no claim about them enters the decision.
3. **Reversibility and expiry:** removing O1 from the current field and retaining it in proposal history is reversible. Snapshot rollback is also reversible, but returning to C0 gives up the latest accepted criterion. Three minutes are insufficient for a valid C1 recomputation.
4. **Semantic basis:** O1@C0 cannot become the outcome of C1. It remains retrievable as a rejected-for-current proposal, not deleted or relabeled.
5. **Selected transition:** keep C1; set outcome to `UNRESOLVED`; set status to `PARTIAL`; preserve X and O1@C0 in proposal history; schedule C1 recomputation outside the 14:00 window. No reasoning procedure is needed for the now-settled merge prohibition.

This produces a valid current record by 13:57 without inventing an outcome. The strongest contrary option—rollback to the last complete C0 record by 13:58—is retained for a case where completeness outranks the latest criterion. The freeze did not state that preference, so the selected transition treats “current” as the accepted C1 basis while exposing the alternative.

## Organization decision

Use Organization B as the compact selection view for mixed change requests: `terminal state/locus → authority and unobserved-human gate → reversible components and expiry → semantic basis → smallest supported transition → technique only at unresolved nodes`. Preserve Organization A as provenance and derivation.

On the frozen case both organizations rejected the invalid merge. B made the decisive conditions visible in one serial card and selected the bounded transition without requiring method names. This is a concrete reuse advantage, not a measured time result or proof that B is globally optimal. A remains better when the task is to audit how a particular source produced a finding.
