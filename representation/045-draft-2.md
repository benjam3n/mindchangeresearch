## Draft 1

By the third week on the coast, Sela could draw the harbor from her desk without looking through the window. She was making a map of the inlets farther north, places she had once passed on a train without knowing their names. Each evening she copied the day's marks onto a cleaner sheet. The new sheet never looked quite as certain as the old one, which still carried the marks she had crossed out.

Her father's letter arrived folded around a page with one line on it: Tell us where you are. She laid it beside the clean map. There was room below the line for another sentence, but he had written nothing else. She read the words once as a question about the coast and once as a question about coming home. The empty part of the page gave her no way to choose between them.

An unused return ticket lay in the small drawer below her drawing board. Sela brought it out and put it next to the letter. The route on the ticket ended at a name she knew without needing a map. She could take that route. For the length of a kettle boiling in the next room, she left the two pieces of paper together and did no drawing.

Then she took a copy of the coastal map and made a small dot at the harbor. Beside it she wrote the name of the rooming house and the street. The dot covered a place where she had carefully shaded the water; she rubbed out that patch and moved the mark onto land. She folded the map to fit an envelope and addressed it to her father.

The ticket went back into the drawer. Sela put the envelope by the door for the morning post and returned to the northern edge of the map. She had not drawn that part yet. No answer to her letter had arrived; the map was still in the room with her. She sharpened a pencil and left the space beyond the last inlet empty.
