# A proposed reminder design remains a human hypothesis

Starting working state: The available starting approach is a reusable note with a descriptive title. It is a strong ordinary reference. Whether a person needs a cue, a reminder, or no further encounter is not observed.

Input and execution scope: This is application 3 of `ma` in the representation line. The actor whose working state is observed is this model in this local research run. Human effects are untested unless explicitly identified otherwise.

Source: [original ma](../sources/representation-ma.md); [separate requirements](../sources/representation-ma.requirements.txt). Emitted source SHA-256 `bbf09161a65364d43e76f438375bb8ddcbc5e507b5e792b190413be31c05c4bf`. The complete receipt is in [source-receipts.json](source-receipts.json).

Depth accounting: Original 8x floors met: six dimensions, six options each, 36 distinct combinations evaluated, six explicit cross-dimension interactions, constraint filtering, four evaluation aspects, and three diverse shortlist prototypes. 46,656 is the space size, not a claim that every combination received a substantive evaluation.

Problem: Construct several feasible ways a person could encounter a prior distinction again without assuming that a saved note will be remembered. Interpretation: structured comparison and generation of unexplored configurations.

The dimensions are separately choosable design parameters. Some pairs conflict under this target; those interactions are filtered rather than hidden inside a dimension. Values are a bounded design vocabulary, not an exhaustive inventory of every possible representation.

| Dimension | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trigger | next similar task | chosen calendar time | visible desk object | conversation question | error recurrence | voluntary search |
| Medium | short text | spoken script | diagram | printed card | interactive prompt | paired example |
| Content | exact distinction | minimal counterexample | scope boundary | own prior report | new transfer case | open question |
| Pace | one encounter | self-paced review | optional pause | three spaced encounters | on-demand only | stop after recognition |
| Choice | default with decline | two meaningful options | free selection | private response | public discussion | no response requested |
| Evaluation | reported experience | classification task | later conduct | retrieval observation | no measurement | criterion revision |

Combination count: 6^6 = 46,656. The source permits systematic sampling above 1,000. All combinations were enumerated to verify the count, and the 36-case slice is retained in [the full evaluated sample](ma-3-combinations.md) and its exact [data](ma-3-combinations.json). Constraints and evaluations precede the shortlist. No hidden score is described as measured value.

Cross-dimension interactions:

1. Calendar trigger × repeated encounters requires a real scheduling mechanism; a written agenda is not scheduling.
2. No-response choice × classification measure conflicts unless the task becomes optional and missing responses remain unmeasured.
3. Public discussion × own prior report changes disclosure conditions; it cannot be assumed equivalent to private reflection.
4. On-demand trigger × retrieval evaluation selects people who already searched, so it cannot by itself show improved spontaneous recall.
5. Diagram × spoken medium needs an explicit verbal mapping, not merely a different file extension.
6. Criterion revision × later-conduct evaluation requires preserving both old and new criteria; otherwise a changed judgment can masquerade as changed behavior.

Shortlist, constructed outputs, and transfer tests:

A. Task cue: “When the next statement says ‘every’, try one mixed case. Keep the original scope beside the result.” Medium: text; choice: default with decline; evaluation: optional classification.

B. Private card: “Did this reminder change your question, your distinction, your action, or nothing you want to report?” Medium: print-ready plain text; pace: on demand; evaluation: attributed self-report only.

C. Paired example: `[1,0]` and `[1,?]`, with the exact question “Which refutes every?” Medium: paired example with legend; pace: one voluntary encounter; evaluation: task response.

Local tests show that A has a cue, B permits non-disclosure, and C distinguishes unknown from false. The candidates were compared with the ordinary titled note: the note is simpler and user-initiated; the new candidates add a trigger, a response route, or a discrimination demand. No person encountered any of them, no automation was created, and no later conduct was observed.

Certificate: claim—one of these cue designs improves this person’s memory or benefit—is unresolved. The design properties and constraint filter are inspectable, but the relevant preference, encounter, and outcome are absent. Strong contrary—a well-titled reference may be all the person wants, so extra prompting can add burden. Keep the three candidates as distinct untested approaches, not established interventions.

Benefit: The actual design and finite tests are recorded; model construction effort, semantic preservation, and untested human outcomes remain separate. The verdict concerns the incremental effect relative to the strong ordinary baseline.
