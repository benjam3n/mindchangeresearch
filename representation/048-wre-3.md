# READY-anchored restart timer requirements

Actual starting judgment: “Wait five seconds” seemed to state a duration adequately. It did not identify whether display, receipt or readiness starts the interval, nor what a repeated READY does.

Concrete input: Duration five seconds. In one active round, accept the first READY; ignore duplicate READY; CANCEL clears the round; a later READY starts a fresh round. Required checks: READY 3/6 then poll 8; cancel 4 then READY 10/polls 14,15; held-out READY 11/13/polls 15,16.

Actor and scope: This model derives and tests protocol prose against stipulated event traces. No participant’s useful attention, preference or response quality is observed.

## Stage 0 — artifact definition

Type: timer protocol note. Venue: an interactive lesson’s control specification. Reader: an implementer who receives READY/CANCEL/POLL events but has no implicit anchor policy. Baseline: can compare timestamps and store round state. Goal: produce one deadline per active round and deterministic restart behavior. After-state: compute remaining/due on all supplied traces. Core question: Which event starts the five-second interval, and how do repeats/cancellation change it? Scope: one clock domain and five-second duration; not participant benefit or delivery guarantees. Stakes: ambiguous anchoring yields deadlines 5 or 8; unconditional deduplication blocks a legitimate restart.

## Stage 1 — thesis object

Core thesis: In a single clock domain, the first READY of an active round sets `deadline=ready_at+5`; duplicates do nothing until CANCEL clears the round, after which a later READY can set a fresh deadline.

Claim type: explanatory/technical. Weaker claim: a timer needs a start. Stronger claim refused: readiness anchoring always gives every participant more useful thinking time; refused because immediate readiness can make intervals equal and no participant use is observed. Actual claim: the declared state machine determines the supplied deadlines and remaining values.

Supporting claims: C1 event type and timestamp jointly identify the anchor. C2 duplicate suppression is scoped to the active round. C3 cancellation removes the old anchor and re-enables a later READY. Out of scope: O1 due time equals actual delivery time. O2 response receipt time identifies choice time. O3 equal durations imply equal human opportunity.

Sensitivity: anchor at display 0 gives deadline 5; READY at 3 gives 8. Restart on every READY changes the active anchor from 3 to 6 and leaves three seconds at poll 8; first-ready policy is due at 8. CANCEL at 4 clears the old anchor; READY 10 gives deadline 15, so poll 14 has one second and poll 15 is due. Held-out READY 11/duplicate 13 gives one second at 15 and due at 16. A poll at 10 for deadline 8 reports due then; it does not create delivery at 8.

Adversarial review: A session-global first-READY rule passes the duplicate trace but fails after cancellation by ignoring READY 10. Scoping “first” to an active round repairs both. The protocol still lacks a concurrent-round identifier and transport model; those are exclusions, not silently solved features.
