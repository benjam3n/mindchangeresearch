Constraints and evaluations precede the shortlist. No hidden score is described as measured value.

Cross-dimension interactions:

1. Unknown outcome × numeric vector requires a distinct unknown symbol; otherwise a missing observation can become zero.
2. Empty domain × ordinary “all” wording needs a convention; everyday presupposition does not settle formal truth.
3. Spoken design × side-by-side columns requires serialization; two-dimensional location alone is insufficient.
4. Source audit × counterexample-first entry keeps the source link but makes it a second destination; appropriate for use, weaker for audit.
5. All-improve case × minimal-counterexample support conflicts unless a second case is added; an all-true vector cannot itself separate the negations.
6. New-vocabulary transfer × fixed familiar labels requires replacing the predicate while retaining its formal scope; surface novelty alone does not test transfer.

Shortlist, constructed outputs, and transfer tests:

A. Plain case: “Two operations ran. One improved the result; the other did not. At least one did not improve, but they did not both fail to improve.” Test `[1,0]`: correct distinction; four clauses bind the members explicitly.

B. Predicate table: `not(all p)` is true and `all(not p)` false on `[1,0]`; on `[]` they are false and true respectively. This carries the empty-domain case more directly, but requires predicate literacy.

C. Serial question: “Did every operation improve? No. Did every operation fail to improve? No. Which observation answers both? One improvement and one non-improvement.” This makes the two questions explicit but uses more text than the baseline.

All three were applied to `[0,0]`, `[1,1]`, and `[1,0]`; the truth pairs are `(true,true)`, `(false,false)`, `(true,false)`. Each preserves the tested mapping. No prototype adds an inference or access capability demonstrated absent from the baseline for the current model.

Certificate: exact claim—the morphological expansion produces additional useful local access over the direct counterexample—is not demonstrated. The direct baseline answers all three tests and the empty-domain exception with less constructed machinery. Strong contrary—a different intended reader may benefit from the serial question or table; no such reader evidence is present. The representation candidates remain viable, but the current incremental-benefit claim is rejected.

Actual mind change: I generated and evaluated the full stated 36-configuration sample, constructed three materially different shortlisted representations, and changed the candidate disposition as shown in the prototype comparison.

Benefit: The actual design and finite tests are recorded; model construction effort, semantic preservation, and untested human outcomes remain separate. The verdict concerns the incremental effect relative to the strong ordinary baseline.

Verdict: REJECT — incremental value of this morphological expansion for the already-solved scope task.

Organization: The six-dimensional box and exact 36 rows remain accessible, while only the tested shortlist is an entry surface. Enumeration does not earn keep credit by itself.
