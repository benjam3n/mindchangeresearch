# Ingredient evidence preserved from the supplied catalog

These notes and links were imported from the researched catalog. They describe the cited studies' scope, not a new replication or empirical validation of the assembled recipes. The audit adds logical results and implementations; it does not upgrade these evidence levels.

<a id="s1"></a>
## S1: Roediger & Karpicke (2006)

In two experiments with prose passages, repeated study outperformed prior testing at five minutes, but prior testing produced greater retention at two days or one week. Restudying also increased confidence. Supports a divergence between immediate performance, confidence, and delayed recall in these learning tasks; does not establish that difficulty always helps. [Roediger & Karpicke (2006)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16507066/)

<a id="s2"></a>
## S2: Deslauriers et al. (2019)

Randomly assigned college physics students learned more with active instruction while reporting a lower feeling of learning than students receiving passive instruction with the same content. A result about these instructional conditions; self-reported benefit remains useful evidence about experience and cannot alone establish learning. [Deslauriers et al. (2019)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31484770/)

<a id="s3"></a>
## S3: Schultz et al. (2007)

Household energy feedback about average neighborhood consumption produced savings among higher consumers and an undesirable increase among lower consumers. Adding a message of social approval or disapproval eliminated the boomerang effect in the experiment. Direct evidence that the sign of a norm message can depend on the recipient's starting behavior; the result does not make all normative messages beneficial. [Schultz et al. (2007)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/6261413_The_Constructive_Destructive_and_Reconstructive_Power_of_Social_Norms)

<a id="s4"></a>
## S4: Wood & Porter (2019)

Across five experiments involving more than 10,100 participants and 52 issues, the researchers found no factual corrections that produced the tested backfire effect. Corrective information does not routinely strengthen the corrected false belief. This result does not rule out relational, motivational, or other adverse consequences. [Wood & Porter (2019)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11109-018-9443-y)

<a id="s5"></a>
## S5: Eskreis-Winkler et al. (2019)

In a preregistered randomized field experiment involving 1,982 high school students, giving motivational advice to younger students improved advisers' grades in math and a self-selected target class across an academic quarter. Supports an effect of the advice-giving intervention in this setting. The abstract does not establish one uniquely responsible psychological mechanism or a large universal effect. [Eskreis-Winkler et al. (2019)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31285348/)

<a id="s6"></a>
## S6: Glickman & Sharot (2025)

Across experiments with 1,401 participants, human–AI feedback loops amplified human biases in perceptual, emotional, and social judgments. Participants often underestimated the influence. Supports harmful feedback effects in the studied tasks. It does not establish that every chatbot interaction has these effects or that beneficial amplification is symmetrical. [Glickman & Sharot (2025)](https://cris.biu.ac.il/en/publications/how-humanai-feedback-loops-alter-human-perceptual-emotional-and-s/)

<a id="s7"></a>
## S7: Fernbach et al. (2013)

Generating detailed mechanistic policy explanations reduced an illusion of understanding and moderated attitudes in the experiments; enumerating reasons for preferences did not have the same effect. Supports a difference between two elicited operations in these tasks. Moderation is not itself a general measure of truth, and this is not a universal prescription to moderate beliefs. [Fernbach et al. (2013)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23620547/)

<a id="s8"></a>
## S8: Yeager et al. (2019)

A brief growth-mindset intervention improved grades among lower-achieving students and increased overall advanced-mathematics enrollment in a nationally representative US high-school sample; effects varied with context. Supports context-sensitive motivational intervention effects, not guaranteed or uniformly large benefits from generic encouragement. [Yeager et al. (2019)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1466-y)

<a id="s9"></a>
## S9: Hulleman & Harackiewicz (2009)

A randomized field experiment asked students to connect science coursework with their lives. Interest and course grades increased for students with low expectations of success. Supports learner-generated relevance in the specified subgroup and context. It does not imply that imposing a supposedly relevant motive will work. [Hulleman & Harackiewicz (2009)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19965759/)

<a id="s10"></a>
## S10: Fazio et al. (2015)

The experiments investigated repetition-based increases in perceived truth, including cases where participants had relevant knowledge. Supports concern about familiarity being used as evidence of truth. It does not establish that repeating a false claim within a clear correction routinely backfires.
 [Fazio et al. (2015)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26301795/)

<a id="s11"></a>
## S11: Mellers et al. (2014)

In a geopolitical forecasting tournament, probability training, team collaboration, and tracking top performers were associated with improved calibration and resolution. Training included reference classes and combining estimates. Tracking selected high performers, so it must not be interpreted as a purely randomized training effect. Supports concrete forecasting ingredients in this setting, not guaranteed individual calibration. [Mellers et al. (2014)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24659192/)

<a id="s12"></a>
## S12: Gentner, Loewenstein & Thompson (2003)

Three studies of novice negotiation learning found benefits from comparing cases for extracting and transferring common structure; support for comparison improved transfer. Supports explicit relational comparison in those tasks. It does not establish that every analogy, example pair, or newly assembled transfer sequence works. [Gentner, Loewenstein & Thompson (2003)](https://groups.psych.northwestern.edu/gentner/papers/GentnerLoewensteinThompson03.pdf)

<a id="s13"></a>
## S13: Kornell & Bjork (2008)

Participants learned artists' styles from paintings presented in blocks or interleaved with other artists. Interleaving improved classification of new paintings, although participants judged blocked presentation more effective. Supports discrimination and subjective-performance divergence in this category-learning task. It is not evidence that all kinds of contextual variation or difficulty improve every skill. [Kornell & Bjork (2008)](https://web.williams.edu/Psychology/Faculty/Kornell/Publications/Kornell.Bjork.2008a.pdf)

<a id="s14"></a>
## S14: van der Bles et al. (2020)

Experiments on communicating uncertainty about facts and numbers found increased perceived uncertainty with only small decreases in trust, mostly for verbal uncertainty communication. Supports specifying uncertainty without assuming catastrophic loss of trust. It does not promise preserved trust for every formulation, source, audience, or topic. [van der Bles et al. (2020)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32205438/)

<a id="s15"></a>
## S15: Carrera et al. (2018)

In a randomized study with 877 gym members, selecting intended attendance days and times did not increase attendance relative to recording prior exercise. The authors reported a tightly estimated null despite participant engagement with planning. Direct evidence that performing a plausible planning procedure can fail to change repeated behavior; the catalog's proposed diagnostic branches require their own validation. [Carrera et al. (2018)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30336306/)

<a id="s16"></a>
## S16: Lally et al. (2010)

Volunteers chose a behavior to repeat daily in the same context over 12 weeks and reported behavior and automaticity. The study modeled habit development from these longitudinal observations. It supports investigating context, repetition, and changing automaticity, but does not supply a universal completion date or randomized proof of the catalog's habit-building recipes. [Lally et al. (2010)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ejsp.674)

<a id="s17"></a>
## S17: Gross (1998)

In a laboratory study using a disgusting film, reappraisal and expressive suppression both reduced expressive behavior, but differed in reported disgust and physiological response. Supports distinguishing appraisal, feeling, expression, and physiology. It does not establish that reframing improves every emotion, that suppression is always harmful, or that ordinary conversational recipes treat clinical problems. [Gross (1998)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9457784/)

<a id="s18"></a>
## S18: Walton et al. (2023)

A randomized study with 26,911 students at 22 institutions evaluated a brief social-belonging intervention. Benefits for first-year full-time progress depended on student group and college context; effects occurred where groups had opportunities to belong. Supports treating actual opportunity as a consequential condition, not assuming a message can compensate for every exclusionary environment. [Walton et al. (2023)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37141344/)

<a id="s19"></a>
## S19: Hoffrage et al. (2000)

Natural-frequency representations improved reasoning about statistical information in the studied tasks. The relevant representation preserves joint counts and the appropriate denominator; merely replacing every percentage with an isolated count is not the same operation. Supports the representational ingredient of the probability example, not a universal advantage for every table or numerical format. [Hoffrage et al. (2000)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11188724/)

<a id="s20"></a>
## S20: Renkl, Atkinson & Große (2004)

Two experiments investigated fading worked solution steps in a computer-based learning environment. Fading supported learning, with fewer unproductive learning events; the proposed increase in productive self-explanation events was not found. Supports a specified instructional ingredient while illustrating why a plausible mediator must not be presented as established. [Renkl, Atkinson & Große (2004)](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/how-fading-worked-solution-steps-works-a-cognitive-load-perspecti/)

<a id="s21"></a>
## S21: Bastani et al. (2025)

A high-school mathematics field experiment compared ordinary GPT assistance with a tutor design containing safeguards. Unguarded assistance improved practice performance but harmed later unassisted performance; the tutor design largely mitigated that harm. Supports separating assisted output from independent learning and attending to tool design. It does not show that all AI assistance harms learning or that this catalog's scaffolds are validated. An author-hosted version is available at https://hamsabastani.github.io/education_llm.pdf. [Bastani et al. (2025)](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122)

<a id="s22"></a>
## S22: Milkman et al. (2021)

A megastudy tested 54 four-week digital programs among 61,293 fitness-chain members; effects differed across programs, with 45% significantly increasing weekly visits during the intervention. This motivates comparative evaluation of complete procedures in a common setting. Statistical significance is not a per-person success probability, and a selected top performer is not automatically best in a new setting. [Milkman et al. (2021)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8822539/)

<a id="s23"></a>
## S23: Milkman et al. (2011)

A field experiment on a one-time vaccination opportunity found a 4.2 percentage-point increase from prompting people to write both a date and a time. A date-only prompt had a smaller, nonsignificant difference. Supports a specific planning intervention with an available opportunity; it neither guarantees action nor transfers automatically to recurring behavior. [Milkman et al. (2011)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21670283/)
