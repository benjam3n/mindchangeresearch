# Allow a distinction to take form

Use when an idea is dismissed as unintelligible, irrelevant or unserious before it can become a candidate for comparison. This page proposes ways to make the encounter informative. It does not promise that every dismissed idea contains an insight.

## A concrete encounter design

Invite the person who experiences the distinction to identify one occurrence where it matters and one superficially similar occurrence where it does not. If words are inadequate, ask for an available demonstration, arrangement, drawing or participation. Obtain an actual account; do not author imaginary consent or testimony.

Preserve the order in which details become salient. Ask what a newcomer would miss if they treated the two occurrences as interchangeable. Attempt a reconstruction in the available medium and let the person correct what it changes or leaves out. Their recognition supports an interpretation of their account; it does not by itself prove every factual or normative claim in it.

Only after a usable difference becomes available should a comparison require a precise category for that difference. If no usable difference appears, record which attempts failed and what remains unknown. More verbal eloquence alone is not evidence that the distinction has arrived.

## Worked simulation and its limit

In the [hours example](leave-a-correct-account.md), asking each participant to restate their four-hour total cannot recover overlap. Arranging both intervals on the same timeline makes the relation available. The two cases supply a concrete discriminatory medium. The exact distinction is present in the stipulated data; this is a simulation of making it accessible, not an observed study of anyone's perception.

The next difference might be joint interruptibility, shared attention or a meaning the timetable cannot express. Do not force it to fit overlap just because overlap was the previous success. A new practice or encounter may change what an adequate expression is.

## Do not confuse the commitments

Allowing development does not endorse the resulting claim. Endorsing a claim does not authorize every action suggested by it. Acting successfully in one case does not certify universal adequacy. These differences permit inquiry before settled comparison while retaining accountability for claims and actions.

The relation between a changed expression, a changed body, a learned skill and a changed environment remains open. A bodily or perceptual change cannot be inferred from this text. Its absence from a text-only result is a reason to identify the needed encounter or capability, not to define those possibilities out of the research.
