Current case state: colleague A initiates most topics; B responds to specific questions but rarely initiates. What works: B provides substantive corrections when asked. What does not yet match the desired state: initiation is uneven. B’s perspective is unknown; unequal initiation alone does not establish dislike. No private history or failed attempts are invented.

Eight gaps: initiation frequency; clarity of invitation; topic ownership; response timing; room for disagreement; follow-through on accepted tasks; ability to decline; shared knowledge of desired relationship. A controls clear invitations, their own contributions, honest boundaries, and investment; A does not control B’s interest, feelings, priorities, or initiation. Structural possibilities include different availability and incompatible desired formats.

These are proposed actions, not sent messages. Signals: an actual stated preference, actual initiated topic, or explicit declined invitation. Review after several voluntarily chosen exchanges, not a fixed psychological recovery deadline.

Minimum acceptable in this constructed case: honest scope, no forced participation, and no repeated unacknowledged broken commitment.
