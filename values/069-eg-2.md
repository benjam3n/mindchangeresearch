Synthesis: convergence is one outcome, alongside clearer conflict, bounded trial, informed abstention, and agreed division of authority. Biggest surprise: a trial can proceed with explicit dissent if the relevant authority and exposure permit it; unanimity is not built into the definition of cooperation. This is a case possibility, not a recommendation for every group.

Three later applications executed: a mock decision record says “trial permitted, conclusion disputed”; a facilitation prompt asks for the strongest unresolved objection after selecting a reversible test; a handoff says who may change which object. These are actual generated artifacts using the explored distinctions. They do not show that a group will accept them.

Open questions: which disagreements are genuine constraints rather than misunderstandings; whether dissenters have a real option not to bear risk; whether an apparent reversible trial imposes irreversible time or reputation costs. Next direction: deepen the authority/risk boundary, not force an agreement outcome.

Certificate: the constructed permitted trial with retained dissent is a counterexample to “cooperation requires shared conclusion.” It does not imply that any group should proceed over objections. The specific permission and exposure premises remain load-bearing.

Actual mind change: The candidate outcome space now includes bounded cooperation with explicit dissent. Real cooperative effects remain untested.
