# A shared exhibition with seven different success conditions

Application: 117-collab-1. Actor: this assistant's current working model and generated artifacts unless a constructed case is explicitly identified. Human outcomes are not observed.

Starting working judgment: A compromise on exhibition size and interaction level seems a plausible starting point, but it averages positions before identifying what each party needs.

Depth: Original 8x floors: seven stakeholders, eight assumption gaps, seven integrative options, seven matrix layers. Executed all floors, plus seven-party stress test and three remaining outcome tests.

Interpretation: integrate perspectives. Constructed project: a temporary exhibition of visitors’ changing questions. No actual stakeholders are consulted. Missing voice explicitly included: the future visitor who will see only the final labels, not the design discussion.

Position-interest test for all seven: an unchanged position that fails the named interest is insufficient. A mutable but meaningless display fails A; anonymous but recognizable raw notes can fail B; equal speaking time without relevant evidence can fail C; a log without the preserved originals fails D; an audio file that omits the actual question can fail E; small scale with late reset fails F; concise labels with no usable entry fail G.

Eight assumption gaps: A/D disagree about whether “changeable” includes originals; A/B disagree about whether contribution means publication; C/F differ on whether process time is flexible; E/A differ on whether a visual relation can be represented in another modality; G/A differ on the background a visitor possesses; D/F differ on whether reset removes provenance; B/C differ on whether silence grants permission; everyone’s “approved” can refer to aesthetics, access, attribution, or hours. Each gap yields a specific failure if left unmarked: damaged original, unapproved note, overrun, inaccessible entry, obscure label, lost version, false assent, or cross-domain approval.

Hidden agreements: all can accept a reversible replica trial; raw notes need not be public to preserve the exhibition’s question theme; reset and provenance can coexist in a versioned record. Real disagreements: A’s wish to use a particular original can conflict with D’s preservation rule; B’s refusal to publish raw content cannot be averaged into half-permission. Pseudo-disagreement: “change the work” and “preserve the work” can refer to different objects/versions.

Seven integrative options: O1 mutable replicas plus protected originals (A/D, costs fabrication); O2 opt-in finalized excerpts plus unshared raw notes (A/B, less immediacy); O3 a visible question with an audio/text relational description (A/E/G, extra design work); O4 fixed interaction interval plus documented reset (A/F/D, shorter play); O5 contributor-controlled withdrawal field before opening (B/C, curatorial uncertainty); O6 two-stage review—content scope then encounter design (C/E/B, extra coordination); O7 a small reversible pilot with an explicit unresolved-objection record (all, limited initial reach). No option claims to meet all interests for free.

Chosen candidate bundle O1+O2+O3+O4 within O7. Stress test: A gets mutable relations, conditional on replicas; B’s raw notes remain private, provided exact excerpts are approved; C has separate input moments but cannot force participation; D gets provenance if logged before rearrangement; E gets a candidate second modality but effectiveness untested; F gets a proposed fixed reset, duration untested; G gets a plain entry prompt, comprehension untested. All seven constraints are represented; three outcome tests remain open.

Remaining risks: assumption gap over “finalized”; commitment asymmetry because visitor exposure happens after designers leave; communication debt if contributors think approval is reversible after publication. Proposed mitigation is exact pre-display scope and separate version/permission fields, not a claim of real agreement.

Subsequent application: a generated exhibition brief now contains mutable replica set, approved-excerpt set, access-draft status, and timed-reset plan as distinct outputs. It does not say “all stakeholders agree.” The synthesis is an actual artifact, but no group has accepted or implemented it.

Actual mind change: The proposed design integrates distinct interests through object, timing, and permission boundaries; actual cooperation remains untested.

Benefit: The artifact exposes what each party receives and gives up rather than averaging incompatible permissions.
