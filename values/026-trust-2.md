# Repair after an unapproved public attribution

Application: 026-trust-2. Actor: this assistant's current working model and generated artifacts unless a constructed case is explicitly identified. Human outcomes are not observed.

Starting working judgment: An apology and stronger future collaboration seem plausible ingredients, but neither directly repairs an unapproved attribution already published in a fictional community project.

Source: ../sources/values-trust.md. Requirements receipt: ../sources/values-trust.receipt.txt. Original loaded through read_original.py; byte checks are recorded separately in source-integrity.json.

Depth: Original 8x floors: 6+ party pairs, 3 dimensions plus structural and system analysis, 12 events, 10 interventions. Executed: six unordered pairs in both directions, twelve events, ten interventions, each with costs/prerequisites and a scoped signal assessment.

Interpretation: repair. Entire social situation is constructed. Each fact below is stipulated; trust levels are conditional reliance assessments generated from those facts, not measurements of a human’s feelings. H/M/L/U mean high/medium/low/unknown within the stated task. No broad allegation of reciprocal blame is imported from the source. The corruption-pre-inoculation original was also loaded and retained.

Parties: A proposer; B participant whose words were quoted; C facilitator; D community archive editor. B’s draft comment was published as endorsement although B had permitted discussion only.

Asymmetry and history: The case has an explicit one-sided permission violation. B’s refusal is consistent with the available evidence; no counter-violation is invented to force symmetry. D’s prompt correction repairs the artifact, but B’s willingness to rely on D afterwards is unknown.

System analysis: Public-credit incentives favored endorsement claims; approval evidence was not visible. A possessed forwarding power, D publication power, and B the represented voice. The new approval field improves detectability but fails if D publishes unchecked content anyway. Structural rationality is partial until the next actual permission decision is observed.

Specific interventions, ten:

| Intervention | Target and concrete act | Mechanism, cost, signal and prerequisite |
|---|---|---|
| I1 | B→A integrity: A identifies exactly which forwarding exceeded permission. | No motive claim; reputational cost to A; high specificity, effect unknown; acknowledgement truthful |
| I2 | B→D process: retain a visible correction beside any cached reference. | Artifact remedy; archive work; observable scope, no emotional guarantee |
| I3 | B→A autonomy: A withdraws the immediate new-endorsement request. | Removes a new demand; A loses promotional option; requires genuine optionality |
| I4 | All publication pairs: default approval status to unapproved. | Structural control; editing effort; detectable status; D uses it |
| I5 | D→A integrity: require the approver’s exact scope record. | Verification; coordination cost; narrow strong evidence; record available |
| I6 | A→B benevolence: offer B no obligation to review the whole project. | Vulnerability from A; less promotion; meaning depends on actual respect |
| I7 | B→C process: private channel for factual correction without reconciliation demand. | Separates remedy and relationship; facilitator time; B may decline |
| I8 | C→D competence: inspect one removed quote and one permitted quote. | Different scope examples; review cost; tests archive behavior |
| I9 | D→B integrity: return what was removed and where. | Verifiable completion; audit cost; does not require forgiveness |
| I10 | All: review the next proposed quotation before publication. | Prospective checkpoint; slows release; observable operation; permission source known |

Repair/design sequence: Immediate repair: remove misattribution and retain a correction trace. Next: avoid requesting replacement endorsement. After one future permission decision and again after three publication cycles, compare stated approval scope with actual use. The actual relationship can remain distant even if the process works. The checkpoints are proposed opportunities to observe commitments, not a forecast that a person’s trust will recover in a fixed number of days. Actual trust change would require observed recipient reliance, disclosures, or reports across later interactions; none exists in this constructed application.

Uncomfortable case contribution: A’s desired restoration of collaboration is not an obligation on B. A can incur the cost of accurate correction without receiving renewed endorsement. This concerns the fictional party’s role, not a discovered trait of the real user. Confidence is moderate for the map’s logical fit to stipulated facts and low for any reaction prediction. Weakest assumption: how parties interpret the same event; their actual perspectives were not collected.

Source-claim boundary: this application does not assume that violations always outweigh trust-building, that erosion is always bidirectional, or that every integrity breach should be answered by over-delivery. The original research on response to alleged competence and integrity violations examined different conditions; it does not license false denial or a universal repair rule. [Kim, Ferrin, Cooper and Dirks, primary study](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/lkcsb_research/2368/)

Certificate: the dimensional assessment depends on event-specific evidence; a favorable event on competence does not supply an integrity commitment, and an unknown benevolence judgment is not a negative finding. The strongest alternative is that a hidden event changes the map, so the resulting allocation remains scoped to this case.

Subsequent application, executed: Later generated invitation: “You can check whether this correction accurately describes your permitted scope; you do not need to endorse the project or resume participation.” This differs from an apology followed by a new endorsement request, but no real recipient receives it or responds.

Actual mind change: The repair plan targets representation permission and separates correction from renewed cooperation. The social outcome is still untested.

Benefit: A concrete repair and opt-in invitation exist; no forgiveness, restored trust, or increased interest is claimed.

Verdict: UNRESOLVED

Organization: The content preserves unknown dimensions and unilateral as well as reciprocal failure possibilities. The directional table retains risk and system exposure beside each assessment; intervention plans are distinct from observed outcomes.
