Expanded scope is stated rather than inventing an 8x certification. Five hooks, eight dependent pulls, three tangents, three reversals, a rejected dead end, and a later representation.

Subject: the same ordinary cup, described without inventing its history. Hooks: a handle is both gap and material; the rim can be a border or an opening; the cup is empty even when occupied by air; its front changes with the holder; a stain records use without narrating it.

Eight pulls along the handle: (1) The hole gives a finger a place while material gives the hand leverage. (2) Removing material can make an object easier to hold, up to the point of breakage. (3) The interesting property is not “more” or “less” material but placement. (4) Placement refers to a user’s relation to the cup; upside down, the same opening serves differently. (5) A drawing can preserve outline while hiding thickness, so two “same handles” differ in use. (6) Naming both “handle” compresses the difference that a practical test would reveal. (7) Suspending the label lets the opening, contour, and contact surfaces become separate attention targets. (8) Restoring the label now carries a richer representation, even if no fact about the cup has changed.

Tangents: a catalogue of useful holes; instructions for drawing only the spaces between objects; a room photographed from the level of its doorknobs. No usefulness score is assigned during the tangents.

Reversal experiments: a solid ring with no hole is no longer grasped by insertion; a cup with no rim is an incomplete boundary rather than a vessel; a cup combined with a calendar can use wear marks as entries, though this invented artwork supplies no reliable dates. Unexpected connection: categorization can both enable efficient reference and hide task-relevant differences in a case. The connection is representational, not a measured psychological effect.

Dead end: “the cup contains the universe” produces no concrete distinction and is discarded. It only sounds expansive.
