Hypothetical internal narrative, six sentences:

“I understand that public updates can bring useful opportunities. I still do not want unfinished comments attributed to me. I want room to revise a thought without managing an audience for each revision. A finalized result is different from a rough discussion note. I am willing to help produce an accurate public result within that boundary. More proof that publicity works will not by itself change what I am choosing to expose.”

Twelve additional independent findings developed from the context and divergences:

F11. F1 and F2 together mean an additional outreach statistic does not answer R’s stated objection; information about efficacy and priority over privacy are different propositions.
F12. A finalized result preview is relevant because R explicitly permits finalized sharing; it is a candidate within the boundary, not a substitute demand for raw notes.
F13. A refusal to publicize raw discussion is compatible with continued private collaboration, so “R is withdrawing from the project” does not follow.
F14. Anonymization cannot be presumed acceptable because R’s permitted scope may concern unfinished content as well as name attribution.
F15. If O controls the website, R’s inability to edit it creates a reliance exposure that a verbal reassurance does not remove by itself.
F16. R’s acceptance of outreach benefits prevents the model from explaining resistance solely as ignorance without contradicting the input.
F17. If R later accepts attributed rough notes without conditions, the current strict-boundary model needs revision rather than an invented hidden fear.
F18. If R rejects even finalized results, the stated finalized/raw distinction was incomplete or changed; the model must retain that possible update.
F19. A public-only project can remain incompatible with R’s chosen participation, even after complete mutual understanding.
F20. The least-powerful voice in this case holds unique authority over its own endorsement; formal website control does not settle that scope.
F21. An accurate objection summary can preserve both the factual benefit and the chosen privacy cost; treating one as false loses a declared value.
F22. The next useful inquiry is about acceptable content and exposure, because those variables remain unsettled while efficacy is already accepted.

Model synthesis: R accepts the claimed opportunity benefit and chooses a narrower publicity boundary. Key divergences are F1, F2, F4 and F7. R needs the actual proposed content and a respected scope choice, not an invented diagnosis of fear. A factual summary of the boundary could make R recognize the description; whether it makes R feel heard is unobserved. Predicted rejection of another generic efficacy argument follows from F1/F2, not from a supposed personality trait. Confidence is high for the explicit value difference and low for unstated emotions. Weakest assumption is whether “finalized” has the same meaning for both. Disconfirmers are R’s revised stated boundary or evidence that the efficacy claim was not actually accepted.

Source boundary: the archive’s assertions about universal local rationality and identity threats shutting down rational processing are not adopted as case facts. In the primary perspective-taking experiments, imagining another person’s perspective did not consistently improve accuracy; direct perspective-getting improved accuracy in the final reported experiment. These results motivate a limit on the confidence of this constructed model, not a universal instruction to interrogate others. [Eyal, Steffel and Epley, primary article](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/324232939_Perspective_mistaking_Accurately_understanding_the_mind_of_another_requires_getting_perspective_not_taking_perspective)

Certificate: the model is justified only to the extent the stipulated information and explicit preferences discriminate it from simpler alternatives. A plausible internal narrative alone has no confirmatory force. Every speculative motive remains open to contradiction by the person’s actual reply; no human change is reported.
