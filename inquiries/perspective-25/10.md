Verbatim inquiry extract. Its numbered entries are explored alternatives, not an exhaustive possibility space or new demonstrated findings.

**10. Improve what counts as improvement without appointing a new unquestionable judge.**

A standard does two things that must be separated: it can supply a reason for preferring something, and it can decide which kinds of reasons are allowed to count. The central mistake occurs when its useful first role is silently promoted into unconditional authority over the second.

Fifteen alternatives reveal different ways that authority could fail.

1. **Predictive success.** Prefer the account that anticipates observations. This can reject understanding whose immediate contribution is changing which observations should be distinguished.
2. **Demonstrative certainty.** Prefer what follows from stated premises. This can reject the conceptual reconstruction needed to recognize that the premises or objects were incorrectly posed.
3. **Internal consistency.** Prefer mutually compatible claims. This can preserve an incomplete vocabulary that achieves consistency by being unable to express a consequential distinction.
4. **Simplicity.** Prefer fewer commitments. This can reject necessary distinctions as needless elaboration, while complexity can also hide a failure to understand.
5. **Explanatory reach.** Prefer an account that covers more cases. This can reward a vocabulary that calls unlike things the same and therefore appears universal.
6. **Practical success.** Prefer what achieves an aim. This can reject an understanding that reveals the aim itself to be mistaken.
7. **Experienced intelligibility.** Prefer what makes immediate sense. This can exclude understanding whose concepts are not yet available to the evaluator.
8. **Transformation of ability.** Prefer what enables previously unavailable action or discrimination. This can mistake increased power for improved judgment about its use.
9. **Retrospective endorsement.** Prefer what a later, informed person approves. This can fail when the transition produced the approval it then cites as justification.
10. **Agreement among capable inquirers.** Prefer what survives diverse examination. This can preserve an error shared by their training, access, or selection into the group.
11. **Accountability to affected people.** Prefer what those bearing consequences can challenge. This can reveal excluded reasons, while agreement among affected people need not establish factual correctness.
12. **Correction of characteristic failure.** Prefer a change that removes a demonstrated obstruction. This supplies a local reason without proving that the resulting account is best overall.
13. **Preservation across changes of representation.** Prefer what remains warranted under several ways of expressing it. This can expose dependence on arbitrary notation, but several representations may share the same omission.
14. **Generation of worthwhile possibilities.** Prefer an account that makes further understanding possible. This can distinguish a fertile reorganization from sterile closure, while fertility alone also admits endless distraction.
15. **Exact recognition of limits.** Prefer an account that correctly identifies where its authority ends. This can avoid false certainty, but mere modesty does not establish that the stated limits are real.

The strongest response is initially to combine these standards and prevent any one from dominating. But a weighted combination appoints a new judge: the choice of standards, weights, and conflict rules. Even a rule requiring unanimous agreement gives each included standard veto power and excludes absent standards. Combining judges does not remove the question of jurisdiction.

There is a deeper failure than unfair weighting. Sound standards can divide their work along the wrong units. Suppose a description of who controls a meeting is ranked against a description of how long each person speaks as competing accounts of participation. Neither must defeat the other: their relation can reveal a form of control that equal durations conceal. Increasing the fairness of their comparison preserves the mistaken demand that one account win or that their verdicts be averaged. The needed change dissolves that comparison and supplies a different object to understand. This possibility must remain available before any procedure begins eliminating candidates one by one.

One continuation seeks a superior criterion capable of explaining the others. For example, “prefer what supports accurate understanding” could subordinate simplicity, prediction, and consistency to their contribution to accuracy. This is sensible where accuracy has a determinate object and assessment. It does not resolve the present question when what counts as understanding or as the relevant object is itself disputed. Repeating the ambition at a higher level does not supply the missing discrimination.

A second continuation replaces ranking with specific demonstrations of inadequacy. If one account cannot distinguish coerced assent from informed agreement, and both occur within the class it purports to explain, its claim to explain agreement is incomplete. That conclusion does not require already possessing a perfected account of agreement. The starting account contributes a claim with implications; a difference it collapses matters to those implications. It can lose universal authority without every useful distinction it contains being discarded.

The strongest rival replies that deciding which distinctions matter still invokes a standard. Correct. This continuation avoids an all-purpose judge, not every act of judgment. Its force is conditional and specific: an account that undertakes to explain voluntary endorsement cannot treat the difference between coercion and voluntary endorsement as irrelevant without changing its undertaking. The argument establishes a defect relative to a claim. It does not compel someone who rejects that undertaking to value it. Universal normative authority has not been derived.

A third continuation allows transformation to establish access to reasons that were formerly unintelligible. Someone learning a mathematical distinction can later understand why their earlier objection missed the issue. The earlier vocabulary could not fully certify the understanding it lacked. The transition can therefore precede complete antecedent justification. But this does not license “whatever changes me is better.” The later account owes an explanation of the earlier objection, the new distinction, and why the transition warrants anything beyond its own endorsement. Where such an explanation remains unavailable, the transformation may be valuable but is not certified as perfected understanding.

This continuation preserves neither an absolute predecessor veto nor an absolute successor veto. A successor can explain why a predecessor's standard was too narrow; a predecessor's unanswered objection can still expose a successor's error. The authority belongs to the particular reason and its demonstrated bearing, provisionally understood, rather than to being early or late. That itself is an account open to criticism, not a mechanism proven to escape all circularity.

The appropriate eliminations are correspondingly exact. Eliminate the claim that later approval alone proves improvement. Eliminate the claim that success under a supplied aim settles whether the aim deserved pursuit. Eliminate the inference from joint agreement among existing standards to absence of a shared defect. Retain prediction, proof, coherence, consequences, and testimony wherever their bearing can be established. Their limits do not make them worthless, and their usefulness does not restore unconditional jurisdiction.

What would exceed our present standards? An account that initially looks less simple because it exposes a difference we cannot afford to omit; one that suspends an apparently decisive answer because the question conflates incompatible demands; one that changes what evidence can establish without denying any observation; one that reveals a concern whose significance our existing ranking cannot express. These are structural possibilities, not a claim that any deliberately difficult or unfamiliar account is superior.

What is established is the difference between using a standard and granting it unrestricted authority over admissible successors. We can remove particular overclaims, identify specific gains, and preserve unresolved disputes without pretending they have been resolved. The exact remaining obstacle is a warrant for final authority that neither assumes the disputed standards nor merely appeals to the successor's self-endorsement. No such warrant has been supplied here. A procedure that concealed this gap would reproduce the original mistake while announcing that it had overcome it.

Disposition: 1–8 and 10–15 survive as sources of particular reasons; each claim to unconditional jurisdiction is eliminated. 9 survives as evidence about later endorsement, not sufficient evidence of improvement. 12 supplies local eliminations, not final ranking. Combining the survivors remains conditional on correct units and relationships. Their completeness and any universally authoritative ordering remain unproved.
