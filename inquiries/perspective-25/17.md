Verbatim inquiry extract. Its numbered entries are explored alternatives, not an exhaustive possibility space or new demonstrated findings.

**17. The immediate verdict that an idea is unserious.**

1. The verdict detects a contradiction under identifiable premises. Here dismissal can be justified within that scope; the same contradiction does not establish that every reinterpretation of the words is incoherent.
2. The idea fails to distinguish any conceivable alternatives. Its vocabulary promises a difference but supplies none. This can justify withholding assent while leaving open whether a substantive claim can be articulated.
3. The verdict applies the wrong burden. A proposed experiment, stipulated possibility, expressive protest, and assertion of established fact require different support. An intelligible proposal can look absurd when judged as a completed demonstration.
4. Familiarity substitutes for an assessment of the claim. An unfamiliar inference is experienced as weak because its intermediate moves are unavailable to the hearer. Its unfamiliarity neither establishes nor defeats it.
5. Social affiliation substitutes for content. The person, vocabulary, or venue makes the claim seem disreputable. The judgment may track a useful warning while failing to identify what is wrong here.
6. The claim lacks a place in the hearer's available distinctions. Apparent nonsense can mark failure of translation into the existing account rather than absence of a possible coherent account.
7. Dismissal means that examination would cost too much. A decision about attention is then expressed as a verdict about truth or importance. The suppressed claim is “I will not investigate this now.”
8. The claim makes an unsupported jump in scope. A useful local relation becomes a universal explanation. The seriousness failure can reside in the quantifier even when the underlying connection is valuable.
9. The utterance is interpreted in the wrong genre. Deliberate exaggeration intended to expose a neglected dependency is assessed as a literal measurement. Conversely, rhetorical status can conceal an actual false assertion.
10. Presentation quality determines intellectual standing. Awkward language can hide a strong distinction, while elegant language can hide an empty one. The hearer responds to fluency rather than the commitment being proposed.
11. A discipline's customary method is treated as the only route to standing. The exclusion can protect hard-won standards while also preventing recognition of evidence those standards were never designed to assess.
12. Moral aversion becomes a judgment of unintelligibility. An idea may be intelligible and false, intelligible and dangerous, or intelligible and morally demanding. Those verdicts require different reasons.
13. Acceptance would threaten the judge's identity or investments. What feels like rigor can protect a previous commitment from revision. The threatened interest explains a possible bias; it does not establish the opposing claim.
14. One weak commitment contaminates a larger proposal. A bundle is dismissed because one component fails, although another component could survive independently and change the inquiry substantially.
15. Conventionality itself becomes a disqualification. A demand for radicality can reproduce the same exclusion mechanism with reversed prestige: familiar truths become inadmissible because they fail to feel transformative.
16. An idea is inappropriate for a present decision but potentially important for inquiry. A speculative design can be unsuitable for immediate deployment without deserving elimination from the space of conceivable designs.

Three strong explanations compete: **competent discrimination**, **protection of standing**, and **failure of available representation**. The immediate feeling can look identical under all three. A person can have a reliable detector of a real incompatibility; defend a social or personal commitment; or lack the distinctions needed to express what the proposal says. The intensity and speed of dismissal cannot distinguish these causes.

The strongest version of competent discrimination should not be weakened into mere prejudice. Suppose a proposal explicitly requires both that a finite resource be wholly assigned to one exclusive use and that the same resource remain wholly available for another exclusive use at the same time. If the meanings remain fixed, the incompatibility is substantive. Replacing disdain with respect does not repair it. But suppose the proposal actually concerns two sequential uses and the hearer silently supplies simultaneity. The same apparent contradiction now exposes the hearer's interpretation.

This gives a usable departure from the original verdict. Separate the objection's explicit dependencies from the idea's original presentation. An objection earns authority over a commitment to which those dependencies apply. It does not automatically earn authority over the unknown interpretations it has prevented from being articulated. If the objection depends on simultaneity, establishing sequentiality changes its jurisdiction. If it depends on a universal quantifier, retaining a local claim changes its jurisdiction. If no dependency can be supplied beyond “serious people do not say this,” there is no substantive elimination yet.

The protection account has its own burden. A person's threatened interests do not refute their reasoning. Demonstrating an incentive to reject a claim establishes a reason to inspect the rejection, not a reason to accept the claim. Likewise, representational failure cannot be a universal escape: “you lack the concepts” is empty unless some recoverable distinction, relation, consequence, or mode of engagement is supplied. A proposal can be meaningful before complete formalization, but the alleged meaning cannot obtain permanent immunity from becoming more determinate.

These considerations permit local elimination without installing a sovereign replacement judge. Each ruling states which commitment it rules out, under which meanings, and which rival interpretation would alter its reach. The legitimacy of that scope remains challengeable. This is weaker than a proof that all worthwhile ideas receive admission, but stronger than indiscriminate suspension of judgment. Admission to articulation, permission to consume substantial resources, and acceptance as true are three different decisions.

Disposition: **1** retains scoped contradiction detection. **2** retains withholding assent to an undifferentiated claim, with articulation still possible. **3–4** are retained as burden and familiarity corrections. **5** retains a warning about provenance but loses authority as a content-independent refutation. **6** remains a live explanation requiring eventual substantive articulation or distinguishable performance. **7** is retained only as an allocation decision. **8** supports eliminating the unsupported extension while preserving the local claim. **9–10** retain genre and presentation distinctions without exempting propositions from criticism. **11** retains method-specific authority and eliminates its unsupported universal monopoly. **12** separates intelligibility, truth, and moral assessment. **13** warrants inspection without reversal of the verdict. **14** requires component-level disposition. **15** eliminates unconventionality as an independent warrant for truth or depth. **16** separates immediate adoption from continued inquiry.

The unresolved obstruction is access to what cannot yet be articulated. No procedure operating only on currently recognizable proposals can guarantee admission to every valuable proposal whose formation those procedures suppress. Perfected understanding would have to govern the formation of recognizability itself, while still distinguishing a missing articulation from a claim that has no substance.
