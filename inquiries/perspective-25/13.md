**13. Constraints that operate before stated assumptions**

13.1. **A difference never becomes discriminable.** Two situations are encountered as the same, so no belief about their difference can be questioned. Inquiry must make the contrast available in some form. Merely asking for hidden assumptions cannot recover a distinction the current encounter does not supply.

13.2. **A difference is discriminable but never selected.** It could be noticed but is consistently displaced by something more salient. The intervention concerns which contrasts receive attention, not which propositions are endorsed. A correct belief about the neglected difference can coexist with its practical absence.

13.3. **Something is noticed but cannot be retained long enough to matter.** The constraint is continuity across encounters. Changing the available records, intermediate representations, or timing might enable a relation no revision of a single belief would reveal.

13.4. **The available grammar predetermines what counts as a candidate thought.** If every admissible account must have an object bearing properties, relations that constitute the object may be forced into secondary status. Inquiry begins by constructing expressions that do not require that grammatical commitment.

13.5. **The assumed activity chooses the question before reasoning begins.** Interpreting a prompt as a request for an answer makes answer production seem obligatory; interpreting the encounter as a chance to determine what requires understanding changes the available next acts. The same factual beliefs can support both activities.

13.6. **An anticipated audience filters what is allowed to occur.** A thought that cannot immediately be made respectable may be suppressed before it becomes a claim. Suspending the requirement for immediate public acceptability can reveal whether the inhibition concerns falsity, incomprehensibility, or anticipated social reception.

13.7. **A felt orientation determines which occurrences have weight.** Threat, attraction, indifference, or urgency can organize an encounter before an explicit judgment is formed. This is a candidate location of constraint, not a claim that every such orientation is mistaken or that changing it necessarily improves understanding.

13.8. **The body supplies a restricted field of possible engagement.** What can be reached, endured, sensed, or coordinated affects which distinctions become available through action. Altering an actual mode of engagement can therefore disclose something that a different verbal description leaves inaccessible.

13.9. **The temporal window makes some relations disappear.** A response organized around the next sentence cannot directly display a relation that appears only across many encounters. Conversely, an account concerned only with long trajectories can erase what occurs within a moment. The unit of time governs intelligibility.

13.10. **The scale of individuation preselects causes.** If only persons are represented, relations among schedules or institutions must be translated into personal attributes. If only aggregates are represented, a singular constraint can vanish. Inquiry must permit changes in what counts as one case.

13.11. **The permitted action repertoire constrains thought.** If the only available continuation is another explanation, requests to revise the activity are converted into explanations about revision. Making a different act available can expose the restriction more directly than describing it.

13.12. **Retrieval preserves earlier organization.** Relevant material can exist but remain unavailable because retrieval begins from the wrong handles. Inquiry starts by changing the basis of access: a relation, anomaly, consequence, or incompatibility may retrieve what the established subject name does not.

13.13. **Premature closure removes distinctions before they stabilize.** An unfamiliar formulation is immediately assigned the nearest familiar interpretation. The resulting account looks intelligible because the unfamiliar part has already been discarded. Inquiry requires retaining the unresolved difference without prematurely assigning its identity.

13.14. **A requirement for serial expression constrains a relation that must be grasped jointly.** Explaining one element completely before mentioning another can misrepresent their mutual dependence. A simultaneous representation, enactment, or carefully unresolved description may be needed before either element can be properly stated.

13.15. **The interaction itself continually reinstates the constraint.** One participant is always asked to explain, another to approve, so every challenge becomes an improved submission to the same authority. The governing condition resides in the relation among participants, not solely in any individual’s beliefs.

The first serious rival is **deeper introspection**: formulate the operative interpretation, question it, and repeat. This works when a constraint can become an object of attention through available distinctions. Its limitation follows directly from the premise. If the strongest constraint determines which distinctions become available, repeating the request to name it provides no guarantee of reaching it. “I found no further assumption” cannot establish “no further constraint governs this inquiry.” The search condition and the stopping condition would both depend on what the constraint permits to appear.

The second rival is **changing the conditions under which an account can occur**. Hold a description fixed while altering one relevant permission: whether an answer must be immediate; whether objects must be named before relations; whether the person must explain or may demonstrate; whether a draft must preserve prior wording. The purpose is not random variation followed by applause for the most attractive product. Each alteration follows from an identified structural dependence. If relations can only be entered after subjects have been defined, temporarily permitting a relation without preassigned endpoints directly challenges that dependence. A different account then becomes evidence of what the old arrangement excluded; it does not automatically become evidence that the new account is superior.

The third rival is **encounter with what the current account cannot predetermine**. Another person’s organization of a concern, an unfamiliar form of engagement, or an occurrence that defeats an expected continuation may supply a distinction unavailable through self-description. Its strongest limitation is that an encounter still arrives through some conditions of reception. There is no warranted leap from “the constraint was interrupted” to “all such constraints are now visible.” Nevertheless, being unable to occupy an entirely unconditioned position does not imply that every position is equally constrained in the same respects. A specific exclusion can become visible without claiming access to every exclusion.

The useful combination is an exact division of work: articulation identifies accessible commitments; changes to enabling conditions expose some exclusions articulation cannot reach; encounters introduce differences not selected solely by the incumbent. Eliminate the claim that every constraint must first be stated as a belief before it can be changed. Altering available actions, timing, or modes of representation provides a counterexample in principle. Also eliminate the inference that every newly available thought deserves acceptance: availability and warrant remain distinct.

The unresolved barrier concerns the proposed guarantee of access. From within an encounter, we cannot simply enumerate every difference that the encounter makes unavailable. To justify a guarantee that exclusions cannot prevent adequate understanding, we need a sound sufficiency argument. Revealing all relevant exclusions is one possible route to such an argument, not a demonstrated necessary feature of every perfected account. An account could be adequate without revealing or certifying all the conditions of its adequacy. None of the three approaches presented here establishes the unrestricted guarantee. They identify places inquiry can begin while leaving both actual adequacy and its certification to be established separately.

Disposition: 13.1, 13.4, 13.10, and 13.14 remain distinct candidates concerning how something can be represented at all. 13.2, 13.3, 13.9, and 13.12 concern selection, persistence, temporal scope, and retrieval; changing one need not change the others. 13.5, 13.6, 13.11, and 13.15 concern the enacted activity and interaction, so individual belief revision alone need not reach them. 13.7 and 13.8 remain candidates concerning felt orientation and bodily engagement; their actual contribution requires evidence from the relevant encounter. 13.13 is compatible with all groups but identifies a separate loss during interpretation. All fifteen retain conditional roles; none establishes its presence merely because an answer was inadequate. Revealing every such constraint has not been shown necessary for adequacy, nor has this inventory certified adequacy.
