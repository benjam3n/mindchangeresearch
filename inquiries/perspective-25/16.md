**16. Before anything becomes recognizable as requiring resolution.**

The following are different accounts of how something acquires standing as a matter of inquiry. They are candidates with different commitments, not an exhaustive partition of everything intelligence could encounter.

1. A concern starts when an encountered condition differs from an already preferred condition. The account works for noticing an interrupted task; it must explain how the first preference arose.
2. Something becomes consequential before anyone wants anything concerning it. Discovering an irreversible change can create concern. Existing preferences need not explain its arrival, although whether the change matters remains a separate question.
3. An attractive possibility creates the concern. Someone content with an activity encounters a previously inconceivable way of continuing it. Nothing had to be experienced as missing beforehand.
4. A newly available distinction creates an inquiry. Two previously interchangeable circumstances become distinguishable, and the distinction changes which future actions make sense. The inquiry begins with differentiation rather than dissatisfaction.
5. Participation creates a new stake. Becoming involved in an activity can produce an attachment that was neither an antecedent goal nor a discovered hidden preference. Inquiry can change who has interests.
6. An encounter creates an obligation. Learning that another person depends on one's undertaking can make something matter without making it attractive. The origin of concern cannot then be reduced to personal preference satisfaction.
7. An expressive means creates an answerable question. A notation, gesture, instrument, or coordinated practice makes a difference articulable for the first time. What can be asked depends on what can be expressed or done.
8. An available action turns a tolerated condition into a matter for intervention. A difficulty can remain unnoticed as such until a possible departure becomes available. Possibility helps constitute the experienced deficiency.
9. A practice teaches what deserves notice. What a participant experiences as an obvious concern can be an acquired requirement of the practice, including requirements that are arbitrary or destructive.
10. A change in who counts as affected creates the concern. Expanding or contracting the relevant community can transform an acceptable event into an intolerable one while leaving its physical description unchanged.
11. A change in temporal reach creates the concern. An activity appears adequate over one interval and self-defeating over another. Inquiry can begin by discovering which duration its earlier judgment silently privileged.
12. An interruption acquires attention before its significance is understood. Concern need not arrive already classified as threat, opportunity, error, or obligation. Inquiry can concern what kind of interruption has occurred.
13. Bodily availability determines what can matter in practice. A difference can be verbally understood while failing to recruit attention or action. Changing that availability can initiate an inquiry without adding a proposition.
14. Competition for attention makes an issue emerge. Something becomes a named concern through recurrent displacement of other activities. Its apparent importance may reflect its success in securing attention rather than its warrant.
15. An utterance creates an undertaking. A promise, invitation, or declaration can establish something that subsequently demands thought. Inquiry sometimes responds to a reality partly created by speaking.
16. Ongoing activity differentiates into concern, inquirer, and object together. There need not first be an independently specified person holding a question about an independently specified thing. Their boundaries can emerge through the encounter.

Consider three distinct general accounts. Under **antecedent preference**, every concern ultimately derives from a prior ranking. Under **encountered consequence**, an event can disclose a reason that the person's existing ranking failed to register. Under **formation through participation**, the encounter can help create the person whose ranking subsequently matters. They become incompatible where an account requires every concern to exist as a ranked interest before the encounter and another denies that requirement; without such exclusive commitments, these processes can coexist.

Their difference is substantial. Suppose someone joins an unfamiliar practice and later cares deeply about continuing it. Antecedent preference interprets the development as satisfaction of an earlier preference, perhaps for discovery. Encountered consequence interprets it as recognition of a value already present. Formation through participation interprets it as the emergence of a commitment that did not previously exist in that person. All three can accommodate the sentence “I discovered something I care about.” That sentence does not settle their disagreement.

Calling every later commitment a previously hidden preference makes the first account impossible to distinguish from its competitors. It loses its explanation of origin unless it specifies what would establish that the preference existed independently of the commitment it supposedly explains. Conversely, novelty alone does not establish the third account: a person can newly notice an old commitment. Nor does felt obligation establish the second account's strongest claim that an independently authoritative reason has been discovered. Compulsion, habit, and manipulation can also feel authoritative.

There is nevertheless a definite result. An account that requires the identified concern, inquirer, and criterion to remain fixed throughout excludes three distinct possibilities: discovering what deserves concern, becoming someone with different concerns, and changing the boundaries of the undertaking. Beginning provisionally with these terms does not itself exclude their revision. Adding more effective responses while requiring the original terms to remain fixed does not recover those exclusions.

This does not establish that every generated concern deserves pursuit. The genesis of a concern and its authority are separate matters. If a repeated distraction becomes compelling, its success at recruiting attention does not establish a reason to organize life around it. If an encounter creates a commitment, that commitment can still be exploitative. A complete account must explain both how something can acquire standing and how that standing can be revised without reducing the revision to the interests that existed first.

The disposition is therefore specific: **1** remains useful for established tasks; its universal hidden-preference extension is eliminated as an explanation without independent content. **2** remains a rival account of discovered reasons. **3** is retained as possibility preceding dissatisfaction. **4** is retained as differentiation preceding an articulated target. **5** remains a rival to the claim that all interests preexist inquiry. **6** is retained as a distinction between obligation and attraction; objective authority remains unsettled. **7–8** are compatible conditions on expressibility and action. **9** is retained as a possible source of both competence and distortion. **10–11** retain the revision of affected parties and duration. **12** preserves concern before classification. **13** preserves practical availability beyond verbal assent. **14** explains acquired prominence without justifying it. **15** retains the creation of undertakings. **16** remains a stronger rival to accounts that require a preidentified agent and object.

The obstruction to perfected understanding is now exact: we lack an account of how newly constituted concerns and newly constituted inquirers acquire justified authority over antecedent ones. Assuming that the earlier person must approve every transformation settles that question by preventing some of its possible answers.
