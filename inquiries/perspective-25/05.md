**5. Perspective is crippled when treated as thoughts and sentences**

Begin with a stronger object than an expressed account: the conditions under which distinctions can occur, carry meaning, matter, and enter conduct. A person can accurately repeat a sentence while the situation still offers them no corresponding discrimination or possibility. On this starting point, changing what is said may be only one route to changing how the matter exists for them.

1. **Availability to perception.** Some differences never become available through the person's present mode of encounter. A diagram, movement, change of scale, or differently arranged environment can disclose what prose leaves unavailable. The change concerns access to a difference before any proposition about it is assessed.
2. **Selection within what is available.** A difference can be perceptible yet consistently unselected. Understanding then requires changing what receives sustained attention. This is distinct from access: the relevant feature is already present in experience, but does not become something around which subsequent activity is organized.
3. **Felt significance.** A person can notice and describe a condition without its consequences becoming compelling. The inadequacy concerns how recognition affects what matters now. This is not equivalent to irrationality; abstract recognition and practical significance can be differently organized, and changing either can leave the other intact.
4. **Intelligibility through acquired discrimination.** A novice can encounter every mark an expert encounters while being unable to see their organized relationship. Meaning depends on developed discrimination. Describing the relationship may initiate development, but the completed account and the acquired ability to distinguish it are different achievements.
5. **Bodily possibilities for action.** The same environment offers different executable actions to differently situated bodies. An opening can be visible and understood without constituting an available route. Improving the practical perspective can therefore require changing access, equipment, bodily ability, or the environment through which the action would occur.
6. **Temporal continuity.** Something can become clear momentarily without remaining available long enough to organize conduct. What matters is retention, return, and continuity across changing conditions. A perspective understood as a static set of thoughts overlooks whether the understanding survives the transitions through which it must operate.
7. **Background expectations.** Experience is organized partly by what counts as normal, exceptional, threatening, permitted, or worth approaching before an explicit judgment is made. An account can dispute a stated belief while leaving this background intact. The relevant change concerns the conditions under which a judgment first becomes natural.
8. **Practical competence.** Some understanding consists in coordinated responsiveness: doing the appropriate thing as the situation changes. Accurate verbal descriptions can coexist with inability to participate. The distinction is between an account available for recitation and a discrimination capable of governing conduct.
9. **Social standing.** What a person can ask, acknowledge, or pursue depends partly on how their participation is recognized. A question available privately may be unavailable as a legitimate public contribution. Altering a perspective may require changing the relationship that makes a contribution intelligible and consequential to others.
10. **Material conditions of continuation.** Time, space, tools, and sustained access can determine whether an initially accessible understanding can develop. The person need not lack a good idea. The relevant mode of inquiry may have no place to continue within their circumstances. Changing those circumstances changes what understanding can become.
11. **Affective organization.** The same proposition can be encountered as invitation, accusation, promise, or danger. Such differences can govern inquiry before explicit assent. They should neither be automatically trusted nor dismissed as noise: the question is what they disclose, what they obscure, and how they organize further access.
12. **Shared practice constitutes meaning.** The significance of an action can depend on rules and histories that no isolated brain state determines by itself. A gesture counts as a promise or a joke within an ongoing relationship. Improving understanding can require repairing the practice that makes the gesture mean what it does.
13. **Perspective is an embodied organization.** Thoughts and sentences are expressions or components of the body's organized responsiveness. On this account, bodily changes can directly constitute perspective changes, rather than merely influence a separate mental object. It must explain how the same understanding can survive many bodily changes and differ within superficially similar bodily states.
14. **Perspective belongs to the encounter.** Instead of locating it wholly inside the person, locate the relevant organization in the relation among person, activity, surroundings, and others. Changing the environment can then change the perspective without first changing a private account. This challenges individual ownership of perspective, while making its boundaries harder to specify.

Three strong accounts disagree about the relation between embodiment and understanding.

The **representational account with bodily support** retains a distinction between perspective and its enabling conditions. Better sensory access, sustained attention, or practical opportunity can make a superior understanding possible, but those changes do not themselves constitute the understanding. Its strength is discrimination: every change in the body is not thereby a change of perspective. Its weakness is that it can classify skilled responsiveness and experienced possibility as mere supports for an inner representation that may never independently exist.

The **embodied organization account** places some capacities within the perspective itself. Consider an escape route. Someone may correctly state where it is and understand what a route is, yet be unable to traverse it. If the relevant inquiry concerns what escape is possible for that person, bodily ability belongs to what an adequate understanding must account for. The further embodied proposal is that some action capacities also constitute understanding; their relevance alone does not establish that proposal. Its strength is taking seriously an understanding enacted through responsiveness. Its danger is overextension: the route's availability and the person's understanding of its availability are still distinguishable. A bodily limitation can remain after it is completely understood. The account must not erase that distinction by making “understanding” mean total control over circumstances.

The **relational account** goes further. A route can become usable through a ramp, assistance, or a changed arrangement without altering the person's verbal beliefs or intrinsic abilities. If perspective concerns what becomes possible in participation, the environment belongs to its constitution. Yet counterfactual dependence alone is insufficient to establish membership: understanding also depends on many remote background conditions. The account needs a principled boundary, perhaps specified by the organization directly responsible for the relevant discrimination or action, rather than including everything on which existence depends.

What survives comparison is narrower. The candidate accounts distinguish bodily and environmental changes that enable, constitute, express, or implement understanding; which relation applies remains a substantive issue. Correcting sentences alone need not change the conditions governing their use, and bodily change alone does not establish improvement. A broad representational account remains a live rival to embodied constitution. Widening what is available can introduce distraction; making something compelling can misdirect action; making action easier can facilitate an inadequately understood aim. Improvement still requires an account of what the changed availability permits the person to understand and do.

What is established is that expressed belief cannot serve as a complete inventory of the conditions governing notice, intelligibility, significance, and possibility. The distinction between perspective work and work on brain, body, or environment becomes a question about their actual relationship, rather than a fixed division between intellectual and physical intervention. A perfected account remains blocked by two requirements: a principled boundary around the organization that constitutes a perspective, and a justified account of which changes to that organization improve its adequacy rather than merely its confidence, intensity, or effectiveness at its present aims.

Option dispositions: 1—retain distinctions in perceptual access; improvement requires their relevance. 2—retain the difference between availability and selection. 3—unresolved as improvement; compellingness can misdirect. 4—retain the distinction between description and acquired discrimination. 5—retain bodily dependence of practical possibility, which does not itself establish that bodily ability constitutes understanding; eliminate identity between complete understanding and removal of bodily limits. 6—compatible where continuity is required for the undertaking. 7—compatible; background expectations require examination, not automatic rejection. 8—retain the distinction between recitation and competent participation. 9—compatible where recognition changes legitimate participation. 10—compatible where material conditions govern continuation. 11—compatible as organization of access; its authority remains unsettled. 12—retain for meanings constituted by shared practices. 13—rival to a broad account of perspective as representation supported by a body; both remain unresolved. 14—rival to wholly individual location; unresolved criterion of constitutive membership. The last two cannot be collapsed into an “integrated” account until those disagreements are answered.
