Verbatim inquiry extract. Its numbered entries are explored alternatives, not an exhaustive possibility space or new demonstrated findings.

**20. Every accepted proof and piece of evidence can be interpreted within an inadequate account.**

1. The evidence concerns the wrong identified object. Accurate measurements of individual episodes can miss that the relevant object is their recurring production. More episode-level precision leaves that identification untouched.
2. What is measured excludes the consequential dimension. A process can be accurately ranked for speed while the disputed matter is authority, reversibility, or learning. Measurement accuracy cannot establish that the selected dimension should govern.
3. The record systematically excludes some occurrences. The interpretation treats the observed collection as representative of a larger domain without warrant. Accumulation within the same selection rule can preserve the omission.
4. The categories merge distinctions that would change the conclusion. More examples assigned to the same categories do not restore the erased differences. Reclassification can alter what the existing evidence establishes.
5. The causal account is underdetermined by the accepted observations. Different proposed dependencies can explain the same recorded sequence. Increasing records of that sequence need not distinguish what would happen under a different intervention.
6. The selected scale hides a dependency. An accurate local explanation can coexist with a different explanation of why those local circumstances recur. Evidence can establish one without making the other irrelevant.
7. A proof establishes a formal conclusion whose application is misidentified. The derivation can be valid while the correspondence between its terms and the encountered situation is wrong or incomplete.
8. The boundary excludes effects that change the verdict. A locally successful operation can shift costs elsewhere. Further confirmation inside the same boundary does not establish success under the expanded undertaking.
9. Evidence answers a question whose relevance is assumed. Establishing that a proposal works does not establish that this is the undertaking that should govern. Strong answers can preserve an unexamined question.
10. Aggregation removes a distribution that matters. A true total can coexist with a pattern that defeats the interpretation placed on that total. More precise totals do not recover the pattern.
11. The comparison set excludes a transformative alternative. Evidence that one option exceeds recognized rivals cannot establish its superiority to alternatives the account prevents from being formulated.
12. A value judgment is smuggled into an evidential conclusion. Establishing a trade-off does not itself determine who may impose it or which side should prevail. Additional factual precision can leave that authorization untouched.
13. Accepted authority structures what receives evidential standing. Different weights assigned to testimony or methods can preserve the dispute even when the underlying statements are shared. The weighting itself becomes an object of inquiry.
14. The means of inquiry changes what is encountered. An intervention, classification, or public description can help produce the behavior later treated as evidence of an independent underlying kind.
15. The account cannot express a missing possibility. Nothing is recorded as evidence concerning it because no available distinction makes such recording intelligible. Absence from the record can then be mistaken for evidence of absence.
16. The account grants privileged authority to its own rules of revision. Every challenge is required to justify itself in a form those rules already recognize, leaving their adequacy outside the permitted scope of evidence.

The premise does not say that evidence is irrelevant or that proofs cease to prove anything. It says that the account organizing their significance is inadequate. A valid derivation can remain valid; an occurrence can remain accurately recorded; their relation to the governing question can still be misunderstood. Treating this distinction as permission to disregard inconvenient findings would repeat the same mistake by granting a preferred interpretation immunity from them.

Three distinct diagnoses matter. **Insufficient discrimination** says the existing kinds of evidence are appropriate but do not yet distinguish relevant alternatives. **Misrepresentation** says the present evidence is being organized into the wrong objects, categories, or comparisons. **Misplaced authority** says even a correctly represented evidential answer has been granted authority over a question it cannot settle. These diagnoses can coexist; they conflict when one is asserted to be the exclusive explanation of the same inadequacy and explicitly rules out the others.

Consider a hypothetical allocation rule proved to minimize total cost within a specified feasible set. The proof can be entirely correct. Insufficient discrimination might concern uncertainty about the actual costs. Misrepresentation might concern whether the specified feasible set omits a way of changing the undertaking. Misplaced authority might concern whether minimizing total cost authorizes imposing a particular burden on someone. Resolving cost uncertainty does not repair an omitted alternative. Adding the alternative does not establish a right to impose the burden. Rejecting the imposition does not refute the minimization proof.

This produces a determinate method of combination and elimination. Preserve the proof's conditional conclusion. Separate the empirical claims needed to apply it from the formal premises. Separate both from the claim that its conclusion governs the decision. A finding can then eliminate an application, a premise, a scope claim, or an asserted authority without being misreported as defeating every component. The account has changed when these components cease to inherit authority automatically from one another.

Evidence can also transform the organizing account. An occurrence can make a previously collapsed distinction necessary; an intervention can separate formerly equivalent explanations; encountering an excluded consequence can make the original boundary untenable for a particular undertaking. Therefore “all evidence is interpreted” does not entail “interpretation cannot be constrained.” Constraint need not require an uninterpreted observation with automatic global authority. It can arise from an account's inability to accommodate distinctions and commitments that it already makes, and it can also arise as previously unavailable forms of encounter become possible.

There is a real limit. If two accounts make identical commitments about every observation and intervention currently available, those encounters alone cannot select between them. One must identify an additional consequence, a different available encounter, a contradiction elsewhere in their commitments, or a nonempirical distinction that actually bears on the question. Inventing a score and declaring the higher-scoring account perfected would conceal this underdetermination. Conversely, identical answers to current observations do not establish identical future reach: an account can enable a new action or distinction without changing its description of the old record.

Disposition: **1–2** retain object identification and dimension choice as revisable. **3** retains selection as a possible defect, requiring an identified exclusion rather than a blanket allegation. **4** retains reclassification where the distinction changes a commitment. **5** retains causal rivals until an available difference distinguishes them. **6–8** retain scale, formal application, and boundary as separate dependencies. **9** retains relevance as a question evidence does not automatically settle. **10** retains distribution alongside true totals. **11** eliminates the inference from best recognized option to best possible option. **12** eliminates factual precision as sufficient authorization. **13** retains standards of authority as revisable without treating all testimony as equal. **14** retains inquiry-induced effects where the interaction can be specified. **15** remains a limitation of current expressibility, not a license to assert any absent possibility. **16** identifies the original judge's exemption and denies that exemption; it does not establish a uniquely legitimate successor.

The obstruction to perfected understanding is not merely having too little evidence. It is the absence of a justified account of when a finding warrants changing an object, a category, a rule of inference, an undertaking, or a standard of authority. Those transformations have different requirements. Treating all of them as the same kind of update without preserving these distinctions conceals differences the inquiry would need to govern; a common label alone does not erase them.

These options are proposed accounts with different consequences, not an exhaustive inventory of everything that could become thinkable. Calling them exhaustive would already decide the issue in inquiry 25. Each numbered set makes a finite range explicit; the subsequent derivation identifies what can actually be combined, rejected, or left undecided. An unlisted account remains possible, including one that changes what should count as an account.
