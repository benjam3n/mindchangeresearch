Intended mind change: Move creative attention from the protagonist’s presumed emotion to spatial access and a possible recipient.

Actual starting judgment: My strongest immediate reading of the gate seed was resignation: setting down a usable key refuses entry. That is a coherent interpretation, not a deliberately poor baseline.

Original: ../sources/methods-unx.original.md. Separate requirements receipt: ../sources/methods-unx.requirements.txt.

Depth: UNX has no numerical 8x table. Three layers, four selected disruption techniques, four unexpected candidates with explicit rejections, structural checks and a distinct later creative application are present. Expert surprise is not empirically established.

INPUT, a newly authored seed: “At the locked gate, she set the key down. The house kept its light on.” Task: find an interesting direction for continuing this text without treating the first emotional interpretation as settled. Actor: this model, changing attention and creative continuation.

Known-optimal check: no unique continuation is specified by these two sentences. The desired result is a usable continuation that preserves the seed and reveals a materially different interpretation.

LAYER 1 — OBVIOUS: resignation or refusal. The woman has a means of entry but puts it aside. The lit house remains available, making refusal more conspicuous. A conventional continuation is: “She had carried it for seven years. Leaving it behind was the first easy thing.” This is coherent and preserves the seed. It also fixes the key as her access instrument and the woman as the chooser.

Four disruptions, with their actual products:

| Operation | Removed or inverted premise | Produced candidate | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First-answer rejection | Key belongs to her | The key is left for a person still inside. | The gesture becomes provision rather than renunciation. |
| Assumption elimination | Locked gate is an obstacle to her | She is guarding a boundary from within. | The house can be sanctuary or confinement; direction of travel matters. |
| Domain transfer: stage blocking | Interpretation must be a paragraph | Place the actor on opposite sides of the gate in two productions. | Identical words acquire different action possibilities without being rewritten. |
| Provocation | The house must be inert background | Give the next line to the gate’s keeper, who cannot touch the key. | Control becomes distributed: possession, access and permission separate. |

LAYER 2 — UNEXPECTED candidates:

A. A choreography with no added backstory: first image has the woman outside, second has her inside, both with the key just beyond her reach. This changes spatial access, not wording. Feasible as two staging directions; relevant because the seed leaves side unspecified. Contextually non-obvious compared with the initial resignation reading.
B. A continuation in which the key is a promise to someone else: “From the upstairs window, a small hand opened. She raised her own, empty now, and waited.” This shifts attention from an internal resolve to a reciprocal action. It does not prove who is upstairs.
C. Make the gate speak in legal language about property. Structural difference exists, but the arbitrary voice would dominate the sparse seed. Rejected for need-fit; eccentricity alone contributes no benefit.
D. Replacement after rejecting C: a prop inventory containing two keys, only one of which has appeared in the text. Rejected: the second key adds unsupported plot rather than discovering an interpretation available in the seed.

LAYER 3 — REFRAMED: the task is not to decide what the woman “really means”; it is to make an unresolved spatial relation available for exploration. The next artifact is two short scene directions that retain every word of the seed.

Performed artifact:

Scene OUTSIDE: The woman places the key on the pavement outside the gate. She steps away, leaving the lit house beyond a barrier. A person inside can see the key but cannot reach it.

Scene INSIDE: The woman places the key through the bars onto the pavement outside. She remains inside. The lit house is behind her; the key is now available to an arriving person.

Both scenes preserve the seed. OUTSIDE makes relinquished access salient; INSIDE makes transferred access salient. Neither requires a claim about her emotion. The physical side and recipient became new attention targets.

Structural difference check: the baseline adds explanatory history; A changes blocking and B changes recipient; the reframe changes the product from a settled reading to a paired scene. Conventionality limit: no claim of worldwide literary novelty is made. The local break from the initial path is inspectable, but what a literary expert would find surprising is unobserved.

Later application, a different seed: “He left the chair beside the door.” The first continuation would describe abandonment. Applying the spatial/recipient distinction produced: “He turned the seat toward the corridor. When the footsteps came, nobody had to stand.” The chair becomes an invitation. This is an actual creative continuation using the changed attention, not a report that a human’s feelings changed.

Actual mind change: The continuation now tracks which side of a boundary the actor occupies and who can use the object. The chair continuation uses that same changed attention to create an invitation.

Benefit: Two concrete artifacts preserve the seeds while opening a direction excluded by the initial emotional reading. This is a local creative capability/attention result; global novelty and reader response remain unknown.

Verdict: KEEP

Organization: Paired staging directions make the unresolved spatial relation inspectable faster than a catalog of emotional interpretations. The rejected speaking-gate and added-key candidates remain visible.
