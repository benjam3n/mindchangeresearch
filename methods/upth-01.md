# UPTH 01 — from a spatial insight to a usable creative operation

Actual starting judgment: UNX-01 already produced a useful shift from presumed emotion to spatial access. All six stages below are addressed through observable artifacts, three formal steps, nine case dispositions and a refinement. Human expert elicitation and newcomer testing are unavailable; those source stages remain unperformed. This is a partial original execution with a scoped local output benefit.

Domain: creative continuation of a short text containing an actor and an object. Task: create a materially different direction while preserving the seed and distinguishing authored additions from facts already entailed by it.

Observable steps in UNX-01: the seed placed a woman, key, locked gate and lit house; the first continuation supplied resignation and a seven-year history; the alternative staging placed the actor on opposite sides of the gate; a second seed placed a chair beside a door; its continuation oriented the chair toward an arriving person. These are visible differences between written artifacts, not a transcript of private thought.

The visible decision points are whether to add an emotional explanation, whether to change an unspecified spatial relation, and whether to give the object a future user. The seed does not specify the gate side or the chair's future occupant. The phrase “find a different interpretation” conceals these distinct choices. The observable contrast supports an operation on unspecified relations; it does not show that a human expert used that operation unconsciously.

Tacit-knowledge inventory: knowledge of ordinary gate/key and chair/seat relations is visible in the written staging. Whether an expert can articulate the selection of a salient relation is unknown; no expert interview occurred. A hypothesis worth testing is that recipient selection expands an object's apparent purpose while preserving its identity. The hypothesis is evaluated below as a creative construction, not as a causal account of a person's thought.

The formalized operation has three steps. First, list the seed's explicit commitments and its unspecified actor–object–recipient relations. Second, vary one unspecified relation and write one continuation that makes the variation consequential; label every added fact as authored continuation. Third, compare the continuation with the explicit commitments and with a boundary where the relation is already fixed. If a candidate contradicts an explicit commitment, discard it. If no relevant relation is open, use another creative operation rather than forcing this one.

The table contains four accepted authored continuations, four rejected inferences or contradictions, and one inapplicable figurative case. These are nine inspected dispositions under the explicit contract, not a nine-person preference test. Expert agreement, reader surprise and newcomer usability have not been measured.

The first version of the operation said merely “change an unspecified relation.” Its failure was visible in the map case: changing a relation can introduce a new use, which is valid creative writing but not evidence of what the seed meant. The refinement adds the authored-addition label and a separate entailment check. This preserves the difference between creating a possible continuation and discovering an existing fact.

Remaining tacit knowledge: choosing a aesthetically effective relation, setting rhythm and deciding whether surprise serves a scene remain unresolved. Explicitness is high for commitment checks; completeness is limited for creative quality; nonexpert usability is unknown. Optional GAPF/DXPT/NUSR integration is a future candidate, not a silently claimed expert validation.

Distinct later application: seed “She left the last page on the table.” The operation first records a page, table and placement, with recipient and orientation open. Product: “She turned the writing toward the empty chair and waited.” This creates an invitation without declaring an emotion or claiming that the seed already entailed one. Boundary: if the seed adds “the writing faced the wall,” the same-instant orientation change would contradict the text and must become an explicit later action instead. The operation supplied both the continuation and the boundary disposition.

Actual mind change: My creative working procedure now distinguishes an authored continuation from an interpretation entailed by the seed. It used that distinction on the new last-page case.

Benefit: The new continuation preserves the supplied commitments while opening a recipient-based direction, and the boundary check rejects a contradictory same-instant staging. No aesthetic superiority or human learning outcome is established.

Verdict: KEEP — scoped creative operation and boundary; original expert/newcomer stages remain partial.

Organization: Keep a three-step operation beside accepted, rejected and inapplicable cases. A list of successful invitations alone would hide when the operation fails. The full UNX source result retains provenance, without receiving duplicate discovery credit.
