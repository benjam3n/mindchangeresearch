Intended mind change: Evaluate a reusable memory aid by the distribution of its possible use, without treating an expected saving as a typical outcome or counting the same benefit twice.

# BENF 01 — when a cue earns back its creation cost

Actual starting judgment: A cue can make later lookup shorter, but that fact does not yet decide whether creating this cue is worthwhile. Its use frequency, creation cost and unmeasured human effects have not been combined for this case.

Original: ../sources/root-benf.md; separate original-reader requirements: ../sources/root-benf.requirements.txt. BENF has no numerical 8x definition. All five stages are executed with eleven potential benefits, a full three-outcome distribution, correlation checks, sensitivity boundaries and a later changed-input decision. Integration links to EFRT/CBA/RATN are optional follow-ups, not mandatory invocations in this source.

Input: a proposed cue costs ten minutes to create and saves four minutes on each later lookup within one declared review period. The number of lookups is stipulated to be 0 with probability 3/10, 2 with probability 1/2, and 10 with probability 1/5. The cue's correctness and four-minute saving are model assumptions. These probabilities are not estimates from this user's behavior.

Potential direct benefits: B1 less lookup time for the reader; B2 a visible starting cue when the item is encountered; B3 fewer source openings if the cue supplies the needed answer; B4 less ambiguity about which version to use if the version is retained. Potential indirect benefits: B5 fewer interruptions to a different task; B6 less frustration during retrieval; B7 easier resumption after interruption. Potential downstream benefits: B8 a reusable operation on a future similar item; B9 a reusable cue template; B10 a clearer record of what did and did not transfer; B11 less rebuilding when the source remains applicable. B2–B11 are candidate mechanisms or design features, not observed psychological outcomes.

Only B1 has a supplied quantitative effect. B3 and B5 overlap the same retrieval event and must not be added as extra minutes without a separate nonoverlapping measure. B4 can be checked as a property of a created artifact, but its later benefit depends on an actual version question. Frustration, resumption, durable memory, transfer and reuse of the template have no supplied effect size or calibrated probability; they remain qualitative unknowns rather than invented bonuses.

| Lookups in the period | Stipulated probability | Gross minutes saved | Net after ten-minute creation |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | 3/10 | 0 | −10 |
| 2 | 1/2 | 8 | −2 |
| 10 | 1/5 | 40 | 30 |

Expected gross saving is 0×3/10 + 8×1/2 + 40×1/5 = 12 minutes. Expected net saving is 2 minutes. Yet the probability of a positive net saving is only 1/5. The mean is positive because the ten-lookup outcome is large, not because most outcomes save time. The exact computation is in root-benefit-case.json.

The chance of at least one lookup-related event is 7/10 in this declared distribution. Lookup time, source openings and interruptions rise or fall with the same use event; independent realization is not established. The creation cost occurs in every outcome. No probability has been assigned to human frustration reduction or long-term transfer.

Total quantified benefit is 12 expected gross minutes within the supplied period; the cost-adjusted comparison is 2 expected net minutes. The possible net range is −10 to 30, containing all probability mass in this finite model. This is a model support interval with 100% coverage under the stipulated distribution, not a statistical confidence interval about a real learner. Confidence in the conditional arithmetic is high; empirical confidence in the supplied behavioral distribution is unestablished.

The strongest upside is repeated applicable use beyond ten lookups; the strongest downside is no use or a source change that invalidates the cue. Benefits start at the first applicable lookup. A benefit half-life is unknown; declaring a decay constant without observations would add false precision. If a source changes immediately, the cue can lose applicability immediately rather than decay smoothly.

Sensitivity: with saving s per use and fixed cost c, positive net saving requires N>c/s. Here N>2.5, so at least three integer uses are needed. At two uses the cue must take less than eight minutes to create to produce positive net time. If the objective is expected net time alone and the declared distribution is accepted, creating it wins by 2. If a decision rule instead requires at least 80% probability of positive net saving, this option fails. That criterion is a separate supplied preference, not an inferred user value.

Distinct later application: replace the uncertain use distribution with exactly two planned uses and keep every other number fixed. Gross saving is 8 and net saving −2, so the time-only recommendation changes to use the existing source. The unmeasured motivation benefits do not get assigned enough value to rescue the preferred answer. If a real learner values making the cue as an activity, that is an additional goal requiring its own evaluation.

Actual mind change: My working estimate now distinguishes expected net saving from probability of a positive outcome, and the later two-use case changes the time-only recommendation.

Benefit: The distribution makes the cost of a rarely used aid visible and avoids double-counting correlated consequences. The result is a conditional estimate, not proof of actual saved time or changed memory.

Verdict: KEEP — finite benefit-estimation and changed-input decision; empirical probabilities and human effects remain unresolved.

Organization: Retain the outcome distribution, break-even condition and unquantified benefits separately. A single “+2 minutes” badge would hide the 80% probability of a nonpositive net outcome in this model.

Next attempts: Measure actual use and creation time; compare a cue that is enjoyable to make with a purely instrumental cue; examine an aid whose source changes before the third use.
