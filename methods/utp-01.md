# UTP 01 — an ideal conversation with room for different kinds of change

Actual starting judgment: The user wants both participants' minds and the conditions of thinking to improve, with automatic technique selection. The present prompt specifies many tests, but it has not yet been examined against a concrete ideal daily experience and all seven utopia stress tests.

Original: ../sources/root-utp.md; separate requirements: ../sources/root-utp.requirements.txt. There is no numerical 8x definition. The five original stages are executed below through the full ideal-state questions, seven stress tests, five actionable distinctions and three concrete dialogue variants. Optional integration skills are preserved as next approaches rather than claimed executions.

Domain: an ongoing conversation and its supporting environment. Scope: an individual human and a model's working state, including their tools and chosen contexts. This is a designed ideal and authored dialogue; no human response or consent is fabricated.

Ideal experience: the person can bring a question, a feeling, a creative impulse or a changing situation. The system understands the intended kind of engagement, locates the best available evidence and representation, and selects a useful next move with little unnecessary selection work. A change of pace, place or goal is as available as a revised factual answer. The person can pursue curiosity for its own sake, and the model can preserve disagreement or uncertainty when that serves understanding.

The solved problems in the imagined state are avoidable repetition, unsupported confidence, missing context, inaccessible representations, forgotten useful distinctions and compulsory technique selection. Available capabilities include asking a discriminating question, showing a diagram, preserving a counterexample, changing a tool or environment when authorized, pausing until an opportunity exists, and recovering an earlier useful operation. The relationship allows either participant to correct the shared working understanding; it does not make agreement the success metric. People spend their time on the activity they value, including exploration, play, deliberate practice and action.

Values maximized in the ideal are truthfulness where truth is at issue, meaningful choice, useful capability, understanding, intrinsic interest where desired and appropriate use of attention. Avoidable effort, misleading persuasion and redundant rebuilding are minimized. The imagined ideal resolves the need to choose between accessible form and retained meaning by supplying a form that preserves the needed distinctions. It removes needless interface friction and repeated context reconstruction. Differences in values need not disappear; the conversation can preserve them without losing mutual understanding.

Conspicuously absent are forced agreement, a hidden universal utility score, a requirement that every thought become an immediate action, and the need for the user to select a technique before each question. Unsolved logical constraints are not removed by the ideal: incompatible options under a real shared resource bound cannot all be chosen simultaneously.

Direction: improve automatic selection while preserving the user's valued activity and the scope of evidence. Nearest milestone: use a short selection statement that identifies whether the current move serves answering, exploring, making, practicing or acting, then perform it without making the user choose a named technique. Neglected dimension: the value of staying with an interesting question even when no immediate decision is required. False constraint: a complete record need not be the user's primary reading surface; a concise working view can retain access to a full derivation. Real constraints: unavailable evidence, contradictory requirements and finite attention remain real.

Three authored variants test that milestone on different inputs. For “Is every surprising claim worth investigating?”, the next move is a counterexample and an inquiry-selection distinction. For “I want to stay with this image a little longer,” the next move is a new variation of the image's temporal or spatial relation. For “I have ten minutes and need a usable answer,” the next move is the strongest supported answer and its decision-relevant uncertainty. The variants choose different operations from the actual stated activity; they do not infer that speed is always wanted or that exploration is always better.

Most useful insight for the current prompt: a beneficial change can improve the ability to pursue a chosen activity, including open exploration. A requirement to resolve every ambiguity or accelerate every exchange would erase some of the user's explicitly broad goal. This follows from the ideal's stated values and the concrete stress cases; it is not a discovery of the user's hidden preferences.
