# A procedure can exclude the discovery it was meant to enable

The [exploratory goal record](../systems/045-gd-3.md) begins with an unfamiliar starting point and allows the consequential relationship to be discovered. The [representation-order record](../representation/023-op-1.md) distinguishes observing differences from evaluating them under a criterion. The later [near-guarantee record](../post-allocation/cycle-03/capability/01-spg-near-guarantee.md) instead makes a frozen target state and witness an entry condition. Those requirements are useful for evaluating a specified intervention, but they cannot be universal prerequisites for an inquiry whose target may itself change.

The conflict is logical. Let E be an authorized inquiry in which selecting a worthwhile target is one of the required operations. Let I require that target to be selected before E may start. Then I either prevents E from starting or causes an unsupported target to be supplied merely to satisfy the entry condition. In either case it does not realize E as specified. More faithful execution of I cannot remove that conflict.

**The user can fix the purpose without fixing the interpretation that fulfills it.** Here the purpose is beneficial, efficient mind change. The particular framing—72 state labels, 100 communication moves, a typed transition compiler, or a deductive theory—is a candidate means. Revising that framing can fulfill the purpose more closely; treating it as untouchable mistakes a prior implementation for the governing aim.

Three distinct operations consequently require different starting commitments:

| Operation | What can already be fixed | What must remain open |
|---|---|---|
| Execute a specified change | The intended change and its relevant success condition | The means, unless explicitly required |
| Discover a useful change | The object or concern and governing constraints | The question, distinction, or destination revealed by examining it |
| Reconsider what counts as improvement | The existing criterion, evidence, and reasons for revisiting it | The replacement criterion and resulting ranking |

A new criterion must not overwrite the historical result of an old criterion. But preserving history does not require freezing future judgment. If criterion C0 ranks A above B and C1 ranks B above A, both rankings can be correctly recorded. Choosing C1 still requires an account of why it better serves the governing purpose; neither the old rank nor the new rank proves that account by itself.

The same separation applies to observation. One can first observe that two representations expose different relationships and later decide which relationship matters. The observation does not require a prior benefit score. A claim that one representation is more beneficial does require a supported criterion. The old universal entry rule incorrectly merged these two requirements.

## The prior answer changed the efficiency objective

The prior answer proposed minimizing the number of uncertain psychological transitions needed to produce a useful task result. That is a legitimate instrumental objective for some tasks. It does not follow from maximizing beneficial mind change per effort.

Take two explicitly constructed routes that both finish the same task. Route A costs one unit and produces no additional valued understanding; route B costs two units and produces a valued conceptual distinction as well as finishing the task. If task completion is the entire value, A can win. If the distinction has sufficient value, B can win. The shared task result does not determine the ranking. Thus minimizing changes is not equivalent to maximizing beneficial change per effort unless the utility model excludes the additional changes or already accounts for their value.

The repair preserves direct assistance while restoring the full outcome space. Interest, a new question, a different value ordering, a newly available distinction, and altered attention may themselves be the valued result. They do not have to be justified solely by a later productivity gain.

## Actual application to this revision

The initial plan treated the expanded catalog as the object to improve. Examining the alternative perspectives revealed that its organizing unit was itself unsettled. The revised work therefore derives recipe adequacy, information sufficiency, and answer-preserving organization before choosing how the catalog should be used. It retains the original catalog as a candidate inventory and changes the repository's primary entry point to completed consequences.

This is an actual revision of the current task interpretation and work selection. It does not require claiming that the user felt persuaded or that the model acquired permanent capabilities.
