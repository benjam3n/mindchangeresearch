# Mind Change Inventory

Each of the original **590 entries** now identifies candidate direct contributors and conditions that can make the proposed relationship work, fail, or produce a different change. The **26 functional AI self-change entries** receive the same treatment. Entry identities and original definitions are preserved.

“Direct” here means a specified nearby dependency at the functional or experiential level: an input that alters the target, an operation that realizes the change, a connection that makes one process govern another, or a condition that enables or obstructs it. It does not mean an unmediated influence on the brain. These roles should be distinguished when constructing a particular intervention.

Semicolon-separated contributors are possibilities to examine, not a claim that every one is necessary, sufficient, independent, beneficial, or appropriate for every person. **Unmarked contributor relationships are an original functional synthesis and candidate map, not separately established causal findings.** Source markers support the particular attached statement at the scope described in the source note; they do not validate the whole entry. Physiological descriptions identify possible contributors without supplying individualized treatment instructions.

Coverage is complete relative to the inherited inventory. Both the inventory and the contributor sets remain open to further distinctions. More change is not automatically better: the direction, affected concerns, timing, duration, transfer, and cost determine what would contribute.

**Choosing the relevant contribution**

| What is missing in the particular case | A contribution that addresses that dependency | Nearby change that can leave it unresolved |
|---|---|---|
| A distinction | Cases requiring different treatment, with the diagnostic difference made available | Another synonym for an already understood idea |
| Access to an intention when it matters | A detectable event linked to a retained, specific intended action | Stronger general agreement with the goal |
| An executable operation | Its required inputs, a usable performance, and correction of the unavailable component | A description that assumes the component can already be performed |
| Actual influence over an outcome | An available action with a consequential effect | Language describing the person as empowered |
| Correction governing later conduct | A recognizable reuse condition and a replacement operation that consumes the correction | Acknowledgment or storage without applicable use |

These are separating examples, not a universal sequence or a complete classification of dependencies. The detailed entries preserve other possibilities.

**Purpose-specific systems constructed for this use**

C1–C4 are new local specifications in this inventory. They are not claimed to be unchanged original procedures or already validated canonical systems. Their component subjects and existing source systems remain separately identified.

**C1. Consequence contribution construction.** Object: supplied premises, already available consequences, the current missing contribution, and a receiving operation. Condition: the claim's scope and interpretation are sufficiently determinate for the intended derivation. Operations: identify a consequence the receiving operation can use; construct its intermediate relations; mark added assumptions; inspect any consequential unsupported transition with the applicable reasoning system; incorporate the resulting consequence or the exact missing dependency. Result: a usable derivation, construction, or scoped unresolved relation. Failure: repeating the premise, inventing an intermediate capability, or calling a possible consequence established. Its canonical basis is R3 above; Expression contribution construction governs its presentation.

**C2. Capability contribution construction.** Object: a particular operation the actor needs to perform, its conditions, and the unavailable component. Condition: enough context exists to identify a usable contribution; missing context remains explicit. Operations: locate whether the missing part is a distinction, input, practiced transformation, cue, resource, interface, or coordination relation; supply that part through an executable example, tool, demonstration, corrected component, or other fitting contribution; connect it to the actual operation; distinguish produced support from observed performance; retain the support conditions for another use. Result: the supplied component and a scoped account of which ability it makes available or leaves unresolved. Failure: instructions whose crucial step assumes the absent ability. R6 supplies its canonical determinations; achievement assessment applies only to justified conditions with actual observations.

**C3. Experience and agency contribution construction.** Object: a specified felt, motivational, bodily, or agency-related change and its relevant circumstances. Operations: preserve differences among experience, expression, appraisal, willingness, capability, and real control; identify target-specific candidate contributors from the relevant entries and evidence; match the available contribution to the proposed relationship; construct the appropriate expression, interaction, practical support, or opportunity within the task; incorporate the recipient's actual response when available. Result: a concrete contribution with its intended target and remaining uncertainty. Failure: treating tone as proof of feeling, inferred feeling as fact, or encouragement as supplied control. R7 provides explanatory and response determinations; human findings are not automatically transferred to AI functioning.

**C4. Correction retention and application.** Object: an actual correction, the failure it addresses, and later applicable work. Operations: record the trigger and exclusion boundary; specify the replacement operation; retain the justification and accessible source; retrieve or reuse the correction when its trigger is present; let it alter the dependent contribution; distinguish actual uptake from acknowledgment, storage, or overgeneralization. Result: changed applicable conduct and a reusable correction record. Failure: repeating the same failed contribution with a new declaration of intent. R8 provides retention, reuse, uptake, generalization, and persistence determinations. Improvement candidate revision applies when the comparative benefit of a replacement needs determination.

**Application in this expansion**

R1 preserved the accepted inventory and selected the missing contributor relationships as the new work. R2 produced a consistent target–contributor–condition form. R3 separated access, execution, coupling, and target effect where an inference between them would be unsupported. R4 retained materially different contributors instead of substituting a universal instruction such as “provide feedback.” R5 used the user's accepted scope and explicit corrections rather than another conceptual introduction. R6 supplied a reusable entry-level map while leaving recipient capability unclaimed. R7 preserved distinctions between experience, expression, bodily conditions, and actual control. R8 made the standing instruction govern system selection and preserved its future triggers here.

[Discovery contribution assessment](https://github.com/benjam3n/subjectsystems/blob/8605d44181fab127087ba27c60448f24cdc3b1c7/systems/discovery-contribution-assessment.md) separates what this work supplies from what it establishes: the produced contribution is a complete expansion of the inherited entries and a concrete system-use rule. It is not an observed demonstration that all proposed interventions work, that every possible contributor has been found, or that a particular recipient effect has occurred.
