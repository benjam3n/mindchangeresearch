## Perspectives

1. **Distinction organization** — A perspective organizes which distinctions, interpretations, and responses become available.
2. **Observation position** — A perspective is a position from which some relations become visible and others disappear.
3. **Commitments** — A perspective is a set of commitments governing what counts as an intelligible account.
4. **Attention allocation** — A perspective is a way of allocating attention among consequential differences.
5. **Operation repertoire** — A perspective is a repertoire of operations available for working on a subject.
6. **Expectation** — A perspective is an expectation structure that makes particular continuations seem natural.
7. **Question structure** — A perspective is an arrangement of questions whose answers determine what matters next.
8. **Practical orientation** — A perspective is a practical orientation toward what can be changed.
9. **Lived world** — A perspective is an experienced world, including its felt possibilities and demands.
10. **Provisional model** — A perspective is a provisional model whose usefulness depends on its operating conditions.
11. **Selection** — A perspective is a selection from possibilities that exceed any one representation.
12. **Situated relationship** — A perspective is a relationship between a subject, an interpreter, and a situation.
13. **Interpretive inheritance** — A perspective is an inherited pattern of distinctions acquired through participation.
14. **Communicative intervention** — A perspective is an intervention that changes what another person can understand.
15. **Capacity interface** — A perspective is an interface between existing capacities and an encountered matter.
16. **Certainty distribution** — A perspective is a distribution of certainty, uncertainty, relevance, and neglect.
17. **Response rule** — A perspective is a rule for deciding which differences require different responses.
18. **Inquiry path** — A perspective is a path through an inquiry, including the branches left unexplored.
19. **Provisional settlement** — A perspective is a temporary settlement of questions that remain reopenable.
20. **Participation in reality** — A perspective is part of the reality it interprets because interpretation changes subsequent activity.

## Reality

21. **Material processes** — Reality consists of material processes; experience belongs to their organization.
22. **Experiential reality** — Reality consists of experience; physical objects are stable structures within experience.
23. **Dual aspects** — Reality contains both experience and physical processes without reducing either to the other.
24. **Relations** — Reality consists primarily of relations; apparently independent things are stable relational patterns.
25. **Events** — Reality consists primarily of events; enduring objects are continuities among events.
26. **Observer independence** — Reality is what remains independent of anyone's interpretation of it.
27. **Common constraint** — Reality is what every adequate perspective must eventually accommodate.
28. **Possible interactions** — Reality is the total field of possible interactions and their constraints.
29. **Resource configuration** — Reality is a changing configuration of resources, uses, and consequences.
30. **Open process** — Reality is an unfinished process whose later forms are not fully settled.
31. **Complete temporal structure** — Reality is a complete temporal structure within which change is locally experienced.
32. **Resistance** — Reality is encountered through resistance when expectations fail to determine events.
33. **Social reality** — Reality includes social facts created and sustained through collective recognition.
34. **Representational limits** — Reality exceeds what can be represented in any finite vocabulary.
35. **Plural levels** — Reality is organized at several levels whose descriptions need not compete.
36. **Consequential difference** — Reality is the space of differences capable of making further differences.
37. **Nonhuman organization** — Reality has no privileged human scale or observer-centered organization.
38. **Invariance** — Reality becomes intelligible through invariants across changes in observation.
39. **Unrecognized possibility** — Reality includes possibilities excluded by current habits of thought.
40. **Access distinction** — Reality and our access to reality require distinct accounts.

## Existence

41. **Causal participation** — Something exists when it participates in causal interaction.
42. **Experiential availability** — Something exists when it can be encountered within experience.
43. **Explanatory requirement** — Something exists when an adequate account requires reference to it.
44. **Observer independence** — Something exists independently of whether anyone has discovered it.
45. **Social consequence** — Something exists socially when shared practices give it operative consequences.
46. **Relational participation** — Existence is participation in a network of relations rather than isolated presence.
47. **Process persistence** — Existence is the persistence of an organized process through changing components.
48. **Access distinction** — Existence and availability are different; an inaccessible thing may still exist.
49. **Detection distinction** — Existence and detectability are different; an undetectable thing is not thereby absent.
50. **Ontological admission** — Existence claims depend partly on which kinds of entities a perspective admits.
51. **Fictional existence** — Fictional entities exist as elements of shared representational practices.
52. **Independent abstraction** — Abstract objects exist as structures independent of their physical instances.
53. **Constructed abstraction** — Abstract objects are constructions used to organize concrete possibilities.
54. **Possible existence** — Possible entities belong to deliberation without belonging to present actuality.
55. **Relational absence** — An absence exists relationally as something missing from an expected arrangement.
56. **Unexercised capacity** — A capacity exists before it is exercised if its enabling conditions can be met.
57. **Institutional enactment** — An institution exists only while activities reproduce its operative relations.
58. **Plural kinds** — Existence is not one uniform status across physical, social, and imagined subjects.
59. **Identity criterion** — The identity of an existing thing depends on what continuity is being tracked.
60. **Recognition rules** — Disagreement about existence can originate in different rules for recognizing entities.

## Difference

61. **Practical consequence** — A difference matters when it changes what can happen or be done.
62. **Independent variation** — A difference exists independently of whether an observer notices it.
63. **Discriminating capacity** — A difference becomes available only within a capacity for discrimination.
64. **Comparison relation** — Differences are relations between cases rather than isolated properties of cases.
65. **Descriptive variation** — Some apparent differences arise entirely from different descriptions of the same arrangement.
66. **Concealed distinction** — Some apparent similarities conceal conditions requiring different responses.
67. **Threshold** — Small differences can be decisive when they cross an operative threshold.
68. **Practical irrelevance** — Large differences can be irrelevant when they leave the selected operation unchanged.
69. **Information condition** — Difference is the minimum condition for information to become available.
70. **Constructed division** — Differences can be created by classification rather than merely discovered.
71. **Multiple dimensions** — Difference has several dimensions that need not share a common measure.
72. **Hidden distribution** — Differences between averages can conceal greater differences within the compared groups.
73. **Expected absence** — A consequential absence is a difference between an expected and encountered arrangement.
74. **Explanation distinction** — Distinguishing a difference and explaining it are separate achievements.
75. **Goal relation** — The same difference can matter differently under different goals.
76. **Translation loss** — Differences that disappear under translation may belong to the representation.
77. **Persistent distinction** — Differences that survive many representations constrain their possible equivalence.
78. **Discovery** — Novel understanding begins when a previously unavailable difference becomes discriminable.
79. **Excessive division** — Excessive distinction can fragment cases that require a shared account.
80. **Selective neglect** — Every perspective includes a judgment about which differences may be ignored.

89. **Network primacy** — A network of relations can be the primary subject of explanation.
90. **Conditional existence** — Relations are context-dependent rather than permanent features of paired objects.
91. **Indirect influence** — An indirect relation can have greater consequences than a direct connection.
92. **Multiple participants** — Relations among several participants cannot always be reconstructed from pairs.
93. **Overlapping relations** — The same participants can be connected through conflicting relation types.
94. **Continuing interaction** — Relationships persist through recurring interaction rather than through labels alone.
95. **Relational transformation** — Changing a relation can transform a situation without replacing its components.
96. **Origins of connection** — Relations can be imposed, negotiated, inherited, discovered, or imagined.
97. **Attributed properties** — An apparently intrinsic property can express a relation to an environment.
