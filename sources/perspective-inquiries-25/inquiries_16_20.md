**16. Before anything becomes recognizable as requiring resolution.**

The following are different accounts of how something acquires standing as a matter of inquiry. They are candidates with different commitments, not an exhaustive partition of everything intelligence could encounter.

1. A concern starts when an encountered condition differs from an already preferred condition. The account works for noticing an interrupted task; it must explain how the first preference arose.
2. Something becomes consequential before anyone wants anything concerning it. Discovering an irreversible change can create concern. Existing preferences need not explain its arrival, although whether the change matters remains a separate question.
3. An attractive possibility creates the concern. Someone content with an activity encounters a previously inconceivable way of continuing it. Nothing had to be experienced as missing beforehand.
4. A newly available distinction creates an inquiry. Two previously interchangeable circumstances become distinguishable, and the distinction changes which future actions make sense. The inquiry begins with differentiation rather than dissatisfaction.
5. Participation creates a new stake. Becoming involved in an activity can produce an attachment that was neither an antecedent goal nor a discovered hidden preference. Inquiry can change who has interests.
6. An encounter creates an obligation. Learning that another person depends on one's undertaking can make something matter without making it attractive. The origin of concern cannot then be reduced to personal preference satisfaction.
7. An expressive means creates an answerable question. A notation, gesture, instrument, or coordinated practice makes a difference articulable for the first time. What can be asked depends on what can be expressed or done.
8. An available action turns a tolerated condition into a matter for intervention. A difficulty can remain unnoticed as such until a possible departure becomes available. Possibility helps constitute the experienced deficiency.
9. A practice teaches what deserves notice. What a participant experiences as an obvious concern can be an acquired requirement of the practice, including requirements that are arbitrary or destructive.
10. A change in who counts as affected creates the concern. Expanding or contracting the relevant community can transform an acceptable event into an intolerable one while leaving its physical description unchanged.
11. A change in temporal reach creates the concern. An activity appears adequate over one interval and self-defeating over another. Inquiry can begin by discovering which duration its earlier judgment silently privileged.
12. An interruption acquires attention before its significance is understood. Concern need not arrive already classified as threat, opportunity, error, or obligation. Inquiry can concern what kind of interruption has occurred.
13. Bodily availability determines what can matter in practice. A difference can be verbally understood while failing to recruit attention or action. Changing that availability can initiate an inquiry without adding a proposition.
14. Competition for attention makes an issue emerge. Something becomes a named concern through recurrent displacement of other activities. Its apparent importance may reflect its success in securing attention rather than its warrant.
15. An utterance creates an undertaking. A promise, invitation, or declaration can establish something that subsequently demands thought. Inquiry sometimes responds to a reality partly created by speaking.
16. Ongoing activity differentiates into concern, inquirer, and object together. There need not first be an independently specified person holding a question about an independently specified thing. Their boundaries can emerge through the encounter.

Consider three distinct general accounts. Under **antecedent preference**, every concern ultimately derives from a prior ranking. Under **encountered consequence**, an event can disclose a reason that the person's existing ranking failed to register. Under **formation through participation**, the encounter can help create the person whose ranking subsequently matters. They become incompatible where an account requires every concern to exist as a ranked interest before the encounter and another denies that requirement; without such exclusive commitments, these processes can coexist.

Their difference is substantial. Suppose someone joins an unfamiliar practice and later cares deeply about continuing it. Antecedent preference interprets the development as satisfaction of an earlier preference, perhaps for discovery. Encountered consequence interprets it as recognition of a value already present. Formation through participation interprets it as the emergence of a commitment that did not previously exist in that person. All three can accommodate the sentence “I discovered something I care about.” That sentence does not settle their disagreement.

Calling every later commitment a previously hidden preference makes the first account impossible to distinguish from its competitors. It loses its explanation of origin unless it specifies what would establish that the preference existed independently of the commitment it supposedly explains. Conversely, novelty alone does not establish the third account: a person can newly notice an old commitment. Nor does felt obligation establish the second account's strongest claim that an independently authoritative reason has been discovered. Compulsion, habit, and manipulation can also feel authoritative.

There is nevertheless a definite result. An account that requires the identified concern, inquirer, and criterion to remain fixed throughout excludes three distinct possibilities: discovering what deserves concern, becoming someone with different concerns, and changing the boundaries of the undertaking. Beginning provisionally with these terms does not itself exclude their revision. Adding more effective responses while requiring the original terms to remain fixed does not recover those exclusions.

This does not establish that every generated concern deserves pursuit. The genesis of a concern and its authority are separate matters. If a repeated distraction becomes compelling, its success at recruiting attention does not establish a reason to organize life around it. If an encounter creates a commitment, that commitment can still be exploitative. A complete account must explain both how something can acquire standing and how that standing can be revised without reducing the revision to the interests that existed first.

The disposition is therefore specific: **1** remains useful for established tasks; its universal hidden-preference extension is eliminated as an explanation without independent content. **2** remains a rival account of discovered reasons. **3** is retained as possibility preceding dissatisfaction. **4** is retained as differentiation preceding an articulated target. **5** remains a rival to the claim that all interests preexist inquiry. **6** is retained as a distinction between obligation and attraction; objective authority remains unsettled. **7–8** are compatible conditions on expressibility and action. **9** is retained as a possible source of both competence and distortion. **10–11** retain the revision of affected parties and duration. **12** preserves concern before classification. **13** preserves practical availability beyond verbal assent. **14** explains acquired prominence without justifying it. **15** retains the creation of undertakings. **16** remains a stronger rival to accounts that require a preidentified agent and object.

The obstruction to perfected understanding is now exact: we lack an account of how newly constituted concerns and newly constituted inquirers acquire justified authority over antecedent ones. Assuming that the earlier person must approve every transformation settles that question by preventing some of its possible answers.

**17. The immediate verdict that an idea is unserious.**

1. The verdict detects a contradiction under identifiable premises. Here dismissal can be justified within that scope; the same contradiction does not establish that every reinterpretation of the words is incoherent.
2. The idea fails to distinguish any conceivable alternatives. Its vocabulary promises a difference but supplies none. This can justify withholding assent while leaving open whether a substantive claim can be articulated.
3. The verdict applies the wrong burden. A proposed experiment, stipulated possibility, expressive protest, and assertion of established fact require different support. An intelligible proposal can look absurd when judged as a completed demonstration.
4. Familiarity substitutes for an assessment of the claim. An unfamiliar inference is experienced as weak because its intermediate moves are unavailable to the hearer. Its unfamiliarity neither establishes nor defeats it.
5. Social affiliation substitutes for content. The person, vocabulary, or venue makes the claim seem disreputable. The judgment may track a useful warning while failing to identify what is wrong here.
6. The claim lacks a place in the hearer's available distinctions. Apparent nonsense can mark failure of translation into the existing account rather than absence of a possible coherent account.
7. Dismissal means that examination would cost too much. A decision about attention is then expressed as a verdict about truth or importance. The suppressed claim is “I will not investigate this now.”
8. The claim makes an unsupported jump in scope. A useful local relation becomes a universal explanation. The seriousness failure can reside in the quantifier even when the underlying connection is valuable.
9. The utterance is interpreted in the wrong genre. Deliberate exaggeration intended to expose a neglected dependency is assessed as a literal measurement. Conversely, rhetorical status can conceal an actual false assertion.
10. Presentation quality determines intellectual standing. Awkward language can hide a strong distinction, while elegant language can hide an empty one. The hearer responds to fluency rather than the commitment being proposed.
11. A discipline's customary method is treated as the only route to standing. The exclusion can protect hard-won standards while also preventing recognition of evidence those standards were never designed to assess.
12. Moral aversion becomes a judgment of unintelligibility. An idea may be intelligible and false, intelligible and dangerous, or intelligible and morally demanding. Those verdicts require different reasons.
13. Acceptance would threaten the judge's identity or investments. What feels like rigor can protect a previous commitment from revision. The threatened interest explains a possible bias; it does not establish the opposing claim.
14. One weak commitment contaminates a larger proposal. A bundle is dismissed because one component fails, although another component could survive independently and change the inquiry substantially.
15. Conventionality itself becomes a disqualification. A demand for radicality can reproduce the same exclusion mechanism with reversed prestige: familiar truths become inadmissible because they fail to feel transformative.
16. An idea is inappropriate for a present decision but potentially important for inquiry. A speculative design can be unsuitable for immediate deployment without deserving elimination from the space of conceivable designs.

Three strong explanations compete: **competent discrimination**, **protection of standing**, and **failure of available representation**. The immediate feeling can look identical under all three. A person can have a reliable detector of a real incompatibility; defend a social or personal commitment; or lack the distinctions needed to express what the proposal says. The intensity and speed of dismissal cannot distinguish these causes.

The strongest version of competent discrimination should not be weakened into mere prejudice. Suppose a proposal explicitly requires both that a finite resource be wholly assigned to one exclusive use and that the same resource remain wholly available for another exclusive use at the same time. If the meanings remain fixed, the incompatibility is substantive. Replacing disdain with respect does not repair it. But suppose the proposal actually concerns two sequential uses and the hearer silently supplies simultaneity. The same apparent contradiction now exposes the hearer's interpretation.

This gives a usable departure from the original verdict. Separate the objection's explicit dependencies from the idea's original presentation. An objection earns authority over a commitment to which those dependencies apply. It does not automatically earn authority over the unknown interpretations it has prevented from being articulated. If the objection depends on simultaneity, establishing sequentiality changes its jurisdiction. If it depends on a universal quantifier, retaining a local claim changes its jurisdiction. If no dependency can be supplied beyond “serious people do not say this,” there is no substantive elimination yet.

The protection account has its own burden. A person's threatened interests do not refute their reasoning. Demonstrating an incentive to reject a claim establishes a reason to inspect the rejection, not a reason to accept the claim. Likewise, representational failure cannot be a universal escape: “you lack the concepts” is empty unless some recoverable distinction, relation, consequence, or mode of engagement is supplied. A proposal can be meaningful before complete formalization, but the alleged meaning cannot obtain permanent immunity from becoming more determinate.

These considerations permit local elimination without installing a sovereign replacement judge. Each ruling states which commitment it rules out, under which meanings, and which rival interpretation would alter its reach. The legitimacy of that scope remains challengeable. This is weaker than a proof that all worthwhile ideas receive admission, but stronger than indiscriminate suspension of judgment. Admission to articulation, permission to consume substantial resources, and acceptance as true are three different decisions.

Disposition: **1** retains scoped contradiction detection. **2** retains withholding assent to an undifferentiated claim, with articulation still possible. **3–4** are retained as burden and familiarity corrections. **5** retains a warning about provenance but loses authority as a content-independent refutation. **6** remains a live explanation requiring eventual substantive articulation or distinguishable performance. **7** is retained only as an allocation decision. **8** supports eliminating the unsupported extension while preserving the local claim. **9–10** retain genre and presentation distinctions without exempting propositions from criticism. **11** retains method-specific authority and eliminates its unsupported universal monopoly. **12** separates intelligibility, truth, and moral assessment. **13** warrants inspection without reversal of the verdict. **14** requires component-level disposition. **15** eliminates unconventionality as an independent warrant for truth or depth. **16** separates immediate adoption from continued inquiry.

The unresolved obstruction is access to what cannot yet be articulated. No procedure operating only on currently recognizable proposals can guarantee admission to every valuable proposal whose formation those procedures suppress. Perfected understanding would have to govern the formation of recognizability itself, while still distinguishing a missing articulation from a claim that has no substance.

**18. Correct facts and profound misunderstanding of another person.**

1. The same words concern different things. One person hears a forecast where the other makes a promise. Accurate prediction statistics can leave the latter's undertaking entirely unaddressed.
2. Agreed facts participate in different inferences. The parties accept the same event description but disagree about what follows from it. Repeating the description cannot settle the missing inferential relation.
3. Different consequences occupy the foreground. Agreement on a complete list does not establish agreement about which consequence organizes the matter. What one treats as a detail can constitute the other's central concern.
4. The disagreement concerns ordering rather than information. Both know the cost and benefit; one rejects the exchange. Correcting estimates cannot resolve a refusal to authorize that trade.
5. The relevant time interval differs. A temporary sacrifice can appear acceptable in an isolated decision and destructive as a recurring practice. The parties can accept every episode while dispute concerns the sequence.
6. The relevant bearer of the consequence differs. One evaluates aggregate improvement; another asks what may be imposed on a particular person. Agreement on totals leaves the authority to distribute costs unsettled.
7. The utterance performs a different act than the hearer assumes. A protest, boundary, request, confession, or refusal can be misheard as an explanatory hypothesis and answered with irrelevant factual correction.
8. Different actions are available to the parties. Advice that is coherent from one position can require powers, security, or permission absent from the other. Correct external facts need not make an action available.
9. A commitment helps constitute the person's identity or relationships. Abandoning it is not experienced as exchanging one opinion for another. The disagreement includes what would remain of the undertaking after revision.
10. The parties recognize different authorities for settling uncertainty. They can agree on established facts while disagreeing about whom to trust where facts remain unsettled, or who may pronounce the dispute closed.
11. An experience has different practical force for each person. A verbally acknowledged burden can remain remote for one and continuously organizing for another. Shared descriptions do not establish shared availability of the stakes.
12. Different errors carry different costs. One fears acting on a false alarm; another fears dismissing a real claim. The same estimate can support different thresholds without a disagreement about that estimate.
13. The person does not occupy one internally unified position. Competing commitments can coexist without having been reconciled. A charitable reconstruction that invents total coherence can misunderstand them as thoroughly as contempt does.
14. Disagreement concerns who may create the situation being evaluated. A cost can be small and still be imposed without authority. The relevant question can be whether someone was entitled to make it another person's burden.
15. The person rejects the question's imposed alternatives. An answer that seems evasive may resist a framing in which every permitted response concedes something disputed. Their position cannot be represented as another option in that menu.
16. The encounter itself changes what can be said. Distrust, dependence, or a demand for submission can make an utterance respond to the relationship between speakers, rather than only to the nominal topic.

Take a hypothetical promise. A person promises to perform an inconvenient task. Later they argue that nobody will notice its omission and that omission has little expected material cost. Suppose every statistical assertion is correct. Those facts do not by themselves answer whether making the promise created an obligation. Treating the promise as a forecast has changed the kind of thing under examination while leaving the factual inventory intact.

Three accounts must remain distinct. **Explanatory understanding** asks what makes the person's position causally or biographically intelligible. **Inferential understanding** reconstructs what they take themselves to be committed to and why. **Normative understanding** asks whether those commitments actually justify their judgment. One can succeed at the first and fail at the second, or succeed at both while rejecting the third. A manipulator may understand what makes another person vulnerable without recognizing the force of their reasons. An accurate reconstruction of someone's reasons need not establish that those reasons are sound.

The promise example also has serious rivals. One account says undertaking itself creates an obligation, defeasible only under specified conditions. Another says only consequences ultimately matter, with promises serving as generally useful arrangements. A third says the central question is who has authority to release whom from the undertaking. These accounts can share the same predictions and recommend different actions. The encounter improves understanding when that disagreement becomes available for consideration; it does not improve understanding merely because each participant learns a more persuasive way to restate their original answer.

Now extend the example. Suppose the person asked to fulfill the promise says, “You keep deciding that a small inconvenience is something you can impose on me.” The subject has changed again. The disagreement is no longer exhausted by the isolated undertaking. It concerns a standing relation of authority. A calculation establishing that every individual inconvenience is small could be entirely correct while failing to examine whether anyone has permission to impose the series. The other's position reveals something about the matter itself that the first account had failed to make an object of inquiry.

Understanding from within does not require endorsing every self-description. Someone can misunderstand their motives, deny a consequence they accept elsewhere, or strategically conceal an intention. Their account deserves standing as evidence of how they understand themselves, not automatic sovereignty over every interpretation. Conversely, replacing their account with a convenient diagnosis is not deeper understanding merely because the diagnosis is possible. Where several explanations fit, the ambiguity must survive.

Disposition: **1** requires changing the identified kind of undertaking when warranted. **2** retains disagreement about inference. **3** retains the organizing importance of salience. **4** retains genuine disputes about permitted exchange. **5–6** retain duration and the bearer of consequences as separate variables. **7** retains the act performed by an utterance. **8** retains differences in available action. **9** retains constitutive commitments without making them immune to revision. **10** retains authority as an object of disagreement. **11–12** retain practical force and asymmetric error costs without inferring factual disagreement. **13** eliminates compulsory charitable coherence. **14** remains an independent challenge to cost-only evaluation. **15** requires allowing rejection of the menu itself. **16** retains the present relationship as part of what the utterance concerns.

These sixteen accounts can coexist, but none may be used as a universal explanation of dissent. The remaining obstruction is determining which kind of disagreement is operative when statements, behavior, self-interpretation, and relations support several accounts. Perfected understanding would have to distinguish understanding a person, understanding their reasons, and judging the shared matter, while allowing success in any of these to revise the others.

**19. A more developed understanding might find our central questions badly posed.**

1. We ask how to resolve a difficulty whose boundaries manufacture it. A later account identifies the activity that creates the recurring difficulty and no longer treats each recurrence as an independent unit.
2. We ask which available alternative is best when changing how alternatives arise is the decisive act. The later inquiry concerns generation and transformation of possibilities, not selection from a fixed menu.
3. We ask which viewpoint should govern when the apparent disagreement results from differently indexed claims. The later account identifies the missing conditions and derives where each claim applies.
4. We ask how to unify accounts whose disagreement expresses genuinely incompatible commitments. The later inquiry concerns which incompatibilities can be resolved and how remaining ones can be governed without falsely erasing them.
5. We ask what a thing is independently of relations that partly constitute it. The later account does not add relational detail to the same object; it changes what can count as that object.
6. We ask how to improve explicit understanding when the obstacle is the availability of that understanding for noticing and acting. The later account treats articulation and operative access as different accomplishments.
7. We ask which representation is adequate when some of the required understanding exists in an acquired ability to participate. A description of the ability no longer serves as its completion criterion.
8. We ask how to satisfy an aim that the inquiry itself should transform. The later account distinguishes preserving a commitment, fulfilling it, outgrowing it, and discovering that its formulation betrayed it.
9. We ask which individual possesses an ability that depends on coordinated roles, artifacts, and environments. The later account changes the boundary of the bearer instead of assigning more intelligence to a person.
10. We ask for one explanation where different scales identify different legitimate dependencies. The later account specifies translations and limits rather than selecting a scale merely because it feels fundamental.
11. We ask which established subject owns a question whose construction crosses the divisions that created those subjects. The later account follows the dependency and allows the supposed subjects to dissolve or change identity.
12. We ask which procedure discovers a structure while that procedure partly produces the structure encountered. The later account distinguishes what survives changes of procedure from what belongs to their interaction.
13. We ask for a final state when the relevant achievement is reliable capacity across transformations. The later account demands substantive guarantees about preserving and extending understanding, rather than declaring willingness to revise sufficient.
14. We ask whether something is certain without identifying what is being held fixed. The later account can establish exact conditional necessity while refusing to turn it into unrestricted authority.
15. We ask a universal question whose vocabulary includes every possible answer by redefinition. The later account treats inability to distinguish alternatives as a failure of the question, not evidence of an ultimate unification.
16. We ask for greater richness where a later account discovers a much stricter invariant. What looked like irreducible variety becomes derivable from a common constraint. Development can demand simplification rather than multiplication of viewpoints.

Three distinct future possibilities deserve equal initial seriousness. **Unification** says the plurality reflects missing invariants. **Irreducibility** says the desire for one governing answer mistakes distinct commitments for incomplete versions of the same commitment. **Participation** says some of what we seek cannot be completed by an account because the relevant achievement includes changed abilities and relations. Unification and irreducibility conflict where they make opposed claims about the reducibility of the same commitments; participation can accompany either.

An imagined advanced thinker who merely uses the word “relational” has changed nothing. The difference must appear in the identity of the question, the available operations, or what would constitute its resolution. If unification is the advance, apparently incompatible claims should become derivable with explicit differences in conditions, or one should fail under the shared conditions. If irreducibility is the advance, a precise incompatibility should remain after translation, rather than being attributed vaguely to complexity. If participation is the advance, a specified ability or relationship must make a difference that verbal acknowledgment does not accomplish.

Take the question “Which perspective should replace all others?” Unification might replace it with “Which transformations preserve the invariant that explains their valid distinctions?” Irreducibility might replace it with “Which demands cannot simultaneously be satisfied, and what authorizes a settlement?” Participation might replace it with “What changes must occur in those involved before the relevant difference can even be encountered?” These are different inquiries with different possible achievements. They cannot be combined simply by placing their answers in one document.

Nor does the possibility of a future reversal establish that any current question is confused. That would grant an imagined future authority without an argument. A candidate departure can be developed before a defect in the incumbent account has been identified. To warrant the claim that the current question is badly posed, a relevant difference must eventually become available for assessment. That difference can arise through participation, a new concern, or an unavailable operation; it need not begin as a refutation recognizable within the old question. The old inquiry may cease to govern without being declared false: its answer can remain correct about a narrower undertaking that no longer exhausts what matters.

The preservation requirement also needs limits. Demanding that every new account retain all old questions would prevent genuine departure. What needs examination is what becomes of the considerations that gave those questions their importance: explained, preserved under a narrower scope, transformed, or explicitly abandoned. Abandonment is possible, but “advanced thinkers no longer care” supplies no reason for it. The authority of the original concern and the authority of its proposed successor must both become available for assessment.

Disposition: **1–2** retain revisions of recurrence and possibility formation. **3** remains a conditional unification account, not a presumption that every conflict is merely verbal. **4** remains its rival where commitments genuinely conflict. **5** remains a candidate change in object identity. **6–7** retain operative access and participation as distinct achievements. **8–9** retain revision of aims and the bearer of ability. **10–11** retain scale and disciplinary boundaries as revisable. **12** retains the interaction between procedure and what is encountered. **13** retains transformation-spanning competence but rejects calling mere revisability perfection. **14** retains exact conditional authority. **15** eliminates universal explanation by unrestricted redefinition. **16** remains a substantive rival to the assumption that development necessarily means greater plurality.

The unresolved obstruction is discriminating a departure that understands more from one that merely stops asking difficult questions. We cannot solve this by requiring victory under every original standard, because some standards are the object of revision. We also cannot solve it by admiring novelty. What is missing is a warranted account of continuity across changes in what constitutes understanding and why it matters.

**20. Every accepted proof and piece of evidence can be interpreted within an inadequate account.**

1. The evidence concerns the wrong identified object. Accurate measurements of individual episodes can miss that the relevant object is their recurring production. More episode-level precision leaves that identification untouched.
2. What is measured excludes the consequential dimension. A process can be accurately ranked for speed while the disputed matter is authority, reversibility, or learning. Measurement accuracy cannot establish that the selected dimension should govern.
3. The record systematically excludes some occurrences. The interpretation treats the observed collection as representative of a larger domain without warrant. Accumulation within the same selection rule can preserve the omission.
4. The categories merge distinctions that would change the conclusion. More examples assigned to the same categories do not restore the erased differences. Reclassification can alter what the existing evidence establishes.
5. The causal account is underdetermined by the accepted observations. Different proposed dependencies can explain the same recorded sequence. Increasing records of that sequence need not distinguish what would happen under a different intervention.
6. The selected scale hides a dependency. An accurate local explanation can coexist with a different explanation of why those local circumstances recur. Evidence can establish one without making the other irrelevant.
7. A proof establishes a formal conclusion whose application is misidentified. The derivation can be valid while the correspondence between its terms and the encountered situation is wrong or incomplete.
8. The boundary excludes effects that change the verdict. A locally successful operation can shift costs elsewhere. Further confirmation inside the same boundary does not establish success under the expanded undertaking.
9. Evidence answers a question whose relevance is assumed. Establishing that a proposal works does not establish that this is the undertaking that should govern. Strong answers can preserve an unexamined question.
10. Aggregation removes a distribution that matters. A true total can coexist with a pattern that defeats the interpretation placed on that total. More precise totals do not recover the pattern.
11. The comparison set excludes a transformative alternative. Evidence that one option exceeds recognized rivals cannot establish its superiority to alternatives the account prevents from being formulated.
12. A value judgment is smuggled into an evidential conclusion. Establishing a trade-off does not itself determine who may impose it or which side should prevail. Additional factual precision can leave that authorization untouched.
13. Accepted authority structures what receives evidential standing. Different weights assigned to testimony or methods can preserve the dispute even when the underlying statements are shared. The weighting itself becomes an object of inquiry.
14. The means of inquiry changes what is encountered. An intervention, classification, or public description can help produce the behavior later treated as evidence of an independent underlying kind.
15. The account cannot express a missing possibility. Nothing is recorded as evidence concerning it because no available distinction makes such recording intelligible. Absence from the record can then be mistaken for evidence of absence.
16. The account grants privileged authority to its own rules of revision. Every challenge is required to justify itself in a form those rules already recognize, leaving their adequacy outside the permitted scope of evidence.

The premise does not say that evidence is irrelevant or that proofs cease to prove anything. It says that the account organizing their significance is inadequate. A valid derivation can remain valid; an occurrence can remain accurately recorded; their relation to the governing question can still be misunderstood. Treating this distinction as permission to disregard inconvenient findings would repeat the same mistake by granting a preferred interpretation immunity from them.

Three distinct diagnoses matter. **Insufficient discrimination** says the existing kinds of evidence are appropriate but do not yet distinguish relevant alternatives. **Misrepresentation** says the present evidence is being organized into the wrong objects, categories, or comparisons. **Misplaced authority** says even a correctly represented evidential answer has been granted authority over a question it cannot settle. These diagnoses can coexist; they conflict when one is asserted to be the exclusive explanation of the same inadequacy and explicitly rules out the others.

Consider a hypothetical allocation rule proved to minimize total cost within a specified feasible set. The proof can be entirely correct. Insufficient discrimination might concern uncertainty about the actual costs. Misrepresentation might concern whether the specified feasible set omits a way of changing the undertaking. Misplaced authority might concern whether minimizing total cost authorizes imposing a particular burden on someone. Resolving cost uncertainty does not repair an omitted alternative. Adding the alternative does not establish a right to impose the burden. Rejecting the imposition does not refute the minimization proof.

This produces a determinate method of combination and elimination. Preserve the proof's conditional conclusion. Separate the empirical claims needed to apply it from the formal premises. Separate both from the claim that its conclusion governs the decision. A finding can then eliminate an application, a premise, a scope claim, or an asserted authority without being misreported as defeating every component. The account has changed when these components cease to inherit authority automatically from one another.

Evidence can also transform the organizing account. An occurrence can make a previously collapsed distinction necessary; an intervention can separate formerly equivalent explanations; encountering an excluded consequence can make the original boundary untenable for a particular undertaking. Therefore “all evidence is interpreted” does not entail “interpretation cannot be constrained.” Constraint need not require an uninterpreted observation with automatic global authority. It can arise from an account's inability to accommodate distinctions and commitments that it already makes, and it can also arise as previously unavailable forms of encounter become possible.

There is a real limit. If two accounts make identical commitments about every observation and intervention currently available, those encounters alone cannot select between them. One must identify an additional consequence, a different available encounter, a contradiction elsewhere in their commitments, or a nonempirical distinction that actually bears on the question. Inventing a score and declaring the higher-scoring account perfected would conceal this underdetermination. Conversely, identical answers to current observations do not establish identical future reach: an account can enable a new action or distinction without changing its description of the old record.

Disposition: **1–2** retain object identification and dimension choice as revisable. **3** retains selection as a possible defect, requiring an identified exclusion rather than a blanket allegation. **4** retains reclassification where the distinction changes a commitment. **5** retains causal rivals until an available difference distinguishes them. **6–8** retain scale, formal application, and boundary as separate dependencies. **9** retains relevance as a question evidence does not automatically settle. **10** retains distribution alongside true totals. **11** eliminates the inference from best recognized option to best possible option. **12** eliminates factual precision as sufficient authorization. **13** retains standards of authority as revisable without treating all testimony as equal. **14** retains inquiry-induced effects where the interaction can be specified. **15** remains a limitation of current expressibility, not a license to assert any absent possibility. **16** identifies the original judge's exemption and denies that exemption; it does not establish a uniquely legitimate successor.

The obstruction to perfected understanding is not merely having too little evidence. It is the absence of a justified account of when a finding warrants changing an object, a category, a rule of inference, an undertaking, or a standard of authority. Those transformations have different requirements. Treating all of them as the same kind of update without preserving these distinctions conceals differences the inquiry would need to govern; a common label alone does not erase them.
