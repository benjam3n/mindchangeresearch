# Inquiries 21–25

These options are proposed accounts with different consequences, not an exhaustive inventory of everything that could become thinkable. Calling them exhaustive would already decide the issue in inquiry 25. Each numbered set makes a finite range explicit; the subsequent derivation identifies what can actually be combined, rejected, or left undecided. An unlisted account remains possible, including one that changes what should count as an account.

## 21. Combining our best perspectives could preserve their deepest shared mistake

1. **They are different coordinate descriptions of the same relations.** Their separation comes from notation or reference points. A successful transformation between coordinates makes integration largely unnecessary: retain whichever description exposes the relevant relation. Two descriptions that disagree about an invariant cannot be reconciled merely by calling them coordinates.

2. **They are selective views of one independently identifiable subject.** Each omits a different aspect, so their combination can recover a richer account. This requires showing that the aspects belong to the same subject and that their descriptions coexist. Two observations of one object qualify; two incompatible definitions of what constitutes that object may not.

3. **They answer different questions mistakenly treated as rivals.** “Why did the bridge collapse?” might request a physical mechanism, a decision history, or an explanation of why maintenance was deferred. Joining answers is useful only after identifying their explanatory roles. The apparent disagreement could disappear without any answer changing.

4. **They make genuinely incompatible claims under shared conditions.** One says a specified event occurred; another says it did not, with the same event, time, and meaning. Integration cannot make their conjunction true. The shared mistake may be insisting every valuable account must contribute an accepted proposition to the final account.

5. **Their instruments partly determine what can appear.** Different measurements may produce data requiring different interpretation or may alter the measured situation. The relevant unit becomes the relation among intervention, observation, and object. Treating instrument outputs as pieces of an untouched object conceals that relation.

6. **Their practices help constitute the entities they describe.** A debt depends partly on arrangements through which it is recorded, recognized, and enforced. Combining economic, legal, and psychological descriptions may preserve the mistake of treating debt as an independent object with disciplinary properties attached afterward.

7. **They preserve different interests.** What appears as a disagreement about efficiency may concern whose burdens and benefits count. An averaged conclusion could erase the conflict while representing neither party adequately. Understanding the conflict requires making those interests and the authority to distribute burdens visible.

8. **They operate at different resolutions.** A pattern can be stable at one scale and variable at another. Their combination should state the mapping between resolutions and where it loses distinctions. A single enlarged description may be less useful than an exact account of when each resolution supports an inference.

9. **Their separation is inherited from a division of labor.** Subjects can appear naturally independent because different specialists, institutions, or archives maintain them. The deeper unit of inquiry might cross all these divisions. Combining disciplinary conclusions would preserve the boundaries that prevented certain relations from being investigated.

10. **They describe stages that help generate one another.** An earlier understanding makes a later one learnable, while the later one changes what the earlier one meant. Their relation is developmental rather than additive. A catalogue containing both does not reproduce the transition through which either becomes usable.

11. **They require incompatible forms of participation.** Knowing a conversation as a participant and recording its every measurable detail can involve different activities. Whether both can be enacted together is a separate question from whether both can be described. Integration at the level of sentences does not establish integration in a person's activity.

12. **They share an unexamined boundary around the bearer of understanding.** Each assumes that a perspective belongs to one mind, then disagrees about its contents. An alternative could locate some understanding in coordinated activity among people, tools, and surroundings. Combining individual accounts would leave that possibility unavailable.

13. **Their disagreement produces distinctions neither could generate alone.** Encountering another account may change what both recognize as their subject. What emerges is not a synthesis of their previously identifiable contents. For example, an apparent disagreement about a student's ability could become an inquiry into how an assessment makes abilities manifest or invisible.

14. **Their apparent plurality depends on an imposed classification.** An observer sorts utterances into “economic,” “moral,” and “psychological” perspectives, although the people speaking do not experience these as separate commitments. The act of combining may repair a separation the classifier created. The classifier's categories must therefore enter the inquiry.

15. **They preserve the same model of what understanding is for.** Every account might optimize prediction, explanation, or control while excluding the possibility that the activity ought to transform what is desirable. Their diversity then conceals a shared restriction. More combinations would elaborate that restriction rather than leave it.

Three rivals must remain distinct. Under the **partial-description account**, the subject is identifiable independently of the views. If one view establishes relation A and another establishes compatible relation B, their conjunction can improve the description. But compatibility is a substantive requirement: shared nouns do not prove shared reference, and apparent noncontradiction does not prove the accounts describe the same conditions. This account earns integration when a mapping identifies the referents, conditions, and inferential roles without discarding the distinctions responsible for either account's value.

Under the **constitutive-practice account**, the independently given subject is exactly what has not been established. Consider an institution's categories of “qualified” and “unqualified.” One account describes their distribution; another describes assessment rules; another describes access to preparation. Adding those accounts while treating qualification as a fixed property can preserve the mistake. The alternative subject is the activity through which a particular ability becomes recognizable as qualification. The original statistics can remain true. Their interpretation changes because the entity being counted is understood differently. This is a warranted change of inquiry, not a proof that every category is socially constituted.

Under the **genuine-conflict account**, some incompatibility survives every legitimate clarification. If two claims retain identical conditions and opposite truth values, accepting both is not deeper integration. One can preserve the explanation of why a mistaken claim seemed compelling without preserving it as true. However, this only excludes their conjunction under the specified interpretation. It does not confer universal authority on whichever present perspective labels another contradictory; reference and interpretation can still be disputed.

An explicit account of which relation holds where could demonstrate how the three accounts coexist. Their coexistence does not depend on our producing that articulation, but it has not been established here by announcing that every disagreement is “partly all three.” A coordinate transformation requires invariants; a constitutive reconstruction can change what is individuated; a genuine conflict requires enough shared interpretation for incompatibility to be determinate. These are different commitments. To claim a demonstrated reconciliation in these pages, the proposal would need to identify which relation it uses and what the others would deny. A less articulable reconciliation remains possible without thereby being demonstrated here.

The warranted elimination is the rule **“preserve every strongest perspective by combining its conclusions.”** That rule fails whenever the conclusions genuinely contradict. The warranted preservation is narrower: a rejected conclusion can still disclose an unnoticed question, experience, or condition of intelligibility. The unresolved obstacle is how to recognize a shared mistake that shapes the very relations by which the views are compared. None of the fifteen options guarantees escape from that mistake. A further encounter may have to change what counts as a perspective, a disagreement, or a subject before comparison becomes adequate.

Disposition of all options: **1** permits equivalence only with an adequate transformation. **2, 3, 8** permit combination only after establishing shared reference, distinct explanatory roles, or a relation between resolutions. **4** excludes the conjunction of genuine contradictions. **5, 6, 13** remain candidates for changing the subject's individuation; none establishes that every subject is constituted in this way. **7** preserves conflicts of interest as matters requiring understanding, without treating sincerity as factual correctness. **9, 14** remain explanations of apparent separations that require case-specific support. **10, 11, 12** identify developmental, participatory, and distributed relations that additive integration does not establish. **15** remains a live diagnosis of a shared restriction, requiring an actual demonstration in the accounts at issue. No option has been selected as the universal explanation.

## 22. Assume perfected perspective is possible; reconsider what “perfected” means

1. **Perfected means a completely true account of everything.** No relevant qualification limits its scope. This keeps the strongest ambition explicit, but truth alone does not establish that the account is intelligible, accessible, or capable of guiding anything. A complete inscription and a complete understanding are different candidates.

2. **Perfected means complete understanding of every relation that matters, including why it matters.** This exceeds a list of facts by requiring their intelligibility. It leaves a serious question: are there facts that do not matter in any respect, and who or what could establish that exclusion without reinstating a limited judge?

3. **Perfected means nothing is misrepresented as what it is not.** Every distinction, uncertainty, absence, and dependency has its proper status. This could allow unknown facts if they are understood exactly as unknown. It is weaker than knowing all facts, so it cannot quietly substitute for that stronger claim.

4. **Perfected means universal adequacy across every possible occasion of understanding.** The understanding can encounter any situation without a relevant deficiency. This is a disposition extending beyond currently exercised knowledge. Establishing present success on many occasions would not establish the universal claim.

5. **Perfected means exact adequacy to one fully specified occasion.** A proof or judgment might be perfect for its task while the bearer remains ignorant elsewhere. This is a serious kind of perfection, but it satisfies the unrestricted ambition only if the unrestricted ambition was itself shown to be confused, rather than silently narrowed.

6. **Perfected means a form that generates the exact understanding required as conditions change.** Its content can change while its way of responding contains no deficiency. Mere willingness or ability to revise is insufficient: the generated understanding must actually be adequate in every included case.

7. **Perfected means complete present understanding that includes its own future transformations.** Change would unfold an already adequate understanding rather than repair it. This is stronger than adaptation after surprise. It raises whether anticipated transformations can themselves alter the future they describe.

8. **Perfected means an exact relation between understanding and what is understood.** Perfection belongs to a relation, not an isolated mental object. A description could become inadequate solely because its object changes. The candidate must explain which relations count as exact without replacing the ambition with a convenient local metric.

9. **Perfected means the removal of every avoidable obstruction to understanding.** Ignorance caused by inaccessible facts might remain. This identifies a substantive practical target, but it is conditional perfection under constraints. The possibility that the constraints themselves can change prevents “unavoidable” from being declared too quickly.

10. **Perfected means complete mastery of how understanding comes into existence.** Particular conclusions follow from that mastery when needed. Yet mastery of formation does not automatically imply complete factual knowledge, just as knowing a language does not imply knowing every true sentence expressible in it.

11. **Perfected means the absence of any better successor.** This is a maximality claim. It leaves open whether several incomparable maximal forms exist and whether a form can be maximal because no better successor is reachable rather than because nothing better is possible.

12. **Perfected means every comparison is itself adequately understood.** A perfected perspective would understand why one perspective is better, worse, equivalent, or incomparable without privileging an inadequate standard. This moves perfection into the authority of evaluation; it does not settle the original subject merely by settling comparisons.

13. **Perfected means exact coordination among different situated understandings.** No individual needs to contain the whole, provided nothing required is missing from their organized relations. This must confront disagreements, access failures, and failures of coordination as actual defects, rather than counting mere diversity as perfection.

14. **Perfected means the activity and its understanding no longer come apart where their separation creates inadequacy.** A person can state the correct relation yet act through a contrary one. On this account, perfection concerns operative understanding as well as assent. It does not entail unlimited control over physical events.

15. **Perfected means certainty, completeness, finality, and permanence coincide.** This familiar maximal package is a distinct hypothesis. Each property requires an argument connecting it to the others. An unchanging conviction can be false; a correct temporary judgment can concern a changing state; complete conditional understanding need not produce certainty about an unobserved condition.

The **complete-content account** must first distinguish possessing all true propositions from understanding them. Suppose, conditionally, a person has access to a complete collection but cannot distinguish which statement bears on the present inquiry. The collection is complete, yet the person's understanding is deficient. Therefore, if perfected perspective concerns understanding, propositional completeness cannot by itself be sufficient. It might remain necessary under the strongest interpretation. Adding relevance, inference, intelligibility, and access changes the candidate from a perfect archive into a perfect activity of understanding. This does not refute complete understanding; it identifies what its completeness must include.

The **universally adequate formation account** offers another exact ambition. It does not promise improvement without end. It promises that the response generated for every included circumstance has no relevant inadequacy. Consider changing conditions represented by different present states. A constant answer about the present cannot track incompatible successive states. A changing answer can be exactly right at each stage. Therefore, permanence of content does not follow from perfection in understanding changing conditions. Conversely, a complete account expressed with time indices could remain unchanged while describing the changes correctly. Neither permanent content nor changing content is automatically disqualified. The issue is what each claims to represent.

The **absolute total-understanding account** remains stronger than both local adequacy and flawless response under limited access. Suppose two situations are indistinguishable to the inquirer but differ in a fact the inquirer must correctly identify. Under those stipulated access conditions, the same determinate identification cannot be correct in both. This conditional result identifies a requirement: absolute understanding must overcome the stipulated indistinguishability, possess some additional access, or show that the demanded distinction was misconceived. Declaring perfect uncertainty would satisfy a different, weaker target. The argument does not establish that absolute understanding is impossible, because the stipulated access conditions have not been shown unavoidable under every possible bearer or configuration.

Certainty requires separate treatment. Felt certainty can occur without correctness. Correctness can occur without recognition of its own correctness. Justified certainty requires an adequate relation between the claim and its justification. The possibility of perfected understanding does not by itself settle whether that understanding must certify its own perfection. The additional demand “perfect, and able to establish infallibly that it is perfect” is stronger unless a derivation connects the two.

Finality also divides. No further correction may be needed while further articulation remains possible. No deeper understanding may be possible while new objects still come into existence. A final specification might govern changing contents; a final content might describe an unchanging object. Treating these cases as the same would manufacture a contradiction by mixing their scopes.

The warranted rejection is **“perfection simply means remaining open to revision.”** Openness does not establish adequacy and can coexist with perpetual misunderstanding. The warranted combination is exact understanding of both subject and conditions of understanding, wherever the stronger candidate requires both. The unresolved question is whether any bearer can realize unrestricted adequacy, and what such realization would be. These options do not discover or certify it. They keep the strong hypothesis exposed to its actual requirements instead of making it true by definition.

Disposition of all options: **1, 2, 4, 7, 10, 12** remain strong candidate requirements or forms; their realization and their equivalence have not been established. **3, 5, 9** identify restricted achievements that cannot substitute for unrestricted perfection without a further argument. **6, 8, 13, 14** can describe exact realization through changing contents, relations, coordination, or activity; they require actual adequacy and cannot be satisfied by openness alone. **11** requires distinguishing genuine unsurpassability from isolation or lack of a reachable successor. **15** remains a possible conjunction of properties, but the inference from any one of its properties to all the others is rejected. No strongest candidate has been disproved, realized, or certified.

## 23. The specification must itself be discovered through what it specifies

1. **The specification is an initial hypothesis about the activity.** It starts the inquiry without entitlement to survive it. If its initial goal suppresses a relevant distinction, the goal changes. Its role is causal and provisional, not a constitutional right to govern every successor.

2. **The specification describes an achievement recognized only retrospectively.** We may learn what an adequate transition required through undergoing it. Earlier rules cannot fully authorize the transition in advance. Retrospective intelligibility must still be distinguished from a story invented merely to endorse whichever change occurred.

3. **The specification is an evolving set of relations among commitments.** Changing one commitment changes what others require. No single sentence is the permanent judge. This can reveal internal dependencies, but an internally coherent collection can remain jointly inadequate to what it concerns.

4. **The specification emerges from the subject's resistance.** An attempted account encounters something it cannot make intelligible on its own terms. The encounter may require changing the account's standards of intelligibility. Resistance cannot be reduced to a failed score without settling too early what counts as encountering it.

5. **The specification is constituted by the aim.** We discover requirements by asking what would actually fulfill the aim. If the aim itself is inadequate, those requirements must change with it. This account cannot assume the aim is the one stable anchor while calling everything else revisable.

6. **The specification helps constitute the aim.** Describing what an activity permits or excludes can reveal a different aspiration. A demand to provide correct answers might become a demand to make unnoticed matters intelligible. The specification does not merely implement a preexisting desire.

7. **The specification concerns enabling conditions before judgments.** Changing memory access, tools, language, attention, or social relations may make new judgments possible. A rulebook limited to evaluating articulated alternatives would miss the activity that supplies its alternatives.

8. **The specification distributes authority among participants.** Different people can expose deficiencies no single participant can identify. However, agreement, voting, and assigned expertise are candidate arrangements, not automatic sources of correctness. Their own effects on what can be said must be examinable.

9. **The specification contains exact local obligations with revisable scope.** Within an explicitly accepted interpretation, a commitment can entail a consequence. Revision can change that interpretation, but then the earlier derivation no longer licenses the same conclusion unchanged. Precision need not imply unrestricted permanence.

10. **The specification governs transitions without dictating endpoints.** It might require a transition to disclose what it changes and preserves. This makes change inspectable, but a transition requirement can itself suppress transformations that cannot yet be expressed in its format.

11. **The specification is partly tacit competence.** A person may recognize or enact a distinction before producing an adequate rule for it. Written instructions could then be evidence about a competence rather than its full realization. This does not establish that tacit judgment is reliable or beyond scrutiny.

12. **The specification changes the language in which specifications are possible.** New distinctions can make old requirements appear malformed rather than false. The revision then includes what a requirement is, not merely which requirements are listed.

13. **There are several presently incomparable specifications.** Each makes a different activity intelligible, with no established common ordering. Carrying them forward does not require averaging them. Nor does present incomparability prove that a future relation between them cannot be found.

14. **The specification eventually becomes exact and final.** A revisable beginning might discover requirements that genuinely need no revision. The absence of initial immunity does not entail permanent uncertainty. The difficulty is establishing that a particular ending has this status rather than merely losing access to alternatives.

15. **A complete specification cannot precede its own realization because realization changes the subject.** For example, an inquiry into collective understanding can create new collective capacities. Its later requirements concern possibilities that its earlier activity helps produce. A complete timeless account remains a rival hypothesis, not something this example disproves.

The first rival is **discovery of a fixed correct specification**. On this account, an adequate process has determinate requirements whether or not we know them. An early specification can be wrong, and the process can discover why. No contradiction arises merely because a provisional method discovers defects in itself. A ladder need not remain part of the structure it helped someone reach. But the claim that the eventual specification is fixed requires more than the observation that the latest version works or appears compelling.

The second rival is **formation of requirements through changing activity**. The specification is not only an imperfect description of a preexisting process. Its implementation creates capacities, concerns, and relations to which subsequent requirements respond. If an isolated learner becomes part of a coordinated group, what counts as available memory, an intelligible contribution, or an unresolved disagreement can change. A fixed specification could theoretically describe this whole development, but the mere possibility of writing such a description is not evidence that the current specification already has the right terms or authority.

The third rival is **no complete separation between specifying and understanding**. A specification itself has to be understood, applied, and interpreted. Adding a rule for interpretation still leaves an activity of interpreting that rule. This observation defeats the claim that more written instructions automatically remove judgment. It does not prove that understanding can never become exact. The relevant possibility is that exactness belongs to the realized activity, with specifications expressing some of it, rather than to an indefinitely extended rulebook standing outside it.

A concrete sequence follows without appointing a permanent judge. Start with an actual specification and make its constitutive commitments explicit: what it treats as an object, concern, admissible change, and reason. These commitments can enable a successor without requiring that successor to satisfy their evaluation unchanged. Let the successor develop far enough to state what the predecessor could not individuate or regard as relevant. Then establish the relation where possible: translation, restricted preservation, genuine contradiction, changed reference, or an unresolved break. None of those relations automatically selects a winner. Their purpose is to prevent a departure from being misdescribed as an improvement already proved by the old terms.

This is a proposed way of making the transition inspectable. It has no exemption from the problem it addresses. A successor could reveal that demanding an explicit transition description excluded the understanding at issue. Then that demand must lose its veto, while the absence of such a description remains an honest limitation on what can currently be demonstrated to others.

The warranted elimination is **“we must finalize the subject, goal, and standards before any legitimate inquiry can begin.”** That requirement makes the discovery of inadequate starting conditions illegitimate by construction. The opposite rule, “every departure counts as progress,” also fails: the absence of a predecessor's veto is not evidence of improvement. The unresolved obstacle is establishing the authority of a successor when the terms of establishment are among what changes. No universal certificate is supplied here. Whether such a certificate is possible is itself still open; ordinary procedural rigor cannot be presented as having already obtained it.

Disposition of all options: **1** is retained as a way to begin without granting the beginning permanent authority. **2** remains a possible form of recognition, with retrospective endorsement insufficient by itself. **3, 9, 13** provide conditional ways to state relations among commitments; none is a universal certificate. **4, 7, 8, 11, 12, 15** remain substantive mechanisms or accounts whose application must be established, not assumed. **5, 6** can be combined as mutual determination of aims and requirements; the combination eliminates the aim's automatic exemption from revision. **10** has been used here to make transitions inspectable and simultaneously deprived of immunity. **14** remains possible: a revisable beginning does not entail an endlessly revisable ending.

## 24. Change your mind about what improvement fundamentally involves

1. **Correct false representations.** Improvement occurs when a belief better matches what it describes. This remains indispensable wherever error is the defect. It does not address a situation in which all stated beliefs are true but exclude the relevant matter from consideration.

2. **Change which truths organize the activity.** The same true propositions can support different attention and inference when their priority changes. A person might know both that a procedure is efficient and that it excludes some people, yet understand the situation primarily through only one of those truths.

3. **Change what is distinguished as an entity.** A supposedly individual defect could become a relation among person, task, and environment. The original observations may remain true while the unit to which explanations attach changes. This is more than adding a contextual footnote.

4. **Change which relations make a thing intelligible.** A statement understood as a factual report might instead be understood as a promise, warning, or test of trust. More accurate transcription does not determine its role. The activity in which it occurs supplies distinctions the isolated sentence lacks.

5. **Change what can become noticeable.** A person may encounter the same surroundings but begin registering a previously undifferentiated pattern. Improvement then includes the formation of discrimination, not simply adopting a proposition asserting that the pattern exists.

6. **Change what a person can bear to encounter.** An implication can be verbally acknowledged while its significance is continually evaded. Under this account, understanding changes when the person can remain engaged with it. Whether a particular intervention does that is an empirical question, not established by the account.

7. **Change what can be enacted.** Someone can describe a coordination exactly and still be unable to perform it. Improvement can involve gaining the operative competence through which the distinction becomes usable. A complete description of a competence is not automatically possession of that competence.

8. **Change what is desired.** An aim can lose its apparent necessity when the relations sustaining its desirability change. Greater success at the old aim may then cease to count as improvement. This must be distinguished from simply losing the ability or willingness to pursue something still recognized as desirable.

9. **Change who or what has authority.** A person can learn to hear a formerly discounted speaker or question an institution whose verdict seemed definitive. The content of a claim may stay constant while its opportunity to enter inquiry changes. Increased authority is not itself proof of the claim.

10. **Change the medium that carries understanding.** A spatial arrangement, diagram, interaction, or skilled action can expose relations a sentence sequence obscures. Translation back into prose may preserve some relations and lose others. The medium is not necessarily a neutral container for an independently complete content.

11. **Change the distribution of understanding.** A task previously borne by one person becomes intelligible through coordinated contributions. Improvement belongs partly to their relations. It cannot be assessed solely by testing whether each person now possesses the same enlarged internal account.

12. **Change the capacity to generate distinctions.** A transformation can matter because it makes later, presently unspecified insights possible. Its significance exceeds the immediate conclusion. Yet the promise of future possibilities alone does not establish that those possibilities will be adequate or valuable.

13. **Change the distinction between understanding and its conditions.** Bodily state, available tools, social access, and material arrangement may participate in the understanding rather than merely influencing an inner representation. This remains a substantive account requiring boundaries, not permission to call every physical change intellectual progress.

14. **Change what counts as an improvement.** An earlier preference for simple unified accounts could yield to recognizing distinctions that unity erased. This change may be warranted without proving simplicity worthless. The direction of change cannot be certified simply by measuring the successor's simplicity.

15. **Change whether the inquiry should continue in its current form.** An account can be true and useful without deserving to organize the next investigation. Departure may follow from an untouched possibility rather than a discovered error. Continuing to elaborate correctness can itself occupy the resources needed for deeper understanding.

My strongest initial account would be: improvement changes representations and their organization so that understanding becomes more accurate, accessible, and capable of supporting appropriate action. It already includes relevance and access, so defeating a caricature limited to fact correction would accomplish little. Its remaining governing commitment is that there is a sufficiently identified situation to represent, a bearer whose representations change, and an independently recognizable direction called appropriate understanding or action.

The **representational account** can include relations, capacities, goals, and standards as represented contents. One defense is that any intelligible change can, afterward, be described. But describability does not establish that the description is the thing that changed or the mechanism through which change became possible. If two agents possess the same verbal account and one has access to a shared workspace that enables relevant distinctions to be made, the difference is not captured by comparing their verbal accounts. This challenges an account restricted to verbal representation. A representational account that already includes the workspace, use, and organization need not change to accommodate it. The strongest initial account was not explicitly restricted to the verbal, so this case does not refute that account.

The **operative-relation account** treats understanding as occurring through a relation among bearer, encountered matter, and available activity. It explains why identical sentences need not imply identical understanding. But its breadth creates a danger: if every condition affecting action is included in the same undifferentiated way, the account no longer distinguishes understanding from causal influences on it. Explanatory precision might come from a boundary, or from distinctions among relations without a separable bounded object. One candidate distinction asks whether a change alters what can be distinguished, inferred, taken as significant, or enacted as understood. That distinction is proposed, not established; whether an adequate account needs any fixed boundary remains open. A change in raw strength can enlarge what is physically possible without changing understanding; learning a discriminable movement can change both.

The **formation-of-subject-and-concern account** goes further. What improves may include the way a situation first becomes an identifiable matter with stakes. Consider a request to make an institution's classification more accurate. The original task assumes the classification has the right object and purpose. A departure could retain every accurate observation while showing that the classification joins unlike cases or gives authority to a distinction whose role has not been understood. The improvement, if established, would concern the activity that makes the task appear appropriate. It would not be a more successful completion of that task.

I therefore change the initial account's organizing role without claiming to have refuted its strongest form. Two narrower conclusions are justified. First, improvement in verbal descriptions does not by itself establish a corresponding change in operative discrimination. A sufficiently broad representational account can include that distinction. Second, correcting false propositions is not necessary for every substantial departure: a change can preserve the original account's truth while changing its organization, use, or authority. Because the initial account already included organization, that conclusion does not establish that representational change is unnecessary. The universality of representational reorganization remains unresolved. What changes in this inquiry is that the representational account no longer determines which other ways of understanding the matter may be developed.

The successor candidate is that improvement can concern the formation and enactment of intelligibility, including what becomes distinguishable, significant, and available for further understanding. This is not yet a complete definition or a demonstrated refutation of the broad representational rival. It makes these relations the starting objects of inquiry instead of requiring them first to establish their status as representations. Accurate representation remains a genuine achievement whose relation to those activities must be understood.

The warranted combination joins representational correction to changes in the relations that make representation usable and relevant wherever those dependencies hold. The warranted rejection is the inference that identical true verbal accounts establish identical understanding. The broad representational thesis remains a live rival; departure from its organizing role does not require proving it false. A further unresolved obstacle is the normative word “improvement”: enlarging intelligibility can also enable manipulation, confusion, or pursuit of a misconceived aim. The revision changes what is examined without pretending to have perfected the standard that would certify every such change.

Disposition of all options: **1** retains factual correction without explaining defects in accounts containing only truths. **2** retains reorganized relevance; a sufficiently broad interpretation remains a candidate account of every improvement and has not been defeated here. **3, 4, 5, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12** remain distinct candidate transformations; they can coexist where the concrete relations among individuation, intelligibility, discrimination, competence, authority, medium, distribution, and further generation are established. **6** states a possible mechanism whose realization requires empirical evidence. **8, 14** keep desire and evaluative standards revisable without certifying any particular revision as better. **13** becomes part of the working successor candidate, with its relational scope and any need for a fixed boundary explicitly unresolved. **15** establishes that truth alone does not obligate continuation; it does not establish that every departure improves understanding.

## 25. A perspective changes which further perspectives can become thinkable

1. **All alternatives exist, but attention makes different ones available.** The change selects from a fixed repertoire. This predicts that a suitable cue could reveal an alternative without altering the underlying repertoire. It explains some newly noticed possibilities without establishing that all novelty has this form.

2. **The repertoire is fixed, but practical reachability changes.** An alternative may be representable in principle yet inaccessible within available time, memory, or steps. A new representation can shorten the route. Describing the destination as already possible does not explain how the route becomes available.

3. **New distinctions extend what can be expressed.** A language lacking a distinction gains it through new contrasts or practices. Translating its new terms into old words may fail to preserve their use. The existence of a string in an old alphabet does not establish the prior availability of its later meaning.

4. **A new competence changes what can be understood.** Acquiring a skill makes relations discernible that a verbal description could not previously make usable. What changes is the bearer's ability to discriminate and act. Whether those relations were metaphysically possible beforehand is a separate question.

5. **External construction creates new cognitive resources.** A notation, instrument, or collaborative arrangement permits distinctions and operations unavailable to the previous configuration. The relevant bearer has changed. Counting only alterations inside an individual would misidentify the transition.

6. **Changed concerns create new candidate understandings.** A distinction can become thinkable because something newly matters. The alteration concerns relevance and desire, not only expressive power. An inventory of all expressible propositions would not identify which could become a live inquiry.

7. **Action creates new objects to understand.** Establishing an institution or inventing a practice produces relations that did not previously exist as actual relations. A later perspective concerns those created relations. This does not by itself establish that their possibility was absent beforehand.

8. **New forms of coordination create new bearers.** Several people can acquire a way of understanding through interaction that none could enact alone. The change is not simply movement by the same observer through a fixed list of viewpoints. Observer identity itself becomes part of what must be represented.

9. **Development changes what counts as the same perspective.** Two earlier accounts that seemed different may become equivalent, while one earlier account splits into several distinctions. A changing catalogue can therefore change its own counting units. Cardinality claims made before this reinterpretation need not retain the same meaning.

10. **History partly constitutes meaning.** A perspective formed through one encounter may differ from a verbally identical perspective formed through another because their commitments and available continuations differ. Listing sentence collections loses these differences. A historical description might preserve them, but that is a richer candidate space.

11. **Acquisition changes which routes can still be taken.** Learning or commitment can enable some transitions and obstruct others. The process need not be reversible or path independent. A map of endpoints alone cannot establish that a desired endpoint remains reachable from the present.

12. **Reflexive anticipation changes the continuation.** A system capable of understanding a prediction of its next response can sometimes use that prediction as a reason to depart. This requires the capacity and opportunity to depart. It shows that a prediction can participate in what it predicts, not that every future is unpredictable.

13. **A fixed higher-order space contains every change of lower-order space.** Grammar changes, new agents, and new practices are states or histories in a sufficiently broad formal representation. This is a strong rival to literal creation of possibilities. Its challenge is to specify the representation and its adequacy, rather than merely assert that such a space must exist.

14. **Possible forms have no complete prior individuation.** What alternatives there are becomes determinate only through transformations that establish new distinctions. This is stronger than human ignorance of a fixed list. A coherent account must explain how it differs from option 13 without assuming the very fixed units it denies.

15. **Different senses of possibility have been conflated.** Logical consistency, physical realizability, expressibility, learnability, present accessibility, and recognized admissibility can diverge. A transition may create possibility in one sense and reveal it in another. Settling one does not settle all the others.

The **fixed-repertoire account** is adequate for some kinds of change. If a person knows several approaches but habitually considers only one, a prompt can bring another into use. The option was available as a competence before it became available as a live continuation. The new perspective changes access, not necessarily the total repertoire. This account should be retained wherever its stated conditions hold. It becomes inadequate when it treats every later competence as previously possessed merely because an outside observer can describe a physical route by which it could be acquired.

The **constructive-development account** distinguishes a possible acquisition from a possessed alternative. A person can potentially learn a language without already understanding its sentences. If a newly acquired distinction changes which subsequent distinctions can be learned, perspective formation has a productive structure: its changes alter the conditions of further change. Enumerating endpoints then omits something essential. One must also understand how a transition alters the bearer, the available operations, the conditions of access, and the way alternatives are individuated. Describing every such transition as search in a larger space is possible as a proposal, but it has not yet provided that understanding.

The **fixed-total-space account** remains a serious competitor. A formal model can include changing alphabets, agents, standards, and environments within its states. Thus, observing lower-level novelty does not establish that no fixed higher-level description exists. Equally, asserting that “everything is a state somewhere” supplies no adequate specification of those states or their meanings. The present investigation cannot eliminate either a sufficiently rich fixed totality or the stronger claim that possibilities themselves lack prior complete individuation. The metaphysical dispute is not settled by using the words “emergence” or “search space.”

A concrete implication survives that unresolved dispute. An enumeration of sentence-level alternatives is not an enumeration of all usable perspectives. The same sentence sequence can have different inferential roles, available continuations, histories, or bearers. Conversely, different sequences can enact the same distinctions. To make an exhaustiveness claim, one must specify what makes two perspectives different, which configurations and histories are included, and why every permissible transformation remains within the enumerated domain. If inquiry may change those very specifications, their current closure cannot certify unrestricted exhaustiveness.

This is not a proof that exhaustive understanding is impossible. It identifies what an actual proof of exhaustiveness would have to establish. A finite catalogue is exhaustive only relative to stated boundaries. An unbounded generative rule is not automatically exhaustive: one must establish that nothing relevant lies outside what it generates, and that its outputs correspond to the intended objects. Enumerating all strings would not establish either usable understanding or an adequate individuation of perspectives.

Perfected understanding of this process, on the unrestricted interpretation, would therefore have to comprehend more than destinations. It would have to comprehend how bearers, distinctions, concerns, operations, standards, and conditions of access can change; which changes are possible; which become reachable from which conditions; and why its account of those possibilities is adequate even when those changes affect the account's own terms. Perhaps one exact account can do this. Perhaps exact understanding must be realized through a form whose transformations are themselves perfectly adequate. Neither has been demonstrated here.

The warranted combination is a layered distinction among latent access, acquired competence, constructed conditions, changed individuation, and the still-disputed status of total possibility. The warranted rejection is **“list every candidate first, then find perfection solely by combining or deleting entries.”** That method is sufficient only if the list's units and closure are already adequate—precisely what this inquiry makes uncertain. It can operate within a justified bounded case; it cannot certify its own unrestricted completeness merely by exhausting its entries.

Disposition of all options: **1, 2** are retained under their stated conditions as changes in access or reachability, without promotion into universal accounts. **3, 4, 5, 6** distinguish expressive development, acquired competence, constructed resources, and changed concerns; none alone proves that metaphysical possibilities are created. **7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12** require any adequate account to accommodate the specified changes in objects, bearers, individuation, history, routes, and reflexive participation wherever those cases occur. **13, 14** remain incompatible strong rivals about the status of total possibility; neither has been eliminated. **15** is used throughout to prevent a conclusion about one sense of possibility from being presented as a conclusion about all of them.

Applied to these pages, the method has changed its own initial unit. The numbered options began as accounts expressed in sentences. Inquiry 24 established why that unit can omit operative competence and conditions of access; inquiry 25 established why counting sentences cannot establish a count of usable perspectives. Consequently, the 75 entries are retained as articulated candidates, and the claim that they enumerate the underlying forms of understanding is withheld. Their conditional derivations survive this change of unit; their hypothetical status does not become proof through inclusion in the list. In particular, the proposed working successor in inquiry 24 remains a candidate rather than becoming the judge that validates every other section.

Across these five inquiries, 75 materially different options have been made explicit. Several unconditional rules have been rejected, and several conditional combinations justified. No exhaustive domain of all perspectives has been established, and no perfected perspective has been found or certified. That is the exact defeat admitted here. It is not evidence that perfection is impossible. It prevents this finite inquiry from claiming the achievement whose conditions it has only begun to expose.
