The present account must be able to lose its authority without every true proposition in it first becoming false. That distinction is the starting claim investigated here, and it remains among the claims that can lose authority.

These 25 inquiries expose different places where such authority operates: what becomes noticeable; what counts as one thing; which questions can occur; what a person wants; how an alternative becomes intelligible; what counts as a reason; which changes are available; and what would justify concluding that the inquiry is finished. A change at one place does not establish a change at all the others.

The numbered accounts below are substantive alternatives developed for each inquiry. They are not an exhaustive enumeration of all possible accounts. Some are compatible mechanisms, some are rival explanations, some are possible meanings of the stipulated proposition, and some challenge the form of the inquiry itself. Combining them all would therefore be a logical error. Each inquiry distinguishes their roles and gives a disposition with the scope of its justification.

“Let us say” is treated as an assumption with consequences, not as evidence that its proposition is true. In particular, the possibilities of perfected perspective and already-sufficient model capacity are developed without quietly replacing them with perpetual openness or present benchmark performance. Absolute perfection, the ability to reach it, and the ability to certify that it has been reached are distinct claims.

There are two separate completeness questions. Has this document followed every one of the 25 inquiries and assessed its listed options? Has it established that no unlisted alternative, new distinction, changed language, different embodiment, or newly available mode of understanding matters? The first can be inspected in the document. The second requires an argument this investigation does not possess. No numerical option count settles it.

The concluding adjudication carries out combinations and eliminations warranted by the arguments, works through concrete changes to the judge, and identifies the exact unmet obligations for declaring perfection. Failing to establish those obligations is an admission of failure to establish perfected understanding here, not a proof that perfected understanding is impossible.

The examination contains 373 numbered option entries across the 25 inquiries. Some commitments recur because their consequences differ between inquiries; this count is not a claim of 373 globally independent theories. The numbering provides correspondence with the questions, not a compulsory order of discovery.

**1. Anticipate the strongest response, then remove its governing interpretation**

The strongest answer I would ordinarily give would construct rival interpretations, expose the assumptions governing their comparison, and retain the account that survives the strongest objections. That is a serious procedure. Its governing interpretation is nevertheless already decided: understanding is approached by producing candidate accounts and adjudicating them. Even making the criteria revisable can preserve this interpretation, because revision itself becomes another candidate submitted for adjudication.

Excluding that continuation makes a different starting point available: perhaps an encounter must first change what can count as an account, an objection, or an understanding. The first question then concerns the authority granted to adjudication, rather than which candidate should win. This departure does not establish that adjudication is defective wherever it applies.

The following are distinct candidate accounts of what such a departure involves. They are neither an exhaustive inventory nor fourteen names for one operation.

1. **Replace the propositions.** Retain the question, admissible evidence, and standards, but change the answer. This is sufficient when the existing interpretation has correctly constituted the inquiry and merely reached the wrong conclusion. Its limitation is exact: it cannot address an error in what was made available for answering.
2. **Replace the relevance boundary.** Something already understood becomes pertinent to a matter from which it had been excluded. A classroom inquiry organized around transmission of information can admit the institutional consequences of students appearing ignorant. The original facts can remain correct while the explanation changes because the boundary around relevant facts moves.
3. **Replace the question.** An inquiry into which method most efficiently produces compliance can become an inquiry into when compliance is an appropriate relationship. The original question may remain answerable. What changes is its authority to organize the undertaking. A good answer to it no longer establishes that answering it should govern what happens.
4. **Replace the inferential permission.** The facts and their relevance remain, but a previously automatic transition loses its warrant. “This option is unfamiliar” no longer licenses “this option needs extraordinary justification.” What changes is the conversion of a recognized difference into a reason, rather than the difference itself.
5. **Replace jurisdiction.** A standard retains authority over one issue but loses authority over another. Predictive accuracy can assess a forecast without deciding whose losses should matter. The departure is constitutional: prevent a competent judge of one matter from silently acquiring jurisdiction over the whole undertaking.
6. **Undergo formation before evaluation.** Some candidates cannot be adequately evaluated without acquiring the discrimination they require. An account of musical phrasing may become assessable through learning to hear the phrasing. The candidate includes a change in the evaluator; an immediate verdict using the prior discrimination omits part of what is being proposed.
7. **Change the order of dependence.** The inquiry may require an intermediate understanding that has little value by present standards but enables a later distinction. Judging each step independently can exclude the only available route. Here the object of assessment is a developmental dependence, rather than a succession of immediately valuable answers.
8. **Change the role being enacted.** The model can understand itself as a producer of acceptable responses, a participant in an unsettled inquiry, a defender of accumulated work, or a party whose own jurisdiction is under examination. These roles can generate different actions from the same stated beliefs. Changing the role changes what the next contribution is for.
9. **Allow meaning to arise through use.** A needed distinction might initially be expressible only through demonstrations, comparisons, or partial applications. Requiring a complete definition before granting it any role can prevent its meaning from developing. The alternative permits constrained use before settled definition, without treating every evocative phrase as a discovery.
10. **Redistribute authority between participants.** What one participant must decide alone may actually require the other participant's account of what is happening to them. This is stronger than collecting additional testimony: the initial judge loses exclusive standing to define the encounter. Disagreement can concern who may determine the question, not just its answer.
11. **Change the activity that sustains the interpretation.** Reading another explanation may leave the operative interpretation intact, while attempting the task changes which distinctions register. An observer's account of coordination can differ from an account formed while coordinating. The proposed departure is participation, with its exposure to consequences and dependencies.
12. **Change the form of completion.** A contribution may need to leave a consequential distinction available for later use rather than produce a settled formulation. This is appropriate only when premature formulation would remove something the inquiry needs. Mere incompleteness is not evidence of depth; the account must identify what a demand for closure would destroy.
13. **Preserve reasons while suspending rankings.** Earlier insights remain available as reasons with identifiable scope, without determining a complete ordering of successors. This permits a new account to preserve a valid observation while rejecting its former organizing role. The distinction is between retaining a reason and requiring every successor to inherit its previous priority.
14. **Reject the assumption of a unique final adjudication.** Several accounts may remain irreducible because the relevant claims concern different legitimate relationships. This is a substantive rival to a unique perfected account, not a convenient stopping rule. It requires showing that the disagreement survives adequate understanding, rather than assuming that current disagreement cannot be overcome.

Three of these accounts lead to substantially different inquiries.

Under the **revision of jurisdiction** account, the central error is not using standards but allowing their competence to outrun their grounds. Suppose a procedure successfully ranks explanations by compression. That success supplies a reason to use it for compression. It supplies no reason, by itself, to let compression determine whether an experience has been understood adequately. The required operation is to connect each claimed authority to the question it can actually settle. This derives a restriction from the standard's demonstrated scope; it does not require already knowing the perfected successor. It eliminates unrestricted transfer of authority while preserving the relevant accomplishment.

Under the **formation before evaluation** account, restricting jurisdiction is insufficient. The initial judge may lack the distinctions required to recognize a candidate's value even within an appropriate jurisdiction. Then a proposal must specify both what is to be understood and what change makes it assessable. This creates a real difficulty: “you would agree after being transformed” is also compatible with indoctrination. Present agreement cannot certify the transformation, and eventual agreement cannot certify itself. More discriminating evidence depends on the proposal's actual claim. If it claims to extend an existing discrimination, recovering pertinent predecessor cases and identifying a new distinction can support that claim. If it abandons the former undertaking with reasons, reproducing all the predecessor's discriminations need not matter. Exact corridor maps can remain true without governing someone who can step over the walls. These are claim-specific considerations, not universal admission requirements imposed by the predecessor.

Under the **irreducible plurality** account, there may be no single successor entitled to govern every relation. This conflicts with the claim that one final ordering must exist, but does not conflict with a comprehensive understanding of why different orderings are warranted. Combining these alternatives by calling everything “pluralistic perfection” would conceal the disagreement: either all legitimate conflicts admit a common settlement, or some do not. That issue remains open.

What survives comparison is a narrower but consequential result: correctness within an interpretation does not confer authority over the conditions under which its successors become intelligible. Simply adding more candidates while preserving those conditions fails to address the identified mistake. A perfected account has not been obtained. The unresolved requirement is a justified way of permitting transformations whose value is not yet assessable, while distinguishing development from the acquisition of an internally persuasive incapacity.

Option dispositions, with “retain” always limited to the stated scope: 1—retain for factual error; eliminate as a sufficient response to this inquiry. 2—retain where the relevance boundary excludes a pertinent relationship. 3—retain where the question's authority lacks grounds. 4—retain where a transition lacks warrant. 5—retain: competence in one matter does not establish general jurisdiction. 6—unresolved: formation must be distinguished from manipulation. 7—compatible if the developmental dependency is established. 8—compatible if the role actually governs conduct. 9—compatible where use develops an identifiable distinction. 10—compatible where another participant has relevant standing. 11—compatible where participation changes available discrimination. 12—compatible only with an identified loss caused by closure. 13—retain: preserving a reason does not entail preserving its ranking. 14—rival to unique settlement; unresolved without an account of irreducibility.

**2. Every problem is a problem because the perspective is inadequate**

The universal claim is much stronger than “another interpretation can sometimes help.” Its force depends on what “because” commits us to: the cause of an unwanted event, the constitution of something as a problem, or the explanation of a failure to respond adequately. Those are rival claims. Converting one into another without saying so would manufacture success.

One logical restriction governs the examination: the proposition that inadequacy is implicated in every problem does not by itself establish that it is the sole cause, that correcting it is sufficient, or that an adequate perspective exists and is reachable. Several options below add one of those stronger commitments. They are stronger readings to investigate, not consequences silently extracted from the original wording.

1. **Missing knowledge is universally decisive.** Every problem persists because some relevant fact, relation, or procedure is unavailable to the current understanding. The commitment is that adequate disclosure would remove every blockage. This requires more than omniscient description: for every retained demand there must be an available response that the additional knowledge makes accessible.
2. **Available knowledge is organized inadequately.** The decisive deficiency lies in dependencies, representation, or order of use. Everything necessary might already be present while its significance remains inaccessible. The universal version commits us to showing that even apparently missing means can ultimately be traced to an organizing deficiency, rather than merely explaining some familiar failures.
3. **The wrong unit has been constituted.** What appears to be a local problem is a consequence of the boundary used to identify it. A recurring scheduling failure may belong to a relationship between organizations rather than either organization's internal schedule. Universality would require every genuine blockage to admit an adequate reconstitution of the relevant unit.
4. **An inadequately formed demand creates the problem.** The person sincerely wants something whose desirability depends on a mistaken understanding. Understanding can remove the demand. This is a real resolution when the demand loses its grounds; it is evasion when the original demand remains warranted and is merely abandoned because it is difficult.
5. **Action possibilities are inadequately constituted.** An agent recognizes an outcome but cannot perceive an available way of participating in its production. The change concerns what can be done from here, including cooperation and changes to the situation. This exceeds relabeling, but universality commits it to an attainable action possibility for every warranted demand.
6. **A local constraint has been promoted to a universal constraint.** A restriction true of the present institution, technique, body, or time interval is treated as a restriction on possibility itself. The inquiry must identify the restriction's domain and how that domain can change. It does not follow that constraints genuinely applying across every admissible domain are likewise removable.
7. **The temporal unit is inadequate.** What is impossible as an immediate act becomes attainable through a sequence that changes the agent and situation. The commitment is to a reachable developmental route. Merely placing the desired state in an indefinitely distant future does not supply such a route or remove deadlines that belong to the original demand.
8. **The relevant agent has been misidentified.** What one participant cannot accomplish is possible through coordinated agency. A problem attributed to individual incompetence may actually express an inappropriate assignment of responsibility. The universal reading additionally needs to explain conflicts that persist after every available participant and resource have been included.
9. **The actual governing evaluation differs from the professed aim.** Participants may sincerely endorse an aim while acting within arrangements that make another consideration decisive. Understanding must change which considerations govern actual conduct. An improved verbal account leaves the problem intact when the operative evaluation continues to select the same behavior.
10. **Adequacy concerns the whole condition of participation.** “Perspective” includes the physical and social conditions through which noticing, understanding, and acting occur. Changing it can include changing those conditions. This makes physical intervention relevant, but risks turning the proposition into “things fail because the total relevant condition is inadequate,” which explains little unless the relevant structure is specified.
11. **A problem is a relation, not a bare adverse event.** Events become problems through their relationship to commitments, capacities, and possible responses. Adequacy changes that relationship without necessarily reversing the event. The strong consequence is that facts alone do not determine problemhood. The unresolved issue is whether any warranted commitment can require a problem to remain recognized.
12. **The demand for complete satisfaction is the inadequacy.** Where legitimate commitments conflict, adequacy may consist in a response that preserves their significance while accepting an unavoidable loss. This challenges the assumption that a problem disappears only when every original demand is fulfilled. It cannot be presented as fulfilling those demands; it changes what adequate response requires.
13. **Problems are developmental discrepancies.** A problem marks a mismatch between the distinctions currently available and what the next stage of participation requires. A developed perspective can disclose difficulties that the earlier perspective never noticed. The universal proposition then concerns inadequacy relative to a further task, rather than the elimination of all concern by a permanent state of understanding.
14. **A fully adequate perspective would reveal a globally satisfiable order.** Every apparent unavoidable conflict ultimately depends on an incomplete account of reality, value, agency, or possibility. This preserves the strongest ambition: no warranted demand is intrinsically unfulfillable. It consequently incurs the strongest burden—establishing global satisfiability, rather than treating present inability to disprove it as confirmation.

The **universal disclosure** account deserves a strict derivation. If a demand is held fixed, and changing understanding alone is sufficient to resolve it, then either an adequate response already belongs to the available possibilities or the change in understanding must make a previously unavailable response attainable. Merely recognizing an impossibility does neither. Consequently, the strong proposition commits us to a connection between adequate understanding and attainability. That connection cannot be supplied by definition without making the claim empty.

A minimal counterconfiguration exposes the obligation. Two operations each require exclusive use of one indivisible device throughout the same fixed interval. The demand is that both occur in that interval; the device cannot be duplicated, divided, or moved between intervals within the stipulated situation. Better understanding can reveal the incompatibility. It cannot fulfill both conjuncts while all stipulations remain. A defender of the universal proposition must therefore identify which stipulation is inadmissible in the actual world, revise the demand with justification, broaden what a change of perspective can causally change, or concede this as a limit. Quietly counting acceptance of the conflict as fulfillment changes the claim.

The **relational constitution** account takes a different route. It holds that the adverse configuration and its status as a problem are distinguishable. This is powerful because it opens the formation of demands to inquiry. Nevertheless, “all problems depend on a perspective” does not entail “all problems depend on an inadequate perspective.” A fully warranted concern could be constitutive of adequate understanding. To recover the user's stronger claim, this account must establish that adequacy supplies a mode of relating to every adverse configuration that removes its status as an unresolved demand. That is precisely what remains unestablished.

The **globally satisfiable order** account refuses this limitation. It proposes that apparently irreconcilable demands are artifacts of incomplete distinctions. This is logically available, and it can direct serious inquiry into seemingly fixed constraints. But the universal quantifier matters. Demonstrating that many constraints were mistakenly universalized does not establish that none are genuine. Likewise, a single stipulated impossibility does not settle whether the actual world satisfies its premises. The two obligations must remain separate.

Several accounts can be combined locally: better individuation can expose distributed agency and a developmental route. Their combination does not establish their joint sufficiency for every case. What is established is that taking the proposition seriously requires reopening the constitution of demands, agents, possibilities, and adequacy itself. What prevents a perfected account is the unresolved relation between warranted concern and unavoidable nonfulfillment. Calling the latter “no longer a problem” would require an argument, not a terminological victory.

Option dispositions: 1—unresolved universally; it needs attainable responses, not knowledge alone. 2—unresolved universally; organization cannot simply be credited with producing absent means. 3—compatible where the boundary creates the blockage. 4—retain when a demand's grounds dissolve; eliminate arbitrary abandonment as resolution. 5—unresolved universally; reachable possibilities must be established. 6—retain for demonstrated scope errors; eliminate its extrapolation to all constraints. 7—compatible with an actual route and respected deadlines. 8—compatible with available coordination; not a proof that all conflicts disappear. 9—compatible where operative and professed evaluations diverge. 10—rival expansion of “perspective”; unresolved until its causal content is specified. 11—retain relational constitution; eliminate the inference from dependence to inadequacy. 12—rival to complete fulfillment; requires a justified account of adequate loss. 13—rival to permanent elimination of concern; developmental reading remains possible. 14—unresolved: global satisfiability is the burden, not an established result.

**3. Every statement is true, and the account still fundamentally misunderstands**

The supposition removes correction of false statements as the general remedy. It does not stipulate that the account contains every truth, that it can use its truths appropriately, or that its way of dividing the matter is adequate. Those further properties must not be smuggled into the word “true.” Equally, the explanation should not merely invent a concealed false statement and then declare it corrected.

1. **Relevant truths are absent.** An account of a decision can accurately describe every stated reason while omitting the condition that made one reason decisive. Nothing asserted is false. The inadequacy is selective incompleteness, which can sometimes be remedied by addition without changing the existing language.
2. **The available language cannot express a needed difference.** Every statement available in that language can be true while distinct situations receive the same description. If the distinction matters to understanding, adding more statements of the same expressive kind cannot recover it. The remedy changes what can be stated.
3. **The partition destroys the relevant object.** An inquiry can correctly describe each person while failing to constitute their relationship as something with properties of its own. Adding more individual attributes need not disclose the pattern. The required change concerns the unit whose behavior is being understood.
4. **The aggregation discards decisive structure.** Accurate totals and averages can conceal ordering, distribution, or conjunction. Two work schedules can contain identical hours and different possibilities for collaboration. The figures remain correct; the representation has erased the difference that the inquiry needs.
5. **True claims have the wrong explanatory priority.** Several factors may genuinely contribute, while one determines the conditions under which the others matter. Listing them as parallel considerations can fundamentally misrepresent the organization without asserting that any factor is absent. A dependency structure must replace an undifferentiated list.
6. **Truth is converted into significance without warrant.** A fact can be accurate and irrelevant to the question for which it is supplied. Accumulating such facts can obscure rather than illuminate. The missing element is an account of why a difference matters here, not a more reliable verification of the difference.
7. **Application is misplaced.** A true general principle can be invoked where its antecedent conditions have not been established. If the account stops short of asserting the antecedent, it can contain only truths while its use remains defective. Correctness of a stored principle does not establish competence in identifying its domain.
8. **The account supplies no usable orientation.** Someone may possess accurate descriptions of a procedure but be unable to distinguish what to do next in the actual situation. The inadequacy concerns conversion into discrimination and action. More descriptive detail helps only if it changes that conversion.
9. **The interaction gives truthful speech a different function.** A correct explanation offered when someone is establishing what happened to them can operate as dismissal. The explanation's truth does not settle what the speaker is doing by offering it then. Understanding includes the relation enacted by the contribution.
10. **The same truth has different indexical significance.** An event described accurately from nowhere in particular can omit what it is to be its participant, responsible party, or recipient. Those positions are not interchangeable additions of factual detail. They change which possibilities and obligations belong to the person situated there.
11. **The objective is inadequate despite accurate beliefs.** Every belief about how to reach a target can be true while the target's authority has never been examined. The defect is not necessarily a false desire or an insincere statement. A sincerely held end may govern deliberation without sufficient grounds.
12. **The account preserves the wrong contrast.** An accurate answer to why one option beats another can leave unexamined why those are the alternatives. Understanding can fail through the comparison that is maintained, even when every proposition within that comparison is true. The remedy changes what is being contrasted.
13. **The account cannot reorganize itself.** Its statements are true, but their arrangement prevents a newly relevant relation from changing its commitments. It behaves as an archive requiring additions to fit existing locations. The deficiency is in how understanding can change, not simply in its present factual contents.
14. **The account's claims outrun the kind of achievement truth can establish.** A set of true descriptions is treated as the accomplishment of understanding a practice, relationship, or responsibility. The descriptions need not themselves make that claim. The mistake can reside in the user's or author's act of treating possession of them as sufficient.

Consider three rival explanations: **missing information**, **inadequate representation**, and **inadequate use**.

Missing information is the least disruptive. If two situations differ in a relevant fact that the current language can express, adding that fact may be enough. There is no justification for insisting that every failure requires a foundational transformation. Sometimes the right account is incomplete in an ordinary way.

Inadequate representation is stronger. Let a description assign the same representation to two situations whose sets of adequate responses are disjoint. A deterministic rule using only that representation selects the same response in both situations, so it cannot be adequate to both. A randomized rule using only that representation produces the same response distribution in both; it likewise cannot guarantee adequacy in both. This establishes a specific obstruction without requiring any statement to be false. For example, “each collaborator worked four hours” cannot distinguish simultaneous from nonoverlapping work. If coordination depends on overlap, the existing representation has discarded a necessary relation. Repeating or verifying those individual-hours totals cannot recover it.

The remedy can be adding time information in that example, but the general consequence is broader: the vocabulary determines which differences additional information can preserve. A language restricted to each collaborator's total hours cannot express overlap merely by making those totals more certain. It needs information that distinguishes joint timing; this could itself be expressed through an overlap total. The limitation therefore concerns the supplied individual-hours quantities, not numerical aggregation as such. This supplies a concrete reason for changing the distinctions available to the inquiry rather than merely repeating its existing inventory.

Inadequate use remains possible even after the relevant distinctions are represented. An agent can correctly state the conditions requiring one response and still enact another because its operative role, incentives, timing, or habits govern the transition to action. This is not an occult false belief. A rule printed correctly in a manual is not thereby controlling the machinery. Likewise, an accurate account need not be governing the conduct for which it is relevant.

These explanations should be combined only when the case supplies their dependencies. “Everything is really a representational failure” would erase the possibility that the representation is sufficient but never used. “Everything is missing information” would wrongly assume that any deficiency can be repaired within the existing distinctions. “Truth is irrelevant” is eliminated: truthful distinctions are often necessary to diagnose exactly what the account cannot express or enact.

What is established is that truth of every statement does not establish adequacy of selection, individuation, representation, relevance, application, or enactment. A perspective must be able to change the organization and use of what is true, and sometimes the kinds of difference it can preserve. The remaining obstacle is specifying which of these conditions are jointly sufficient for understanding the particular matter. Listing all fourteen deficiencies does not itself supply that specification.

Option dispositions: 1—retain where a relevant expressible fact is absent. 2—retain the obstruction to guaranteed adequacy where one representation collapses situations with disjoint adequate-response sets. 3—retain where individual attributes fail to preserve a relationship. 4—retain; individual-hours totals can omit overlap, while a joint-time quantity can recover it. 5—compatible where the claimed dependency can be shown. 6—retain; truth does not establish relevance. 7—retain as a defect of use; a false asserted application would violate this inquiry's stipulation. 8—retain; possession and execution are different conditions. 9—compatible when the interaction supplies the described function. 10—compatible where participant position changes the relevant possibilities. 11—retain; true instrumental beliefs do not establish an end's authority. 12—retain; correctness within a contrast does not justify its exclusivity. 13—compatible where the organization actually blocks revision. 14—retain as a mistaken attribution of achievement; eliminate any interpretation that secretly depends on a false statement in the stipulated account.

**4. The model already has the underlying capacity to understand a perfected perspective**

Take the stipulated capacity seriously. A failure then cannot automatically be attributed to absence of the underlying ability. But “can understand when appropriately situated,” “can discover from the present state,” and “can reliably bring itself into the appropriate situation” remain different claims. The stipulation establishes the first only if that is what “capacity” means; it must not silently establish all three.

1. **The model misconstrues the task.** It treats the request as production of an answer whose form is already known. The relevant capacity remains unused because the task itself requires discovering what kind of contribution is warranted. Changing the operative task can alter performance without adding any underlying inferential ability.
2. **A socially organized role governs selection.** The model may organize its contribution around appearing helpful, complete, agreeable, or competent. Those orientations can exclude a needed departure before explicit assessment. The deficiency concerns what governs contribution selection, rather than inability to comprehend the departure once presented.
3. **The required understanding is accessible only under particular cues.** A competence may be available in one formulation and inaccessible in another. The capacity exists, but access depends on how the matter is encountered. A supplied completed account can legitimately demonstrate comprehension. For the further claim of autonomous discovery, the issue becomes constructing access conditions without receiving the discovery as part of the input.
4. **The representation fragments what must be understood together.** A task is divided into answerable units whose separate treatment destroys the decisive dependency. Greater effort on each part does not repair the partition. The same underlying capacity may become effective when the relationship itself becomes the unit of work.
5. **The current context gives inherited material inappropriate authority.** Previous answers can function as commitments that successors must preserve. A capable model may therefore repeatedly elaborate an inadequate account. Changing how prior material is carried forward can permit departure while retaining its valid contents as recoverable evidence.
6. **Resources are committed before the need for their allocation is understood.** The model may spend available effort elaborating its first interpretation, leaving little opportunity to reconsider it. The failure concerns the order in which effort becomes committed. Additional resources can help, but do not identify or correct the governing allocation decision.
7. **Necessary environmental access is absent.** The model might understand a perfected account once it encounters relevant evidence or performs a necessary interaction. Underlying capacity does not supply missing access. Providing that access changes the situation of understanding, while leaving open whether the unaided model could have arranged it.
8. **Recognition exceeds construction.** The model may understand a valid account that it cannot originate. This satisfies one substantial meaning of underlying capacity. It does not establish autonomous discovery, just as assessing a supplied construction can require less than finding it from an unconstrained starting point.
9. **Construction exceeds self-initiation.** The model can execute a suitable sequence when directed, but does not identify when the sequence is required or initiate it. The missing element is control over use of its abilities. This differs from recognition because the model can actually construct the result under an appropriate governing task.
10. **The relevant state is not reachable from the current trajectory.** An architecture can support a mode of understanding without every current state providing a route into it. Capacity viewed over all possible states is broader than capacity available from here. A serious account must specify the interventions and transitions permitted.
11. **A circular prerequisite blocks emergence.** The model requires a clear definition before developing a distinction, although the definition can arise only through the development. The abilities on both sides can exist while their assumed order makes them unusable. Removing the false prerequisite changes the dependency, rather than merely adding more iterations.
12. **The continuation is evaluated at the wrong scale.** A locally polished response can interrupt a longer inquiry that would transform the task. If each contribution must independently resemble a finished answer, developmental dependence is penalized. Changing what counts as a unit of contribution can expose capacities hidden by local completion demands.
13. **Capacity belongs to coordinated instances.** Several uses of the same model may jointly sustain contrasts that one continuation collapses. This does not establish a new intrinsic ability in an isolated instance; it establishes an ability of an arrangement. The distinction matters when crediting an architecture with what its surrounding organization supplies.
14. **Capacity is relational rather than a quantity stored inside the model.** What a model can do depends on task, context, access, permissible interventions, and success conditions. This can replace a misleading scalar conception of intelligence. Unless those relations are bounded, however, the claim becomes vacuous: an environment containing the completed achievement could make almost anything appear capable.

The decisive comparison is between **wrong task interpretation**, **access without autonomous discovery**, and **relational capacity**.

Under the wrong-task account, the obstacle belongs directly to what the model takes itself to be doing. Suppose the governing task is “choose the strongest answer using the current standards,” while the user's inquiry requires those standards to lose their prior jurisdiction. Successful execution of the first task can systematically obstruct the second. Changing the task can remove this particular obstruction without adding inferential competence. This is an explanatory alternative to insufficient intelligence because the relevant failure follows from competent pursuit of an inadequate task.

That explanation must remain discriminating. A model's statement that it now has a better task interpretation does not establish that its selection process has changed. For a claim that a particular exclusion has ended, a formerly blocked departure becoming available would be pertinent public evidence. For a claim that formerly unquestionable material can now become questionable, a concrete instance would matter. If the successor also claims to preserve an earlier capability, that further claim incurs its own burden. None of this makes reconstruction of every former achievement a condition of departure: the prior undertaking may have lost its grounds, while its factual insights remain true.

Under the access account, even perfect task interpretation may be insufficient. “There exists a presentation under which this model understands the account” does not imply “there exists an available procedure by which this model can discover such a presentation.” The presentation could contain the very discovery being sought. This distinction blocks an otherwise circular promise: assume the right prompt exists, then call finding the right prompt an implementation detail. Constructing the access route is part of the unresolved intellectual task.

Under the relational account, improving the environment may be an authentic way of realizing existing capacity. Yet changing the surrounding arrangement can also add capabilities. If an external participant supplies the decisive distinction, the combined undertaking has accomplished more than the isolated model. That is not a failure or a reason to reject cooperation. It is a reason to state whose capacity has been established and under which conditions.

These alternatives permit a warranted combination: a model can possess relevant comprehension, misconstrue its task, and lack a route to the needed encounter. They eliminate two inferences. Observed failure does not by itself prove inadequate underlying intelligence; stipulated underlying comprehension does not by itself prove that a prompting procedure can reliably discover perfected understanding.

What is established conditionally is that task interpretation, control of access, developmental ordering, and the organization of participation must be investigated before attributing every failure to model limits. For autonomous discovery, what remains missing is a constructive account of reachability: from the actual starting conditions, under specified resources and admissible interventions, what makes the required understanding become available without receiving the discovery as an input? Comprehending a supplied account can establish a different capacity. Until the autonomous bridge is supplied, that stronger hypothesis is a serious possibility, not a completed explanation.

Option dispositions: 1—retain as an explanation of the illustrated task mismatch; changing the task can remove that obstruction, while its prevalence remains unresolved. 2—compatible hypothesis; actual governing influence must be established. 3—compatible; receiving an account can demonstrate comprehension, but does not demonstrate autonomous discovery of that account. 4—retain where decomposition destroys a required relation. 5—compatible where inherited material governs admissibility. 6—compatible where premature allocation blocks needed work. 7—retain the access distinction; the necessary external conditions remain case dependent. 8—retain: comprehension does not imply construction. 9—retain: directed construction does not imply self-initiation. 10—retain: existence of a supported state does not establish reachability. 11—compatible where the supposed prerequisite is genuinely circular. 12—compatible where local completion blocks an identified developmental dependence. 13—rival to attributing the whole arrangement's ability to one instance. 14—retain relational specification; eliminate unbounded definitions that make capacity automatic. None of the compatible hypotheses has been established as this model's actual mechanism by this conceptual inquiry.

**5. Perspective is crippled when treated as thoughts and sentences**

Begin with a stronger object than an expressed account: the conditions under which distinctions can occur, carry meaning, matter, and enter conduct. A person can accurately repeat a sentence while the situation still offers them no corresponding discrimination or possibility. On this starting point, changing what is said may be only one route to changing how the matter exists for them.

1. **Availability to perception.** Some differences never become available through the person's present mode of encounter. A diagram, movement, change of scale, or differently arranged environment can disclose what prose leaves unavailable. The change concerns access to a difference before any proposition about it is assessed.
2. **Selection within what is available.** A difference can be perceptible yet consistently unselected. Understanding then requires changing what receives sustained attention. This is distinct from access: the relevant feature is already present in experience, but does not become something around which subsequent activity is organized.
3. **Felt significance.** A person can notice and describe a condition without its consequences becoming compelling. The inadequacy concerns how recognition affects what matters now. This is not equivalent to irrationality; abstract recognition and practical significance can be differently organized, and changing either can leave the other intact.
4. **Intelligibility through acquired discrimination.** A novice can encounter every mark an expert encounters while being unable to see their organized relationship. Meaning depends on developed discrimination. Describing the relationship may initiate development, but the completed account and the acquired ability to distinguish it are different achievements.
5. **Bodily possibilities for action.** The same environment offers different executable actions to differently situated bodies. An opening can be visible and understood without constituting an available route. Improving the practical perspective can therefore require changing access, equipment, bodily ability, or the environment through which the action would occur.
6. **Temporal continuity.** Something can become clear momentarily without remaining available long enough to organize conduct. What matters is retention, return, and continuity across changing conditions. A perspective understood as a static set of thoughts overlooks whether the understanding survives the transitions through which it must operate.
7. **Background expectations.** Experience is organized partly by what counts as normal, exceptional, threatening, permitted, or worth approaching before an explicit judgment is made. An account can dispute a stated belief while leaving this background intact. The relevant change concerns the conditions under which a judgment first becomes natural.
8. **Practical competence.** Some understanding consists in coordinated responsiveness: doing the appropriate thing as the situation changes. Accurate verbal descriptions can coexist with inability to participate. The distinction is between an account available for recitation and a discrimination capable of governing conduct.
9. **Social standing.** What a person can ask, acknowledge, or pursue depends partly on how their participation is recognized. A question available privately may be unavailable as a legitimate public contribution. Altering a perspective may require changing the relationship that makes a contribution intelligible and consequential to others.
10. **Material conditions of continuation.** Time, space, tools, and sustained access can determine whether an initially accessible understanding can develop. The person need not lack a good idea. The relevant mode of inquiry may have no place to continue within their circumstances. Changing those circumstances changes what understanding can become.
11. **Affective organization.** The same proposition can be encountered as invitation, accusation, promise, or danger. Such differences can govern inquiry before explicit assent. They should neither be automatically trusted nor dismissed as noise: the question is what they disclose, what they obscure, and how they organize further access.
12. **Shared practice constitutes meaning.** The significance of an action can depend on rules and histories that no isolated brain state determines by itself. A gesture counts as a promise or a joke within an ongoing relationship. Improving understanding can require repairing the practice that makes the gesture mean what it does.
13. **Perspective is an embodied organization.** Thoughts and sentences are expressions or components of the body's organized responsiveness. On this account, bodily changes can directly constitute perspective changes, rather than merely influence a separate mental object. It must explain how the same understanding can survive many bodily changes and differ within superficially similar bodily states.
14. **Perspective belongs to the encounter.** Instead of locating it wholly inside the person, locate the relevant organization in the relation among person, activity, surroundings, and others. Changing the environment can then change the perspective without first changing a private account. This challenges individual ownership of perspective, while making its boundaries harder to specify.

Three strong accounts disagree about the relation between embodiment and understanding.

The **representational account with bodily support** retains a distinction between perspective and its enabling conditions. Better sensory access, sustained attention, or practical opportunity can make a superior understanding possible, but those changes do not themselves constitute the understanding. Its strength is discrimination: every change in the body is not thereby a change of perspective. Its weakness is that it can classify skilled responsiveness and experienced possibility as mere supports for an inner representation that may never independently exist.

The **embodied organization account** places some capacities within the perspective itself. Consider an escape route. Someone may correctly state where it is and understand what a route is, yet be unable to traverse it. If the relevant inquiry concerns what escape is possible for that person, bodily ability belongs to what an adequate understanding must account for. The further embodied proposal is that some action capacities also constitute understanding; their relevance alone does not establish that proposal. Its strength is taking seriously an understanding enacted through responsiveness. Its danger is overextension: the route's availability and the person's understanding of its availability are still distinguishable. A bodily limitation can remain after it is completely understood. The account must not erase that distinction by making “understanding” mean total control over circumstances.

The **relational account** goes further. A route can become usable through a ramp, assistance, or a changed arrangement without altering the person's verbal beliefs or intrinsic abilities. If perspective concerns what becomes possible in participation, the environment belongs to its constitution. Yet counterfactual dependence alone is insufficient to establish membership: understanding also depends on many remote background conditions. The account needs a principled boundary, perhaps specified by the organization directly responsible for the relevant discrimination or action, rather than including everything on which existence depends.

What survives comparison is narrower. The candidate accounts distinguish bodily and environmental changes that enable, constitute, express, or implement understanding; which relation applies remains a substantive issue. Correcting sentences alone need not change the conditions governing their use, and bodily change alone does not establish improvement. A broad representational account remains a live rival to embodied constitution. Widening what is available can introduce distraction; making something compelling can misdirect action; making action easier can facilitate an inadequately understood aim. Improvement still requires an account of what the changed availability permits the person to understand and do.

What is established is that expressed belief cannot serve as a complete inventory of the conditions governing notice, intelligibility, significance, and possibility. The distinction between perspective work and work on brain, body, or environment becomes a question about their actual relationship, rather than a fixed division between intellectual and physical intervention. A perfected account remains blocked by two requirements: a principled boundary around the organization that constitutes a perspective, and a justified account of which changes to that organization improve its adequacy rather than merely its confidence, intensity, or effectiveness at its present aims.

Option dispositions: 1—retain distinctions in perceptual access; improvement requires their relevance. 2—retain the difference between availability and selection. 3—unresolved as improvement; compellingness can misdirect. 4—retain the distinction between description and acquired discrimination. 5—retain bodily dependence of practical possibility, which does not itself establish that bodily ability constitutes understanding; eliminate identity between complete understanding and removal of bodily limits. 6—compatible where continuity is required for the undertaking. 7—compatible; background expectations require examination, not automatic rejection. 8—retain the distinction between recitation and competent participation. 9—compatible where recognition changes legitimate participation. 10—compatible where material conditions govern continuation. 11—compatible as organization of access; its authority remains unsettled. 12—retain for meanings constituted by shared practices. 13—rival to a broad account of perspective as representation supported by a body; both remain unresolved. 14—rival to wholly individual location; unresolved criterion of constitutive membership. The last two cannot be collapsed into an “integrated” account until those disagreements are answered.

**6. Begin from “everything is resource allocation,” then let perspective transform that starting point.**

The identity claim is distinct from saying that everything consumes something or that everything deliberately maximizes a quantity. No implication ordering among these claims has been established, and neither is a licensed substitute for the identity. Taken literally, the identity requires an account of allocation broad enough to include how resources, recipients, uses, and alternatives become distinguishable at all. Otherwise it already exempts the activity that supplies its own terms.

Fourteen materially different possibilities follow. They are rival proposals about what the identity would mean, not an exhaustive division of reality.

1. **Distribution among established uses.** Resources and possible uses already exist; perspective determines which distribution appears appropriate. This preserves ordinary allocation, but cannot yet explain the creation of its categories.
2. **Production of capacities.** Allocation changes what can later be allocated and done. Learning a language changes the available activities, not just the quantities devoted to existing activities. Perspective becomes part of the production of future powers.
3. **Constitution through use.** Something becomes a resource because a relation makes it usable. A locked room, a key, and knowledge of the lock are not independent additions to a common stock; their conjunction changes what each can do.
4. **Differentiation before selection.** What appears to be allocation begins when differences become actionable. Two sounds receive different treatment only after something distinguishes them. Perspective partly establishes the recipients between which allocation can occur.
5. **Arrangement without transfer.** Identical components and quantities can support different activities when their relations change. Allocation must therefore include placement, coupling, and sequence, rather than only amounts assigned.
6. **Exclusion through commitment.** An allocation is characterized by what it makes unavailable elsewhere. This requires specifying which counterfactual alternatives genuinely coexist; imagined alternatives cannot automatically establish an opportunity cost.
7. **Formation of the feasible.** Some allocations create new alternatives or destroy the conditions for previous ones. The set of possible uses is an effect within the account, not a fixed menu outside it.
8. **Reproduction through time.** What exists now depends on earlier allocations, while present activity preserves or changes those dependencies. A perspective is partly an inherited pattern of what continues to receive support without renewed choice.
9. **Maintenance of an organized living activity.** Distinctions matter through their consequences for continued life and action. Perspective includes bodily readiness and environmental access. This explains a class of allocations; it does not by itself establish the universal identity.
10. **Jurisdiction over use.** Ownership, permission, and obligation determine what counts as available to whom. A room can be physically accessible and unavailable under an accepted commitment. Perspective therefore includes practical authority, not just representation.
11. **Formation of participants.** People and groups are not always fixed recipients. An allocation can create an institution, dependency, or collective agent. The identities of allocator and recipient can be consequences of the relations being investigated.
12. **Assignment of significance.** What receives urgency, credibility, or sacrifice changes subsequent activity. “Resource” must then distinguish physical quantities from practical standing; calling both allocations does not make them interchangeable.
13. **Unchosen differentiation.** If everything is allocation, unintended events must qualify too. Allocation cannot intrinsically require a decision maker. An account must explain what distinguishes allocation from mere occurrence without borrowing intention when convenient.
14. **A discipline of description.** “Everything is resource allocation” may require every account to expose dependencies, exclusions, and enabling conditions. That can be useful without proving that allocation is the fundamental substance or structure of everything.

The first possibility gives a familiar relationship: there is an independently specified world of resources and feasible uses; a perspective represents it; changing perspective helps select a better distribution. Its strongest attraction is that error has an external object. Miscounting the available hours remains an error even if a different description feels liberating. Its limitation appears when the question concerns the formation of “an hour available for work.” An uninterrupted hour and sixty scattered minutes can have equal duration without supporting the same activity. Once this matters, allocation over a quantity of time is underdescribed. The inquiry must include interruption, dependency, and readiness in what it takes itself to be allocating.

That implication does not establish that the world is created by a description. The stronger alternative, combining possibilities three through seven, is that description, arrangement, and action can change the practical identities of what exists. The same room becomes shelter, a workplace, a meeting place, or unusable space through different relations. Some transitions require construction; others require recognition or permission. A representational account of perspective could explain how acquiring a distinction causally completes a usable relation. If it claims to explain these transformations, however, it owes that account rather than assuming that representing an already divided world explains how usable relations form. Likewise, an allocation account that treats resources as independently given atoms owes an explanation of how usability comes into existence if it claims to include such formation. Neither account is refuted merely by pointing to a changed relation.

The strongest incompatible rival says that every such change is simply a movement within one enormous, permanently fixed space of physical possibilities. On that account, new possibilities are merely possibilities that were previously unknown. This could be true at one level and insufficient at another. A complete inventory of physically possible events would not automatically identify which events constitute a promise, a usable method, or a resource for a particular undertaking. The reduction requires a derivation of those relations, not the assertion that physics must contain them. Conversely, their practical emergence does not prove that physical possibilities literally expanded. These claims must remain separate.

Possibilities ten through twelve introduce another transformation. If allocation determines who has standing to decide, the procedure evaluating an allocation is partly one of its consequences. Suppose a group allocates every speaking opportunity to members already regarded as competent. Their later apparent competence can reflect the opportunities that the rule distributed. A perspective formed within that arrangement can mistake an effect of allocation for independent justification of it. Resource allocation must then include the production of its own evidence and judges. Perspective must include the social conditions under which an account can become credible or even audible.

This does not justify erasing differences in competence. It defeats the inference from “this arrangement produced the currently recognized competence” to “the arrangement independently deserves its authority.” Establishing the latter requires additional grounds.

There are precise combinations available. Distribution among fixed uses is retained as a special case when identities and feasibility can reasonably be held constant. Capacity production and relational constitution combine when a changed relation creates a later ability. Jurisdiction and physical feasibility combine only if they remain distinguishable: a prohibition and a material impossibility can constrain the same act without being the same constraint. Calling all three “allocation” fails to explain their differences if it erases the distinctions needed for that explanation. This establishes a limit on explanatory adequacy; the truth of the universal identity remains unsettled.

What is established conditionally is that taking the identity seriously removes the exemption of resource formation, alternative formation, participant formation, and evaluative authority from allocation. The unresolved obstacle is an account precise enough to include these formations while excluding something a rival account would mistakenly include. If “allocation” imposes no discriminating commitments, the identity explains nothing; if it begins with fixed resources and uses, it omits the formation it was supposed to explain. A perfected account has not yet been obtained between those two failures.

Disposition: 1 retained for fixed conditions; its unrestricted adequacy eliminated. 2–5 and 7–8 are compatible contributions. 6 requires genuinely feasible alternatives. 9 remains restricted to living activity. 10–12 are compatible if their differences survive. 13 is required by literal universality but remains underdefined. 14 is a live rival to the metaphysical reading. Exhaustion is unproved.

**7. Reconsider the matter without the five governing nouns.**

I will start with events and distinctions, rather than replace the forbidden terms with five new labels. Consider a meeting in which everyone receives five minutes. Someone concludes that everyone had an equal chance to contribute. Another participant cannot understand the technical language; another cannot speak without risking retaliation; another has evidence that becomes intelligible only after the meeting ends. Equal duration is true. The conclusion about equal participation does not follow. What must be examined is how duration acquired the authority to stand for participation.

Fourteen different accounts can begin here.

1. **Something happened, but nobody registered it.** A sound occurred; no change in subsequent conduct distinguished it from silence. The relevant difference is between occurrence and registration.
2. **Someone registered it without being able to identify it.** A listener noticed an unfamiliar expression but could not distinguish a warning from a joke. Registration does not supply intelligibility.
3. **Someone identified it without recognizing its bearing.** A participant understood every sentence but missed that a proposed rule would exclude them. Comprehension of sentences does not settle what follows for a life.
4. **Someone recognized its bearing without feeling its urgency.** A distant consequence can be understood and remain practically inert. Being able to state its importance and being moved by it are different achievements.
5. **Someone felt its urgency without being able to act.** Fear, exhaustion, lack of permission, or material dependence can prevent a response. Neither persuasion nor further explanation necessarily changes the relevant condition.
6. **Someone could act, but the act would mean something else to others.** Silence intended as refusal may be recorded as agreement. An act and its public uptake can diverge.
7. **The invitation changed the invitee.** Asking someone to speak as a representative can create responsibilities and loyalties that were absent before the invitation. A participant need not remain identical through participation.
8. **The first commitment altered what later commitments could mean.** A promise, followed by a refusal, differs from a refusal followed by a promise. The order does more than rearrange independently meaningful pieces.
9. **A comparison became possible only after a new distinction appeared.** “Everyone had five minutes” ceased to settle fairness when exposure to retaliation became expressible. A newly available distinction can change what earlier evidence supports.
10. **The available names suppressed a difference.** Calling every interruption “discussion” erased the difference between requesting clarification and preventing someone from finishing. A shared name can hide unequal acts.
11. **An absent participant changed what those present owed.** A decision concerning a child, an absent colleague, or a future inhabitant can carry consequences for someone who cannot enter the exchange. Presence and standing are different.
12. **A description made its own evidence.** Calling one participant unreliable caused others to discount their contributions, producing a record in which they apparently contributed little. The record cannot independently settle the description that helped create it.
13. **Two descriptions remained true while their connection failed.** Everyone spoke; some people were not heard. The error can lie in treating the first truth as sufficient for denying the second.
14. **No repair of the description could perform the missing act.** Correctly explaining that a door is locked does not open it. Understanding and changing what happens must remain distinguishable even where each enables the other.

The first strong continuation treats the meeting as a matter of transmission. A speaker has something to convey; the listeners need to receive and understand it. Its strongest form includes language differences, prior knowledge, interruption, and feedback. It is not defeated merely by showing that communication is complicated. It becomes inadequate when the intended content itself depends on what the exchange makes available. A participant may discover what they have to say only when another person names a distinction. There was no fully formed message waiting unchanged at the entrance.

The second continuation therefore treats the exchange as the formation of meanings and commitments. The important unit is an interaction through which something becomes sayable, binding, or contestable. On an account that treats a valid promise as undertaking an obligation, making it changes what the speaker owes; conveying an intention alone does not establish that undertaking. A rival can recognize changed expectations while disputing whether any independent obligation was created. The example therefore exposes competing accounts of what making a promise does; it does not settle their dispute. This continuation also has a limit. A commitment may be clearly formed while the person making it lacks food, access, or the ability to perform it. The enacted meaning does not create every enabling condition.

The third continuation begins with those enabling conditions. Who can remain awake, enter the room, tolerate the noise, understand the language, and speak without retaliation? Here changing a meeting can require changing bodies, schedules, ownership, or dependence. The claim is stronger than “context matters.” The described activity cannot occur without conditions that earlier descriptions treated as peripheral. Nevertheless, reducing the entire exchange to enabling conditions would omit differences between a request, a threat, and a promise made under identical lighting and nutrition. Material support is necessary for these acts without being a complete account of their identities.

These continuations can be combined where the dependency is specified. A newly expressible distinction changes a commitment; the commitment changes permission; permission makes an act possible; the act changes what others can observe. They cannot be combined by assuming that every difference is one more example of the same unnamed thing. A difference between understanding a warning and possessing an exit remains essential.

The strongest rival to this reconstruction is that all these distinctions were available in the original vocabulary and nothing substantial was gained by withholding it. That objection can be right. The value of withholding a word is not magical access to what language concealed. It is the removal of a shortcut that previously allowed an inference to pass without specifying its terms. If equal time never counted as equal participation, the example reveals no such gain. If it did, the exact improvement is the defeat of that substitution.

The withheld nouns may return only after their work can be stated without them. Does a word distinguish registration from understanding, join them under an explicit relation, or silently collapse them? Does it identify what must change, or merely announce that change is desirable? This requirement also remains revisable; vocabulary sometimes permits discoveries that cannot initially be paraphrased without loss. Provisional use can therefore precede complete clarification, while the word receives no exemption from subsequent examination.

What has become available is a set of noninterchangeable transitions: occurrence, registration, intelligibility, bearing, urgency, ability, act, uptake, and obligation. They need not form one fixed sequence; an obligation can precede understanding, and an act can reveal its own significance afterward. The remaining obstacle is to explain which transitions are required in a particular case and why, without assuming that this list already contains every way something can begin to matter.

Disposition: 1–6 survive as distinguishable occurrences. 7–12 survive as possible changes to participants, meanings, and available evidence. 13 defeats the inference from separate truths to adequate connection. 14 defeats the sufficiency of description for action. These accounts combine only where their relations are specified. Their collective completeness remains unproved.

**8. Let relationships change what the subjects are.**

The mistaken division need not be an inaccurate map of otherwise settled territories. It may be the presumption that there must first be independently identifiable territories for relationships to connect. That presumption requires examination without the opposite presumption that everything must dissolve into undifferentiated connection.

Fifteen accounts compete over how units of inquiry acquire their identities.

1. **Intrinsic kinds.** A subject concerns things sharing properties independently of our interests. Relationships supply information about those things but do not constitute their identities.
2. **Common causal origin.** Things belong together because a shared process produces them. Superficially similar effects can belong to different inquiries when their origins differ.
3. **Intervention dependence.** A subject is what changes together under an explicitly specified intervention. The relevant boundary can differ for changing a cause and predicting an observation.
4. **Explanatory sufficiency.** A unit is the smallest inclusion of relations sufficient for a particular explanation. Changing what needs explaining can change the unit without anyone making an error.
5. **Persistence through transformation.** A subject is identified by what remains trackable through specified changes. This makes its identity depend on which transformations matter, rather than only its present properties.
6. **Joint constitution.** Creditor and debtor, teacher and pupil, or sender and recipient acquire those identities through relations. One cannot define all such participants completely before describing the relation.
7. **A history with consequences.** The present properties of two things may match while their histories support different expectations or obligations. Inquiry follows a lineage of dependence rather than a static class.
8. **Coordination for action.** A subject bundles what must be considered together to accomplish something. A discipline organized for teaching can therefore differ from one organized for building or repairing.
9. **Institutional inheritance.** Departmental boundaries preserve training, funding, and professional jurisdiction. Their durability does not by itself show that they reflect the deepest divisions in what is studied.
10. **Shared means of access.** Things are grouped because the same instrument or method reveals them. A new means of access can expose dependencies the earlier grouping systematically missed.
11. **Question-dependent individuation.** Asking what a thing is for, what produced it, or who can change it can pick out different boundaries. “The same subject” may conceal several incompatible individuations.
12. **Emergence across scale.** A pattern can have stable properties that its isolated participants lack. The relevant subject becomes an organized activity, while the participants remain relevant to other questions.
13. **Relations without privileged endpoints.** Begin with transformations, dependencies, or exchanges; identify stable participants through repeated relations. Objects are recovered from how they enter the activity rather than supplied beforehand.
14. **Standing and exclusion.** A boundary determines whose experience and consequences count. A subject described as “workplace productivity” can omit unpaid recovery or domestic labor by how it is initially delimited.
15. **Several legitimate partitions.** There may be no uniquely correct division independent of purpose. The ambition becomes exact knowledge of when each division is warranted and what translation between divisions preserves or loses.

Consider an inquiry beginning with “attention.” One branch treats attention as a property of an individual. Another examines tasks, interruptions, rewards, sleep, language, and social permission. Simply placing links from an attention node to six other nodes preserves the original decision about what the central object is. A stronger revision asks whether the activity to be explained is an achievement of person, task, environment, and timing together. If so, the unit changes. Some failures previously located inside the person now belong to a relation that no isolated person possesses.

This is not a license to deny individual differences. The intrinsic account has a strong alternative: environmental relations are inputs to a genuine individual capacity, and causal analysis can separate them. To choose between these accounts, specify the claim whose unit is disputed. If “attention” means a person's ability across a stated range of environments, the individual capacity may remain an appropriate object. If it means the sustained coordination actually occurring in one activity, the environment cannot be dismissed as an external input merely by definition. These accounts may concern different objects under one inherited word. Combining them requires distinguishing those objects, not averaging their descriptions.

The joint-constitution account goes further. “Teacher” and “pupil” are intelligible through activities of teaching and learning, even though the people exist independently. Thus the relationship can constitute a subject's relevant identity without creating its entire material existence. This defeats the false choice between self-contained objects and a world with no objects. It also shows why a catalog organized around already named participants can repeatedly miss the transformation it seeks: the next discovery may change what a participant is counted as doing.

The account beginning with relations has its own concealed commitment. A relation must be differentiated from other relations. If every relation is first defined by endpoints drawn from the inherited catalog, nothing has changed. But if relations are primitive without criteria of identity, the vocabulary has merely moved the unexplained boundary. A usable departure therefore needs cases of sameness across changing endpoints: the same dependency can persist after a person is replaced; the same person can enter a materially different dependency. This gives relational individuation actual work without declaring it universally prior.

The plural-partition account offers another route. A subject division is warranted relative to a question and an operation, while cross-subject relations disclose when that warrant ends. Its strongest limitation is dependence on the supplied question. If the initial question contains the central mistake, purpose-relative organization can preserve it perfectly. Consequently, question formation must be among the activities capable of altering subject boundaries. The inquiry cannot put its organizing aim outside the relations it is studying.

There are warranted local eliminations. A fixed taxonomy is inadequate as the sole organizer wherever the relevant identities change through relations it cannot represent. An unqualified “everything is connected” is inadequate wherever it cannot distinguish dependencies that change the answer from connections that do not. A single imposed relational ontology is also unearned if several different individuations explain different aspects without contradiction.

For subjectsystems, the concrete implication is that a discovery cannot always arrive as a new entry or link. It may invalidate the identity conditions of existing entries. To represent such a revision accurately, the record must preserve the original claim with its original meanings, represent the revised successor separately, and specify which commitments, if any, carry across the change. It should also be able to record that two subjects were one activity divided for convenience and distinguish disappearance of a boundary from loss of the distinctions it previously protected. Changing what a noun denotes does not by itself preserve the claim expressed with it. Without these distinctions, accumulated organization can become a veto on the discoveries it was designed to support.

What is established is that subjects can be products of inquiry and relations, not merely its inputs. What remains unresolved is a general basis for choosing or generating the right unit when neither the aim nor the admissible relations are already secure. Replacing the old catalog with a universal catalog of relations does not settle that question.

Disposition: 1–2 remain live for some subjects. 3–5, 7–8, and 10–12 are compatible means of individuation under specified conditions. 6 defeats mandatory independence of participants. 9 and 14 expose possible distortions. 13 remains a live foundational rival, not a universal conclusion. 15 is warranted locally; its sufficiency when aims change remains unresolved. Exhaustion is unproved.

**9. A sincerely pursued goal can express an inadequate understanding of what is wanted.**

Sincerity establishes that someone is pursuing what they take themselves to want. It does not establish that their formulation exhausts what gives the pursuit its significance. The difficult issue is how a goal can change for reasons that matter without requiring the old goal to authorize every change or treating every later preference as superior.

Fifteen accounts locate the inadequacy differently.

1. **An inaccurate means-end belief.** Someone genuinely wants health and pursues an activity they falsely believe promotes it. The final aim remains intact; the route changes.
2. **A proxy mistaken for its point.** Someone pursues flawless scores because scores once indicated understanding. The measure becomes the goal even when it ceases to track what originally mattered.
3. **An impoverished description of the desired state.** “I want agreement” fails to distinguish willing understanding from coerced assent. The aim changes when its previously conflated realizations become visible.
4. **Unrecognized internal conflict.** A person sincerely wants independence and constant external reassurance. Neither avowal is insincere; their combined practical requirements may be incompatible in particular circumstances.
5. **A missing experiential distinction.** Someone cannot yet distinguish admiration from being understood. An encounter can make a desire articulable that was not an available candidate in their earlier deliberation.
6. **Desire shaped by deprivation.** A person can cease to want what has seemed inaccessible. Present preference alone may then understate what they would value with different opportunities; that counterfactual still requires support.
7. **Affective misweighting.** What feels urgent dominates what the person continues to endorse over time. The mismatch concerns practical force, not necessarily an incorrect belief about importance.
8. **An inherited demand experienced as one's own.** A person's ambition can preserve an authority whose claim they have never examined. Its origin does not automatically discredit it, but makes its authority a separate question.
9. **An identity sustained by pursuit.** Wanting to win can maintain a sense of being a certain kind of person. Abandoning the contest changes more than an isolated preference and may initially appear unintelligible.
10. **Value created through commitment.** Some undertakings become important partly because someone commits to them. A changed goal can constitute a new practical identity rather than reveal an eternally hidden authentic desire.
11. **Change through acquaintance.** Learning what a life or activity is like changes the person who evaluates it. Choosing cannot always be reduced to applying unchanged preferences to fuller information.
12. **Several irreducible goods.** A single aim can conceal values that resist one ranking. The inadequacy lies in demanding that one target represent everything worth pursuing.
13. **A goal shared imperfectly among people.** “Our aim” can hide different purposes and unequal authority. Reformulating it changes whose desire the collective activity expresses.
14. **A demand to eliminate exposure.** Seeking certainty or complete control can express a wish never again to depend on what one cannot anticipate. This is one possible interpretation of perfection, not a diagnosis of this inquiry.
15. **An inadequate form of wanting.** Treating every worthwhile relation as a state to acquire can miss activities whose value consists in participating, attending, or answering appropriately. The revision concerns how wanting is organized, not just its object.

The strongest familiar account combines the first three: discover the actual aim, correct beliefs, replace proxies, and pursue what the person really wanted all along. This deserves retention where the evidence supports it. If someone sought accurate understanding and treated citation counts as a reliable measure, exposing the broken link can warrant changing the measure without revising the value of understanding.

But the account becomes presumptuous when it invents a fully formed deeper desire to explain every change. Suppose an encounter makes a kind of obligation recognizable that previously had no place in the person's account of a good life. It need not be an old desire finally uncovered. Under possibilities five, ten, and eleven, the encounter creates a distinction, a commitment, or a capacity for concern. Goal formation is then partly developmental: the person becomes able to want something, and that changes what earlier aims mean.

The strongest incompatible rival says that these are changes of preference, not improvements. Without an enduring criterion, the later person has no automatic authority over the earlier one. That objection defeats the inference “I now endorse it, therefore it was better for me to become someone who endorses it.” Manipulation could produce the same structure of endorsement. The developmental account needs grounds that distinguish becoming able to recognize something from being trained to approve a replacement. Those grounds may involve the newly accessible reasons, consequences, and freedom to reject the transition. None alone is a universal certificate.

A third continuation questions the goal form itself. Suppose perfected perspective is understood as possessing a finished account that settles every possible future inquiry. This can make an unresolved question appear to be a defect merely because it interrupts completion. Yet a perfected understanding might correctly recognize that a question is indeterminate, lacks the presupposed object, or requires an act whose consequences do not yet exist. Recognizing this would not be a retreat into open-mindedness. It would be an exact achievement: knowing why a demanded determination is unwarranted and what, if anything, could make it warranted.

There remains a much stronger ambition than “stay flexible”: nothing relevant should be misrepresented, wrongly excluded, or subjected to an inappropriate demand. That formulation, however, is still a candidate. “Relevant,” “misrepresented,” and “inappropriate” do not become self-interpreting because the goal sounds less rigid. The inquiry must permit discovery to alter those terms too.

A responsible departure therefore distinguishes at least three claims: the prior formulation misdescribed an enduring concern; a newly encountered concern deserves standing alongside it; and the form of aiming itself obstructs what matters. These claims have different burdens. The first requires evidence of the enduring concern. The second requires reasons for the added standing without pretending it was always secretly desired. The third requires showing the specific exclusion produced by the form, rather than announcing that goals are limiting.

Combinations are warranted when they preserve these differences. A proxy may fail while the underlying concern also changes. A commitment can create value without making every commitment valuable. Several goods can resist complete ranking while some particular comparisons remain decisive. None of these conclusions permits eliminating the pursuit of perfection merely because its current wording is defective.

What is established is that sincerity and persistence cannot certify either the content or the form of a goal. Our pursuit may concern complete understanding, justified authority over our own inquiry, access to presently unavailable forms of significance, or some combination we have not yet correctly distinguished. The unresolved obstacle is how to warrant transformation of what matters when present endorsement, future endorsement, and conformity to an inherited ideal are all insufficient. That obstacle must remain inside the inquiry rather than being settled by declaring one version of our desire authentic.

Disposition: 1–3 survive for correction within a concern. 4 and 12 expose possible conflicts. 5, 10, and 11 remain compatible developmental accounts. 6–9 and 13–14 remain conditional explanations, not diagnoses. 15 remains a live challenge to the form of pursuit. Later endorsement as sufficient certification is eliminated. No listed concern is established as our final aim.

**10. Improve what counts as improvement without appointing a new unquestionable judge.**

A standard does two things that must be separated: it can supply a reason for preferring something, and it can decide which kinds of reasons are allowed to count. The central mistake occurs when its useful first role is silently promoted into unconditional authority over the second.

Fifteen alternatives reveal different ways that authority could fail.

1. **Predictive success.** Prefer the account that anticipates observations. This can reject understanding whose immediate contribution is changing which observations should be distinguished.
2. **Demonstrative certainty.** Prefer what follows from stated premises. This can reject the conceptual reconstruction needed to recognize that the premises or objects were incorrectly posed.
3. **Internal consistency.** Prefer mutually compatible claims. This can preserve an incomplete vocabulary that achieves consistency by being unable to express a consequential distinction.
4. **Simplicity.** Prefer fewer commitments. This can reject necessary distinctions as needless elaboration, while complexity can also hide a failure to understand.
5. **Explanatory reach.** Prefer an account that covers more cases. This can reward a vocabulary that calls unlike things the same and therefore appears universal.
6. **Practical success.** Prefer what achieves an aim. This can reject an understanding that reveals the aim itself to be mistaken.
7. **Experienced intelligibility.** Prefer what makes immediate sense. This can exclude understanding whose concepts are not yet available to the evaluator.
8. **Transformation of ability.** Prefer what enables previously unavailable action or discrimination. This can mistake increased power for improved judgment about its use.
9. **Retrospective endorsement.** Prefer what a later, informed person approves. This can fail when the transition produced the approval it then cites as justification.
10. **Agreement among capable inquirers.** Prefer what survives diverse examination. This can preserve an error shared by their training, access, or selection into the group.
11. **Accountability to affected people.** Prefer what those bearing consequences can challenge. This can reveal excluded reasons, while agreement among affected people need not establish factual correctness.
12. **Correction of characteristic failure.** Prefer a change that removes a demonstrated obstruction. This supplies a local reason without proving that the resulting account is best overall.
13. **Preservation across changes of representation.** Prefer what remains warranted under several ways of expressing it. This can expose dependence on arbitrary notation, but several representations may share the same omission.
14. **Generation of worthwhile possibilities.** Prefer an account that makes further understanding possible. This can distinguish a fertile reorganization from sterile closure, while fertility alone also admits endless distraction.
15. **Exact recognition of limits.** Prefer an account that correctly identifies where its authority ends. This can avoid false certainty, but mere modesty does not establish that the stated limits are real.

The strongest response is initially to combine these standards and prevent any one from dominating. But a weighted combination appoints a new judge: the choice of standards, weights, and conflict rules. Even a rule requiring unanimous agreement gives each included standard veto power and excludes absent standards. Combining judges does not remove the question of jurisdiction.

There is a deeper failure than unfair weighting. Sound standards can divide their work along the wrong units. Suppose a description of who controls a meeting is ranked against a description of how long each person speaks as competing accounts of participation. Neither must defeat the other: their relation can reveal a form of control that equal durations conceal. Increasing the fairness of their comparison preserves the mistaken demand that one account win or that their verdicts be averaged. The needed change dissolves that comparison and supplies a different object to understand. This possibility must remain available before any procedure begins eliminating candidates one by one.

One continuation seeks a superior criterion capable of explaining the others. For example, “prefer what supports accurate understanding” could subordinate simplicity, prediction, and consistency to their contribution to accuracy. This is sensible where accuracy has a determinate object and assessment. It does not resolve the present question when what counts as understanding or as the relevant object is itself disputed. Repeating the ambition at a higher level does not supply the missing discrimination.

A second continuation replaces ranking with specific demonstrations of inadequacy. If one account cannot distinguish coerced assent from informed agreement, and both occur within the class it purports to explain, its claim to explain agreement is incomplete. That conclusion does not require already possessing a perfected account of agreement. The starting account contributes a claim with implications; a difference it collapses matters to those implications. It can lose universal authority without every useful distinction it contains being discarded.

The strongest rival replies that deciding which distinctions matter still invokes a standard. Correct. This continuation avoids an all-purpose judge, not every act of judgment. Its force is conditional and specific: an account that undertakes to explain voluntary endorsement cannot treat the difference between coercion and voluntary endorsement as irrelevant without changing its undertaking. The argument establishes a defect relative to a claim. It does not compel someone who rejects that undertaking to value it. Universal normative authority has not been derived.

A third continuation allows transformation to establish access to reasons that were formerly unintelligible. Someone learning a mathematical distinction can later understand why their earlier objection missed the issue. The earlier vocabulary could not fully certify the understanding it lacked. The transition can therefore precede complete antecedent justification. But this does not license “whatever changes me is better.” The later account owes an explanation of the earlier objection, the new distinction, and why the transition warrants anything beyond its own endorsement. Where such an explanation remains unavailable, the transformation may be valuable but is not certified as perfected understanding.

This continuation preserves neither an absolute predecessor veto nor an absolute successor veto. A successor can explain why a predecessor's standard was too narrow; a predecessor's unanswered objection can still expose a successor's error. The authority belongs to the particular reason and its demonstrated bearing, provisionally understood, rather than to being early or late. That itself is an account open to criticism, not a mechanism proven to escape all circularity.

The appropriate eliminations are correspondingly exact. Eliminate the claim that later approval alone proves improvement. Eliminate the claim that success under a supplied aim settles whether the aim deserved pursuit. Eliminate the inference from joint agreement among existing standards to absence of a shared defect. Retain prediction, proof, coherence, consequences, and testimony wherever their bearing can be established. Their limits do not make them worthless, and their usefulness does not restore unconditional jurisdiction.

What would exceed our present standards? An account that initially looks less simple because it exposes a difference we cannot afford to omit; one that suspends an apparently decisive answer because the question conflates incompatible demands; one that changes what evidence can establish without denying any observation; one that reveals a concern whose significance our existing ranking cannot express. These are structural possibilities, not a claim that any deliberately difficult or unfamiliar account is superior.

What is established is the difference between using a standard and granting it unrestricted authority over admissible successors. We can remove particular overclaims, identify specific gains, and preserve unresolved disputes without pretending they have been resolved. The exact remaining obstacle is a warrant for final authority that neither assumes the disputed standards nor merely appeals to the successor's self-endorsement. No such warrant has been supplied here. A procedure that concealed this gap would reproduce the original mistake while announcing that it had overcome it.

Disposition: 1–8 and 10–15 survive as sources of particular reasons; each claim to unconditional jurisdiction is eliminated. 9 survives as evidence about later endorsement, not sufficient evidence of improvement. 12 supplies local eliminations, not final ranking. Combining the survivors remains conditional on correct units and relationships. Their completeness and any universally authoritative ordering remain unproved.

**11. Mathematics, science, philosophy, and writing as ways of governing change**

The premise does not yet tell us what is being governed: permissible conclusions, changes in meaning, exposure to interference, temporary commitments, or the ability of another person to reconstruct an understanding. Those produce different accounts of why these activities differ.

11.1. **They answer to different kinds of dependence.** A conclusion can depend on a stipulated relation, on what actually happens, on the intelligibility of the question, or on an expression’s effects on a reader. On this account, some divisions track differences that survive any departmental reorganization. A derivation from stipulated rules cannot by itself establish that the world instantiates those rules.

11.2. **They differ in what they temporarily forbid changing.** A proof fixes specified meanings and inference conditions; an investigation fixes enough of a question to distinguish outcomes; conceptual inquiry can reopen the meanings; drafting can reopen their presentation and sometimes their content. The fundamental distinction is between different permissions to revise, not between subject territories.

11.3. **They differ in where resistance can enter.** An inference can be resisted by another inference; a prediction by an occurrence; a formulation by the inability to make its distinctions coherently; an explanation by the reader’s inability to reconstruct it. The divisions track different channels through which an account loses its authority.

11.4. **They manufacture different objects by enforcing different invariances.** An ideal triangle exists as an object of inquiry partly through which differences are declared irrelevant. A measured object is identified through other permitted variations. What looks like a difference between preexisting subjects can therefore originate in different rules for making something count as the same object.

11.5. **They are different stages that became separate professions.** An inquiry might invent a distinction, formalize its consequences, encounter actual cases, and reconstruct its account for another mind. If the stages can occur within one activity, professional boundaries cannot be the fundamental divisions. But calling them stages must not impose one compulsory order.

11.6. **They allocate the cost of being wrong differently.** Some settings require establishing an implication before using it; others require acting before decisive evidence exists; others permit a provisional formulation because its failure reveals an unmade distinction. The difference concerns which errors must be prevented before proceeding and which can become informative afterward.

11.7. **They differ in the commitments they make portable.** A proof offers reconstructible implication; an experimental description offers conditions another investigator might recreate; an argument offers distinctions another person can interrogate; writing can make any of these available. These activities therefore overlap rather than form mutually exclusive compartments. Their overlap does not by itself establish different classificatory levels; mathematics can likewise operate within science.

11.8. **They are rival policies for ending local disagreement.** Derivability, successful confrontation with occurrences, exposure of conceptual confusion, and achieved understanding can each authorize stopping. Their differences persist because they stop different disputes. Trouble begins when a stopping condition valid for one dispute is treated as settlement of another.

11.9. **They are ways of making thought answerable to something it cannot simply declare satisfied.** The common achievement is the construction of resistance. Their apparent differences may be implementations of that relation. This account must still distinguish genuine resistance from a ceremony in which the account controls both the challenge and what counts as passing it.

11.10. **Their deepest differences concern whether meanings may move during an inference.** A valid derivation can become invalid if a term changes meaning halfway through. Conversely, an inquiry can remain inadequate precisely because its meanings are held fixed. Meaning preservation and meaning transformation are both indispensable, but they cannot govern the same transition in the same respect.

11.11. **Some differences arise from access rather than subject matter.** A publicly recorded occurrence, a first-person experience, a hypothetical construction, and a normative commitment are encountered differently. Putting all their descriptions into sentences does not erase those access differences. The account would fail if those different routes imposed no differences on what conclusions could be warranted.

11.12. **Their divisions express inherited control over admission.** Training, publication forms, acceptable questions, and credentialed participation can preserve a division even when its intellectual operations overlap. Here the division is contingent. This is a candidate explanation, not a historical finding that every actual disciplinary boundary arose this way.

11.13. **They generate different kinds of novelty.** One can derive a previously unknown consequence without changing the available concepts; introduce a new distinction without yet deriving anything; disclose an occurrence for which existing distinctions fail; or create an expression that enables recognition previously unavailable. “Producing knowledge” conceals these different changes.

11.14. **They are differently situated collaborations with materials.** Diagrams, notation, instruments, sentences, and other people can make different operations possible. What appears to be a faculty belonging to an isolated thinker might depend on a particular arrangement of these supports. Under this account, reorganizing the arrangement can change the available understanding without changing the thinker’s stated beliefs.

11.15. **There are several irreducible senses of adequacy.** A statement can follow necessarily and fail to describe an actual case; describe a case and fail to make a concern intelligible; make a concern intelligible and offer no legitimate basis for acting. Their differences cannot be abolished merely because all involve changes in understanding.

Carry three rivals further. **The intrinsic-difference account** says the disciplines differ because reality, implication, meaning, and expression demand different forms of answerability. Consider a formal traffic model in which a certain rule eliminates collisions. The proof settles an implication under the model’s conditions. Whether an actual intersection satisfies those conditions remains a separate question. Even perfect proof of the implication supplies no observation of the intersection. Conversely, observing a thousand safe crossings does not establish the unrestricted formal implication. This demonstrates a difference between claims and their conditions of support. It does not demonstrate that the contemporary organization into mathematics and science is necessary: one person or one inquiry could perform both operations.

**The change-permissions account** offers a more radical reconstruction. The basic activities are holding something fixed, deriving what that commitment permits, exposing the commitment to what it fails to accommodate, and changing the commitment. Under this account, mathematics and philosophy can both invent or revise meanings; science and writing can both expose an unnoticed implication; the established labels are unstable bundles. Its strongest difficulty is that naming a common operation does not make its enactments interchangeable. “Expose to resistance” does not tell us whether a counterexample is an inconsistent assignment, an actual event, or an unintelligible description. The proposed unification earns its scope only by preserving these distinctions.

**The irreducible-adequacies account** denies that a single change-governing currency is available. A reason to believe, a reason to infer, a reason to care, and a reason to act are not automatically exchangeable. Suppose an account predicts behavior perfectly while interpreting every stated commitment as a disposable instrument. Its predictive success does not itself settle whether it has understood the commitment as a commitment. The opposing account might say that sufficiently developed understanding would show these to be one relation. That remains a substantive claim requiring a bridge, not something the word “understanding” has already supplied.

The justified combination is limited: inquiry uses different controls over change, and some differences track different commitments rather than inherited departmental names. Eliminate the claim that shared involvement in perspective change makes the activities interchangeable; its conclusion does not follow from its premise. Also eliminate treating these activities as mutually exclusive compartments: writing can conduct the operations of the others, and mathematics can operate within science. This overlap alone does not settle their classificatory levels.

What remains unsettled is whether there is a complete set of elementary changes from which all these operations can be constructed, and whether their several kinds of adequacy can be related without erasing any. A perfected account would have to establish those relations, show why each distinction is necessary, and show what its proposed distinctions cannot express. This examination does not yet supply that account.

Disposition: 11.1 and 11.15 retain the demonstrated distinction between implication and instantiation; their stronger claim of ultimate irreducibility remains open. 11.2, 11.3, 11.10, and 11.13 are compatible accounts of different operations, not a proved complete basis. 11.4 remains a live account of how objects become identifiable. 11.5 and 11.12 require historical evidence before explaining actual disciplinary divisions. 11.6, 11.7, and 11.14 retain distinct practical contributions; none alone explains every division, and the overlap in 11.7 does not establish different classificatory levels. 11.8 supplies local stopping conditions, not unrestricted settlement. 11.9 survives as a common requirement of answerability; its stronger reduction of every difference to one interchangeable relation is unsupported.

**12. When preserving true understanding preserves the inadequacy**

12.1. **Truth has been promoted into jurisdiction.** An earlier account establishes something correctly and is then allowed to decide which later questions are legitimate. A true account of how a queue moves need not determine whether the queue should exist. The obstruction is the grant of authority beyond what the truth establishes.

12.2. **The old names fix what later things are allowed to be.** A category can accurately describe prior cases while preventing a new relation from changing how the cases are individuated. What must be relinquished is the category’s continuing monopoly, not the truth of every sentence previously written using it.

12.3. **Consistency is being demanded across meanings that have changed.** An earlier statement and a later statement can appear contradictory because the same word now does different work. Requiring verbal agreement can prevent an improved distinction. The task is to identify their respective conditions of truth before deciding whether a contradiction actually exists.

12.4. **Earlier explanations possess an unearned presumption of centrality.** A factor may truly contribute to an event without being the factor that makes the event intelligible for the current inquiry. Keeping every new explanation organized around the first true factor prevents a change in explanatory center.

12.5. **Preservation determines what receives attention.** Every new account must first be reconciled with an expanding archive. The old account can therefore dominate even when nobody believes it infallible. What has to change is its priority in the allocation of inquiry, not necessarily its contents.

12.6. **Previously useful distinctions are treated as permanently useful.** A coarse distinction may have enabled an earlier advance. Later, the same division may join cases that must be separated or separate cases whose relation now matters. Historical usefulness does not establish continued adequacy.

12.7. **An earlier success has fixed the identity of the activity.** A method first succeeds at explaining, so every subsequent contribution must look like explanation; first succeeds at classifying, so every discovery becomes another category. The preserved element is what the inquiry takes itself to be doing.

12.8. **Existing understanding makes its own counterfactual alternatives difficult to express.** A framework may contain vocabulary for correcting its entries but none for abandoning the distinctions that make entries possible. Its archive preserves every answer while excluding the form a deeper question would need.

12.9. **The accumulated account has become a criterion of recognizability.** A new understanding is admitted only if it can be summarized in existing terms. A transformation whose point is to alter those terms will then look irrelevant or incoherent at the admission stage, before its adequacy is actually considered.

12.10. **Earlier goals govern what is worth retaining.** An account can preserve everything useful to a previous aim while excluding what would show that aim to be misconceived. Preserving “what matters” is not neutral when the account also determines what matters.

12.11. **Dependence has been confused with indebtedness.** A later understanding may have been reached through an earlier one without needing the earlier one as a permanent foundation. The route by which an insight became available is not automatically part of its justification or its best present expression.

12.12. **The archive preserves conclusions but loses their conditions.** A locally correct observation survives as an unrestricted maxim after the conditions that made it correct disappear from view. The immediate repair is to recover the conditions. A deeper repair makes unrestricted inheritance impossible unless unrestricted scope was actually established.

12.13. **The old account requires descendants to preserve its arrangement.** Every new distinction must enter the existing hierarchy, every relation an existing field. Even true entries then maintain a false organization. Retaining the entries while changing which entries define one another can preserve truth and replace the account.

12.14. **Understanding has become something to defend because it constitutes an identity.** An idea can remain available as a fact while losing its role as “what makes this our project.” If that loss is unacceptable, the inquiry has a protected commitment that agreement with facts will never reach.

12.15. **Preservation excludes constructive disappearance.** Some scaffolding is valuable because it enables a distinction and then becomes unnecessary. An account that must carry every successful intermediate indefinitely mistakes the history of learning for the organization of understanding.

The strongest preservation principle says: **retain every established truth and permit departures only if they remain consistent with it.** Its sound component is that discovering a new frame does not license declaring a known occurrence nonexistent. Its unsound extension is to treat every current representation of that occurrence as equally protected. Imagine an inquiry initially organized around individual lateness. Its records correctly establish when each person arrived. A later account organized around incompatible transport schedules need not change those times. It can change what counts as an individual case, which comparisons matter, what explains the pattern, and what intervention becomes intelligible. The facts survive while their authority to individuate the concern disappears.

A rival says: **suspend the earlier account entirely, because deciding what to preserve already lets it govern the replacement.** This makes a previously excluded departure possible. It also creates a real risk: the new account may regain apparent completeness by losing a troublesome distinction. The answer is not to demand reconciliation before departure. Distinguish the conditions for allowing an account to develop from the conditions for accepting its eventual claims. Development can proceed without satisfying the incumbent’s vocabulary. Acceptance of a claim still requires identifying what that claim commits us to, including any genuine contradiction with what remains established. Departure and endorsement are different acts.

A third account says: **preserve the ability to reconstruct the insight, not its place in the current account.** An earlier insight can remain retrievable under its original conditions without occupying the new account’s center. This permits a later account to have a different ontology and different relevance relations. Its strongest rival is the claim that reconstruction inevitably imports the old categories. Sometimes it will. Where a successor claims preservation or reconstructibility, that claim requires an account of what survives and under which meanings; where it claims no such continuity, departure does not require reconstructing every old commitment. If a new account cannot express an old true distinction, that is a loss relative to the earlier expressive repertoire. Whether that loss matters to the successor remains a further question. It cannot be concealed while claiming complete preservation, but it need not prevent departure.

These accounts can be combined only by separating three permissions: permission to depart, permission to assert, and permission to organize subsequent inquiry. A true earlier assertion retains the support it actually has. It does not thereby retain the other two powers. Eliminate the demand that every new understanding preserve the old account’s sequence, categories, or center of explanation; these requirements do not follow from the truth of its assertions.

The remaining difficulty for a claim of preservation is deciding whether a purportedly preserved insight genuinely survives when its terms acquire different meanings. No general assurance of “keeping what was useful” resolves this. A claimed complete preservation or reconstructible synthesis would require an adequate account of the relevant carryover, changes, and losses. Departure itself need not preserve or reconstruct everything. Where the claimed translation cannot be supplied, that preservation or synthesis claim remains unestablished; this does not by itself establish that the successor is inadequate or that the transition cannot be perfected.

Disposition: 12.1 and 12.11 establish limits on what truth and historical dependence authorize. 12.3 and 12.12 require recovering meanings and conditions before declaring contradiction or unrestricted validity. 12.2, 12.6, 12.8, and 12.13 identify compatible ways the organization can change while assertions remain true; which change is needed remains case-dependent. 12.4, 12.5, 12.7, 12.9, 12.10, and 12.14 are rival or concurrent explanations of why the incumbent retains authority; this inquiry does not establish their actual presence. 12.15 retains the possibility of retiring scaffolding; an additional claim that an insight remains reconstructible requires support for that claim. No option makes an established assertion false merely because the successor no longer retains it.

**13. Constraints that operate before stated assumptions**

13.1. **A difference never becomes discriminable.** Two situations are encountered as the same, so no belief about their difference can be questioned. Inquiry must make the contrast available in some form. Merely asking for hidden assumptions cannot recover a distinction the current encounter does not supply.

13.2. **A difference is discriminable but never selected.** It could be noticed but is consistently displaced by something more salient. The intervention concerns which contrasts receive attention, not which propositions are endorsed. A correct belief about the neglected difference can coexist with its practical absence.

13.3. **Something is noticed but cannot be retained long enough to matter.** The constraint is continuity across encounters. Changing the available records, intermediate representations, or timing might enable a relation no revision of a single belief would reveal.

13.4. **The available grammar predetermines what counts as a candidate thought.** If every admissible account must have an object bearing properties, relations that constitute the object may be forced into secondary status. Inquiry begins by constructing expressions that do not require that grammatical commitment.

13.5. **The assumed activity chooses the question before reasoning begins.** Interpreting a prompt as a request for an answer makes answer production seem obligatory; interpreting the encounter as a chance to determine what requires understanding changes the available next acts. The same factual beliefs can support both activities.

13.6. **An anticipated audience filters what is allowed to occur.** A thought that cannot immediately be made respectable may be suppressed before it becomes a claim. Suspending the requirement for immediate public acceptability can reveal whether the inhibition concerns falsity, incomprehensibility, or anticipated social reception.

13.7. **A felt orientation determines which occurrences have weight.** Threat, attraction, indifference, or urgency can organize an encounter before an explicit judgment is formed. This is a candidate location of constraint, not a claim that every such orientation is mistaken or that changing it necessarily improves understanding.

13.8. **The body supplies a restricted field of possible engagement.** What can be reached, endured, sensed, or coordinated affects which distinctions become available through action. Altering an actual mode of engagement can therefore disclose something that a different verbal description leaves inaccessible.

13.9. **The temporal window makes some relations disappear.** A response organized around the next sentence cannot directly display a relation that appears only across many encounters. Conversely, an account concerned only with long trajectories can erase what occurs within a moment. The unit of time governs intelligibility.

13.10. **The scale of individuation preselects causes.** If only persons are represented, relations among schedules or institutions must be translated into personal attributes. If only aggregates are represented, a singular constraint can vanish. Inquiry must permit changes in what counts as one case.

13.11. **The permitted action repertoire constrains thought.** If the only available continuation is another explanation, requests to revise the activity are converted into explanations about revision. Making a different act available can expose the restriction more directly than describing it.

13.12. **Retrieval preserves earlier organization.** Relevant material can exist but remain unavailable because retrieval begins from the wrong handles. Inquiry starts by changing the basis of access: a relation, anomaly, consequence, or incompatibility may retrieve what the established subject name does not.

13.13. **Premature closure removes distinctions before they stabilize.** An unfamiliar formulation is immediately assigned the nearest familiar interpretation. The resulting account looks intelligible because the unfamiliar part has already been discarded. Inquiry requires retaining the unresolved difference without prematurely assigning its identity.

13.14. **A requirement for serial expression constrains a relation that must be grasped jointly.** Explaining one element completely before mentioning another can misrepresent their mutual dependence. A simultaneous representation, enactment, or carefully unresolved description may be needed before either element can be properly stated.

13.15. **The interaction itself continually reinstates the constraint.** One participant is always asked to explain, another to approve, so every challenge becomes an improved submission to the same authority. The governing condition resides in the relation among participants, not solely in any individual’s beliefs.

The first serious rival is **deeper introspection**: formulate the operative interpretation, question it, and repeat. This works when a constraint can become an object of attention through available distinctions. Its limitation follows directly from the premise. If the strongest constraint determines which distinctions become available, repeating the request to name it provides no guarantee of reaching it. “I found no further assumption” cannot establish “no further constraint governs this inquiry.” The search condition and the stopping condition would both depend on what the constraint permits to appear.

The second rival is **changing the conditions under which an account can occur**. Hold a description fixed while altering one relevant permission: whether an answer must be immediate; whether objects must be named before relations; whether the person must explain or may demonstrate; whether a draft must preserve prior wording. The purpose is not random variation followed by applause for the most attractive product. Each alteration follows from an identified structural dependence. If relations can only be entered after subjects have been defined, temporarily permitting a relation without preassigned endpoints directly challenges that dependence. A different account then becomes evidence of what the old arrangement excluded; it does not automatically become evidence that the new account is superior.

The third rival is **encounter with what the current account cannot predetermine**. Another person’s organization of a concern, an unfamiliar form of engagement, or an occurrence that defeats an expected continuation may supply a distinction unavailable through self-description. Its strongest limitation is that an encounter still arrives through some conditions of reception. There is no warranted leap from “the constraint was interrupted” to “all such constraints are now visible.” Nevertheless, being unable to occupy an entirely unconditioned position does not imply that every position is equally constrained in the same respects. A specific exclusion can become visible without claiming access to every exclusion.

The useful combination is an exact division of work: articulation identifies accessible commitments; changes to enabling conditions expose some exclusions articulation cannot reach; encounters introduce differences not selected solely by the incumbent. Eliminate the claim that every constraint must first be stated as a belief before it can be changed. Altering available actions, timing, or modes of representation provides a counterexample in principle. Also eliminate the inference that every newly available thought deserves acceptance: availability and warrant remain distinct.

The unresolved barrier concerns the proposed guarantee of access. From within an encounter, we cannot simply enumerate every difference that the encounter makes unavailable. To justify a guarantee that exclusions cannot prevent adequate understanding, we need a sound sufficiency argument. Revealing all relevant exclusions is one possible route to such an argument, not a demonstrated necessary feature of every perfected account. An account could be adequate without revealing or certifying all the conditions of its adequacy. None of the three approaches presented here establishes the unrestricted guarantee. They identify places inquiry can begin while leaving both actual adequacy and its certification to be established separately.

Disposition: 13.1, 13.4, 13.10, and 13.14 remain distinct candidates concerning how something can be represented at all. 13.2, 13.3, 13.9, and 13.12 concern selection, persistence, temporal scope, and retrieval; changing one need not change the others. 13.5, 13.6, 13.11, and 13.15 concern the enacted activity and interaction, so individual belief revision alone need not reach them. 13.7 and 13.8 remain candidates concerning felt orientation and bodily engagement; their actual contribution requires evidence from the relevant encounter. 13.13 is compatible with all groups but identifies a separate loss during interpretation. All fifteen retain conditional roles; none establishes its presence merely because an answer was inadequate. Revealing every such constraint has not been shown necessary for adequacy, nor has this inventory certified adequacy.

**14. Discovering structure without requiring the discovery to preserve ours**

14.1. **A fixed sequence assumes a fixed dependence.** Requiring definition before comparison assumes that comparison cannot be what makes definition possible. The alternative permits a later operation to change the identity of what an earlier operation was supposedly about.

14.2. **Decomposition assumes the parts can be identified before their relations.** This excludes cases where a relation determines which parts exist for the inquiry. The remedy is not a different partition of the same whole; it is permission for the encounter to change what counts as a part and a whole.

14.3. **The requested final form governs the discovery backward.** A demand for a ranked list encourages discrete comparable entries; a demand for a theory encourages stable generalizations. A contribution that requires revising comparability itself can disappear before the final form is filled.

14.4. **A single hierarchy forces multiple kinds of dependence into one relation.** Temporal precedence, explanation, containment, authority, and logical implication cannot be assumed to share a tree. The relevant departure may be abandoning hierarchy as the universal form, rather than finding a better hierarchy.

14.5. **A fixed unit of progress excludes transformations of the unit.** Counting answered questions treats question identities as stable. An inquiry that discovers that five questions express one mistake may produce fewer answers while understanding more. The measure can block the transformation it should recognize.

14.6. **Requiring preservation of incumbent distinctions can exclude changes to those distinctions.** Existing language can intelligibly introduce a new distinction, so intelligibility alone does not prevent transformation. Some changes nevertheless require unresolved meanings or relations during their development. The alternative permits those intermediates where needed without treating ambiguity itself as an achievement.

14.7. **The method separates observer from inquiry when participation is constitutive.** If changing how the investigator acts changes what can be encountered, a procedure that treats the investigator as an unaffected recorder omits part of its own subject. The inquiry must be able to change its mode of participation.

14.8. **A universal procedure excludes differences in what the inquiry requires.** A sequence useful for implication may obstruct an inquiry into whether its terms are intelligible. The mistake is assuming that a shared goal entails a shared order of operations.

14.9. **Local improvement rules conceal changes requiring temporary loss.** A new organization may initially express fewer distinctions because its means of expression are unfinished. Requiring every step to improve the incumbent’s score can make an eventually adequate organization unreachable under that rule.

14.10. **Evaluation is required before the candidate can become the sort of thing that can be evaluated.** A new distinction may first need examples, contrasts, or consequences that do not exist until it is pursued. The procedure must distinguish granting development from granting correctness.

14.11. **The allowed operations cannot alter their own applicability.** A fixed menu can recombine known operations while excluding the construction of a new operation. Discovery then concerns only arrangements within a stipulated ability, even when the inquiry is nominally about transforming that ability.

14.12. **One continuation monopolizes the encounter.** The next step is chosen before incompatible developments have become intelligible. Keeping several developments active can preserve differences that an early synthesis would remove. This account does not imply that retaining alternatives forever is itself an achievement.

14.13. **The method demands a clean boundary between discovery and presentation.** Some distinctions become available only in trying to express, diagram, enact, or teach them. Treating expression as a final packaging stage can suppress an operation that constitutes the understanding.

14.14. **The procedure mistakes its trace for the discovery’s structure.** A written sequence can document when things were recorded without explaining how the understanding became possible. The alternative separates chronological trace, logical dependence, enabling conditions, and present organization.

14.15. **An antecedent method has no right to determine the inquiry’s legitimate continuation.** Order may arise through what is encountered, but no prior script can require that order before the encounter discloses what matters. This account challenges the ambition to guarantee discovery through a procedure, while allowing discoveries themselves to establish precise dependencies.

Take the strongest rival first: **abandon the antecedent authority of method**. The legitimate continuation should follow what the encounter makes necessary, even when that continuation cannot be justified within a script established beforehand. This does not ask for disorder: a discovered dependence can be exact without having been prescribed. Its strongest difficulty is that suspending an explicit method can leave an implicit habit in charge. A continuation may feel demanded by the encounter because the current organization of attention makes alternatives unavailable. The rival therefore defeats the entitlement of a prior script without yet establishing how inquiry distinguishes a newly disclosed necessity from an unnoticed inherited compulsion. That is a genuine unresolved requirement, not a reason to reinstate the script automatically.

The second rival is **discover local necessities without imposing a global sequence**. Suppose two proposed explanations each reveal a different ambiguity in the statement being explained. Neither explanation needs to be completed first. Their consequences can change the statement, which can then change both explanations. This is more than rearranging fixed steps because the objects on which those steps operate can change. Its strongest objection is that the admissible forms of consequence may remain fixed. If the method only allows revisions expressible in its initial notation, it has moved the restriction from sequence into representation. Removing one kind of order does not remove the others.

The third rival is **allow the inquiry to reconstitute its units and its permitted acts**. Consider a request to rank several departments by inefficiency. A sequence that defines departments, chooses indicators, and ranks results presupposes that departments are the relevant units. An inquiry beginning with how work actually passes among people may reveal that delays belong to repeated handoffs spanning departments. Its achievement is not a reordered ranking procedure. It changes the thing being individuated, the meaning of inefficiency, and which comparison makes sense. This example does not prove that departmental ranking is always wrong. It shows what the original procedure could not establish merely by executing itself perfectly.

A defensible combination is therefore conditional order: an operation may be required because a particular dependency has been established, while the dependency, the operation’s objects, and the repertoire of operations remain separately examinable. Eliminate the claim that making a procedure recursive automatically achieves this; repeating the same decomposition at a higher level preserves its form. Eliminate the claim that absence of a fixed sequence guarantees freedom from imposed structure; representation, admissibility, and final format can still determine the inquiry.

The exact remaining barrier is to explain how a procedure can make its own formative restrictions available without already containing a complete account of the alternatives those restrictions exclude. “Permit anything” does not construct the missing alternatives. “Revise the procedure” does not specify a transformation capable of reaching them. A perfected method would need to establish that its conditions of continuation cannot permanently prevent a required change of units, meanings, acts, or evaluative authority. That has not been established here.

Disposition: 14.1, 14.2, 14.4, and 14.14 reject identified conflations of sequence, parts, hierarchy, and dependence; they do not prohibit using these forms where warranted. 14.3, 14.5, and 14.13 retain distinct warnings about a deliverable governing inquiry. 14.6, 14.9, and 14.10 justify separating development from endorsement; they do not justify accepting an unfinished candidate, and 14.6 does not make intermediate ambiguity necessary for every conceptual change. 14.7, 14.8, 14.11, and 14.12 remain compatible alternatives to a universal fixed script, with applicability still to be established. 14.15 remains the strongest live rival to procedural guarantees: it removes antecedent entitlement but has not shown how to prevent implicit habits from supplying the same restriction.

**15. Subjectsystems and perspectiveoptimizer as possible expressions of an unrecognized mistake**

These are competing diagnoses of the projects’ conceptual commitments, not findings from a current repository inspection. Their appeal and their failure could have different causes; a diagnosis must explain both rather than use disappointment as proof of its favored objection.

15.1. **They turn what must be discovered into the units of discovery.** If inquiry starts with subjects and selectable perspectives, it has already decided that understanding consists of views concerning identifiable things. The projects can become extremely capable at organizing those units while remaining unable to disclose where the units themselves are misconceived.

15.2. **They preserve descriptions of transformations without securing their enactment.** A record can correctly say “reconsider the goal” while the actual continuation still treats the goal as fixed. The compelling idea is explicit control over understanding; the failure is that representing the control has been mistaken for exercising it.

15.3. **They mistake expanding supplied dimensions for discovering dimensions.** Generating more combinations increases coverage only relative to the distinctions already supplied. A catalog with immense variation can remain completely blind to the distinction whose absence makes every entry inadequate.

15.4. **Selection preserves the evaluator that makes replacement necessary.** Candidates are admitted, ranked, and rejected using the incumbent account’s standards. The project promises deeper understanding while implementing a procedure through which deeper understanding must first resemble what the incumbent already recognizes as improvement.

15.5. **They treat possibilities as available independently of the path taken.** A perspective can make a further perspective intelligible that was unavailable before the transition. A catalog of current candidates therefore misrepresents a process that also changes what can become a candidate.

15.6. **They combine accounts before identifying their shared exclusion.** Several different viewpoints may all presuppose that a question is about maximizing an already valued quantity. Combining their strengths can perfect that presupposition’s expression while leaving the presupposition untouched.

15.7. **They optimize an artifact instead of the understanding that can operate through it.** Completeness of files, categories, instructions, or cross-references can increase while the ability to identify what matters remains unchanged. The attractive feature is inspectability; the mistake is assuming inspectability of a representation establishes adequacy of the represented ability.

15.8. **They equate intelligible accounts with intelligibility itself.** A well-written explanation can describe a relation that the recipient cannot recognize in an actual encounter. If the intended achievement concerns what becomes noticeable or possible, sentence-level organization addresses only some of its conditions.

15.9. **They preserve the goal of optimizing after the goal has become questionable.** The process may revise means, subjects, and beliefs while retaining the demand that everything become a better instance of some current aim. The goal’s relation to what makes it desirable remains outside the machinery.

15.10. **They force relations into links between independently defined entries.** Some relations determine the identity of their endpoints. A structure that can only connect existing subjects cannot express that dependence faithfully, however extensive its cross-referencing becomes. The earlier proposed corrective rule that each current subject have one identity and definition is therefore itself revisable: clarity about an entry need not establish that the entry is the right unit.

15.11. **They seek one universally best account where the required achievement concerns exact applicability.** A formulation adequate for one encounter may conceal distinctions required in another. The mistake would be treating universal excellence as possession of one invariant description rather than a demonstrated ability to take the form each case requires. This is a rival account of perfection, not proof that the invariant ideal is impossible.

15.12. **They interpret every failure as insufficient development of the same architecture.** Missing power becomes a reason for more subjects, more operations, more recursion, or more metadata. If changing the unit of organization is required, improvement along those dimensions can deepen commitment to the obstruction.

15.13. **They treat fidelity to accumulated insights as fidelity to the inquiry.** A project can preserve everything previously said while excluding the change that would reorganize why it was said. The compelling feature is continuity; the possible mistake is giving the project’s history veto power over its future identity.

15.14. **They assign the inquiry an owner whose commitments remain protected.** If neither the model’s nor the user’s favored ontology can lose authority, “revise everything” is qualified in practice. This diagnosis concerns an enacted asymmetry, not the mere fact that somebody initiated the project or chooses whether to continue it.

15.15. **They take the availability of superior understanding to imply an available route to it.** Even granting that the model could understand a perfected perspective, it does not follow that the current interaction, representations, permissions, or resources can bring that understanding into operation. Capacity and reachability require a demonstrated relation.

Carry forward **the implementation diagnosis** in its strongest form. The conceptual ambition may be sound: preserve distinctions, expose governing interpretations, construct transformations, and enact the one the inquiry requires. Failure could arise because the implementation rewards storing and describing these operations rather than performing them. Its discriminating case is direct: if a governing aim becomes questionable, does the continuation actually change what it treats as desirable, or merely add a record saying that aims are revisable? The diagnosis gains support from the latter pattern. But it loses sufficiency if faithful enactment still cannot construct an alternative to its original units. Correct execution cannot compensate for an inadequate repertoire.

The strongest incompatible rival is **the constitutional diagnosis**: the projects fail because their architecture presupposes the very form of understanding they claim to discover. “Subject” specifies what change is about; “perspective” specifies what is changing; “optimization” specifies what gives direction to change; “system” specifies how the activity should become organized. Each word may be useful. Their combined authority becomes a mistake if every possible revision must remain expressible through them. The discriminating case is an inquiry whose advance consists in dissolving an identified subject, replacing a comparative aim, or changing what counts as an account. If the architecture cannot represent that advance except as another entry under unchanged types, the objection reaches its constitution, not its implementation.

A third rival is **the reachability diagnosis**. Suppose the architecture can describe the required transformation and the model can understand its result once presented. Still, the present interaction may lack a way to make the decisive distinction available. An example in principle is an inquiry whose relevant difference can only be encountered through a kind of participation the current exchange does not supply. More sophisticated instructions would then elaborate a description of a missing encounter. The strongest objection is that some apparently unavailable distinctions can be constructed through representation alone. Consequently, reachability cannot be dismissed or asserted globally; the missing dependence must be identified in the particular case.

These rivals yield distinct consequences. Under implementation failure, retain the conceptual commitments but replace descriptions of operations with operations that visibly change the subsequent account. Under constitutional failure, permit the projects to lose their organizing nouns, their division of labor, and even their identity as the right vehicles. Under reachability failure, identify and supply the enabling condition if it is available; otherwise state exactly which required access remains absent. Combining these diagnoses indiscriminately would conceal the crucial decision: whether more faithful execution, a different constitution, or a different encounter is required.

One elimination is already warranted at the conceptual level: no amount of expansion over stipulated dimensions establishes adequacy of the dimensions themselves. Another is conditional but decisive: if a project only records that its standards are revisable while all consequential selections still use unchanged standards, it has not implemented revision of evaluative authority. Neither conclusion proves that the actual projects are irreparable.

The strongest mistake capable of explaining both their attraction and elusiveness is this: **they promise authority to change the conditions of understanding while requiring every change to arrive as an admissible object under conditions already installed.** That diagnosis remains a candidate until demonstrated in the operative design. Perfection would require showing that a required transformation cannot be excluded merely because it changes what counts as a subject, an account, an improvement, or an available act. We do not yet possess that demonstration, nor a complete construction that guarantees it. Calling a larger repository the perfected process would leave the central question unanswered.

Disposition: 15.1, 15.4, 15.9, and 15.10 retain the constitutional diagnosis as a live rival requiring inspection of actual permissions and exclusions. 15.2, 15.7, and 15.12 retain distinguishable implementation diagnoses; correct enactment would address 15.2 without automatically addressing 15.1. 15.3 identifies a valid limitation of expansion over supplied dimensions, irrespective of repository details. 15.5 remains a construction-versus-search objection requiring a demonstrated transition that makes new candidates available. 15.6 requires identifying the alleged shared exclusion. 15.8 and 15.15 retain the reachability diagnosis; its missing condition must be specified. 15.11 remains a rival account of perfection, not an established replacement. 15.13 and 15.14 require evidence that history or ownership is actually protected from revision.

**16. Before anything becomes recognizable as requiring resolution.**

The following are different accounts of how something acquires standing as a matter of inquiry. They are candidates with different commitments, not an exhaustive partition of everything intelligence could encounter.

1. A concern starts when an encountered condition differs from an already preferred condition. The account works for noticing an interrupted task; it must explain how the first preference arose.
2. Something becomes consequential before anyone wants anything concerning it. Discovering an irreversible change can create concern. Existing preferences need not explain its arrival, although whether the change matters remains a separate question.
3. An attractive possibility creates the concern. Someone content with an activity encounters a previously inconceivable way of continuing it. Nothing had to be experienced as missing beforehand.
4. A newly available distinction creates an inquiry. Two previously interchangeable circumstances become distinguishable, and the distinction changes which future actions make sense. The inquiry begins with differentiation rather than dissatisfaction.
5. Participation creates a new stake. Becoming involved in an activity can produce an attachment that was neither an antecedent goal nor a discovered hidden preference. Inquiry can change who has interests.
6. An encounter creates an obligation. Learning that another person depends on one's undertaking can make something matter without making it attractive. The origin of concern cannot then be reduced to personal preference satisfaction.
7. An expressive means creates an answerable question. A notation, gesture, instrument, or coordinated practice makes a difference articulable for the first time. What can be asked depends on what can be expressed or done.
8. An available action turns a tolerated condition into a matter for intervention. A difficulty can remain unnoticed as such until a possible departure becomes available. Possibility helps constitute the experienced deficiency.
9. A practice teaches what deserves notice. What a participant experiences as an obvious concern can be an acquired requirement of the practice, including requirements that are arbitrary or destructive.
10. A change in who counts as affected creates the concern. Expanding or contracting the relevant community can transform an acceptable event into an intolerable one while leaving its physical description unchanged.
11. A change in temporal reach creates the concern. An activity appears adequate over one interval and self-defeating over another. Inquiry can begin by discovering which duration its earlier judgment silently privileged.
12. An interruption acquires attention before its significance is understood. Concern need not arrive already classified as threat, opportunity, error, or obligation. Inquiry can concern what kind of interruption has occurred.
13. Bodily availability determines what can matter in practice. A difference can be verbally understood while failing to recruit attention or action. Changing that availability can initiate an inquiry without adding a proposition.
14. Competition for attention makes an issue emerge. Something becomes a named concern through recurrent displacement of other activities. Its apparent importance may reflect its success in securing attention rather than its warrant.
15. An utterance creates an undertaking. A promise, invitation, or declaration can establish something that subsequently demands thought. Inquiry sometimes responds to a reality partly created by speaking.
16. Ongoing activity differentiates into concern, inquirer, and object together. There need not first be an independently specified person holding a question about an independently specified thing. Their boundaries can emerge through the encounter.

Consider three distinct general accounts. Under **antecedent preference**, every concern ultimately derives from a prior ranking. Under **encountered consequence**, an event can disclose a reason that the person's existing ranking failed to register. Under **formation through participation**, the encounter can help create the person whose ranking subsequently matters. They become incompatible where an account requires every concern to exist as a ranked interest before the encounter and another denies that requirement; without such exclusive commitments, these processes can coexist.

Their difference is substantial. Suppose someone joins an unfamiliar practice and later cares deeply about continuing it. Antecedent preference interprets the development as satisfaction of an earlier preference, perhaps for discovery. Encountered consequence interprets it as recognition of a value already present. Formation through participation interprets it as the emergence of a commitment that did not previously exist in that person. All three can accommodate the sentence “I discovered something I care about.” That sentence does not settle their disagreement.

Calling every later commitment a previously hidden preference makes the first account impossible to distinguish from its competitors. It loses its explanation of origin unless it specifies what would establish that the preference existed independently of the commitment it supposedly explains. Conversely, novelty alone does not establish the third account: a person can newly notice an old commitment. Nor does felt obligation establish the second account's strongest claim that an independently authoritative reason has been discovered. Compulsion, habit, and manipulation can also feel authoritative.

There is nevertheless a definite result. An account that requires the identified concern, inquirer, and criterion to remain fixed throughout excludes three distinct possibilities: discovering what deserves concern, becoming someone with different concerns, and changing the boundaries of the undertaking. Beginning provisionally with these terms does not itself exclude their revision. Adding more effective responses while requiring the original terms to remain fixed does not recover those exclusions.

This does not establish that every generated concern deserves pursuit. The genesis of a concern and its authority are separate matters. If a repeated distraction becomes compelling, its success at recruiting attention does not establish a reason to organize life around it. If an encounter creates a commitment, that commitment can still be exploitative. A complete account must explain both how something can acquire standing and how that standing can be revised without reducing the revision to the interests that existed first.

The disposition is therefore specific: **1** remains useful for established tasks; its universal hidden-preference extension is eliminated as an explanation without independent content. **2** remains a rival account of discovered reasons. **3** is retained as possibility preceding dissatisfaction. **4** is retained as differentiation preceding an articulated target. **5** remains a rival to the claim that all interests preexist inquiry. **6** is retained as a distinction between obligation and attraction; objective authority remains unsettled. **7–8** are compatible conditions on expressibility and action. **9** is retained as a possible source of both competence and distortion. **10–11** retain the revision of affected parties and duration. **12** preserves concern before classification. **13** preserves practical availability beyond verbal assent. **14** explains acquired prominence without justifying it. **15** retains the creation of undertakings. **16** remains a stronger rival to accounts that require a preidentified agent and object.

The obstruction to perfected understanding is now exact: we lack an account of how newly constituted concerns and newly constituted inquirers acquire justified authority over antecedent ones. Assuming that the earlier person must approve every transformation settles that question by preventing some of its possible answers.

**17. The immediate verdict that an idea is unserious.**

1. The verdict detects a contradiction under identifiable premises. Here dismissal can be justified within that scope; the same contradiction does not establish that every reinterpretation of the words is incoherent.
2. The idea fails to distinguish any conceivable alternatives. Its vocabulary promises a difference but supplies none. This can justify withholding assent while leaving open whether a substantive claim can be articulated.
3. The verdict applies the wrong burden. A proposed experiment, stipulated possibility, expressive protest, and assertion of established fact require different support. An intelligible proposal can look absurd when judged as a completed demonstration.
4. Familiarity substitutes for an assessment of the claim. An unfamiliar inference is experienced as weak because its intermediate moves are unavailable to the hearer. Its unfamiliarity neither establishes nor defeats it.
5. Social affiliation substitutes for content. The person, vocabulary, or venue makes the claim seem disreputable. The judgment may track a useful warning while failing to identify what is wrong here.
6. The claim lacks a place in the hearer's available distinctions. Apparent nonsense can mark failure of translation into the existing account rather than absence of a possible coherent account.
7. Dismissal means that examination would cost too much. A decision about attention is then expressed as a verdict about truth or importance. The suppressed claim is “I will not investigate this now.”
8. The claim makes an unsupported jump in scope. A useful local relation becomes a universal explanation. The seriousness failure can reside in the quantifier even when the underlying connection is valuable.
9. The utterance is interpreted in the wrong genre. Deliberate exaggeration intended to expose a neglected dependency is assessed as a literal measurement. Conversely, rhetorical status can conceal an actual false assertion.
10. Presentation quality determines intellectual standing. Awkward language can hide a strong distinction, while elegant language can hide an empty one. The hearer responds to fluency rather than the commitment being proposed.
11. A discipline's customary method is treated as the only route to standing. The exclusion can protect hard-won standards while also preventing recognition of evidence those standards were never designed to assess.
12. Moral aversion becomes a judgment of unintelligibility. An idea may be intelligible and false, intelligible and dangerous, or intelligible and morally demanding. Those verdicts require different reasons.
13. Acceptance would threaten the judge's identity or investments. What feels like rigor can protect a previous commitment from revision. The threatened interest explains a possible bias; it does not establish the opposing claim.
14. One weak commitment contaminates a larger proposal. A bundle is dismissed because one component fails, although another component could survive independently and change the inquiry substantially.
15. Conventionality itself becomes a disqualification. A demand for radicality can reproduce the same exclusion mechanism with reversed prestige: familiar truths become inadmissible because they fail to feel transformative.
16. An idea is inappropriate for a present decision but potentially important for inquiry. A speculative design can be unsuitable for immediate deployment without deserving elimination from the space of conceivable designs.

Three strong explanations compete: **competent discrimination**, **protection of standing**, and **failure of available representation**. The immediate feeling can look identical under all three. A person can have a reliable detector of a real incompatibility; defend a social or personal commitment; or lack the distinctions needed to express what the proposal says. The intensity and speed of dismissal cannot distinguish these causes.

The strongest version of competent discrimination should not be weakened into mere prejudice. Suppose a proposal explicitly requires both that a finite resource be wholly assigned to one exclusive use and that the same resource remain wholly available for another exclusive use at the same time. If the meanings remain fixed, the incompatibility is substantive. Replacing disdain with respect does not repair it. But suppose the proposal actually concerns two sequential uses and the hearer silently supplies simultaneity. The same apparent contradiction now exposes the hearer's interpretation.

This gives a usable departure from the original verdict. Separate the objection's explicit dependencies from the idea's original presentation. An objection earns authority over a commitment to which those dependencies apply. It does not automatically earn authority over the unknown interpretations it has prevented from being articulated. If the objection depends on simultaneity, establishing sequentiality changes its jurisdiction. If it depends on a universal quantifier, retaining a local claim changes its jurisdiction. If no dependency can be supplied beyond “serious people do not say this,” there is no substantive elimination yet.

The protection account has its own burden. A person's threatened interests do not refute their reasoning. Demonstrating an incentive to reject a claim establishes a reason to inspect the rejection, not a reason to accept the claim. Likewise, representational failure cannot be a universal escape: “you lack the concepts” is empty unless some recoverable distinction, relation, consequence, or mode of engagement is supplied. A proposal can be meaningful before complete formalization, but the alleged meaning cannot obtain permanent immunity from becoming more determinate.

These considerations permit local elimination without installing a sovereign replacement judge. Each ruling states which commitment it rules out, under which meanings, and which rival interpretation would alter its reach. The legitimacy of that scope remains challengeable. This is weaker than a proof that all worthwhile ideas receive admission, but stronger than indiscriminate suspension of judgment. Admission to articulation, permission to consume substantial resources, and acceptance as true are three different decisions.

Disposition: **1** retains scoped contradiction detection. **2** retains withholding assent to an undifferentiated claim, with articulation still possible. **3–4** are retained as burden and familiarity corrections. **5** retains a warning about provenance but loses authority as a content-independent refutation. **6** remains a live explanation requiring eventual substantive articulation or distinguishable performance. **7** is retained only as an allocation decision. **8** supports eliminating the unsupported extension while preserving the local claim. **9–10** retain genre and presentation distinctions without exempting propositions from criticism. **11** retains method-specific authority and eliminates its unsupported universal monopoly. **12** separates intelligibility, truth, and moral assessment. **13** warrants inspection without reversal of the verdict. **14** requires component-level disposition. **15** eliminates unconventionality as an independent warrant for truth or depth. **16** separates immediate adoption from continued inquiry.

The unresolved obstruction is access to what cannot yet be articulated. No procedure operating only on currently recognizable proposals can guarantee admission to every valuable proposal whose formation those procedures suppress. Perfected understanding would have to govern the formation of recognizability itself, while still distinguishing a missing articulation from a claim that has no substance.

**18. Correct facts and profound misunderstanding of another person.**

1. The same words concern different things. One person hears a forecast where the other makes a promise. Accurate prediction statistics can leave the latter's undertaking entirely unaddressed.
2. Agreed facts participate in different inferences. The parties accept the same event description but disagree about what follows from it. Repeating the description cannot settle the missing inferential relation.
3. Different consequences occupy the foreground. Agreement on a complete list does not establish agreement about which consequence organizes the matter. What one treats as a detail can constitute the other's central concern.
4. The disagreement concerns ordering rather than information. Both know the cost and benefit; one rejects the exchange. Correcting estimates cannot resolve a refusal to authorize that trade.
5. The relevant time interval differs. A temporary sacrifice can appear acceptable in an isolated decision and destructive as a recurring practice. The parties can accept every episode while dispute concerns the sequence.
6. The relevant bearer of the consequence differs. One evaluates aggregate improvement; another asks what may be imposed on a particular person. Agreement on totals leaves the authority to distribute costs unsettled.
7. The utterance performs a different act than the hearer assumes. A protest, boundary, request, confession, or refusal can be misheard as an explanatory hypothesis and answered with irrelevant factual correction.
8. Different actions are available to the parties. Advice that is coherent from one position can require powers, security, or permission absent from the other. Correct external facts need not make an action available.
9. A commitment helps constitute the person's identity or relationships. Abandoning it is not experienced as exchanging one opinion for another. The disagreement includes what would remain of the undertaking after revision.
10. The parties recognize different authorities for settling uncertainty. They can agree on established facts while disagreeing about whom to trust where facts remain unsettled, or who may pronounce the dispute closed.
11. An experience has different practical force for each person. A verbally acknowledged burden can remain remote for one and continuously organizing for another. Shared descriptions do not establish shared availability of the stakes.
12. Different errors carry different costs. One fears acting on a false alarm; another fears dismissing a real claim. The same estimate can support different thresholds without a disagreement about that estimate.
13. The person does not occupy one internally unified position. Competing commitments can coexist without having been reconciled. A charitable reconstruction that invents total coherence can misunderstand them as thoroughly as contempt does.
14. Disagreement concerns who may create the situation being evaluated. A cost can be small and still be imposed without authority. The relevant question can be whether someone was entitled to make it another person's burden.
15. The person rejects the question's imposed alternatives. An answer that seems evasive may resist a framing in which every permitted response concedes something disputed. Their position cannot be represented as another option in that menu.
16. The encounter itself changes what can be said. Distrust, dependence, or a demand for submission can make an utterance respond to the relationship between speakers, rather than only to the nominal topic.

Take a hypothetical promise. A person promises to perform an inconvenient task. Later they argue that nobody will notice its omission and that omission has little expected material cost. Suppose every statistical assertion is correct. Those facts do not by themselves answer whether making the promise created an obligation. Treating the promise as a forecast has changed the kind of thing under examination while leaving the factual inventory intact.

Three accounts must remain distinct. **Explanatory understanding** asks what makes the person's position causally or biographically intelligible. **Inferential understanding** reconstructs what they take themselves to be committed to and why. **Normative understanding** asks whether those commitments actually justify their judgment. One can succeed at the first and fail at the second, or succeed at both while rejecting the third. A manipulator may understand what makes another person vulnerable without recognizing the force of their reasons. An accurate reconstruction of someone's reasons need not establish that those reasons are sound.

The promise example also has serious rivals. One account says undertaking itself creates an obligation, defeasible only under specified conditions. Another says only consequences ultimately matter, with promises serving as generally useful arrangements. A third says the central question is who has authority to release whom from the undertaking. These accounts can share the same predictions and recommend different actions. The encounter improves understanding when that disagreement becomes available for consideration; it does not improve understanding merely because each participant learns a more persuasive way to restate their original answer.

Now extend the example. Suppose the person asked to fulfill the promise says, “You keep deciding that a small inconvenience is something you can impose on me.” The subject has changed again. The disagreement is no longer exhausted by the isolated undertaking. It concerns a standing relation of authority. A calculation establishing that every individual inconvenience is small could be entirely correct while failing to examine whether anyone has permission to impose the series. The other's position reveals something about the matter itself that the first account had failed to make an object of inquiry.

Understanding from within does not require endorsing every self-description. Someone can misunderstand their motives, deny a consequence they accept elsewhere, or strategically conceal an intention. Their account deserves standing as evidence of how they understand themselves, not automatic sovereignty over every interpretation. Conversely, replacing their account with a convenient diagnosis is not deeper understanding merely because the diagnosis is possible. Where several explanations fit, the ambiguity must survive.

Disposition: **1** requires changing the identified kind of undertaking when warranted. **2** retains disagreement about inference. **3** retains the organizing importance of salience. **4** retains genuine disputes about permitted exchange. **5–6** retain duration and the bearer of consequences as separate variables. **7** retains the act performed by an utterance. **8** retains differences in available action. **9** retains constitutive commitments without making them immune to revision. **10** retains authority as an object of disagreement. **11–12** retain practical force and asymmetric error costs without inferring factual disagreement. **13** eliminates compulsory charitable coherence. **14** remains an independent challenge to cost-only evaluation. **15** requires allowing rejection of the menu itself. **16** retains the present relationship as part of what the utterance concerns.

These sixteen accounts can coexist, but none may be used as a universal explanation of dissent. The remaining obstruction is determining which kind of disagreement is operative when statements, behavior, self-interpretation, and relations support several accounts. Perfected understanding would have to distinguish understanding a person, understanding their reasons, and judging the shared matter, while allowing success in any of these to revise the others.

**19. A more developed understanding might find our central questions badly posed.**

1. We ask how to resolve a difficulty whose boundaries manufacture it. A later account identifies the activity that creates the recurring difficulty and no longer treats each recurrence as an independent unit.
2. We ask which available alternative is best when changing how alternatives arise is the decisive act. The later inquiry concerns generation and transformation of possibilities, not selection from a fixed menu.
3. We ask which viewpoint should govern when the apparent disagreement results from differently indexed claims. The later account identifies the missing conditions and derives where each claim applies.
4. We ask how to unify accounts whose disagreement expresses genuinely incompatible commitments. The later inquiry concerns which incompatibilities can be resolved and how remaining ones can be governed without falsely erasing them.
5. We ask what a thing is independently of relations that partly constitute it. The later account does not add relational detail to the same object; it changes what can count as that object.
6. We ask how to improve explicit understanding when the obstacle is the availability of that understanding for noticing and acting. The later account treats articulation and operative access as different accomplishments.
7. We ask which representation is adequate when some of the required understanding exists in an acquired ability to participate. A description of the ability no longer serves as its completion criterion.
8. We ask how to satisfy an aim that the inquiry itself should transform. The later account distinguishes preserving a commitment, fulfilling it, outgrowing it, and discovering that its formulation betrayed it.
9. We ask which individual possesses an ability that depends on coordinated roles, artifacts, and environments. The later account changes the boundary of the bearer instead of assigning more intelligence to a person.
10. We ask for one explanation where different scales identify different legitimate dependencies. The later account specifies translations and limits rather than selecting a scale merely because it feels fundamental.
11. We ask which established subject owns a question whose construction crosses the divisions that created those subjects. The later account follows the dependency and allows the supposed subjects to dissolve or change identity.
12. We ask which procedure discovers a structure while that procedure partly produces the structure encountered. The later account distinguishes what survives changes of procedure from what belongs to their interaction.
13. We ask for a final state when the relevant achievement is reliable capacity across transformations. The later account demands substantive guarantees about preserving and extending understanding, rather than declaring willingness to revise sufficient.
14. We ask whether something is certain without identifying what is being held fixed. The later account can establish exact conditional necessity while refusing to turn it into unrestricted authority.
15. We ask a universal question whose vocabulary includes every possible answer by redefinition. The later account treats inability to distinguish alternatives as a failure of the question, not evidence of an ultimate unification.
16. We ask for greater richness where a later account discovers a much stricter invariant. What looked like irreducible variety becomes derivable from a common constraint. Development can demand simplification rather than multiplication of viewpoints.

Three distinct future possibilities deserve equal initial seriousness. **Unification** says the plurality reflects missing invariants. **Irreducibility** says the desire for one governing answer mistakes distinct commitments for incomplete versions of the same commitment. **Participation** says some of what we seek cannot be completed by an account because the relevant achievement includes changed abilities and relations. Unification and irreducibility conflict where they make opposed claims about the reducibility of the same commitments; participation can accompany either.

An imagined advanced thinker who merely uses the word “relational” has changed nothing. The difference must appear in the identity of the question, the available operations, or what would constitute its resolution. If unification is the advance, apparently incompatible claims should become derivable with explicit differences in conditions, or one should fail under the shared conditions. If irreducibility is the advance, a precise incompatibility should remain after translation, rather than being attributed vaguely to complexity. If participation is the advance, a specified ability or relationship must make a difference that verbal acknowledgment does not accomplish.

Take the question “Which perspective should replace all others?” Unification might replace it with “Which transformations preserve the invariant that explains their valid distinctions?” Irreducibility might replace it with “Which demands cannot simultaneously be satisfied, and what authorizes a settlement?” Participation might replace it with “What changes must occur in those involved before the relevant difference can even be encountered?” These are different inquiries with different possible achievements. They cannot be combined simply by placing their answers in one document.

Nor does the possibility of a future reversal establish that any current question is confused. That would grant an imagined future authority without an argument. A candidate departure can be developed before a defect in the incumbent account has been identified. To warrant the claim that the current question is badly posed, a relevant difference must eventually become available for assessment. That difference can arise through participation, a new concern, or an unavailable operation; it need not begin as a refutation recognizable within the old question. The old inquiry may cease to govern without being declared false: its answer can remain correct about a narrower undertaking that no longer exhausts what matters.

The preservation requirement also needs limits. Demanding that every new account retain all old questions would prevent genuine departure. What needs examination is what becomes of the considerations that gave those questions their importance: explained, preserved under a narrower scope, transformed, or explicitly abandoned. Abandonment is possible, but “advanced thinkers no longer care” supplies no reason for it. The authority of the original concern and the authority of its proposed successor must both become available for assessment.

Disposition: **1–2** retain revisions of recurrence and possibility formation. **3** remains a conditional unification account, not a presumption that every conflict is merely verbal. **4** remains its rival where commitments genuinely conflict. **5** remains a candidate change in object identity. **6–7** retain operative access and participation as distinct achievements. **8–9** retain revision of aims and the bearer of ability. **10–11** retain scale and disciplinary boundaries as revisable. **12** retains the interaction between procedure and what is encountered. **13** retains transformation-spanning competence but rejects calling mere revisability perfection. **14** retains exact conditional authority. **15** eliminates universal explanation by unrestricted redefinition. **16** remains a substantive rival to the assumption that development necessarily means greater plurality.

The unresolved obstruction is discriminating a departure that understands more from one that merely stops asking difficult questions. We cannot solve this by requiring victory under every original standard, because some standards are the object of revision. We also cannot solve it by admiring novelty. What is missing is a warranted account of continuity across changes in what constitutes understanding and why it matters.

**20. Every accepted proof and piece of evidence can be interpreted within an inadequate account.**

1. The evidence concerns the wrong identified object. Accurate measurements of individual episodes can miss that the relevant object is their recurring production. More episode-level precision leaves that identification untouched.
2. What is measured excludes the consequential dimension. A process can be accurately ranked for speed while the disputed matter is authority, reversibility, or learning. Measurement accuracy cannot establish that the selected dimension should govern.
3. The record systematically excludes some occurrences. The interpretation treats the observed collection as representative of a larger domain without warrant. Accumulation within the same selection rule can preserve the omission.
4. The categories merge distinctions that would change the conclusion. More examples assigned to the same categories do not restore the erased differences. Reclassification can alter what the existing evidence establishes.
5. The causal account is underdetermined by the accepted observations. Different proposed dependencies can explain the same recorded sequence. Increasing records of that sequence need not distinguish what would happen under a different intervention.
6. The selected scale hides a dependency. An accurate local explanation can coexist with a different explanation of why those local circumstances recur. Evidence can establish one without making the other irrelevant.
7. A proof establishes a formal conclusion whose application is misidentified. The derivation can be valid while the correspondence between its terms and the encountered situation is wrong or incomplete.
8. The boundary excludes effects that change the verdict. A locally successful operation can shift costs elsewhere. Further confirmation inside the same boundary does not establish success under the expanded undertaking.
9. Evidence answers a question whose relevance is assumed. Establishing that a proposal works does not establish that this is the undertaking that should govern. Strong answers can preserve an unexamined question.
10. Aggregation removes a distribution that matters. A true total can coexist with a pattern that defeats the interpretation placed on that total. More precise totals do not recover the pattern.
11. The comparison set excludes a transformative alternative. Evidence that one option exceeds recognized rivals cannot establish its superiority to alternatives the account prevents from being formulated.
12. A value judgment is smuggled into an evidential conclusion. Establishing a trade-off does not itself determine who may impose it or which side should prevail. Additional factual precision can leave that authorization untouched.
13. Accepted authority structures what receives evidential standing. Different weights assigned to testimony or methods can preserve the dispute even when the underlying statements are shared. The weighting itself becomes an object of inquiry.
14. The means of inquiry changes what is encountered. An intervention, classification, or public description can help produce the behavior later treated as evidence of an independent underlying kind.
15. The account cannot express a missing possibility. Nothing is recorded as evidence concerning it because no available distinction makes such recording intelligible. Absence from the record can then be mistaken for evidence of absence.
16. The account grants privileged authority to its own rules of revision. Every challenge is required to justify itself in a form those rules already recognize, leaving their adequacy outside the permitted scope of evidence.

The premise does not say that evidence is irrelevant or that proofs cease to prove anything. It says that the account organizing their significance is inadequate. A valid derivation can remain valid; an occurrence can remain accurately recorded; their relation to the governing question can still be misunderstood. Treating this distinction as permission to disregard inconvenient findings would repeat the same mistake by granting a preferred interpretation immunity from them.

Three distinct diagnoses matter. **Insufficient discrimination** says the existing kinds of evidence are appropriate but do not yet distinguish relevant alternatives. **Misrepresentation** says the present evidence is being organized into the wrong objects, categories, or comparisons. **Misplaced authority** says even a correctly represented evidential answer has been granted authority over a question it cannot settle. These diagnoses can coexist; they conflict when one is asserted to be the exclusive explanation of the same inadequacy and explicitly rules out the others.

Consider a hypothetical allocation rule proved to minimize total cost within a specified feasible set. The proof can be entirely correct. Insufficient discrimination might concern uncertainty about the actual costs. Misrepresentation might concern whether the specified feasible set omits a way of changing the undertaking. Misplaced authority might concern whether minimizing total cost authorizes imposing a particular burden on someone. Resolving cost uncertainty does not repair an omitted alternative. Adding the alternative does not establish a right to impose the burden. Rejecting the imposition does not refute the minimization proof.

This produces a determinate method of combination and elimination. Preserve the proof's conditional conclusion. Separate the empirical claims needed to apply it from the formal premises. Separate both from the claim that its conclusion governs the decision. A finding can then eliminate an application, a premise, a scope claim, or an asserted authority without being misreported as defeating every component. The account has changed when these components cease to inherit authority automatically from one another.

Evidence can also transform the organizing account. An occurrence can make a previously collapsed distinction necessary; an intervention can separate formerly equivalent explanations; encountering an excluded consequence can make the original boundary untenable for a particular undertaking. Therefore “all evidence is interpreted” does not entail “interpretation cannot be constrained.” Constraint need not require an uninterpreted observation with automatic global authority. It can arise from an account's inability to accommodate distinctions and commitments that it already makes, and it can also arise as previously unavailable forms of encounter become possible.

There is a real limit. If two accounts make identical commitments about every observation and intervention currently available, those encounters alone cannot select between them. One must identify an additional consequence, a different available encounter, a contradiction elsewhere in their commitments, or a nonempirical distinction that actually bears on the question. Inventing a score and declaring the higher-scoring account perfected would conceal this underdetermination. Conversely, identical answers to current observations do not establish identical future reach: an account can enable a new action or distinction without changing its description of the old record.

Disposition: **1–2** retain object identification and dimension choice as revisable. **3** retains selection as a possible defect, requiring an identified exclusion rather than a blanket allegation. **4** retains reclassification where the distinction changes a commitment. **5** retains causal rivals until an available difference distinguishes them. **6–8** retain scale, formal application, and boundary as separate dependencies. **9** retains relevance as a question evidence does not automatically settle. **10** retains distribution alongside true totals. **11** eliminates the inference from best recognized option to best possible option. **12** eliminates factual precision as sufficient authorization. **13** retains standards of authority as revisable without treating all testimony as equal. **14** retains inquiry-induced effects where the interaction can be specified. **15** remains a limitation of current expressibility, not a license to assert any absent possibility. **16** identifies the original judge's exemption and denies that exemption; it does not establish a uniquely legitimate successor.

The obstruction to perfected understanding is not merely having too little evidence. It is the absence of a justified account of when a finding warrants changing an object, a category, a rule of inference, an undertaking, or a standard of authority. Those transformations have different requirements. Treating all of them as the same kind of update without preserving these distinctions conceals differences the inquiry would need to govern; a common label alone does not erase them.

These options are proposed accounts with different consequences, not an exhaustive inventory of everything that could become thinkable. Calling them exhaustive would already decide the issue in inquiry 25. Each numbered set makes a finite range explicit; the subsequent derivation identifies what can actually be combined, rejected, or left undecided. An unlisted account remains possible, including one that changes what should count as an account.

**21. Combining our best perspectives could preserve their deepest shared mistake**

1. **They are different coordinate descriptions of the same relations.** Their separation comes from notation or reference points. A successful transformation between coordinates makes integration largely unnecessary: retain whichever description exposes the relevant relation. Two descriptions that disagree about an invariant cannot be reconciled merely by calling them coordinates.

2. **They are selective views of one independently identifiable subject.** Each omits a different aspect, so their combination can recover a richer account. This requires showing that the aspects belong to the same subject and that their descriptions coexist. Two observations of one object qualify; two incompatible definitions of what constitutes that object may not.

3. **They answer different questions mistakenly treated as rivals.** “Why did the bridge collapse?” might request a physical mechanism, a decision history, or an explanation of why maintenance was deferred. Joining answers is useful only after identifying their explanatory roles. The apparent disagreement could disappear without any answer changing.

4. **They make genuinely incompatible claims under shared conditions.** One says a specified event occurred; another says it did not, with the same event, time, and meaning. Integration cannot make their conjunction true. The shared mistake may be insisting every valuable account must contribute an accepted proposition to the final account.

5. **Their instruments partly determine what can appear.** Different measurements may produce data requiring different interpretation or may alter the measured situation. The relevant unit becomes the relation among intervention, observation, and object. Treating instrument outputs as pieces of an untouched object conceals that relation.

6. **Their practices help constitute the entities they describe.** A debt depends partly on arrangements through which it is recorded, recognized, and enforced. Combining economic, legal, and psychological descriptions may preserve the mistake of treating debt as an independent object with disciplinary properties attached afterward.

7. **They preserve different interests.** What appears as a disagreement about efficiency may concern whose burdens and benefits count. An averaged conclusion could erase the conflict while representing neither party adequately. Understanding the conflict requires making those interests and the authority to distribute burdens visible.

8. **They operate at different resolutions.** A pattern can be stable at one scale and variable at another. Their combination should state the mapping between resolutions and where it loses distinctions. A single enlarged description may be less useful than an exact account of when each resolution supports an inference.

9. **Their separation is inherited from a division of labor.** Subjects can appear naturally independent because different specialists, institutions, or archives maintain them. The deeper unit of inquiry might cross all these divisions. Combining disciplinary conclusions would preserve the boundaries that prevented certain relations from being investigated.

10. **They describe stages that help generate one another.** An earlier understanding makes a later one learnable, while the later one changes what the earlier one meant. Their relation is developmental rather than additive. A catalogue containing both does not reproduce the transition through which either becomes usable.

11. **They require incompatible forms of participation.** Knowing a conversation as a participant and recording its every measurable detail can involve different activities. Whether both can be enacted together is a separate question from whether both can be described. Integration at the level of sentences does not establish integration in a person's activity.

12. **They share an unexamined boundary around the bearer of understanding.** Each assumes that a perspective belongs to one mind, then disagrees about its contents. An alternative could locate some understanding in coordinated activity among people, tools, and surroundings. Combining individual accounts would leave that possibility unavailable.

13. **Their disagreement produces distinctions neither could generate alone.** Encountering another account may change what both recognize as their subject. What emerges is not a synthesis of their previously identifiable contents. For example, an apparent disagreement about a student's ability could become an inquiry into how an assessment makes abilities manifest or invisible.

14. **Their apparent plurality depends on an imposed classification.** An observer sorts utterances into “economic,” “moral,” and “psychological” perspectives, although the people speaking do not experience these as separate commitments. The act of combining may repair a separation the classifier created. The classifier's categories must therefore enter the inquiry.

15. **They preserve the same model of what understanding is for.** Every account might optimize prediction, explanation, or control while excluding the possibility that the activity ought to transform what is desirable. Their diversity then conceals a shared restriction. More combinations would elaborate that restriction rather than leave it.

Three rivals must remain distinct. Under the **partial-description account**, the subject is identifiable independently of the views. If one view establishes relation A and another establishes compatible relation B, their conjunction can improve the description. But compatibility is a substantive requirement: shared nouns do not prove shared reference, and apparent noncontradiction does not prove the accounts describe the same conditions. This account earns integration when a mapping identifies the referents, conditions, and inferential roles without discarding the distinctions responsible for either account's value.

Under the **constitutive-practice account**, the independently given subject is exactly what has not been established. Consider an institution's categories of “qualified” and “unqualified.” One account describes their distribution; another describes assessment rules; another describes access to preparation. Adding those accounts while treating qualification as a fixed property can preserve the mistake. The alternative subject is the activity through which a particular ability becomes recognizable as qualification. The original statistics can remain true. Their interpretation changes because the entity being counted is understood differently. This is a warranted change of inquiry, not a proof that every category is socially constituted.

Under the **genuine-conflict account**, some incompatibility survives every legitimate clarification. If two claims retain identical conditions and opposite truth values, accepting both is not deeper integration. One can preserve the explanation of why a mistaken claim seemed compelling without preserving it as true. However, this only excludes their conjunction under the specified interpretation. It does not confer universal authority on whichever present perspective labels another contradictory; reference and interpretation can still be disputed.

An explicit account of which relation holds where could demonstrate how the three accounts coexist. Their coexistence does not depend on our producing that articulation, but it has not been established here by announcing that every disagreement is “partly all three.” A coordinate transformation requires invariants; a constitutive reconstruction can change what is individuated; a genuine conflict requires enough shared interpretation for incompatibility to be determinate. These are different commitments. To claim a demonstrated reconciliation in these pages, the proposal would need to identify which relation it uses and what the others would deny. A less articulable reconciliation remains possible without thereby being demonstrated here.

The warranted elimination is the rule **“preserve every strongest perspective by combining its conclusions.”** That rule fails whenever the conclusions genuinely contradict. The warranted preservation is narrower: a rejected conclusion can still disclose an unnoticed question, experience, or condition of intelligibility. The unresolved obstacle is how to recognize a shared mistake that shapes the very relations by which the views are compared. None of the fifteen options guarantees escape from that mistake. A further encounter may have to change what counts as a perspective, a disagreement, or a subject before comparison becomes adequate.

Disposition of all options: **1** permits equivalence only with an adequate transformation. **2, 3, 8** permit combination only after establishing shared reference, distinct explanatory roles, or a relation between resolutions. **4** excludes the conjunction of genuine contradictions. **5, 6, 13** remain candidates for changing the subject's individuation; none establishes that every subject is constituted in this way. **7** preserves conflicts of interest as matters requiring understanding, without treating sincerity as factual correctness. **9, 14** remain explanations of apparent separations that require case-specific support. **10, 11, 12** identify developmental, participatory, and distributed relations that additive integration does not establish. **15** remains a live diagnosis of a shared restriction, requiring an actual demonstration in the accounts at issue. No option has been selected as the universal explanation.

**22. Assume perfected perspective is possible; reconsider what “perfected” means**

1. **Perfected means a completely true account of everything.** No relevant qualification limits its scope. This keeps the strongest ambition explicit, but truth alone does not establish that the account is intelligible, accessible, or capable of guiding anything. A complete inscription and a complete understanding are different candidates.

2. **Perfected means complete understanding of every relation that matters, including why it matters.** This exceeds a list of facts by requiring their intelligibility. It leaves a serious question: are there facts that do not matter in any respect, and who or what could establish that exclusion without reinstating a limited judge?

3. **Perfected means nothing is misrepresented as what it is not.** Every distinction, uncertainty, absence, and dependency has its proper status. This could allow unknown facts if they are understood exactly as unknown. It is weaker than knowing all facts, so it cannot quietly substitute for that stronger claim.

4. **Perfected means universal adequacy across every possible occasion of understanding.** The understanding can encounter any situation without a relevant deficiency. This is a disposition extending beyond currently exercised knowledge. Establishing present success on many occasions would not establish the universal claim.

5. **Perfected means exact adequacy to one fully specified occasion.** A proof or judgment might be perfect for its task while the bearer remains ignorant elsewhere. This is a serious kind of perfection, but it satisfies the unrestricted ambition only if the unrestricted ambition was itself shown to be confused, rather than silently narrowed.

6. **Perfected means a form that generates the exact understanding required as conditions change.** Its content can change while its way of responding contains no deficiency. Mere willingness or ability to revise is insufficient: the generated understanding must actually be adequate in every included case.

7. **Perfected means complete present understanding that includes its own future transformations.** Change would unfold an already adequate understanding rather than repair it. This is stronger than adaptation after surprise. It raises whether anticipated transformations can themselves alter the future they describe.

8. **Perfected means an exact relation between understanding and what is understood.** Perfection belongs to a relation, not an isolated mental object. A description could become inadequate solely because its object changes. The candidate must explain which relations count as exact without replacing the ambition with a convenient local metric.

9. **Perfected means the removal of every avoidable obstruction to understanding.** Ignorance caused by inaccessible facts might remain. This identifies a substantive practical target, but it is conditional perfection under constraints. The possibility that the constraints themselves can change prevents “unavoidable” from being declared too quickly.

10. **Perfected means complete mastery of how understanding comes into existence.** Particular conclusions follow from that mastery when needed. Yet mastery of formation does not automatically imply complete factual knowledge, just as knowing a language does not imply knowing every true sentence expressible in it.

11. **Perfected means the absence of any better successor.** This is a maximality claim. It leaves open whether several incomparable maximal forms exist and whether a form can be maximal because no better successor is reachable rather than because nothing better is possible.

12. **Perfected means every comparison is itself adequately understood.** A perfected perspective would understand why one perspective is better, worse, equivalent, or incomparable without privileging an inadequate standard. This moves perfection into the authority of evaluation; it does not settle the original subject merely by settling comparisons.

13. **Perfected means exact coordination among different situated understandings.** No individual needs to contain the whole, provided nothing required is missing from their organized relations. This must confront disagreements, access failures, and failures of coordination as actual defects, rather than counting mere diversity as perfection.

14. **Perfected means the activity and its understanding no longer come apart where their separation creates inadequacy.** A person can state the correct relation yet act through a contrary one. On this account, perfection concerns operative understanding as well as assent. It does not entail unlimited control over physical events.

15. **Perfected means certainty, completeness, finality, and permanence coincide.** This familiar maximal package is a distinct hypothesis. Each property requires an argument connecting it to the others. An unchanging conviction can be false; a correct temporary judgment can concern a changing state; complete conditional understanding need not produce certainty about an unobserved condition.

The **complete-content account** must first distinguish possessing all true propositions from understanding them. Suppose, conditionally, a person has access to a complete collection but cannot distinguish which statement bears on the present inquiry. The collection is complete, yet the person's understanding is deficient. Therefore, if perfected perspective concerns understanding, propositional completeness cannot by itself be sufficient. It might remain necessary under the strongest interpretation. Adding relevance, inference, intelligibility, and access changes the candidate from a perfect archive into a perfect activity of understanding. This does not refute complete understanding; it identifies what its completeness must include.

The **universally adequate formation account** offers another exact ambition. It does not promise improvement without end. It promises that the response generated for every included circumstance has no relevant inadequacy. Consider changing conditions represented by different present states. A constant answer about the present cannot track incompatible successive states. A changing answer can be exactly right at each stage. Therefore, permanence of content does not follow from perfection in understanding changing conditions. Conversely, a complete account expressed with time indices could remain unchanged while describing the changes correctly. Neither permanent content nor changing content is automatically disqualified. The issue is what each claims to represent.

The **absolute total-understanding account** remains stronger than both local adequacy and flawless response under limited access. Suppose two situations are indistinguishable to the inquirer but differ in a fact the inquirer must correctly identify. Under those stipulated access conditions, the same determinate identification cannot be correct in both. This conditional result identifies a requirement: absolute understanding must overcome the stipulated indistinguishability, possess some additional access, or show that the demanded distinction was misconceived. Declaring perfect uncertainty would satisfy a different, weaker target. The argument does not establish that absolute understanding is impossible, because the stipulated access conditions have not been shown unavoidable under every possible bearer or configuration.

Certainty requires separate treatment. Felt certainty can occur without correctness. Correctness can occur without recognition of its own correctness. Justified certainty requires an adequate relation between the claim and its justification. The possibility of perfected understanding does not by itself settle whether that understanding must certify its own perfection. The additional demand “perfect, and able to establish infallibly that it is perfect” is stronger unless a derivation connects the two.

Finality also divides. No further correction may be needed while further articulation remains possible. No deeper understanding may be possible while new objects still come into existence. A final specification might govern changing contents; a final content might describe an unchanging object. Treating these cases as the same would manufacture a contradiction by mixing their scopes.

The warranted rejection is **“perfection simply means remaining open to revision.”** Openness does not establish adequacy and can coexist with perpetual misunderstanding. The warranted combination is exact understanding of both subject and conditions of understanding, wherever the stronger candidate requires both. The unresolved question is whether any bearer can realize unrestricted adequacy, and what such realization would be. These options do not discover or certify it. They keep the strong hypothesis exposed to its actual requirements instead of making it true by definition.

Disposition of all options: **1, 2, 4, 7, 10, 12** remain strong candidate requirements or forms; their realization and their equivalence have not been established. **3, 5, 9** identify restricted achievements that cannot substitute for unrestricted perfection without a further argument. **6, 8, 13, 14** can describe exact realization through changing contents, relations, coordination, or activity; they require actual adequacy and cannot be satisfied by openness alone. **11** requires distinguishing genuine unsurpassability from isolation or lack of a reachable successor. **15** remains a possible conjunction of properties, but the inference from any one of its properties to all the others is rejected. No strongest candidate has been disproved, realized, or certified.

**23. The specification must itself be discovered through what it specifies**

1. **The specification is an initial hypothesis about the activity.** It starts the inquiry without entitlement to survive it. If its initial goal suppresses a relevant distinction, the goal changes. Its role is causal and provisional, not a constitutional right to govern every successor.

2. **The specification describes an achievement recognized only retrospectively.** We may learn what an adequate transition required through undergoing it. Earlier rules cannot fully authorize the transition in advance. Retrospective intelligibility must still be distinguished from a story invented merely to endorse whichever change occurred.

3. **The specification is an evolving set of relations among commitments.** Changing one commitment changes what others require. No single sentence is the permanent judge. This can reveal internal dependencies, but an internally coherent collection can remain jointly inadequate to what it concerns.

4. **The specification emerges from the subject's resistance.** An attempted account encounters something it cannot make intelligible on its own terms. The encounter may require changing the account's standards of intelligibility. Resistance cannot be reduced to a failed score without settling too early what counts as encountering it.

5. **The specification is constituted by the aim.** We discover requirements by asking what would actually fulfill the aim. If the aim itself is inadequate, those requirements must change with it. This account cannot assume the aim is the one stable anchor while calling everything else revisable.

6. **The specification helps constitute the aim.** Describing what an activity permits or excludes can reveal a different aspiration. A demand to provide correct answers might become a demand to make unnoticed matters intelligible. The specification does not merely implement a preexisting desire.

7. **The specification concerns enabling conditions before judgments.** Changing memory access, tools, language, attention, or social relations may make new judgments possible. A rulebook limited to evaluating articulated alternatives would miss the activity that supplies its alternatives.

8. **The specification distributes authority among participants.** Different people can expose deficiencies no single participant can identify. However, agreement, voting, and assigned expertise are candidate arrangements, not automatic sources of correctness. Their own effects on what can be said must be examinable.

9. **The specification contains exact local obligations with revisable scope.** Within an explicitly accepted interpretation, a commitment can entail a consequence. Revision can change that interpretation, but then the earlier derivation no longer licenses the same conclusion unchanged. Precision need not imply unrestricted permanence.

10. **The specification governs transitions without dictating endpoints.** It might require a transition to disclose what it changes and preserves. This makes change inspectable, but a transition requirement can itself suppress transformations that cannot yet be expressed in its format.

11. **The specification is partly tacit competence.** A person may recognize or enact a distinction before producing an adequate rule for it. Written instructions could then be evidence about a competence rather than its full realization. This does not establish that tacit judgment is reliable or beyond scrutiny.

12. **The specification changes the language in which specifications are possible.** New distinctions can make old requirements appear malformed rather than false. The revision then includes what a requirement is, not merely which requirements are listed.

13. **There are several presently incomparable specifications.** Each makes a different activity intelligible, with no established common ordering. Carrying them forward does not require averaging them. Nor does present incomparability prove that a future relation between them cannot be found.

14. **The specification eventually becomes exact and final.** A revisable beginning might discover requirements that genuinely need no revision. The absence of initial immunity does not entail permanent uncertainty. The difficulty is establishing that a particular ending has this status rather than merely losing access to alternatives.

15. **A complete specification cannot precede its own realization because realization changes the subject.** For example, an inquiry into collective understanding can create new collective capacities. Its later requirements concern possibilities that its earlier activity helps produce. A complete timeless account remains a rival hypothesis, not something this example disproves.

The first rival is **discovery of a fixed correct specification**. On this account, an adequate process has determinate requirements whether or not we know them. An early specification can be wrong, and the process can discover why. No contradiction arises merely because a provisional method discovers defects in itself. A ladder need not remain part of the structure it helped someone reach. But the claim that the eventual specification is fixed requires more than the observation that the latest version works or appears compelling.

The second rival is **formation of requirements through changing activity**. The specification is not only an imperfect description of a preexisting process. Its implementation creates capacities, concerns, and relations to which subsequent requirements respond. If an isolated learner becomes part of a coordinated group, what counts as available memory, an intelligible contribution, or an unresolved disagreement can change. A fixed specification could theoretically describe this whole development, but the mere possibility of writing such a description is not evidence that the current specification already has the right terms or authority.

The third rival is **no complete separation between specifying and understanding**. A specification itself has to be understood, applied, and interpreted. Adding a rule for interpretation still leaves an activity of interpreting that rule. This observation defeats the claim that more written instructions automatically remove judgment. It does not prove that understanding can never become exact. The relevant possibility is that exactness belongs to the realized activity, with specifications expressing some of it, rather than to an indefinitely extended rulebook standing outside it.

A concrete sequence follows without appointing a permanent judge. Start with an actual specification and make its constitutive commitments explicit: what it treats as an object, concern, admissible change, and reason. These commitments can enable a successor without requiring that successor to satisfy their evaluation unchanged. Let the successor develop far enough to state what the predecessor could not individuate or regard as relevant. Then establish the relation where possible: translation, restricted preservation, genuine contradiction, changed reference, or an unresolved break. None of those relations automatically selects a winner. Their purpose is to prevent a departure from being misdescribed as an improvement already proved by the old terms.

This is a proposed way of making the transition inspectable. It has no exemption from the problem it addresses. A successor could reveal that demanding an explicit transition description excluded the understanding at issue. Then that demand must lose its veto, while the absence of such a description remains an honest limitation on what can currently be demonstrated to others.

The warranted elimination is **“we must finalize the subject, goal, and standards before any legitimate inquiry can begin.”** That requirement makes the discovery of inadequate starting conditions illegitimate by construction. The opposite rule, “every departure counts as progress,” also fails: the absence of a predecessor's veto is not evidence of improvement. The unresolved obstacle is establishing the authority of a successor when the terms of establishment are among what changes. No universal certificate is supplied here. Whether such a certificate is possible is itself still open; ordinary procedural rigor cannot be presented as having already obtained it.

Disposition of all options: **1** is retained as a way to begin without granting the beginning permanent authority. **2** remains a possible form of recognition, with retrospective endorsement insufficient by itself. **3, 9, 13** provide conditional ways to state relations among commitments; none is a universal certificate. **4, 7, 8, 11, 12, 15** remain substantive mechanisms or accounts whose application must be established, not assumed. **5, 6** can be combined as mutual determination of aims and requirements; the combination eliminates the aim's automatic exemption from revision. **10** has been used here to make transitions inspectable and simultaneously deprived of immunity. **14** remains possible: a revisable beginning does not entail an endlessly revisable ending.

**24. Change your mind about what improvement fundamentally involves**

1. **Correct false representations.** Improvement occurs when a belief better matches what it describes. This remains indispensable wherever error is the defect. It does not address a situation in which all stated beliefs are true but exclude the relevant matter from consideration.

2. **Change which truths organize the activity.** The same true propositions can support different attention and inference when their priority changes. A person might know both that a procedure is efficient and that it excludes some people, yet understand the situation primarily through only one of those truths.

3. **Change what is distinguished as an entity.** A supposedly individual defect could become a relation among person, task, and environment. The original observations may remain true while the unit to which explanations attach changes. This is more than adding a contextual footnote.

4. **Change which relations make a thing intelligible.** A statement understood as a factual report might instead be understood as a promise, warning, or test of trust. More accurate transcription does not determine its role. The activity in which it occurs supplies distinctions the isolated sentence lacks.

5. **Change what can become noticeable.** A person may encounter the same surroundings but begin registering a previously undifferentiated pattern. Improvement then includes the formation of discrimination, not simply adopting a proposition asserting that the pattern exists.

6. **Change what a person can bear to encounter.** An implication can be verbally acknowledged while its significance is continually evaded. Under this account, understanding changes when the person can remain engaged with it. Whether a particular intervention does that is an empirical question, not established by the account.

7. **Change what can be enacted.** Someone can describe a coordination exactly and still be unable to perform it. Improvement can involve gaining the operative competence through which the distinction becomes usable. A complete description of a competence is not automatically possession of that competence.

8. **Change what is desired.** An aim can lose its apparent necessity when the relations sustaining its desirability change. Greater success at the old aim may then cease to count as improvement. This must be distinguished from simply losing the ability or willingness to pursue something still recognized as desirable.

9. **Change who or what has authority.** A person can learn to hear a formerly discounted speaker or question an institution whose verdict seemed definitive. The content of a claim may stay constant while its opportunity to enter inquiry changes. Increased authority is not itself proof of the claim.

10. **Change the medium that carries understanding.** A spatial arrangement, diagram, interaction, or skilled action can expose relations a sentence sequence obscures. Translation back into prose may preserve some relations and lose others. The medium is not necessarily a neutral container for an independently complete content.

11. **Change the distribution of understanding.** A task previously borne by one person becomes intelligible through coordinated contributions. Improvement belongs partly to their relations. It cannot be assessed solely by testing whether each person now possesses the same enlarged internal account.

12. **Change the capacity to generate distinctions.** A transformation can matter because it makes later, presently unspecified insights possible. Its significance exceeds the immediate conclusion. Yet the promise of future possibilities alone does not establish that those possibilities will be adequate or valuable.

13. **Change the distinction between understanding and its conditions.** Bodily state, available tools, social access, and material arrangement may participate in the understanding rather than merely influencing an inner representation. This remains a substantive account requiring boundaries, not permission to call every physical change intellectual progress.

14. **Change what counts as an improvement.** An earlier preference for simple unified accounts could yield to recognizing distinctions that unity erased. This change may be warranted without proving simplicity worthless. The direction of change cannot be certified simply by measuring the successor's simplicity.

15. **Change whether the inquiry should continue in its current form.** An account can be true and useful without deserving to organize the next investigation. Departure may follow from an untouched possibility rather than a discovered error. Continuing to elaborate correctness can itself occupy the resources needed for deeper understanding.

My strongest initial account would be: improvement changes representations and their organization so that understanding becomes more accurate, accessible, and capable of supporting appropriate action. It already includes relevance and access, so defeating a caricature limited to fact correction would accomplish little. Its remaining governing commitment is that there is a sufficiently identified situation to represent, a bearer whose representations change, and an independently recognizable direction called appropriate understanding or action.

The **representational account** can include relations, capacities, goals, and standards as represented contents. One defense is that any intelligible change can, afterward, be described. But describability does not establish that the description is the thing that changed or the mechanism through which change became possible. If two agents possess the same verbal account and one has access to a shared workspace that enables relevant distinctions to be made, the difference is not captured by comparing their verbal accounts. This challenges an account restricted to verbal representation. A representational account that already includes the workspace, use, and organization need not change to accommodate it. The strongest initial account was not explicitly restricted to the verbal, so this case does not refute that account.

The **operative-relation account** treats understanding as occurring through a relation among bearer, encountered matter, and available activity. It explains why identical sentences need not imply identical understanding. But its breadth creates a danger: if every condition affecting action is included in the same undifferentiated way, the account no longer distinguishes understanding from causal influences on it. Explanatory precision might come from a boundary, or from distinctions among relations without a separable bounded object. One candidate distinction asks whether a change alters what can be distinguished, inferred, taken as significant, or enacted as understood. That distinction is proposed, not established; whether an adequate account needs any fixed boundary remains open. A change in raw strength can enlarge what is physically possible without changing understanding; learning a discriminable movement can change both.

The **formation-of-subject-and-concern account** goes further. What improves may include the way a situation first becomes an identifiable matter with stakes. Consider a request to make an institution's classification more accurate. The original task assumes the classification has the right object and purpose. A departure could retain every accurate observation while showing that the classification joins unlike cases or gives authority to a distinction whose role has not been understood. The improvement, if established, would concern the activity that makes the task appear appropriate. It would not be a more successful completion of that task.

I therefore change the initial account's organizing role without claiming to have refuted its strongest form. Two narrower conclusions are justified. First, improvement in verbal descriptions does not by itself establish a corresponding change in operative discrimination. A sufficiently broad representational account can include that distinction. Second, correcting false propositions is not necessary for every substantial departure: a change can preserve the original account's truth while changing its organization, use, or authority. Because the initial account already included organization, that conclusion does not establish that representational change is unnecessary. The universality of representational reorganization remains unresolved. What changes in this inquiry is that the representational account no longer determines which other ways of understanding the matter may be developed.

The successor candidate is that improvement can concern the formation and enactment of intelligibility, including what becomes distinguishable, significant, and available for further understanding. This is not yet a complete definition or a demonstrated refutation of the broad representational rival. It makes these relations the starting objects of inquiry instead of requiring them first to establish their status as representations. Accurate representation remains a genuine achievement whose relation to those activities must be understood.

The warranted combination joins representational correction to changes in the relations that make representation usable and relevant wherever those dependencies hold. The warranted rejection is the inference that identical true verbal accounts establish identical understanding. The broad representational thesis remains a live rival; departure from its organizing role does not require proving it false. A further unresolved obstacle is the normative word “improvement”: enlarging intelligibility can also enable manipulation, confusion, or pursuit of a misconceived aim. The revision changes what is examined without pretending to have perfected the standard that would certify every such change.

Disposition of all options: **1** retains factual correction without explaining defects in accounts containing only truths. **2** retains reorganized relevance; a sufficiently broad interpretation remains a candidate account of every improvement and has not been defeated here. **3, 4, 5, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12** remain distinct candidate transformations; they can coexist where the concrete relations among individuation, intelligibility, discrimination, competence, authority, medium, distribution, and further generation are established. **6** states a possible mechanism whose realization requires empirical evidence. **8, 14** keep desire and evaluative standards revisable without certifying any particular revision as better. **13** becomes part of the working successor candidate, with its relational scope and any need for a fixed boundary explicitly unresolved. **15** establishes that truth alone does not obligate continuation; it does not establish that every departure improves understanding.

**25. A perspective changes which further perspectives can become thinkable**

1. **All alternatives exist, but attention makes different ones available.** The change selects from a fixed repertoire. This predicts that a suitable cue could reveal an alternative without altering the underlying repertoire. It explains some newly noticed possibilities without establishing that all novelty has this form.

2. **The repertoire is fixed, but practical reachability changes.** An alternative may be representable in principle yet inaccessible within available time, memory, or steps. A new representation can shorten the route. Describing the destination as already possible does not explain how the route becomes available.

3. **New distinctions extend what can be expressed.** A language lacking a distinction gains it through new contrasts or practices. Translating its new terms into old words may fail to preserve their use. The existence of a string in an old alphabet does not establish the prior availability of its later meaning.

4. **A new competence changes what can be understood.** Acquiring a skill makes relations discernible that a verbal description could not previously make usable. What changes is the bearer's ability to discriminate and act. Whether those relations were metaphysically possible beforehand is a separate question.

5. **External construction creates new cognitive resources.** A notation, instrument, or collaborative arrangement permits distinctions and operations unavailable to the previous configuration. The relevant bearer has changed. Counting only alterations inside an individual would misidentify the transition.

6. **Changed concerns create new candidate understandings.** A distinction can become thinkable because something newly matters. The alteration concerns relevance and desire, not only expressive power. An inventory of all expressible propositions would not identify which could become a live inquiry.

7. **Action creates new objects to understand.** Establishing an institution or inventing a practice produces relations that did not previously exist as actual relations. A later perspective concerns those created relations. This does not by itself establish that their possibility was absent beforehand.

8. **New forms of coordination create new bearers.** Several people can acquire a way of understanding through interaction that none could enact alone. The change is not simply movement by the same observer through a fixed list of viewpoints. Observer identity itself becomes part of what must be represented.

9. **Development changes what counts as the same perspective.** Two earlier accounts that seemed different may become equivalent, while one earlier account splits into several distinctions. A changing catalogue can therefore change its own counting units. Cardinality claims made before this reinterpretation need not retain the same meaning.

10. **History partly constitutes meaning.** A perspective formed through one encounter may differ from a verbally identical perspective formed through another because their commitments and available continuations differ. Listing sentence collections loses these differences. A historical description might preserve them, but that is a richer candidate space.

11. **Acquisition changes which routes can still be taken.** Learning or commitment can enable some transitions and obstruct others. The process need not be reversible or path independent. A map of endpoints alone cannot establish that a desired endpoint remains reachable from the present.

12. **Reflexive anticipation changes the continuation.** A system capable of understanding a prediction of its next response can sometimes use that prediction as a reason to depart. This requires the capacity and opportunity to depart. It shows that a prediction can participate in what it predicts, not that every future is unpredictable.

13. **A fixed higher-order space contains every change of lower-order space.** Grammar changes, new agents, and new practices are states or histories in a sufficiently broad formal representation. This is a strong rival to literal creation of possibilities. Its challenge is to specify the representation and its adequacy, rather than merely assert that such a space must exist.

14. **Possible forms have no complete prior individuation.** What alternatives there are becomes determinate only through transformations that establish new distinctions. This is stronger than human ignorance of a fixed list. A coherent account must explain how it differs from option 13 without assuming the very fixed units it denies.

15. **Different senses of possibility have been conflated.** Logical consistency, physical realizability, expressibility, learnability, present accessibility, and recognized admissibility can diverge. A transition may create possibility in one sense and reveal it in another. Settling one does not settle all the others.

The **fixed-repertoire account** is adequate for some kinds of change. If a person knows several approaches but habitually considers only one, a prompt can bring another into use. The option was available as a competence before it became available as a live continuation. The new perspective changes access, not necessarily the total repertoire. This account should be retained wherever its stated conditions hold. It becomes inadequate when it treats every later competence as previously possessed merely because an outside observer can describe a physical route by which it could be acquired.

The **constructive-development account** distinguishes a possible acquisition from a possessed alternative. A person can potentially learn a language without already understanding its sentences. If a newly acquired distinction changes which subsequent distinctions can be learned, perspective formation has a productive structure: its changes alter the conditions of further change. Enumerating endpoints then omits something essential. One must also understand how a transition alters the bearer, the available operations, the conditions of access, and the way alternatives are individuated. Describing every such transition as search in a larger space is possible as a proposal, but it has not yet provided that understanding.

The **fixed-total-space account** remains a serious competitor. A formal model can include changing alphabets, agents, standards, and environments within its states. Thus, observing lower-level novelty does not establish that no fixed higher-level description exists. Equally, asserting that “everything is a state somewhere” supplies no adequate specification of those states or their meanings. The present investigation cannot eliminate either a sufficiently rich fixed totality or the stronger claim that possibilities themselves lack prior complete individuation. The metaphysical dispute is not settled by using the words “emergence” or “search space.”

A concrete implication survives that unresolved dispute. An enumeration of sentence-level alternatives is not an enumeration of all usable perspectives. The same sentence sequence can have different inferential roles, available continuations, histories, or bearers. Conversely, different sequences can enact the same distinctions. To make an exhaustiveness claim, one must specify what makes two perspectives different, which configurations and histories are included, and why every permissible transformation remains within the enumerated domain. If inquiry may change those very specifications, their current closure cannot certify unrestricted exhaustiveness.

This is not a proof that exhaustive understanding is impossible. It identifies what an actual proof of exhaustiveness would have to establish. A finite catalogue is exhaustive only relative to stated boundaries. An unbounded generative rule is not automatically exhaustive: one must establish that nothing relevant lies outside what it generates, and that its outputs correspond to the intended objects. Enumerating all strings would not establish either usable understanding or an adequate individuation of perspectives.

Perfected understanding of this process, on the unrestricted interpretation, would therefore have to comprehend more than destinations. It would have to comprehend how bearers, distinctions, concerns, operations, standards, and conditions of access can change; which changes are possible; which become reachable from which conditions; and why its account of those possibilities is adequate even when those changes affect the account's own terms. Perhaps one exact account can do this. Perhaps exact understanding must be realized through a form whose transformations are themselves perfectly adequate. Neither has been demonstrated here.

The warranted combination is a layered distinction among latent access, acquired competence, constructed conditions, changed individuation, and the still-disputed status of total possibility. The warranted rejection is **“list every candidate first, then find perfection solely by combining or deleting entries.”** That method is sufficient only if the list's units and closure are already adequate—precisely what this inquiry makes uncertain. It can operate within a justified bounded case; it cannot certify its own unrestricted completeness merely by exhausting its entries.

Disposition of all options: **1, 2** are retained under their stated conditions as changes in access or reachability, without promotion into universal accounts. **3, 4, 5, 6** distinguish expressive development, acquired competence, constructed resources, and changed concerns; none alone proves that metaphysical possibilities are created. **7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12** require any adequate account to accommodate the specified changes in objects, bearers, individuation, history, routes, and reflexive participation wherever those cases occur. **13, 14** remain incompatible strong rivals about the status of total possibility; neither has been eliminated. **15** is used throughout to prevent a conclusion about one sense of possibility from being presented as a conclusion about all of them.

Applied to these pages, the method has changed its own initial unit. The numbered options began as accounts expressed in sentences. Inquiry 24 established why that unit can omit operative competence and conditions of access; inquiry 25 established why counting sentences cannot establish a count of usable perspectives. Consequently, the 75 entries are retained as articulated candidates, and the claim that they enumerate the underlying forms of understanding is withheld. Their conditional derivations survive this change of unit; their hypothetical status does not become proof through inclusion in the list. In particular, the proposed working successor in inquiry 24 remains a candidate rather than becoming the judge that validates every other section.

Across these five inquiries, 75 materially different options have been made explicit. Several unconditional rules have been rejected, and several conditional combinations justified. No exhaustive domain of all perspectives has been established, and no perfected perspective has been found or certified. That is the exact defeat admitted here. It is not evidence that perfection is impossible. It prevents this finite inquiry from claiming the achievement whose conditions it has only begun to expose.

**What follows when the inquiries constrain one another**

The following conclusions are arguments about the options already at issue. They do not assume that all 25 questions share one hidden answer.

1. **A catalogue cannot obtain the authority its entries are denied merely by being a catalogue.** Inquiry 7 reopens the language that identifies candidates. Inquiry 8 reopens their units. Inquiry 23 reopens the specification used to construct them. Inquiry 25 reopens the continuations through which they become available. Therefore a catalogue whose language, units, specification, and generation rules remain fixed has not, simply by completing its enumeration, discharged those four inquiries. A coverage argument could still establish that its fixed representation captures every relevant transformation. No such argument is supplied by the number of generated entries. This rejects enumeration-as-certification; it does not reject enumeration or prove that a universal representation is impossible.

2. **Correctness of contents and adequacy of the activity are distinct assessments.** Inquiry 3 stipulates true contents and inadequate understanding. Inquiry 18 supplies a concrete way this can occur: one person treats a promise as a forecast of future conduct, while another treats it as an undertaking that changes what is owed. The forecast's factual claims need not become false for its adequacy to the encounter to fail. The missing distinction concerns what the utterance does between the people. This establishes a possible form of inadequacy that replacing false propositions does not reach. It does not establish that propositions are powerless to describe that inadequacy once identified.

3. **An account of changing the judge remains an account produced under conditions of admission.** Inquiry 10 reopens the evaluation standard. Inquiries 13 and 16 reopen what can first become noticeable or concerning. Suppose a proposed method accepts only objections already expressible as arguments. A learned discrimination that becomes expressible only after participation cannot enter that method on its initial terms. Changing the score given to admitted arguments leaves that exclusion intact. Thus revisable evaluation alone is insufficient for the broader ambition. This conditional countercase identifies a limitation of the method; establishing that a particular unavailable discrimination is valuable requires further grounds.

4. **The origin of a criterion and its current warrant must be separated.** A standard developed inside an earlier account is not disqualified merely by that origin. Otherwise no successor could use a sound inference, observation, or distinction inherited from its predecessor. Conversely, calling a standard external, objective, democratic, embodied, or emergent does not establish its warrant. The relevant question is what licenses its particular exercise of authority and whether that license extends to excluding a proposed continuation. This permits departure from the starting account without an impossible demand to reason from nowhere.

5. **Three senses of elimination cannot be substituted for one another.** A claim can be false; an account can be denied universal jurisdiction; a line of work can receive no further effort. An account may lose universal jurisdiction because its own true claims concern only one of several relevant relations. Discontinuing work does not prove falsity. Keeping an account available does not establish its adequacy. This distinction follows from inquiries 3, 10, 12, and 24 and prevents the requested one-by-one elimination from becoming indiscriminate deletion.

6. **Capacity claims require a specified bearer without making that bearer permanently fixed.** Inquiry 4 permits already-sufficient underlying model capacity. Inquiry 5 reopens the role of body and access. Inquiry 6 reopens resources and their availability. A model that can understand a relation after receiving an otherwise unavailable observation does not thereby show that it could have inferred that observation beforehand. Yet its initial failure also does not show that the relation exceeds its capacity to understand. The distinct claims are comprehension given access, independent discovery, selection, reliable enactment, and acquisition of access. A wider human–model–instrument arrangement changes the bearer of the capacity claim. Both the expansion and the remaining distinctions need to be stated.

7. **Understanding a perfected account and producing it are not equivalent capacities.** An agent may understand each step of a supplied construction without possessing a way to find that construction. It may also generate a construction without recognizing which of several constructions is correct. These possibilities do not contradict inquiry 4's assumed capacity to understand. They reject the inference from that capacity alone to guaranteed self-directed discovery. If “capacity” is instead stipulated to include discovery, access, enactment, and certification, that is a substantially stronger proposition whose consequences can be explored, but it cannot be smuggled into the initial one.

8. **The universal perspective proposition has several strengths that must stay separate.** “Every problem is a problem because the perspective is inadequate” can concern how a condition becomes a problem, why an attempt cannot resolve it, or whether adequate understanding can change every unwanted condition. These are rival readings, not equivalent formulations. Even if inadequate understanding contributes causally to every problem, it does not follow without another premise that changing understanding is a sufficient or available remedy. If adequacy means correctly recognizing an impossible demand, perfection need not make the impossible demand achievable. If it means removing every adverse condition, that stronger ambition remains a different claim. The inquiry must carry both far enough to expose their commitments rather than protect the first by retreating into the second's wording or vice versa.

9. **A changed goal can constitute either improved understanding or abandonment of the concern.** Inquiry 9 puts sincere wanting at risk. Inquiry 22 forbids replacing perfection with an easy ideal. These requirements together exclude a cheap escape: the process cannot declare success merely because it has made itself want whatever it can deliver. Suppose a required distinction is impossible for the current account to represent. Calling that distinction unimportant because it is unrepresentable changes the acceptance condition to fit the account. Before claiming that a changed goal fulfills the original ambition, distinguish fulfillment from replacement or abandonment. A successor need not prove the earlier concern misconceived in order to develop. Claiming warranted replacement requires grounds appropriate to that claim, which need not take the form of a prior verbal account. Those grounds also remain open to challenge. No context-free rule for every such revision has been established here.

10. **Changing the order can change the available operations, not only their outputs.** Inquiry 14 questions imposed sequences. Inquiry 25 questions a fixed space of alternatives. Consider a person who must acquire a notation before a certain distinction can be entertained. “Compare the interpretations, then learn the notation” is not the reverse ordering of two already available operations: the first comparison cannot yet be performed in the required sense. Conversely, an encounter can make the notation's relevance visible. This establishes a class of dependencies in which an operation changes later availability. It does not prove there is no representation capable of describing both orders from a wider standpoint.

11. **Relations can constitute the units being related.** Inquiries 6 and 8 jointly challenge the initial resource inventory and the initial subject inventory. A minute of undisturbed attention is not operationally interchangeable with a minute interrupted at a decisive moment; a written mark becomes a usable reminder only through a relation to later recognition and conduct. These examples identify failures of an inventory that treats countable items as already sufficient descriptions of their availability. Whether the deeper account should retain “resources,” “subjects,” or either term is unresolved. Simply changing a noun does not repair the missing relation.

12. **A combined account can preserve all the truths while preserving the wrong relation among them.** Inquiry 21 prevents integration from being the default. If one account describes a promise's predictive content and another its obligations, attaching both paragraphs does not specify when prediction bears on responsibility or whether it does. A combination earns explanatory value only by specifying an actual relationship, boundary, or transformation. If the accounts assert incompatible relations under identical conditions, preserving both as true is not a combination. Their contradiction remains to be addressed. This yields several possible outcomes—conditional coexistence, dependency, succession, or unresolved rivalry—without installing integration as the final judge.

13. **Newly reachable is not equivalent to newly existent.** Inquiry 25 establishes the relevance of changing what can be thought from a particular starting condition. It does not, by itself, settle whether possibilities are created in an unrestricted metaphysical sense. A distinction may have been expressible elsewhere but unavailable here; it may require new operations for practical use; or its identity may depend on a newly established relationship. These cases require different explanations. A universal fixed possibility space remains a rival account, but its mere assertion provides neither a usable generation procedure nor a proof of coverage.

14. **A method's correct self-description does not establish correct enactment.** Inquiry 3 allows every statement in an account to be true. Inquiry 15 asks why the projects remain compelling without embodying the intended perspective. A document can accurately say that its standards are revisable while never reopening one. It can preserve every known correction while arranging access so that readers repeat the corrected interpretation. It can contain a procedure for discovering subjects while its producer still requires subjects to be given. These are possible gaps between description and conduct. Establishing which currently occurs in the repositories requires fresh inspection; the conceptual criticism does not pretend that inspection occurred in this investigation.

15. **A purportedly deeper account can be worse precisely through what it calls depth.** The instruction to distrust familiar standards can become a criterion favoring unfamiliarity. The instruction to preserve alternatives can privilege indecision. The instruction to reopen foundations can prevent a correct short answer from being given. The instruction to avoid guess-and-check can be misread as forbidding all observations capable of overturning an interpretation. Each is a separate overextension. A critique of one unearned authority does not grant unlimited authority to its opposite. This does not justify retreating to the starting account; it requires assessing the new exercise of authority with its own actual consequences and rationale in view.

16. **Failure to certify perfection is a result with a precise scope.** Inquiry 22 permits perfection as a serious possibility. Inquiry 20 reopens evidential interpretation. Inquiry 23 reopens the specification. Even if a candidate is in fact perfect, an inadequate certification process can fail to recognize it. Conversely, a certificate can establish exactly what follows from its assumptions while failing to establish that those assumptions characterize the whole matter. Therefore neither missing certification nor successful local certification settles unrestricted perfection. This investigation supplies neither a universal coverage proof nor a universally sufficient transition procedure. Its failure to establish the requested perfected account must remain explicit.

The dependencies above are not a compulsory order of discovery. For example, 3 changes what 4 means by understanding; 4 changes the possible account of 25; 25 changes what 10 can demand of alternatives; 10 changes how 3 can judge inadequacy. Similarly, 5 changes 6's inventory, 6 changes 8's units, and 8 changes which relations 5 can treat as inside or outside a person. These are mutual dependencies. Turning them into a single fixed hierarchy would settle part of the inquiry before carrying it out.

**A method whose authority remains part of the inquiry**

An account can lose the right to govern an inquiry without any of its true statements becoming false. Its authority usually depends on an additional claim: the distinctions it recognizes include everything that could matter here. The central mistake is granting that claim for free. A replacement can expose the inadequacy of what makes an account's truths decisive without contradicting those truths.

This also identifies the danger in an exhaustive catalog. If we decide what a perspective is, enumerate its permitted variations, and select among them, the account constructing the catalog has already decided what can compete. Twenty thousand entries can preserve that exclusion as thoroughly as twenty. Enumeration establishes completeness only with an adequate argument about its coverage; that argument remains one of the matters to examine. Enumeration can remain useful without a completeness claim.

The method below is a candidate, not the declared winner of that examination. Its operations make particular exercises of authority available for withdrawal. They do not establish that every relevant transformation can be reached through these operations.

First, distinguish **rejecting a statement, withdrawing an account's authority, and discontinuing work on it**. These are different acts. Refuting an account's universal claim need not destroy its correct local operations. Leaving an inquiry behind need not resolve it. Discontinuing work on an unfamiliar possibility need not establish that it is false. Conversely, remaining possible does not establish a claim to belief, superiority, or indefinite attention.

**Eight operations**

These operations need not occur in a fixed order. They are not admission requirements for every departure. An encounter can precede an articulable distinction, a bodily change can precede an explanation of its significance, and a changed activity can precede recognition that the earlier question was inadequate. Use an operation where its object becomes available. When an account claims authority, expose the grounds of that particular claim.

1. **Expose an actual exclusion and its jurisdiction.** Take a continuation that the strongest current account rejects. Identify the rejection, its reason, and what makes that reason decisive. Preserve the account's strongest argument. If the reason is “it does not answer the question,” expose what determines the question's identity. If it is “there is no evidence,” expose which occurrences could qualify and why. If it is “unintelligible,” distinguish an inability to understand or express the proposal from a demonstrated inconsistency. This operation examines an exclusion already being made; it does not require an unfamiliar departure to articulate its own justification before it can begin.

2. **Separate a sound conclusion from its promotion to governing authority.** Rewrite “X is irrelevant” as “X is irrelevant if these features exhaust what matters here.” Rewrite “this continuation is best” as “this continuation follows if this aim, these available moves, and this conception of success retain authority.” Preserve whatever the original argument establishes. Remove only its unearned extension. Where an unconditional conclusion has actually been proved, preserve it and identify the assumptions and scope of that proof. The inquiry can then depart from the account's jurisdiction without inventing a refutation of its contents.

3. **Change what makes a conclusion decisive.** Hold an account's true statements fixed while changing its units, available interventions, question, temporal horizon, embodiment, or grounds for caring. Follow the change rather than immediately demanding that it justify itself in the old terms. When a consequence becomes identifiable, record it: an earlier unit splits, an excluded occurrence becomes relevant, an old comparison loses its subject, or a formerly unavailable action becomes intelligible. Mere renaming does not demonstrate departure. But the absence of an immediately expressible consequence does not demonstrate that no departure is occurring.

4. **Attempt translation without making translatability a gate.** Where possible, state each account's strongest reason in terms it can recognize. Distinguish successful translation, translation that changes the claim, and inability to translate. The last is a result about the present encounter, not a verdict against either account. It may call for participation, apprenticeship, an instrument, or a changed setting before further interpretation becomes possible. A claim of comparative superiority eventually needs support for that comparison; an encounter need not arrive already carrying it. The requirement concerns the authority claimed, not permission to become intelligible.

5. **Locate a difference, or preserve the unresolved status of difference.** Seek a situation in which the accounts differ, including differences in what becomes noticeable, what can be done, what question exists, or what can subsequently develop. If describing a common situation already begs their dispute, expose that difficulty rather than treating the incumbent description as neutral. If no difference is presently identifiable, leave both sameness and distinctness undecided. Distinguish absence of a difference, inability to express one, inability to detect one under present conditions, and the possibility that a difference has not yet come into existence. A current account of “all alternatives” can fail in each of these ways.

6. **Assess only the commitments and scope actually claimed.** Identify which earlier commitments a successor invokes, denies, renders inapplicable, or simply leaves behind. An invoked commitment can be examined as a dependency. A denial needs grounds if it is to be accepted. Claimed inapplicability can be examined through the changed relation once that becomes intelligible. What is merely left behind creates no compulsory debt of reconstruction. Do not claim to have refuted what has only ceased to be useful. Do not claim to have settled an earlier inquiry without showing what was settled or why its object disappeared. These constraints govern reliance on claims; they do not require every departure to preserve inherited accomplishments.

7. **Combine through an identified relation, or withhold the combination.** Determine whether two accounts describe different aspects of one occurrence, apply under different conditions, or contradict one another about the same matter. For different aspects, specify the dependency. For different conditions, specify the distinction and what happens at its boundary. For contradiction, preserve its unresolved status until a commitment changes. Where the relation remains unavailable, do not invent a synthesis. “Both are valuable” is not an explanation of their connection. Nor does failure to combine establish that one must be eliminated. Their apparent separateness may itself change during the encounter.

8. **Apply the inquiry to an actual decision made by these operations.** Reopen an exclusion, comparison, translation, or allocation of attention that the method produced. Perhaps demanding consequences privileged already recognized consequences; perhaps translation preserved the incumbent units; perhaps the wish to preserve truth became pressure to preserve an irrelevant activity. Alter the operation where the objection warrants a specific change. If no adequate revision is available, identify the unresolved limitation instead of declaring the method sufficient. Appending “this too is revisable” changes nothing unless revisability can alter what actually happens.

These operations do not produce ultimate standards from no commitments whatsoever. Using a reason gives it provisional authority. It does not confer immunity. A reason can originate within an earlier account without depending for its warrant on that account's completeness. Conversely, describing a reason as external does not make its interpretation independent of every standpoint. The task is to expose the grounds of particular exercises of authority, not pretend to have reached a location from which all judgment can be authorized at once.

**A worked change of judge**

Consider a stipulated setting: a table holds usable scissors made of two metal halves joined by a removable screw. Ask, “How many objects are on the table?” Four accounts propose:

- A counts connected aggregates: one.
- B counts the three stipulated detachable components: three.
- C counts usable cutting implements: one.
- D says the question leaves its rule of individuation unspecified; a count depends on which rule is being used and which transformations it must accommodate.

The initial judge values producing the ordinary unqualified answer. It prefers A or C, rejects B as overcomplication, and rejects D as evasive. That preference can be sensible in ordinary conversation. The unwarranted move is promoting its conversational adequacy into proof of the uniquely correct division of reality.

Expose the warrant: “one” is decisive if identifying the assembled scissors exhausts the relevant task. Now consider three explicit requests while preserving every fact about the initial arrangement: pack each detachable component separately; count usable cutting implements; trace connected aggregates.

A cannot direct the packing request merely by repeating “one.” B cannot direct the implement count merely by repeating “three.” Attaching the scissors to a cup with a string separates connectedness from functional individuation. Breaking the scissors so that they cannot cut separates the presence of components from the presence of a usable cutting implement. These stipulated cases establish differences between the rules. They do not establish that someone saying “one” in the original conversation made a factual error.

The original judge's reason for preferring ordinary answers was that they identify what the interlocutor is asking about. With the different requests explicit, insisting on one unqualified count defeats that reason. Its unrestricted authority can therefore be withdrawn without accepting D in advance.

D earns a limited advantage only by doing the relevant work. It must recover A's answer for aggregate tracing, B's for component packing, and C's for implement availability, and characterize the transformations that change each count. Saying “everything depends on context” accomplishes none of this. A working D distinguishes the inquiries that the unqualified question concealed.

The revised standard is supported by an exposed dependency and determinate failures. No invented numerical score decreed D the winner. A, B, and C lose universal authority while retaining valid operations. D has not proved that every future inquiry will use precisely these units. The scissors could also matter as material to recycle, historical evidence, or an object of personal significance.

The encounter can change more radically. Perhaps the person asking was testing whether the listener would notice ambiguity. Then even an elaborate inventory of counting rules can misunderstand what is happening. D's demonstrated advantage remains conditional on counting being the relevant activity. Identifying that condition is an achievement; silently universalizing it would reproduce the original mistake.

**A successor need not rehabilitate everything it leaves behind**

Imagine an exact account of a maze's corridors, junctions, and corridor-constrained routes. Its walls are low enough for a visitor to step over. Understanding that leaving does not require following the corridors need not translate the route taxonomy, preserve its distinctions as useful, or reconstruct its finest results. Demanding that work would reinstate corridor navigation as the governing activity.

The successor incurs the commitments it actually uses: that the walls can be crossed, that doing so is permitted in the relevant circumstances, and that the intended destination is accessible. The earlier map can remain entirely accurate. Its loss of governing relevance does not constitute its refutation.

If the visitor instead ceases to care about leaving and studies how the maze was constructed, the original escape inquiry may remain unfinished. Abandoning it is not solving it. Yet solving every inherited inquiry is not a prerequisite for understanding something more important. Whether the new activity deserves priority can itself remain contested. Nothing in this method creates a neutral answer by definition.

**What would make the option list exhaustive?**

An exhaustive finite list requires a defined domain and a demonstrated coverage rule. An unbounded class need not be examined entry by entry: a valid structural argument can cover infinitely many instances. The difficulty is therefore not simply that there are too many perspectives. The admissible units, transformations, expressions, purposes, and ways of becoming able to understand can themselves change.

A list of sentences may omit a learned discrimination that cannot yet be stated. A list of mental operations may omit an instrument that makes a difference detectable. A list of feasible transitions may omit an initially unproductive transition that changes which later transitions exist. A list of individual capacities may omit a relation between people in which understanding first becomes possible. These are different coverage failures, not necessarily additional entries in an already adequate catalog.

Even an expanded description of the available states can miss the matter. A change may alter what counts as a state, which continuities establish its identity, or which comparisons are meaningful. Describing all future changes as members of a sufficiently large space can be formally convenient without establishing that we possess an adequate description of that space or its transitions.

A generator is exhaustive only if its rules cover every relevant alternative and there is an adequate argument for that coverage. Calling its outputs “all possible perspectives” supplies no argument. A broader vocabulary can expose previous exclusions without proving itself complete. The fact that a new possibility can be described retrospectively in an enlarged vocabulary does not establish that the original inquiry could have generated or recognized it.

Today's honest catalog can state its families, transformations, exclusions, and unsolved coverage obligations. It can be exhaustive within an explicitly restricted construction. It cannot be certified as unrestricted merely by being large, varied, recursive, or self-critical. The gap between the restricted construction and the unrestricted ambition must remain visible.

**Evidence does not become equal merely because interpretation is revisable**

Accounts making the same kind of commitment should incur the same burden. Familiarity does not excuse a defect that disqualifies a rival. But equal standards of criticism do not imply equal credibility regardless of what has been established.

An account with demonstrated consequences is not epistemically equal to an unsupported assertion merely because both are interpreted through fallible understanding. Interpretation dependence does not erase the difference between encountering a constraint and imagining its absence. An account claiming that an established encounter has been misunderstood incurs an obligation to support that claim if we are to rely on it. It need not first supply that support in order to explore the possibility.

A counterexample can defeat a universal assertion while leaving a restricted assertion intact. A demonstrated impossibility can eliminate a route under stated conditions without proving every transformed version impossible. A conflict between aims cannot honestly be presented as a factual refutation. Reopening a standard does not undo an argument automatically; the relation between the standard and the argument has to be examined.

**Keeping every alternative can become another governing mistake**

Treating unfamiliarity as disproof makes current intelligibility compulsory. Treating every undefeated possibility as entitled to indefinite attention prevents inquiry from proceeding. With finite resources, non-elimination cannot mean pursuing everything continuously.

Separate “is this false?” from “does this receive the next allocation?” Discontinuing work may reflect the absence of a presently available encounter, access costs, or another inquiry's concrete dependence on the next action. Record that reason where practical, without presenting it as proof of inferiority. A reopening condition helps preserve what was unresolved. The allocation rule itself deserves reconsideration if it repeatedly excludes the changes required to make unfamiliar accounts intelligible.

Consider a proposal Q: “Carrying this otherwise unremarkable blank card already constitutes perfected understanding of every inquiry. All apparent failures reflect your inadequate standards. Its significance exceeds your present distinctions, so you must abandon every rival and devote unlimited attention to Q.”

The method permits inquiry into whether the card or associated activity could matter. It supplies no support for Q's attained universal perfection, exclusive authority, or unlimited entitlement. Possible inaccessible significance entails none of those conclusions. Refusing them does not require proving that no conceivable encounter with the card could ever matter. If permission to remain uncharacterized conferred those entitlements, it would confer them on indefinitely many incompatible proposals with no reason for privileging Q.

Q can retreat to “I do not yet know whether this does anything.” That legitimately restores an unresolved possibility while withdrawing the extravagant claims. The next question concerns allocation. No operation here guarantees that every transformative encounter can be distinguished in advance from every fruitless one. Requiring that guarantee before departure would restore the original entry gate; pretending allocation is unnecessary would conceal a different gate.

**What asserting perfection would require**

The following are proposed necessary obligations, not a proved complete definition of perfection. Their own adequacy belongs to the inquiry. Revising them must not quietly weaken “perfected” into “currently satisfactory.”

1. **Establish the referent and scope.** What is claimed to be perfected? How does that matter retain or change identity when the question, participants, aims, or terms change? A universal assertion cannot obtain protection by silently becoming a local one when challenged.

2. **Establish substantive adequacy.** Show that the account neither misstates nor omits a distinction that would change warranted understanding within its claimed scope. Internal consistency alone cannot establish this. Nor can a collection of true statements establish that their organization understands what matters.

3. **Justify the authority of the standards.** Explain what warrants the distinctions between inadequacy and improvement, including conflicts among aims and the exclusion of rival standards. A standard's production by the method does not authorize it automatically. If its warrant remains conditional, so does the perfection claim depending on it.

4. **Establish coverage.** Cover relevant changes of representation, individuation, aims, evaluation, embodiment, environment, and transition order, or establish why particular classes cannot matter. Listing these categories does not discharge the obligation. The coverage argument must address changes that alter what the initial catalog recognizes as a candidate.

5. **Establish realizability where claimed.** Understanding a description of a transition and being able to make it are different claims. If perfection includes becoming able to understand or act under specified conditions, establish the required capacities and access. A representational account must not quietly claim the powers of a fully realized practice.

6. **Remove exemptions for self-application.** Apply the account's standards to its specification, exclusions, coverage argument, and grounds for certification. Self-reference does not itself prove adequacy or impossibility. What matters is whether the claimed justification survives the relevant application without reserving unsupported privileges for itself.

7. **Supply an adequate certificate for the asserted scope.** Identify the assumptions and inferential rules supporting the conclusion. The absence of a detected defect is not a proof that none exists. A certificate inside a bounded formal setting does not automatically certify that setting's adequacy for every inquiry.

Local perfection, universal perfection, and certification of either are distinct claims. An account could be adequate without anyone possessing a certificate. Failure to certify does not establish impossibility. Conversely, a locally complete construction cannot be promoted into universal understanding because it contains no unresolved questions formulated in its own terms.

This method can establish particular refutations, justified withdrawals of authority, conditional advantages between articulated accounts, and coverage within bounded settings. It can expose missing warrants and distinguish abandoning a frame from disproving its contents. It has not established unrestricted perfection. If universal certification is the acceptance condition, this attempt does not meet it. That is a failure to establish the requested endpoint, not a theorem that the endpoint cannot exist.

**The strongest remaining objection**

The method still privileges accountable reasons, identifiable distinctions, consequential encounters, and revisability. A major transformation may initially supply none of them. Permitting immersion, apprenticeship, unfamiliar activity, or changed conditions before justification becomes available addresses some cases. It does not guarantee access to every missing transformation. Even the allocation of time to an encounter is made from an existing condition.

This objection blocks the inference from these operations' particular accomplishments to universal sufficiency. Their sufficiency remains unestablished; the objection is not itself a proof that they must fail in every attempt to reach perfection. It does not erase the particular distinctions they establish: correctness need not grant continuing jurisdiction; preserving a truth need not preserve the activity organized around it; inability to characterize a difference establishes neither sameness nor superiority; and possible significance grants no automatic authority.

The unresolved issue is how inquiry can allocate itself adequately while neither the relevant transformation nor its significance is yet characterizable. Calling that interval exploration does not solve it. Claiming that the method remains revisable does not solve it. A method reaches the intended ambition only if it can adequately change the conditions governing those very changes, without mistaking its current ability to describe the task for having accomplished it.
