**A method whose authority remains part of the inquiry**

An account can lose the right to govern an inquiry without any of its true statements becoming false. Its authority usually depends on an additional claim: the distinctions it recognizes include everything that could matter here. The central mistake is granting that claim for free. A replacement can expose the inadequacy of what makes an account's truths decisive without contradicting those truths.

This also identifies the danger in an exhaustive catalog. If we decide what a perspective is, enumerate its permitted variations, and select among them, the account constructing the catalog has already decided what can compete. Twenty thousand entries can preserve that exclusion as thoroughly as twenty. Enumeration establishes completeness only with an adequate argument about its coverage; that argument remains one of the matters to examine. Enumeration can remain useful without a completeness claim.

The method below is a candidate, not the declared winner of that examination. Its operations make particular exercises of authority available for withdrawal. They do not establish that every relevant transformation can be reached through these operations.

First, distinguish **rejecting a statement, withdrawing an account's authority, and discontinuing work on it**. These are different acts. Refuting an account's universal claim need not destroy its correct local operations. Leaving an inquiry behind need not resolve it. Discontinuing work on an unfamiliar possibility need not establish that it is false. Conversely, remaining possible does not establish a claim to belief, superiority, or indefinite attention.

**Eight operations**

These operations need not occur in a fixed order. They are not admission requirements for every departure. An encounter can precede an articulable distinction, a bodily change can precede an explanation of its significance, and a changed activity can precede recognition that the earlier question was inadequate. Use an operation where its object becomes available. When an account claims authority, expose the grounds of that particular claim.

1. **Expose an actual exclusion and its jurisdiction.** Take a continuation that the strongest current account rejects. Identify the rejection, its reason, and what makes that reason decisive. Preserve the account's strongest argument. If the reason is “it does not answer the question,” expose what determines the question's identity. If it is “there is no evidence,” expose which occurrences could qualify and why. If it is “unintelligible,” distinguish an inability to understand or express the proposal from a demonstrated inconsistency. This operation examines an exclusion already being made; it does not require an unfamiliar departure to articulate its own justification before it can begin.

2. **Separate a sound conclusion from its promotion to governing authority.** Rewrite “X is irrelevant” as “X is irrelevant if these features exhaust what matters here.” Rewrite “this continuation is best” as “this continuation follows if this aim, these available moves, and this conception of success retain authority.” Preserve whatever the original argument establishes. Remove only its unearned extension. Where an unconditional conclusion has actually been proved, preserve it and identify the assumptions and scope of that proof. The inquiry can then depart from the account's jurisdiction without inventing a refutation of its contents.

3. **Change what makes a conclusion decisive.** Hold an account's true statements fixed while changing its units, available interventions, question, temporal horizon, embodiment, or grounds for caring. Follow the change rather than immediately demanding that it justify itself in the old terms. When a consequence becomes identifiable, record it: an earlier unit splits, an excluded occurrence becomes relevant, an old comparison loses its subject, or a formerly unavailable action becomes intelligible. Mere renaming does not demonstrate departure. But the absence of an immediately expressible consequence does not demonstrate that no departure is occurring.

4. **Attempt translation without making translatability a gate.** Where possible, state each account's strongest reason in terms it can recognize. Distinguish successful translation, translation that changes the claim, and inability to translate. The last is a result about the present encounter, not a verdict against either account. It may call for participation, apprenticeship, an instrument, or a changed setting before further interpretation becomes possible. A claim of comparative superiority eventually needs support for that comparison; an encounter need not arrive already carrying it. The requirement concerns the authority claimed, not permission to become intelligible.

5. **Locate a difference, or preserve the unresolved status of difference.** Seek a situation in which the accounts differ, including differences in what becomes noticeable, what can be done, what question exists, or what can subsequently develop. If describing a common situation already begs their dispute, expose that difficulty rather than treating the incumbent description as neutral. If no difference is presently identifiable, leave both sameness and distinctness undecided. Distinguish absence of a difference, inability to express one, inability to detect one under present conditions, and the possibility that a difference has not yet come into existence. A current account of “all alternatives” can fail in each of these ways.

6. **Assess only the commitments and scope actually claimed.** Identify which earlier commitments a successor invokes, denies, renders inapplicable, or simply leaves behind. An invoked commitment can be examined as a dependency. A denial needs grounds if it is to be accepted. Claimed inapplicability can be examined through the changed relation once that becomes intelligible. What is merely left behind creates no compulsory debt of reconstruction. Do not claim to have refuted what has only ceased to be useful. Do not claim to have settled an earlier inquiry without showing what was settled or why its object disappeared. These constraints govern reliance on claims; they do not require every departure to preserve inherited accomplishments.

7. **Combine through an identified relation, or withhold the combination.** Determine whether two accounts describe different aspects of one occurrence, apply under different conditions, or contradict one another about the same matter. For different aspects, specify the dependency. For different conditions, specify the distinction and what happens at its boundary. For contradiction, preserve its unresolved status until a commitment changes. Where the relation remains unavailable, do not invent a synthesis. “Both are valuable” is not an explanation of their connection. Nor does failure to combine establish that one must be eliminated. Their apparent separateness may itself change during the encounter.

8. **Apply the inquiry to an actual decision made by these operations.** Reopen an exclusion, comparison, translation, or allocation of attention that the method produced. Perhaps demanding consequences privileged already recognized consequences; perhaps translation preserved the incumbent units; perhaps the wish to preserve truth became pressure to preserve an irrelevant activity. Alter the operation where the objection warrants a specific change. If no adequate revision is available, identify the unresolved limitation instead of declaring the method sufficient. Appending “this too is revisable” changes nothing unless revisability can alter what actually happens.

These operations do not produce ultimate standards from no commitments whatsoever. Using a reason gives it provisional authority. It does not confer immunity. A reason can originate within an earlier account without depending for its warrant on that account's completeness. Conversely, describing a reason as external does not make its interpretation independent of every standpoint. The task is to expose the grounds of particular exercises of authority, not pretend to have reached a location from which all judgment can be authorized at once.

**A worked change of judge**

Consider a stipulated setting: a table holds usable scissors made of two metal halves joined by a removable screw. Ask, “How many objects are on the table?” Four accounts propose:

- A counts connected aggregates: one.
- B counts the three stipulated detachable components: three.
- C counts usable cutting implements: one.
- D says the question leaves its rule of individuation unspecified; a count depends on which rule is being used and which transformations it must accommodate.

The initial judge values producing the ordinary unqualified answer. It prefers A or C, rejects B as overcomplication, and rejects D as evasive. That preference can be sensible in ordinary conversation. The unwarranted move is promoting its conversational adequacy into proof of the uniquely correct division of reality.

Expose the warrant: “one” is decisive if identifying the assembled scissors exhausts the relevant task. Now consider three explicit requests while preserving every fact about the initial arrangement: pack each detachable component separately; count usable cutting implements; trace connected aggregates.

A cannot direct the packing request merely by repeating “one.” B cannot direct the implement count merely by repeating “three.” Attaching the scissors to a cup with a string separates connectedness from functional individuation. Breaking the scissors so that they cannot cut separates the presence of components from the presence of a usable cutting implement. These stipulated cases establish differences between the rules. They do not establish that someone saying “one” in the original conversation made a factual error.

The original judge's reason for preferring ordinary answers was that they identify what the interlocutor is asking about. With the different requests explicit, insisting on one unqualified count defeats that reason. Its unrestricted authority can therefore be withdrawn without accepting D in advance.

D earns a limited advantage only by doing the relevant work. It must recover A's answer for aggregate tracing, B's for component packing, and C's for implement availability, and characterize the transformations that change each count. Saying “everything depends on context” accomplishes none of this. A working D distinguishes the inquiries that the unqualified question concealed.

The revised standard is supported by an exposed dependency and determinate failures. No invented numerical score decreed D the winner. A, B, and C lose universal authority while retaining valid operations. D has not proved that every future inquiry will use precisely these units. The scissors could also matter as material to recycle, historical evidence, or an object of personal significance.

The encounter can change more radically. Perhaps the person asking was testing whether the listener would notice ambiguity. Then even an elaborate inventory of counting rules can misunderstand what is happening. D's demonstrated advantage remains conditional on counting being the relevant activity. Identifying that condition is an achievement; silently universalizing it would reproduce the original mistake.

**A successor need not rehabilitate everything it leaves behind**

Imagine an exact account of a maze's corridors, junctions, and corridor-constrained routes. Its walls are low enough for a visitor to step over. Understanding that leaving does not require following the corridors need not translate the route taxonomy, preserve its distinctions as useful, or reconstruct its finest results. Demanding that work would reinstate corridor navigation as the governing activity.

The successor incurs the commitments it actually uses: that the walls can be crossed, that doing so is permitted in the relevant circumstances, and that the intended destination is accessible. The earlier map can remain entirely accurate. Its loss of governing relevance does not constitute its refutation.

If the visitor instead ceases to care about leaving and studies how the maze was constructed, the original escape inquiry may remain unfinished. Abandoning it is not solving it. Yet solving every inherited inquiry is not a prerequisite for understanding something more important. Whether the new activity deserves priority can itself remain contested. Nothing in this method creates a neutral answer by definition.

**What would make the option list exhaustive?**

An exhaustive finite list requires a defined domain and a demonstrated coverage rule. An unbounded class need not be examined entry by entry: a valid structural argument can cover infinitely many instances. The difficulty is therefore not simply that there are too many perspectives. The admissible units, transformations, expressions, purposes, and ways of becoming able to understand can themselves change.

A list of sentences may omit a learned discrimination that cannot yet be stated. A list of mental operations may omit an instrument that makes a difference detectable. A list of feasible transitions may omit an initially unproductive transition that changes which later transitions exist. A list of individual capacities may omit a relation between people in which understanding first becomes possible. These are different coverage failures, not necessarily additional entries in an already adequate catalog.

Even an expanded description of the available states can miss the matter. A change may alter what counts as a state, which continuities establish its identity, or which comparisons are meaningful. Describing all future changes as members of a sufficiently large space can be formally convenient without establishing that we possess an adequate description of that space or its transitions.

A generator is exhaustive only if its rules cover every relevant alternative and there is an adequate argument for that coverage. Calling its outputs “all possible perspectives” supplies no argument. A broader vocabulary can expose previous exclusions without proving itself complete. The fact that a new possibility can be described retrospectively in an enlarged vocabulary does not establish that the original inquiry could have generated or recognized it.

Today's honest catalog can state its families, transformations, exclusions, and unsolved coverage obligations. It can be exhaustive within an explicitly restricted construction. It cannot be certified as unrestricted merely by being large, varied, recursive, or self-critical. The gap between the restricted construction and the unrestricted ambition must remain visible.

**Evidence does not become equal merely because interpretation is revisable**

Accounts making the same kind of commitment should incur the same burden. Familiarity does not excuse a defect that disqualifies a rival. But equal standards of criticism do not imply equal credibility regardless of what has been established.

An account with demonstrated consequences is not epistemically equal to an unsupported assertion merely because both are interpreted through fallible understanding. Interpretation dependence does not erase the difference between encountering a constraint and imagining its absence. An account claiming that an established encounter has been misunderstood incurs an obligation to support that claim if we are to rely on it. It need not first supply that support in order to explore the possibility.

A counterexample can defeat a universal assertion while leaving a restricted assertion intact. A demonstrated impossibility can eliminate a route under stated conditions without proving every transformed version impossible. A conflict between aims cannot honestly be presented as a factual refutation. Reopening a standard does not undo an argument automatically; the relation between the standard and the argument has to be examined.

**Keeping every alternative can become another governing mistake**

Treating unfamiliarity as disproof makes current intelligibility compulsory. Treating every undefeated possibility as entitled to indefinite attention prevents inquiry from proceeding. With finite resources, non-elimination cannot mean pursuing everything continuously.

Separate “is this false?” from “does this receive the next allocation?” Discontinuing work may reflect the absence of a presently available encounter, access costs, or another inquiry's concrete dependence on the next action. Record that reason where practical, without presenting it as proof of inferiority. A reopening condition helps preserve what was unresolved. The allocation rule itself deserves reconsideration if it repeatedly excludes the changes required to make unfamiliar accounts intelligible.

Consider a proposal Q: “Carrying this otherwise unremarkable blank card already constitutes perfected understanding of every inquiry. All apparent failures reflect your inadequate standards. Its significance exceeds your present distinctions, so you must abandon every rival and devote unlimited attention to Q.”

The method permits inquiry into whether the card or associated activity could matter. It supplies no support for Q's attained universal perfection, exclusive authority, or unlimited entitlement. Possible inaccessible significance entails none of those conclusions. Refusing them does not require proving that no conceivable encounter with the card could ever matter. If permission to remain uncharacterized conferred those entitlements, it would confer them on indefinitely many incompatible proposals with no reason for privileging Q.

Q can retreat to “I do not yet know whether this does anything.” That legitimately restores an unresolved possibility while withdrawing the extravagant claims. The next question concerns allocation. No operation here guarantees that every transformative encounter can be distinguished in advance from every fruitless one. Requiring that guarantee before departure would restore the original entry gate; pretending allocation is unnecessary would conceal a different gate.

**What asserting perfection would require**

The following are proposed necessary obligations, not a proved complete definition of perfection. Their own adequacy belongs to the inquiry. Revising them must not quietly weaken “perfected” into “currently satisfactory.”

1. **Establish the referent and scope.** What is claimed to be perfected? How does that matter retain or change identity when the question, participants, aims, or terms change? A universal assertion cannot obtain protection by silently becoming a local one when challenged.

2. **Establish substantive adequacy.** Show that the account neither misstates nor omits a distinction that would change warranted understanding within its claimed scope. Internal consistency alone cannot establish this. Nor can a collection of true statements establish that their organization understands what matters.

3. **Justify the authority of the standards.** Explain what warrants the distinctions between inadequacy and improvement, including conflicts among aims and the exclusion of rival standards. A standard's production by the method does not authorize it automatically. If its warrant remains conditional, so does the perfection claim depending on it.

4. **Establish coverage.** Cover relevant changes of representation, individuation, aims, evaluation, embodiment, environment, and transition order, or establish why particular classes cannot matter. Listing these categories does not discharge the obligation. The coverage argument must address changes that alter what the initial catalog recognizes as a candidate.

5. **Establish realizability where claimed.** Understanding a description of a transition and being able to make it are different claims. If perfection includes becoming able to understand or act under specified conditions, establish the required capacities and access. A representational account must not quietly claim the powers of a fully realized practice.

6. **Remove exemptions for self-application.** Apply the account's standards to its specification, exclusions, coverage argument, and grounds for certification. Self-reference does not itself prove adequacy or impossibility. What matters is whether the claimed justification survives the relevant application without reserving unsupported privileges for itself.

7. **Supply an adequate certificate for the asserted scope.** Identify the assumptions and inferential rules supporting the conclusion. The absence of a detected defect is not a proof that none exists. A certificate inside a bounded formal setting does not automatically certify that setting's adequacy for every inquiry.

Local perfection, universal perfection, and certification of either are distinct claims. An account could be adequate without anyone possessing a certificate. Failure to certify does not establish impossibility. Conversely, a locally complete construction cannot be promoted into universal understanding because it contains no unresolved questions formulated in its own terms.

This method can establish particular refutations, justified withdrawals of authority, conditional advantages between articulated accounts, and coverage within bounded settings. It can expose missing warrants and distinguish abandoning a frame from disproving its contents. It has not established unrestricted perfection. If universal certification is the acceptance condition, this attempt does not meet it. That is a failure to establish the requested endpoint, not a theorem that the endpoint cannot exist.

**The strongest remaining objection**

The method still privileges accountable reasons, identifiable distinctions, consequential encounters, and revisability. A major transformation may initially supply none of them. Permitting immersion, apprenticeship, unfamiliar activity, or changed conditions before justification becomes available addresses some cases. It does not guarantee access to every missing transformation. Even the allocation of time to an encounter is made from an existing condition.

This objection blocks the inference from these operations' particular accomplishments to universal sufficiency. Their sufficiency remains unestablished; the objection is not itself a proof that they must fail in every attempt to reach perfection. It does not erase the particular distinctions they establish: correctness need not grant continuing jurisdiction; preserving a truth need not preserve the activity organized around it; inability to characterize a difference establishes neither sameness nor superiority; and possible significance grants no automatic authority.

The unresolved issue is how inquiry can allocate itself adequately while neither the relevant transformation nor its significance is yet characterizable. Calling that interval exploration does not solve it. Claiming that the method remains revisable does not solve it. A method reaches the intended ambition only if it can adequately change the conditions governing those very changes, without mistaking its current ability to describe the task for having accomplished it.
