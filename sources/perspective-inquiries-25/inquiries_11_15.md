**11. Mathematics, science, philosophy, and writing as ways of governing change**

The premise does not yet tell us what is being governed: permissible conclusions, changes in meaning, exposure to interference, temporary commitments, or the ability of another person to reconstruct an understanding. Those produce different accounts of why these activities differ.

11.1. **They answer to different kinds of dependence.** A conclusion can depend on a stipulated relation, on what actually happens, on the intelligibility of the question, or on an expression’s effects on a reader. On this account, some divisions track differences that survive any departmental reorganization. A derivation from stipulated rules cannot by itself establish that the world instantiates those rules.

11.2. **They differ in what they temporarily forbid changing.** A proof fixes specified meanings and inference conditions; an investigation fixes enough of a question to distinguish outcomes; conceptual inquiry can reopen the meanings; drafting can reopen their presentation and sometimes their content. The fundamental distinction is between different permissions to revise, not between subject territories.

11.3. **They differ in where resistance can enter.** An inference can be resisted by another inference; a prediction by an occurrence; a formulation by the inability to make its distinctions coherently; an explanation by the reader’s inability to reconstruct it. The divisions track different channels through which an account loses its authority.

11.4. **They manufacture different objects by enforcing different invariances.** An ideal triangle exists as an object of inquiry partly through which differences are declared irrelevant. A measured object is identified through other permitted variations. What looks like a difference between preexisting subjects can therefore originate in different rules for making something count as the same object.

11.5. **They are different stages that became separate professions.** An inquiry might invent a distinction, formalize its consequences, encounter actual cases, and reconstruct its account for another mind. If the stages can occur within one activity, professional boundaries cannot be the fundamental divisions. But calling them stages must not impose one compulsory order.

11.6. **They allocate the cost of being wrong differently.** Some settings require establishing an implication before using it; others require acting before decisive evidence exists; others permit a provisional formulation because its failure reveals an unmade distinction. The difference concerns which errors must be prevented before proceeding and which can become informative afterward.

11.7. **They differ in the commitments they make portable.** A proof offers reconstructible implication; an experimental description offers conditions another investigator might recreate; an argument offers distinctions another person can interrogate; writing can make any of these available. These activities therefore overlap rather than form mutually exclusive compartments. Their overlap does not by itself establish different classificatory levels; mathematics can likewise operate within science.

11.8. **They are rival policies for ending local disagreement.** Derivability, successful confrontation with occurrences, exposure of conceptual confusion, and achieved understanding can each authorize stopping. Their differences persist because they stop different disputes. Trouble begins when a stopping condition valid for one dispute is treated as settlement of another.

11.9. **They are ways of making thought answerable to something it cannot simply declare satisfied.** The common achievement is the construction of resistance. Their apparent differences may be implementations of that relation. This account must still distinguish genuine resistance from a ceremony in which the account controls both the challenge and what counts as passing it.

11.10. **Their deepest differences concern whether meanings may move during an inference.** A valid derivation can become invalid if a term changes meaning halfway through. Conversely, an inquiry can remain inadequate precisely because its meanings are held fixed. Meaning preservation and meaning transformation are both indispensable, but they cannot govern the same transition in the same respect.

11.11. **Some differences arise from access rather than subject matter.** A publicly recorded occurrence, a first-person experience, a hypothetical construction, and a normative commitment are encountered differently. Putting all their descriptions into sentences does not erase those access differences. The account would fail if those different routes imposed no differences on what conclusions could be warranted.

11.12. **Their divisions express inherited control over admission.** Training, publication forms, acceptable questions, and credentialed participation can preserve a division even when its intellectual operations overlap. Here the division is contingent. This is a candidate explanation, not a historical finding that every actual disciplinary boundary arose this way.

11.13. **They generate different kinds of novelty.** One can derive a previously unknown consequence without changing the available concepts; introduce a new distinction without yet deriving anything; disclose an occurrence for which existing distinctions fail; or create an expression that enables recognition previously unavailable. “Producing knowledge” conceals these different changes.

11.14. **They are differently situated collaborations with materials.** Diagrams, notation, instruments, sentences, and other people can make different operations possible. What appears to be a faculty belonging to an isolated thinker might depend on a particular arrangement of these supports. Under this account, reorganizing the arrangement can change the available understanding without changing the thinker’s stated beliefs.

11.15. **There are several irreducible senses of adequacy.** A statement can follow necessarily and fail to describe an actual case; describe a case and fail to make a concern intelligible; make a concern intelligible and offer no legitimate basis for acting. Their differences cannot be abolished merely because all involve changes in understanding.

Carry three rivals further. **The intrinsic-difference account** says the disciplines differ because reality, implication, meaning, and expression demand different forms of answerability. Consider a formal traffic model in which a certain rule eliminates collisions. The proof settles an implication under the model’s conditions. Whether an actual intersection satisfies those conditions remains a separate question. Even perfect proof of the implication supplies no observation of the intersection. Conversely, observing a thousand safe crossings does not establish the unrestricted formal implication. This demonstrates a difference between claims and their conditions of support. It does not demonstrate that the contemporary organization into mathematics and science is necessary: one person or one inquiry could perform both operations.

**The change-permissions account** offers a more radical reconstruction. The basic activities are holding something fixed, deriving what that commitment permits, exposing the commitment to what it fails to accommodate, and changing the commitment. Under this account, mathematics and philosophy can both invent or revise meanings; science and writing can both expose an unnoticed implication; the established labels are unstable bundles. Its strongest difficulty is that naming a common operation does not make its enactments interchangeable. “Expose to resistance” does not tell us whether a counterexample is an inconsistent assignment, an actual event, or an unintelligible description. The proposed unification earns its scope only by preserving these distinctions.

**The irreducible-adequacies account** denies that a single change-governing currency is available. A reason to believe, a reason to infer, a reason to care, and a reason to act are not automatically exchangeable. Suppose an account predicts behavior perfectly while interpreting every stated commitment as a disposable instrument. Its predictive success does not itself settle whether it has understood the commitment as a commitment. The opposing account might say that sufficiently developed understanding would show these to be one relation. That remains a substantive claim requiring a bridge, not something the word “understanding” has already supplied.

The justified combination is limited: inquiry uses different controls over change, and some differences track different commitments rather than inherited departmental names. Eliminate the claim that shared involvement in perspective change makes the activities interchangeable; its conclusion does not follow from its premise. Also eliminate treating these activities as mutually exclusive compartments: writing can conduct the operations of the others, and mathematics can operate within science. This overlap alone does not settle their classificatory levels.

What remains unsettled is whether there is a complete set of elementary changes from which all these operations can be constructed, and whether their several kinds of adequacy can be related without erasing any. A perfected account would have to establish those relations, show why each distinction is necessary, and show what its proposed distinctions cannot express. This examination does not yet supply that account.

Disposition: 11.1 and 11.15 retain the demonstrated distinction between implication and instantiation; their stronger claim of ultimate irreducibility remains open. 11.2, 11.3, 11.10, and 11.13 are compatible accounts of different operations, not a proved complete basis. 11.4 remains a live account of how objects become identifiable. 11.5 and 11.12 require historical evidence before explaining actual disciplinary divisions. 11.6, 11.7, and 11.14 retain distinct practical contributions; none alone explains every division, and the overlap in 11.7 does not establish different classificatory levels. 11.8 supplies local stopping conditions, not unrestricted settlement. 11.9 survives as a common requirement of answerability; its stronger reduction of every difference to one interchangeable relation is unsupported.

**12. When preserving true understanding preserves the inadequacy**

12.1. **Truth has been promoted into jurisdiction.** An earlier account establishes something correctly and is then allowed to decide which later questions are legitimate. A true account of how a queue moves need not determine whether the queue should exist. The obstruction is the grant of authority beyond what the truth establishes.

12.2. **The old names fix what later things are allowed to be.** A category can accurately describe prior cases while preventing a new relation from changing how the cases are individuated. What must be relinquished is the category’s continuing monopoly, not the truth of every sentence previously written using it.

12.3. **Consistency is being demanded across meanings that have changed.** An earlier statement and a later statement can appear contradictory because the same word now does different work. Requiring verbal agreement can prevent an improved distinction. The task is to identify their respective conditions of truth before deciding whether a contradiction actually exists.

12.4. **Earlier explanations possess an unearned presumption of centrality.** A factor may truly contribute to an event without being the factor that makes the event intelligible for the current inquiry. Keeping every new explanation organized around the first true factor prevents a change in explanatory center.

12.5. **Preservation determines what receives attention.** Every new account must first be reconciled with an expanding archive. The old account can therefore dominate even when nobody believes it infallible. What has to change is its priority in the allocation of inquiry, not necessarily its contents.

12.6. **Previously useful distinctions are treated as permanently useful.** A coarse distinction may have enabled an earlier advance. Later, the same division may join cases that must be separated or separate cases whose relation now matters. Historical usefulness does not establish continued adequacy.

12.7. **An earlier success has fixed the identity of the activity.** A method first succeeds at explaining, so every subsequent contribution must look like explanation; first succeeds at classifying, so every discovery becomes another category. The preserved element is what the inquiry takes itself to be doing.

12.8. **Existing understanding makes its own counterfactual alternatives difficult to express.** A framework may contain vocabulary for correcting its entries but none for abandoning the distinctions that make entries possible. Its archive preserves every answer while excluding the form a deeper question would need.

12.9. **The accumulated account has become a criterion of recognizability.** A new understanding is admitted only if it can be summarized in existing terms. A transformation whose point is to alter those terms will then look irrelevant or incoherent at the admission stage, before its adequacy is actually considered.

12.10. **Earlier goals govern what is worth retaining.** An account can preserve everything useful to a previous aim while excluding what would show that aim to be misconceived. Preserving “what matters” is not neutral when the account also determines what matters.

12.11. **Dependence has been confused with indebtedness.** A later understanding may have been reached through an earlier one without needing the earlier one as a permanent foundation. The route by which an insight became available is not automatically part of its justification or its best present expression.

12.12. **The archive preserves conclusions but loses their conditions.** A locally correct observation survives as an unrestricted maxim after the conditions that made it correct disappear from view. The immediate repair is to recover the conditions. A deeper repair makes unrestricted inheritance impossible unless unrestricted scope was actually established.

12.13. **The old account requires descendants to preserve its arrangement.** Every new distinction must enter the existing hierarchy, every relation an existing field. Even true entries then maintain a false organization. Retaining the entries while changing which entries define one another can preserve truth and replace the account.

12.14. **Understanding has become something to defend because it constitutes an identity.** An idea can remain available as a fact while losing its role as “what makes this our project.” If that loss is unacceptable, the inquiry has a protected commitment that agreement with facts will never reach.

12.15. **Preservation excludes constructive disappearance.** Some scaffolding is valuable because it enables a distinction and then becomes unnecessary. An account that must carry every successful intermediate indefinitely mistakes the history of learning for the organization of understanding.

The strongest preservation principle says: **retain every established truth and permit departures only if they remain consistent with it.** Its sound component is that discovering a new frame does not license declaring a known occurrence nonexistent. Its unsound extension is to treat every current representation of that occurrence as equally protected. Imagine an inquiry initially organized around individual lateness. Its records correctly establish when each person arrived. A later account organized around incompatible transport schedules need not change those times. It can change what counts as an individual case, which comparisons matter, what explains the pattern, and what intervention becomes intelligible. The facts survive while their authority to individuate the concern disappears.

A rival says: **suspend the earlier account entirely, because deciding what to preserve already lets it govern the replacement.** This makes a previously excluded departure possible. It also creates a real risk: the new account may regain apparent completeness by losing a troublesome distinction. The answer is not to demand reconciliation before departure. Distinguish the conditions for allowing an account to develop from the conditions for accepting its eventual claims. Development can proceed without satisfying the incumbent’s vocabulary. Acceptance of a claim still requires identifying what that claim commits us to, including any genuine contradiction with what remains established. Departure and endorsement are different acts.

A third account says: **preserve the ability to reconstruct the insight, not its place in the current account.** An earlier insight can remain retrievable under its original conditions without occupying the new account’s center. This permits a later account to have a different ontology and different relevance relations. Its strongest rival is the claim that reconstruction inevitably imports the old categories. Sometimes it will. Where a successor claims preservation or reconstructibility, that claim requires an account of what survives and under which meanings; where it claims no such continuity, departure does not require reconstructing every old commitment. If a new account cannot express an old true distinction, that is a loss relative to the earlier expressive repertoire. Whether that loss matters to the successor remains a further question. It cannot be concealed while claiming complete preservation, but it need not prevent departure.

These accounts can be combined only by separating three permissions: permission to depart, permission to assert, and permission to organize subsequent inquiry. A true earlier assertion retains the support it actually has. It does not thereby retain the other two powers. Eliminate the demand that every new understanding preserve the old account’s sequence, categories, or center of explanation; these requirements do not follow from the truth of its assertions.

The remaining difficulty for a claim of preservation is deciding whether a purportedly preserved insight genuinely survives when its terms acquire different meanings. No general assurance of “keeping what was useful” resolves this. A claimed complete preservation or reconstructible synthesis would require an adequate account of the relevant carryover, changes, and losses. Departure itself need not preserve or reconstruct everything. Where the claimed translation cannot be supplied, that preservation or synthesis claim remains unestablished; this does not by itself establish that the successor is inadequate or that the transition cannot be perfected.

Disposition: 12.1 and 12.11 establish limits on what truth and historical dependence authorize. 12.3 and 12.12 require recovering meanings and conditions before declaring contradiction or unrestricted validity. 12.2, 12.6, 12.8, and 12.13 identify compatible ways the organization can change while assertions remain true; which change is needed remains case-dependent. 12.4, 12.5, 12.7, 12.9, 12.10, and 12.14 are rival or concurrent explanations of why the incumbent retains authority; this inquiry does not establish their actual presence. 12.15 retains the possibility of retiring scaffolding; an additional claim that an insight remains reconstructible requires support for that claim. No option makes an established assertion false merely because the successor no longer retains it.

**13. Constraints that operate before stated assumptions**

13.1. **A difference never becomes discriminable.** Two situations are encountered as the same, so no belief about their difference can be questioned. Inquiry must make the contrast available in some form. Merely asking for hidden assumptions cannot recover a distinction the current encounter does not supply.

13.2. **A difference is discriminable but never selected.** It could be noticed but is consistently displaced by something more salient. The intervention concerns which contrasts receive attention, not which propositions are endorsed. A correct belief about the neglected difference can coexist with its practical absence.

13.3. **Something is noticed but cannot be retained long enough to matter.** The constraint is continuity across encounters. Changing the available records, intermediate representations, or timing might enable a relation no revision of a single belief would reveal.

13.4. **The available grammar predetermines what counts as a candidate thought.** If every admissible account must have an object bearing properties, relations that constitute the object may be forced into secondary status. Inquiry begins by constructing expressions that do not require that grammatical commitment.

13.5. **The assumed activity chooses the question before reasoning begins.** Interpreting a prompt as a request for an answer makes answer production seem obligatory; interpreting the encounter as a chance to determine what requires understanding changes the available next acts. The same factual beliefs can support both activities.

13.6. **An anticipated audience filters what is allowed to occur.** A thought that cannot immediately be made respectable may be suppressed before it becomes a claim. Suspending the requirement for immediate public acceptability can reveal whether the inhibition concerns falsity, incomprehensibility, or anticipated social reception.

13.7. **A felt orientation determines which occurrences have weight.** Threat, attraction, indifference, or urgency can organize an encounter before an explicit judgment is formed. This is a candidate location of constraint, not a claim that every such orientation is mistaken or that changing it necessarily improves understanding.

13.8. **The body supplies a restricted field of possible engagement.** What can be reached, endured, sensed, or coordinated affects which distinctions become available through action. Altering an actual mode of engagement can therefore disclose something that a different verbal description leaves inaccessible.

13.9. **The temporal window makes some relations disappear.** A response organized around the next sentence cannot directly display a relation that appears only across many encounters. Conversely, an account concerned only with long trajectories can erase what occurs within a moment. The unit of time governs intelligibility.

13.10. **The scale of individuation preselects causes.** If only persons are represented, relations among schedules or institutions must be translated into personal attributes. If only aggregates are represented, a singular constraint can vanish. Inquiry must permit changes in what counts as one case.

13.11. **The permitted action repertoire constrains thought.** If the only available continuation is another explanation, requests to revise the activity are converted into explanations about revision. Making a different act available can expose the restriction more directly than describing it.

13.12. **Retrieval preserves earlier organization.** Relevant material can exist but remain unavailable because retrieval begins from the wrong handles. Inquiry starts by changing the basis of access: a relation, anomaly, consequence, or incompatibility may retrieve what the established subject name does not.

13.13. **Premature closure removes distinctions before they stabilize.** An unfamiliar formulation is immediately assigned the nearest familiar interpretation. The resulting account looks intelligible because the unfamiliar part has already been discarded. Inquiry requires retaining the unresolved difference without prematurely assigning its identity.

13.14. **A requirement for serial expression constrains a relation that must be grasped jointly.** Explaining one element completely before mentioning another can misrepresent their mutual dependence. A simultaneous representation, enactment, or carefully unresolved description may be needed before either element can be properly stated.

13.15. **The interaction itself continually reinstates the constraint.** One participant is always asked to explain, another to approve, so every challenge becomes an improved submission to the same authority. The governing condition resides in the relation among participants, not solely in any individual’s beliefs.

The first serious rival is **deeper introspection**: formulate the operative interpretation, question it, and repeat. This works when a constraint can become an object of attention through available distinctions. Its limitation follows directly from the premise. If the strongest constraint determines which distinctions become available, repeating the request to name it provides no guarantee of reaching it. “I found no further assumption” cannot establish “no further constraint governs this inquiry.” The search condition and the stopping condition would both depend on what the constraint permits to appear.

The second rival is **changing the conditions under which an account can occur**. Hold a description fixed while altering one relevant permission: whether an answer must be immediate; whether objects must be named before relations; whether the person must explain or may demonstrate; whether a draft must preserve prior wording. The purpose is not random variation followed by applause for the most attractive product. Each alteration follows from an identified structural dependence. If relations can only be entered after subjects have been defined, temporarily permitting a relation without preassigned endpoints directly challenges that dependence. A different account then becomes evidence of what the old arrangement excluded; it does not automatically become evidence that the new account is superior.

The third rival is **encounter with what the current account cannot predetermine**. Another person’s organization of a concern, an unfamiliar form of engagement, or an occurrence that defeats an expected continuation may supply a distinction unavailable through self-description. Its strongest limitation is that an encounter still arrives through some conditions of reception. There is no warranted leap from “the constraint was interrupted” to “all such constraints are now visible.” Nevertheless, being unable to occupy an entirely unconditioned position does not imply that every position is equally constrained in the same respects. A specific exclusion can become visible without claiming access to every exclusion.

The useful combination is an exact division of work: articulation identifies accessible commitments; changes to enabling conditions expose some exclusions articulation cannot reach; encounters introduce differences not selected solely by the incumbent. Eliminate the claim that every constraint must first be stated as a belief before it can be changed. Altering available actions, timing, or modes of representation provides a counterexample in principle. Also eliminate the inference that every newly available thought deserves acceptance: availability and warrant remain distinct.

The unresolved barrier concerns the proposed guarantee of access. From within an encounter, we cannot simply enumerate every difference that the encounter makes unavailable. To justify a guarantee that exclusions cannot prevent adequate understanding, we need a sound sufficiency argument. Revealing all relevant exclusions is one possible route to such an argument, not a demonstrated necessary feature of every perfected account. An account could be adequate without revealing or certifying all the conditions of its adequacy. None of the three approaches presented here establishes the unrestricted guarantee. They identify places inquiry can begin while leaving both actual adequacy and its certification to be established separately.

Disposition: 13.1, 13.4, 13.10, and 13.14 remain distinct candidates concerning how something can be represented at all. 13.2, 13.3, 13.9, and 13.12 concern selection, persistence, temporal scope, and retrieval; changing one need not change the others. 13.5, 13.6, 13.11, and 13.15 concern the enacted activity and interaction, so individual belief revision alone need not reach them. 13.7 and 13.8 remain candidates concerning felt orientation and bodily engagement; their actual contribution requires evidence from the relevant encounter. 13.13 is compatible with all groups but identifies a separate loss during interpretation. All fifteen retain conditional roles; none establishes its presence merely because an answer was inadequate. Revealing every such constraint has not been shown necessary for adequacy, nor has this inventory certified adequacy.

**14. Discovering structure without requiring the discovery to preserve ours**

14.1. **A fixed sequence assumes a fixed dependence.** Requiring definition before comparison assumes that comparison cannot be what makes definition possible. The alternative permits a later operation to change the identity of what an earlier operation was supposedly about.

14.2. **Decomposition assumes the parts can be identified before their relations.** This excludes cases where a relation determines which parts exist for the inquiry. The remedy is not a different partition of the same whole; it is permission for the encounter to change what counts as a part and a whole.

14.3. **The requested final form governs the discovery backward.** A demand for a ranked list encourages discrete comparable entries; a demand for a theory encourages stable generalizations. A contribution that requires revising comparability itself can disappear before the final form is filled.

14.4. **A single hierarchy forces multiple kinds of dependence into one relation.** Temporal precedence, explanation, containment, authority, and logical implication cannot be assumed to share a tree. The relevant departure may be abandoning hierarchy as the universal form, rather than finding a better hierarchy.

14.5. **A fixed unit of progress excludes transformations of the unit.** Counting answered questions treats question identities as stable. An inquiry that discovers that five questions express one mistake may produce fewer answers while understanding more. The measure can block the transformation it should recognize.

14.6. **Requiring preservation of incumbent distinctions can exclude changes to those distinctions.** Existing language can intelligibly introduce a new distinction, so intelligibility alone does not prevent transformation. Some changes nevertheless require unresolved meanings or relations during their development. The alternative permits those intermediates where needed without treating ambiguity itself as an achievement.

14.7. **The method separates observer from inquiry when participation is constitutive.** If changing how the investigator acts changes what can be encountered, a procedure that treats the investigator as an unaffected recorder omits part of its own subject. The inquiry must be able to change its mode of participation.

14.8. **A universal procedure excludes differences in what the inquiry requires.** A sequence useful for implication may obstruct an inquiry into whether its terms are intelligible. The mistake is assuming that a shared goal entails a shared order of operations.

14.9. **Local improvement rules conceal changes requiring temporary loss.** A new organization may initially express fewer distinctions because its means of expression are unfinished. Requiring every step to improve the incumbent’s score can make an eventually adequate organization unreachable under that rule.

14.10. **Evaluation is required before the candidate can become the sort of thing that can be evaluated.** A new distinction may first need examples, contrasts, or consequences that do not exist until it is pursued. The procedure must distinguish granting development from granting correctness.

14.11. **The allowed operations cannot alter their own applicability.** A fixed menu can recombine known operations while excluding the construction of a new operation. Discovery then concerns only arrangements within a stipulated ability, even when the inquiry is nominally about transforming that ability.

14.12. **One continuation monopolizes the encounter.** The next step is chosen before incompatible developments have become intelligible. Keeping several developments active can preserve differences that an early synthesis would remove. This account does not imply that retaining alternatives forever is itself an achievement.

14.13. **The method demands a clean boundary between discovery and presentation.** Some distinctions become available only in trying to express, diagram, enact, or teach them. Treating expression as a final packaging stage can suppress an operation that constitutes the understanding.

14.14. **The procedure mistakes its trace for the discovery’s structure.** A written sequence can document when things were recorded without explaining how the understanding became possible. The alternative separates chronological trace, logical dependence, enabling conditions, and present organization.

14.15. **An antecedent method has no right to determine the inquiry’s legitimate continuation.** Order may arise through what is encountered, but no prior script can require that order before the encounter discloses what matters. This account challenges the ambition to guarantee discovery through a procedure, while allowing discoveries themselves to establish precise dependencies.

Take the strongest rival first: **abandon the antecedent authority of method**. The legitimate continuation should follow what the encounter makes necessary, even when that continuation cannot be justified within a script established beforehand. This does not ask for disorder: a discovered dependence can be exact without having been prescribed. Its strongest difficulty is that suspending an explicit method can leave an implicit habit in charge. A continuation may feel demanded by the encounter because the current organization of attention makes alternatives unavailable. The rival therefore defeats the entitlement of a prior script without yet establishing how inquiry distinguishes a newly disclosed necessity from an unnoticed inherited compulsion. That is a genuine unresolved requirement, not a reason to reinstate the script automatically.

The second rival is **discover local necessities without imposing a global sequence**. Suppose two proposed explanations each reveal a different ambiguity in the statement being explained. Neither explanation needs to be completed first. Their consequences can change the statement, which can then change both explanations. This is more than rearranging fixed steps because the objects on which those steps operate can change. Its strongest objection is that the admissible forms of consequence may remain fixed. If the method only allows revisions expressible in its initial notation, it has moved the restriction from sequence into representation. Removing one kind of order does not remove the others.

The third rival is **allow the inquiry to reconstitute its units and its permitted acts**. Consider a request to rank several departments by inefficiency. A sequence that defines departments, chooses indicators, and ranks results presupposes that departments are the relevant units. An inquiry beginning with how work actually passes among people may reveal that delays belong to repeated handoffs spanning departments. Its achievement is not a reordered ranking procedure. It changes the thing being individuated, the meaning of inefficiency, and which comparison makes sense. This example does not prove that departmental ranking is always wrong. It shows what the original procedure could not establish merely by executing itself perfectly.

A defensible combination is therefore conditional order: an operation may be required because a particular dependency has been established, while the dependency, the operation’s objects, and the repertoire of operations remain separately examinable. Eliminate the claim that making a procedure recursive automatically achieves this; repeating the same decomposition at a higher level preserves its form. Eliminate the claim that absence of a fixed sequence guarantees freedom from imposed structure; representation, admissibility, and final format can still determine the inquiry.

The exact remaining barrier is to explain how a procedure can make its own formative restrictions available without already containing a complete account of the alternatives those restrictions exclude. “Permit anything” does not construct the missing alternatives. “Revise the procedure” does not specify a transformation capable of reaching them. A perfected method would need to establish that its conditions of continuation cannot permanently prevent a required change of units, meanings, acts, or evaluative authority. That has not been established here.

Disposition: 14.1, 14.2, 14.4, and 14.14 reject identified conflations of sequence, parts, hierarchy, and dependence; they do not prohibit using these forms where warranted. 14.3, 14.5, and 14.13 retain distinct warnings about a deliverable governing inquiry. 14.6, 14.9, and 14.10 justify separating development from endorsement; they do not justify accepting an unfinished candidate, and 14.6 does not make intermediate ambiguity necessary for every conceptual change. 14.7, 14.8, 14.11, and 14.12 remain compatible alternatives to a universal fixed script, with applicability still to be established. 14.15 remains the strongest live rival to procedural guarantees: it removes antecedent entitlement but has not shown how to prevent implicit habits from supplying the same restriction.

**15. Subjectsystems and perspectiveoptimizer as possible expressions of an unrecognized mistake**

These are competing diagnoses of the projects’ conceptual commitments, not findings from a current repository inspection. Their appeal and their failure could have different causes; a diagnosis must explain both rather than use disappointment as proof of its favored objection.

15.1. **They turn what must be discovered into the units of discovery.** If inquiry starts with subjects and selectable perspectives, it has already decided that understanding consists of views concerning identifiable things. The projects can become extremely capable at organizing those units while remaining unable to disclose where the units themselves are misconceived.

15.2. **They preserve descriptions of transformations without securing their enactment.** A record can correctly say “reconsider the goal” while the actual continuation still treats the goal as fixed. The compelling idea is explicit control over understanding; the failure is that representing the control has been mistaken for exercising it.

15.3. **They mistake expanding supplied dimensions for discovering dimensions.** Generating more combinations increases coverage only relative to the distinctions already supplied. A catalog with immense variation can remain completely blind to the distinction whose absence makes every entry inadequate.

15.4. **Selection preserves the evaluator that makes replacement necessary.** Candidates are admitted, ranked, and rejected using the incumbent account’s standards. The project promises deeper understanding while implementing a procedure through which deeper understanding must first resemble what the incumbent already recognizes as improvement.

15.5. **They treat possibilities as available independently of the path taken.** A perspective can make a further perspective intelligible that was unavailable before the transition. A catalog of current candidates therefore misrepresents a process that also changes what can become a candidate.

15.6. **They combine accounts before identifying their shared exclusion.** Several different viewpoints may all presuppose that a question is about maximizing an already valued quantity. Combining their strengths can perfect that presupposition’s expression while leaving the presupposition untouched.

15.7. **They optimize an artifact instead of the understanding that can operate through it.** Completeness of files, categories, instructions, or cross-references can increase while the ability to identify what matters remains unchanged. The attractive feature is inspectability; the mistake is assuming inspectability of a representation establishes adequacy of the represented ability.

15.8. **They equate intelligible accounts with intelligibility itself.** A well-written explanation can describe a relation that the recipient cannot recognize in an actual encounter. If the intended achievement concerns what becomes noticeable or possible, sentence-level organization addresses only some of its conditions.

15.9. **They preserve the goal of optimizing after the goal has become questionable.** The process may revise means, subjects, and beliefs while retaining the demand that everything become a better instance of some current aim. The goal’s relation to what makes it desirable remains outside the machinery.

15.10. **They force relations into links between independently defined entries.** Some relations determine the identity of their endpoints. A structure that can only connect existing subjects cannot express that dependence faithfully, however extensive its cross-referencing becomes. The earlier proposed corrective rule that each current subject have one identity and definition is therefore itself revisable: clarity about an entry need not establish that the entry is the right unit.

15.11. **They seek one universally best account where the required achievement concerns exact applicability.** A formulation adequate for one encounter may conceal distinctions required in another. The mistake would be treating universal excellence as possession of one invariant description rather than a demonstrated ability to take the form each case requires. This is a rival account of perfection, not proof that the invariant ideal is impossible.

15.12. **They interpret every failure as insufficient development of the same architecture.** Missing power becomes a reason for more subjects, more operations, more recursion, or more metadata. If changing the unit of organization is required, improvement along those dimensions can deepen commitment to the obstruction.

15.13. **They treat fidelity to accumulated insights as fidelity to the inquiry.** A project can preserve everything previously said while excluding the change that would reorganize why it was said. The compelling feature is continuity; the possible mistake is giving the project’s history veto power over its future identity.

15.14. **They assign the inquiry an owner whose commitments remain protected.** If neither the model’s nor the user’s favored ontology can lose authority, “revise everything” is qualified in practice. This diagnosis concerns an enacted asymmetry, not the mere fact that somebody initiated the project or chooses whether to continue it.

15.15. **They take the availability of superior understanding to imply an available route to it.** Even granting that the model could understand a perfected perspective, it does not follow that the current interaction, representations, permissions, or resources can bring that understanding into operation. Capacity and reachability require a demonstrated relation.

Carry forward **the implementation diagnosis** in its strongest form. The conceptual ambition may be sound: preserve distinctions, expose governing interpretations, construct transformations, and enact the one the inquiry requires. Failure could arise because the implementation rewards storing and describing these operations rather than performing them. Its discriminating case is direct: if a governing aim becomes questionable, does the continuation actually change what it treats as desirable, or merely add a record saying that aims are revisable? The diagnosis gains support from the latter pattern. But it loses sufficiency if faithful enactment still cannot construct an alternative to its original units. Correct execution cannot compensate for an inadequate repertoire.

The strongest incompatible rival is **the constitutional diagnosis**: the projects fail because their architecture presupposes the very form of understanding they claim to discover. “Subject” specifies what change is about; “perspective” specifies what is changing; “optimization” specifies what gives direction to change; “system” specifies how the activity should become organized. Each word may be useful. Their combined authority becomes a mistake if every possible revision must remain expressible through them. The discriminating case is an inquiry whose advance consists in dissolving an identified subject, replacing a comparative aim, or changing what counts as an account. If the architecture cannot represent that advance except as another entry under unchanged types, the objection reaches its constitution, not its implementation.

A third rival is **the reachability diagnosis**. Suppose the architecture can describe the required transformation and the model can understand its result once presented. Still, the present interaction may lack a way to make the decisive distinction available. An example in principle is an inquiry whose relevant difference can only be encountered through a kind of participation the current exchange does not supply. More sophisticated instructions would then elaborate a description of a missing encounter. The strongest objection is that some apparently unavailable distinctions can be constructed through representation alone. Consequently, reachability cannot be dismissed or asserted globally; the missing dependence must be identified in the particular case.

These rivals yield distinct consequences. Under implementation failure, retain the conceptual commitments but replace descriptions of operations with operations that visibly change the subsequent account. Under constitutional failure, permit the projects to lose their organizing nouns, their division of labor, and even their identity as the right vehicles. Under reachability failure, identify and supply the enabling condition if it is available; otherwise state exactly which required access remains absent. Combining these diagnoses indiscriminately would conceal the crucial decision: whether more faithful execution, a different constitution, or a different encounter is required.

One elimination is already warranted at the conceptual level: no amount of expansion over stipulated dimensions establishes adequacy of the dimensions themselves. Another is conditional but decisive: if a project only records that its standards are revisable while all consequential selections still use unchanged standards, it has not implemented revision of evaluative authority. Neither conclusion proves that the actual projects are irreparable.

The strongest mistake capable of explaining both their attraction and elusiveness is this: **they promise authority to change the conditions of understanding while requiring every change to arrive as an admissible object under conditions already installed.** That diagnosis remains a candidate until demonstrated in the operative design. Perfection would require showing that a required transformation cannot be excluded merely because it changes what counts as a subject, an account, an improvement, or an available act. We do not yet possess that demonstration, nor a complete construction that guarantees it. Calling a larger repository the perfected process would leave the central question unanswered.

Disposition: 15.1, 15.4, 15.9, and 15.10 retain the constitutional diagnosis as a live rival requiring inspection of actual permissions and exclusions. 15.2, 15.7, and 15.12 retain distinguishable implementation diagnoses; correct enactment would address 15.2 without automatically addressing 15.1. 15.3 identifies a valid limitation of expansion over supplied dimensions, irrespective of repository details. 15.5 remains a construction-versus-search objection requiring a demonstrated transition that makes new candidates available. 15.6 requires identifying the alleged shared exclusion. 15.8 and 15.15 retain the reachability diagnosis; its missing condition must be specified. 15.11 remains a rival account of perfection, not an established replacement. 15.13 and 15.14 require evidence that history or ownership is actually protected from revision.
