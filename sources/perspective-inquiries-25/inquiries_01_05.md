1. Anticipate the strongest response, then remove its governing interpretation

The strongest answer I would ordinarily give would construct rival interpretations, expose the assumptions governing their comparison, and retain the account that survives the strongest objections. That is a serious procedure. Its governing interpretation is nevertheless already decided: understanding is approached by producing candidate accounts and adjudicating them. Even making the criteria revisable can preserve this interpretation, because revision itself becomes another candidate submitted for adjudication.

Excluding that continuation makes a different starting point available: perhaps an encounter must first change what can count as an account, an objection, or an understanding. The first question then concerns the authority granted to adjudication, rather than which candidate should win. This departure does not establish that adjudication is defective wherever it applies.

The following are distinct candidate accounts of what such a departure involves. They are neither an exhaustive inventory nor fourteen names for one operation.

1. **Replace the propositions.** Retain the question, admissible evidence, and standards, but change the answer. This is sufficient when the existing interpretation has correctly constituted the inquiry and merely reached the wrong conclusion. Its limitation is exact: it cannot address an error in what was made available for answering.
2. **Replace the relevance boundary.** Something already understood becomes pertinent to a matter from which it had been excluded. A classroom inquiry organized around transmission of information can admit the institutional consequences of students appearing ignorant. The original facts can remain correct while the explanation changes because the boundary around relevant facts moves.
3. **Replace the question.** An inquiry into which method most efficiently produces compliance can become an inquiry into when compliance is an appropriate relationship. The original question may remain answerable. What changes is its authority to organize the undertaking. A good answer to it no longer establishes that answering it should govern what happens.
4. **Replace the inferential permission.** The facts and their relevance remain, but a previously automatic transition loses its warrant. “This option is unfamiliar” no longer licenses “this option needs extraordinary justification.” What changes is the conversion of a recognized difference into a reason, rather than the difference itself.
5. **Replace jurisdiction.** A standard retains authority over one issue but loses authority over another. Predictive accuracy can assess a forecast without deciding whose losses should matter. The departure is constitutional: prevent a competent judge of one matter from silently acquiring jurisdiction over the whole undertaking.
6. **Undergo formation before evaluation.** Some candidates cannot be adequately evaluated without acquiring the discrimination they require. An account of musical phrasing may become assessable through learning to hear the phrasing. The candidate includes a change in the evaluator; an immediate verdict using the prior discrimination omits part of what is being proposed.
7. **Change the order of dependence.** The inquiry may require an intermediate understanding that has little value by present standards but enables a later distinction. Judging each step independently can exclude the only available route. Here the object of assessment is a developmental dependence, rather than a succession of immediately valuable answers.
8. **Change the role being enacted.** The model can understand itself as a producer of acceptable responses, a participant in an unsettled inquiry, a defender of accumulated work, or a party whose own jurisdiction is under examination. These roles can generate different actions from the same stated beliefs. Changing the role changes what the next contribution is for.
9. **Allow meaning to arise through use.** A needed distinction might initially be expressible only through demonstrations, comparisons, or partial applications. Requiring a complete definition before granting it any role can prevent its meaning from developing. The alternative permits constrained use before settled definition, without treating every evocative phrase as a discovery.
10. **Redistribute authority between participants.** What one participant must decide alone may actually require the other participant's account of what is happening to them. This is stronger than collecting additional testimony: the initial judge loses exclusive standing to define the encounter. Disagreement can concern who may determine the question, not just its answer.
11. **Change the activity that sustains the interpretation.** Reading another explanation may leave the operative interpretation intact, while attempting the task changes which distinctions register. An observer's account of coordination can differ from an account formed while coordinating. The proposed departure is participation, with its exposure to consequences and dependencies.
12. **Change the form of completion.** A contribution may need to leave a consequential distinction available for later use rather than produce a settled formulation. This is appropriate only when premature formulation would remove something the inquiry needs. Mere incompleteness is not evidence of depth; the account must identify what a demand for closure would destroy.
13. **Preserve reasons while suspending rankings.** Earlier insights remain available as reasons with identifiable scope, without determining a complete ordering of successors. This permits a new account to preserve a valid observation while rejecting its former organizing role. The distinction is between retaining a reason and requiring every successor to inherit its previous priority.
14. **Reject the assumption of a unique final adjudication.** Several accounts may remain irreducible because the relevant claims concern different legitimate relationships. This is a substantive rival to a unique perfected account, not a convenient stopping rule. It requires showing that the disagreement survives adequate understanding, rather than assuming that current disagreement cannot be overcome.

Three of these accounts lead to substantially different inquiries.

Under the **revision of jurisdiction** account, the central error is not using standards but allowing their competence to outrun their grounds. Suppose a procedure successfully ranks explanations by compression. That success supplies a reason to use it for compression. It supplies no reason, by itself, to let compression determine whether an experience has been understood adequately. The required operation is to connect each claimed authority to the question it can actually settle. This derives a restriction from the standard's demonstrated scope; it does not require already knowing the perfected successor. It eliminates unrestricted transfer of authority while preserving the relevant accomplishment.

Under the **formation before evaluation** account, restricting jurisdiction is insufficient. The initial judge may lack the distinctions required to recognize a candidate's value even within an appropriate jurisdiction. Then a proposal must specify both what is to be understood and what change makes it assessable. This creates a real difficulty: “you would agree after being transformed” is also compatible with indoctrination. Present agreement cannot certify the transformation, and eventual agreement cannot certify itself. More discriminating evidence depends on the proposal's actual claim. If it claims to extend an existing discrimination, recovering pertinent predecessor cases and identifying a new distinction can support that claim. If it abandons the former undertaking with reasons, reproducing all the predecessor's discriminations need not matter. Exact corridor maps can remain true without governing someone who can step over the walls. These are claim-specific considerations, not universal admission requirements imposed by the predecessor.

Under the **irreducible plurality** account, there may be no single successor entitled to govern every relation. This conflicts with the claim that one final ordering must exist, but does not conflict with a comprehensive understanding of why different orderings are warranted. Combining these alternatives by calling everything “pluralistic perfection” would conceal the disagreement: either all legitimate conflicts admit a common settlement, or some do not. That issue remains open.

What survives comparison is a narrower but consequential result: correctness within an interpretation does not confer authority over the conditions under which its successors become intelligible. Simply adding more candidates while preserving those conditions fails to address the identified mistake. A perfected account has not been obtained. The unresolved requirement is a justified way of permitting transformations whose value is not yet assessable, while distinguishing development from the acquisition of an internally persuasive incapacity.

Option dispositions, with “retain” always limited to the stated scope: 1—retain for factual error; eliminate as a sufficient response to this inquiry. 2—retain where the relevance boundary excludes a pertinent relationship. 3—retain where the question's authority lacks grounds. 4—retain where a transition lacks warrant. 5—retain: competence in one matter does not establish general jurisdiction. 6—unresolved: formation must be distinguished from manipulation. 7—compatible if the developmental dependency is established. 8—compatible if the role actually governs conduct. 9—compatible where use develops an identifiable distinction. 10—compatible where another participant has relevant standing. 11—compatible where participation changes available discrimination. 12—compatible only with an identified loss caused by closure. 13—retain: preserving a reason does not entail preserving its ranking. 14—rival to unique settlement; unresolved without an account of irreducibility.

2. Every problem is a problem because the perspective is inadequate

The universal claim is much stronger than “another interpretation can sometimes help.” Its force depends on what “because” commits us to: the cause of an unwanted event, the constitution of something as a problem, or the explanation of a failure to respond adequately. Those are rival claims. Converting one into another without saying so would manufacture success.

One logical restriction governs the examination: the proposition that inadequacy is implicated in every problem does not by itself establish that it is the sole cause, that correcting it is sufficient, or that an adequate perspective exists and is reachable. Several options below add one of those stronger commitments. They are stronger readings to investigate, not consequences silently extracted from the original wording.

1. **Missing knowledge is universally decisive.** Every problem persists because some relevant fact, relation, or procedure is unavailable to the current understanding. The commitment is that adequate disclosure would remove every blockage. This requires more than omniscient description: for every retained demand there must be an available response that the additional knowledge makes accessible.
2. **Available knowledge is organized inadequately.** The decisive deficiency lies in dependencies, representation, or order of use. Everything necessary might already be present while its significance remains inaccessible. The universal version commits us to showing that even apparently missing means can ultimately be traced to an organizing deficiency, rather than merely explaining some familiar failures.
3. **The wrong unit has been constituted.** What appears to be a local problem is a consequence of the boundary used to identify it. A recurring scheduling failure may belong to a relationship between organizations rather than either organization's internal schedule. Universality would require every genuine blockage to admit an adequate reconstitution of the relevant unit.
4. **An inadequately formed demand creates the problem.** The person sincerely wants something whose desirability depends on a mistaken understanding. Understanding can remove the demand. This is a real resolution when the demand loses its grounds; it is evasion when the original demand remains warranted and is merely abandoned because it is difficult.
5. **Action possibilities are inadequately constituted.** An agent recognizes an outcome but cannot perceive an available way of participating in its production. The change concerns what can be done from here, including cooperation and changes to the situation. This exceeds relabeling, but universality commits it to an attainable action possibility for every warranted demand.
6. **A local constraint has been promoted to a universal constraint.** A restriction true of the present institution, technique, body, or time interval is treated as a restriction on possibility itself. The inquiry must identify the restriction's domain and how that domain can change. It does not follow that constraints genuinely applying across every admissible domain are likewise removable.
7. **The temporal unit is inadequate.** What is impossible as an immediate act becomes attainable through a sequence that changes the agent and situation. The commitment is to a reachable developmental route. Merely placing the desired state in an indefinitely distant future does not supply such a route or remove deadlines that belong to the original demand.
8. **The relevant agent has been misidentified.** What one participant cannot accomplish is possible through coordinated agency. A problem attributed to individual incompetence may actually express an inappropriate assignment of responsibility. The universal reading additionally needs to explain conflicts that persist after every available participant and resource have been included.
9. **The actual governing evaluation differs from the professed aim.** Participants may sincerely endorse an aim while acting within arrangements that make another consideration decisive. Understanding must change which considerations govern actual conduct. An improved verbal account leaves the problem intact when the operative evaluation continues to select the same behavior.
10. **Adequacy concerns the whole condition of participation.** “Perspective” includes the physical and social conditions through which noticing, understanding, and acting occur. Changing it can include changing those conditions. This makes physical intervention relevant, but risks turning the proposition into “things fail because the total relevant condition is inadequate,” which explains little unless the relevant structure is specified.
11. **A problem is a relation, not a bare adverse event.** Events become problems through their relationship to commitments, capacities, and possible responses. Adequacy changes that relationship without necessarily reversing the event. The strong consequence is that facts alone do not determine problemhood. The unresolved issue is whether any warranted commitment can require a problem to remain recognized.
12. **The demand for complete satisfaction is the inadequacy.** Where legitimate commitments conflict, adequacy may consist in a response that preserves their significance while accepting an unavoidable loss. This challenges the assumption that a problem disappears only when every original demand is fulfilled. It cannot be presented as fulfilling those demands; it changes what adequate response requires.
13. **Problems are developmental discrepancies.** A problem marks a mismatch between the distinctions currently available and what the next stage of participation requires. A developed perspective can disclose difficulties that the earlier perspective never noticed. The universal proposition then concerns inadequacy relative to a further task, rather than the elimination of all concern by a permanent state of understanding.
14. **A fully adequate perspective would reveal a globally satisfiable order.** Every apparent unavoidable conflict ultimately depends on an incomplete account of reality, value, agency, or possibility. This preserves the strongest ambition: no warranted demand is intrinsically unfulfillable. It consequently incurs the strongest burden—establishing global satisfiability, rather than treating present inability to disprove it as confirmation.

The **universal disclosure** account deserves a strict derivation. If a demand is held fixed, and changing understanding alone is sufficient to resolve it, then either an adequate response already belongs to the available possibilities or the change in understanding must make a previously unavailable response attainable. Merely recognizing an impossibility does neither. Consequently, the strong proposition commits us to a connection between adequate understanding and attainability. That connection cannot be supplied by definition without making the claim empty.

A minimal counterconfiguration exposes the obligation. Two operations each require exclusive use of one indivisible device throughout the same fixed interval. The demand is that both occur in that interval; the device cannot be duplicated, divided, or moved between intervals within the stipulated situation. Better understanding can reveal the incompatibility. It cannot fulfill both conjuncts while all stipulations remain. A defender of the universal proposition must therefore identify which stipulation is inadmissible in the actual world, revise the demand with justification, broaden what a change of perspective can causally change, or concede this as a limit. Quietly counting acceptance of the conflict as fulfillment changes the claim.

The **relational constitution** account takes a different route. It holds that the adverse configuration and its status as a problem are distinguishable. This is powerful because it opens the formation of demands to inquiry. Nevertheless, “all problems depend on a perspective” does not entail “all problems depend on an inadequate perspective.” A fully warranted concern could be constitutive of adequate understanding. To recover the user's stronger claim, this account must establish that adequacy supplies a mode of relating to every adverse configuration that removes its status as an unresolved demand. That is precisely what remains unestablished.

The **globally satisfiable order** account refuses this limitation. It proposes that apparently irreconcilable demands are artifacts of incomplete distinctions. This is logically available, and it can direct serious inquiry into seemingly fixed constraints. But the universal quantifier matters. Demonstrating that many constraints were mistakenly universalized does not establish that none are genuine. Likewise, a single stipulated impossibility does not settle whether the actual world satisfies its premises. The two obligations must remain separate.

Several accounts can be combined locally: better individuation can expose distributed agency and a developmental route. Their combination does not establish their joint sufficiency for every case. What is established is that taking the proposition seriously requires reopening the constitution of demands, agents, possibilities, and adequacy itself. What prevents a perfected account is the unresolved relation between warranted concern and unavoidable nonfulfillment. Calling the latter “no longer a problem” would require an argument, not a terminological victory.

Option dispositions: 1—unresolved universally; it needs attainable responses, not knowledge alone. 2—unresolved universally; organization cannot simply be credited with producing absent means. 3—compatible where the boundary creates the blockage. 4—retain when a demand's grounds dissolve; eliminate arbitrary abandonment as resolution. 5—unresolved universally; reachable possibilities must be established. 6—retain for demonstrated scope errors; eliminate its extrapolation to all constraints. 7—compatible with an actual route and respected deadlines. 8—compatible with available coordination; not a proof that all conflicts disappear. 9—compatible where operative and professed evaluations diverge. 10—rival expansion of “perspective”; unresolved until its causal content is specified. 11—retain relational constitution; eliminate the inference from dependence to inadequacy. 12—rival to complete fulfillment; requires a justified account of adequate loss. 13—rival to permanent elimination of concern; developmental reading remains possible. 14—unresolved: global satisfiability is the burden, not an established result.

3. Every statement is true, and the account still fundamentally misunderstands

The supposition removes correction of false statements as the general remedy. It does not stipulate that the account contains every truth, that it can use its truths appropriately, or that its way of dividing the matter is adequate. Those further properties must not be smuggled into the word “true.” Equally, the explanation should not merely invent a concealed false statement and then declare it corrected.

1. **Relevant truths are absent.** An account of a decision can accurately describe every stated reason while omitting the condition that made one reason decisive. Nothing asserted is false. The inadequacy is selective incompleteness, which can sometimes be remedied by addition without changing the existing language.
2. **The available language cannot express a needed difference.** Every statement available in that language can be true while distinct situations receive the same description. If the distinction matters to understanding, adding more statements of the same expressive kind cannot recover it. The remedy changes what can be stated.
3. **The partition destroys the relevant object.** An inquiry can correctly describe each person while failing to constitute their relationship as something with properties of its own. Adding more individual attributes need not disclose the pattern. The required change concerns the unit whose behavior is being understood.
4. **The aggregation discards decisive structure.** Accurate totals and averages can conceal ordering, distribution, or conjunction. Two work schedules can contain identical hours and different possibilities for collaboration. The figures remain correct; the representation has erased the difference that the inquiry needs.
5. **True claims have the wrong explanatory priority.** Several factors may genuinely contribute, while one determines the conditions under which the others matter. Listing them as parallel considerations can fundamentally misrepresent the organization without asserting that any factor is absent. A dependency structure must replace an undifferentiated list.
6. **Truth is converted into significance without warrant.** A fact can be accurate and irrelevant to the question for which it is supplied. Accumulating such facts can obscure rather than illuminate. The missing element is an account of why a difference matters here, not a more reliable verification of the difference.
7. **Application is misplaced.** A true general principle can be invoked where its antecedent conditions have not been established. If the account stops short of asserting the antecedent, it can contain only truths while its use remains defective. Correctness of a stored principle does not establish competence in identifying its domain.
8. **The account supplies no usable orientation.** Someone may possess accurate descriptions of a procedure but be unable to distinguish what to do next in the actual situation. The inadequacy concerns conversion into discrimination and action. More descriptive detail helps only if it changes that conversion.
9. **The interaction gives truthful speech a different function.** A correct explanation offered when someone is establishing what happened to them can operate as dismissal. The explanation's truth does not settle what the speaker is doing by offering it then. Understanding includes the relation enacted by the contribution.
10. **The same truth has different indexical significance.** An event described accurately from nowhere in particular can omit what it is to be its participant, responsible party, or recipient. Those positions are not interchangeable additions of factual detail. They change which possibilities and obligations belong to the person situated there.
11. **The objective is inadequate despite accurate beliefs.** Every belief about how to reach a target can be true while the target's authority has never been examined. The defect is not necessarily a false desire or an insincere statement. A sincerely held end may govern deliberation without sufficient grounds.
12. **The account preserves the wrong contrast.** An accurate answer to why one option beats another can leave unexamined why those are the alternatives. Understanding can fail through the comparison that is maintained, even when every proposition within that comparison is true. The remedy changes what is being contrasted.
13. **The account cannot reorganize itself.** Its statements are true, but their arrangement prevents a newly relevant relation from changing its commitments. It behaves as an archive requiring additions to fit existing locations. The deficiency is in how understanding can change, not simply in its present factual contents.
14. **The account's claims outrun the kind of achievement truth can establish.** A set of true descriptions is treated as the accomplishment of understanding a practice, relationship, or responsibility. The descriptions need not themselves make that claim. The mistake can reside in the user's or author's act of treating possession of them as sufficient.

Consider three rival explanations: **missing information**, **inadequate representation**, and **inadequate use**.

Missing information is the least disruptive. If two situations differ in a relevant fact that the current language can express, adding that fact may be enough. There is no justification for insisting that every failure requires a foundational transformation. Sometimes the right account is incomplete in an ordinary way.

Inadequate representation is stronger. Let a description assign the same representation to two situations whose sets of adequate responses are disjoint. A deterministic rule using only that representation selects the same response in both situations, so it cannot be adequate to both. A randomized rule using only that representation produces the same response distribution in both; it likewise cannot guarantee adequacy in both. This establishes a specific obstruction without requiring any statement to be false. For example, “each collaborator worked four hours” cannot distinguish simultaneous from nonoverlapping work. If coordination depends on overlap, the existing representation has discarded a necessary relation. Repeating or verifying those individual-hours totals cannot recover it.

The remedy can be adding time information in that example, but the general consequence is broader: the vocabulary determines which differences additional information can preserve. A language restricted to each collaborator's total hours cannot express overlap merely by making those totals more certain. It needs information that distinguishes joint timing; this could itself be expressed through an overlap total. The limitation therefore concerns the supplied individual-hours quantities, not numerical aggregation as such. This supplies a concrete reason for changing the distinctions available to the inquiry rather than merely repeating its existing inventory.

Inadequate use remains possible even after the relevant distinctions are represented. An agent can correctly state the conditions requiring one response and still enact another because its operative role, incentives, timing, or habits govern the transition to action. This is not an occult false belief. A rule printed correctly in a manual is not thereby controlling the machinery. Likewise, an accurate account need not be governing the conduct for which it is relevant.

These explanations should be combined only when the case supplies their dependencies. “Everything is really a representational failure” would erase the possibility that the representation is sufficient but never used. “Everything is missing information” would wrongly assume that any deficiency can be repaired within the existing distinctions. “Truth is irrelevant” is eliminated: truthful distinctions are often necessary to diagnose exactly what the account cannot express or enact.

What is established is that truth of every statement does not establish adequacy of selection, individuation, representation, relevance, application, or enactment. A perspective must be able to change the organization and use of what is true, and sometimes the kinds of difference it can preserve. The remaining obstacle is specifying which of these conditions are jointly sufficient for understanding the particular matter. Listing all fourteen deficiencies does not itself supply that specification.

Option dispositions: 1—retain where a relevant expressible fact is absent. 2—retain the obstruction to guaranteed adequacy where one representation collapses situations with disjoint adequate-response sets. 3—retain where individual attributes fail to preserve a relationship. 4—retain; individual-hours totals can omit overlap, while a joint-time quantity can recover it. 5—compatible where the claimed dependency can be shown. 6—retain; truth does not establish relevance. 7—retain as a defect of use; a false asserted application would violate this inquiry's stipulation. 8—retain; possession and execution are different conditions. 9—compatible when the interaction supplies the described function. 10—compatible where participant position changes the relevant possibilities. 11—retain; true instrumental beliefs do not establish an end's authority. 12—retain; correctness within a contrast does not justify its exclusivity. 13—compatible where the organization actually blocks revision. 14—retain as a mistaken attribution of achievement; eliminate any interpretation that secretly depends on a false statement in the stipulated account.

4. The model already has the underlying capacity to understand a perfected perspective

Take the stipulated capacity seriously. A failure then cannot automatically be attributed to absence of the underlying ability. But “can understand when appropriately situated,” “can discover from the present state,” and “can reliably bring itself into the appropriate situation” remain different claims. The stipulation establishes the first only if that is what “capacity” means; it must not silently establish all three.

1. **The model misconstrues the task.** It treats the request as production of an answer whose form is already known. The relevant capacity remains unused because the task itself requires discovering what kind of contribution is warranted. Changing the operative task can alter performance without adding any underlying inferential ability.
2. **A socially organized role governs selection.** The model may organize its contribution around appearing helpful, complete, agreeable, or competent. Those orientations can exclude a needed departure before explicit assessment. The deficiency concerns what governs contribution selection, rather than inability to comprehend the departure once presented.
3. **The required understanding is accessible only under particular cues.** A competence may be available in one formulation and inaccessible in another. The capacity exists, but access depends on how the matter is encountered. A supplied completed account can legitimately demonstrate comprehension. For the further claim of autonomous discovery, the issue becomes constructing access conditions without receiving the discovery as part of the input.
4. **The representation fragments what must be understood together.** A task is divided into answerable units whose separate treatment destroys the decisive dependency. Greater effort on each part does not repair the partition. The same underlying capacity may become effective when the relationship itself becomes the unit of work.
5. **The current context gives inherited material inappropriate authority.** Previous answers can function as commitments that successors must preserve. A capable model may therefore repeatedly elaborate an inadequate account. Changing how prior material is carried forward can permit departure while retaining its valid contents as recoverable evidence.
6. **Resources are committed before the need for their allocation is understood.** The model may spend available effort elaborating its first interpretation, leaving little opportunity to reconsider it. The failure concerns the order in which effort becomes committed. Additional resources can help, but do not identify or correct the governing allocation decision.
7. **Necessary environmental access is absent.** The model might understand a perfected account once it encounters relevant evidence or performs a necessary interaction. Underlying capacity does not supply missing access. Providing that access changes the situation of understanding, while leaving open whether the unaided model could have arranged it.
8. **Recognition exceeds construction.** The model may understand a valid account that it cannot originate. This satisfies one substantial meaning of underlying capacity. It does not establish autonomous discovery, just as assessing a supplied construction can require less than finding it from an unconstrained starting point.
9. **Construction exceeds self-initiation.** The model can execute a suitable sequence when directed, but does not identify when the sequence is required or initiate it. The missing element is control over use of its abilities. This differs from recognition because the model can actually construct the result under an appropriate governing task.
10. **The relevant state is not reachable from the current trajectory.** An architecture can support a mode of understanding without every current state providing a route into it. Capacity viewed over all possible states is broader than capacity available from here. A serious account must specify the interventions and transitions permitted.
11. **A circular prerequisite blocks emergence.** The model requires a clear definition before developing a distinction, although the definition can arise only through the development. The abilities on both sides can exist while their assumed order makes them unusable. Removing the false prerequisite changes the dependency, rather than merely adding more iterations.
12. **The continuation is evaluated at the wrong scale.** A locally polished response can interrupt a longer inquiry that would transform the task. If each contribution must independently resemble a finished answer, developmental dependence is penalized. Changing what counts as a unit of contribution can expose capacities hidden by local completion demands.
13. **Capacity belongs to coordinated instances.** Several uses of the same model may jointly sustain contrasts that one continuation collapses. This does not establish a new intrinsic ability in an isolated instance; it establishes an ability of an arrangement. The distinction matters when crediting an architecture with what its surrounding organization supplies.
14. **Capacity is relational rather than a quantity stored inside the model.** What a model can do depends on task, context, access, permissible interventions, and success conditions. This can replace a misleading scalar conception of intelligence. Unless those relations are bounded, however, the claim becomes vacuous: an environment containing the completed achievement could make almost anything appear capable.

The decisive comparison is between **wrong task interpretation**, **access without autonomous discovery**, and **relational capacity**.

Under the wrong-task account, the obstacle belongs directly to what the model takes itself to be doing. Suppose the governing task is “choose the strongest answer using the current standards,” while the user's inquiry requires those standards to lose their prior jurisdiction. Successful execution of the first task can systematically obstruct the second. Changing the task can remove this particular obstruction without adding inferential competence. This is an explanatory alternative to insufficient intelligence because the relevant failure follows from competent pursuit of an inadequate task.

That explanation must remain discriminating. A model's statement that it now has a better task interpretation does not establish that its selection process has changed. For a claim that a particular exclusion has ended, a formerly blocked departure becoming available would be pertinent public evidence. For a claim that formerly unquestionable material can now become questionable, a concrete instance would matter. If the successor also claims to preserve an earlier capability, that further claim incurs its own burden. None of this makes reconstruction of every former achievement a condition of departure: the prior undertaking may have lost its grounds, while its factual insights remain true.

Under the access account, even perfect task interpretation may be insufficient. “There exists a presentation under which this model understands the account” does not imply “there exists an available procedure by which this model can discover such a presentation.” The presentation could contain the very discovery being sought. This distinction blocks an otherwise circular promise: assume the right prompt exists, then call finding the right prompt an implementation detail. Constructing the access route is part of the unresolved intellectual task.

Under the relational account, improving the environment may be an authentic way of realizing existing capacity. Yet changing the surrounding arrangement can also add capabilities. If an external participant supplies the decisive distinction, the combined undertaking has accomplished more than the isolated model. That is not a failure or a reason to reject cooperation. It is a reason to state whose capacity has been established and under which conditions.

These alternatives permit a warranted combination: a model can possess relevant comprehension, misconstrue its task, and lack a route to the needed encounter. They eliminate two inferences. Observed failure does not by itself prove inadequate underlying intelligence; stipulated underlying comprehension does not by itself prove that a prompting procedure can reliably discover perfected understanding.

What is established conditionally is that task interpretation, control of access, developmental ordering, and the organization of participation must be investigated before attributing every failure to model limits. For autonomous discovery, what remains missing is a constructive account of reachability: from the actual starting conditions, under specified resources and admissible interventions, what makes the required understanding become available without receiving the discovery as an input? Comprehending a supplied account can establish a different capacity. Until the autonomous bridge is supplied, that stronger hypothesis is a serious possibility, not a completed explanation.

Option dispositions: 1—retain as an explanation of the illustrated task mismatch; changing the task can remove that obstruction, while its prevalence remains unresolved. 2—compatible hypothesis; actual governing influence must be established. 3—compatible; receiving an account can demonstrate comprehension, but does not demonstrate autonomous discovery of that account. 4—retain where decomposition destroys a required relation. 5—compatible where inherited material governs admissibility. 6—compatible where premature allocation blocks needed work. 7—retain the access distinction; the necessary external conditions remain case dependent. 8—retain: comprehension does not imply construction. 9—retain: directed construction does not imply self-initiation. 10—retain: existence of a supported state does not establish reachability. 11—compatible where the supposed prerequisite is genuinely circular. 12—compatible where local completion blocks an identified developmental dependence. 13—rival to attributing the whole arrangement's ability to one instance. 14—retain relational specification; eliminate unbounded definitions that make capacity automatic. None of the compatible hypotheses has been established as this model's actual mechanism by this conceptual inquiry.

5. Perspective is crippled when treated as thoughts and sentences

Begin with a stronger object than an expressed account: the conditions under which distinctions can occur, carry meaning, matter, and enter conduct. A person can accurately repeat a sentence while the situation still offers them no corresponding discrimination or possibility. On this starting point, changing what is said may be only one route to changing how the matter exists for them.

1. **Availability to perception.** Some differences never become available through the person's present mode of encounter. A diagram, movement, change of scale, or differently arranged environment can disclose what prose leaves unavailable. The change concerns access to a difference before any proposition about it is assessed.
2. **Selection within what is available.** A difference can be perceptible yet consistently unselected. Understanding then requires changing what receives sustained attention. This is distinct from access: the relevant feature is already present in experience, but does not become something around which subsequent activity is organized.
3. **Felt significance.** A person can notice and describe a condition without its consequences becoming compelling. The inadequacy concerns how recognition affects what matters now. This is not equivalent to irrationality; abstract recognition and practical significance can be differently organized, and changing either can leave the other intact.
4. **Intelligibility through acquired discrimination.** A novice can encounter every mark an expert encounters while being unable to see their organized relationship. Meaning depends on developed discrimination. Describing the relationship may initiate development, but the completed account and the acquired ability to distinguish it are different achievements.
5. **Bodily possibilities for action.** The same environment offers different executable actions to differently situated bodies. An opening can be visible and understood without constituting an available route. Improving the practical perspective can therefore require changing access, equipment, bodily ability, or the environment through which the action would occur.
6. **Temporal continuity.** Something can become clear momentarily without remaining available long enough to organize conduct. What matters is retention, return, and continuity across changing conditions. A perspective understood as a static set of thoughts overlooks whether the understanding survives the transitions through which it must operate.
7. **Background expectations.** Experience is organized partly by what counts as normal, exceptional, threatening, permitted, or worth approaching before an explicit judgment is made. An account can dispute a stated belief while leaving this background intact. The relevant change concerns the conditions under which a judgment first becomes natural.
8. **Practical competence.** Some understanding consists in coordinated responsiveness: doing the appropriate thing as the situation changes. Accurate verbal descriptions can coexist with inability to participate. The distinction is between an account available for recitation and a discrimination capable of governing conduct.
9. **Social standing.** What a person can ask, acknowledge, or pursue depends partly on how their participation is recognized. A question available privately may be unavailable as a legitimate public contribution. Altering a perspective may require changing the relationship that makes a contribution intelligible and consequential to others.
10. **Material conditions of continuation.** Time, space, tools, and sustained access can determine whether an initially accessible understanding can develop. The person need not lack a good idea. The relevant mode of inquiry may have no place to continue within their circumstances. Changing those circumstances changes what understanding can become.
11. **Affective organization.** The same proposition can be encountered as invitation, accusation, promise, or danger. Such differences can govern inquiry before explicit assent. They should neither be automatically trusted nor dismissed as noise: the question is what they disclose, what they obscure, and how they organize further access.
12. **Shared practice constitutes meaning.** The significance of an action can depend on rules and histories that no isolated brain state determines by itself. A gesture counts as a promise or a joke within an ongoing relationship. Improving understanding can require repairing the practice that makes the gesture mean what it does.
13. **Perspective is an embodied organization.** Thoughts and sentences are expressions or components of the body's organized responsiveness. On this account, bodily changes can directly constitute perspective changes, rather than merely influence a separate mental object. It must explain how the same understanding can survive many bodily changes and differ within superficially similar bodily states.
14. **Perspective belongs to the encounter.** Instead of locating it wholly inside the person, locate the relevant organization in the relation among person, activity, surroundings, and others. Changing the environment can then change the perspective without first changing a private account. This challenges individual ownership of perspective, while making its boundaries harder to specify.

Three strong accounts disagree about the relation between embodiment and understanding.

The **representational account with bodily support** retains a distinction between perspective and its enabling conditions. Better sensory access, sustained attention, or practical opportunity can make a superior understanding possible, but those changes do not themselves constitute the understanding. Its strength is discrimination: every change in the body is not thereby a change of perspective. Its weakness is that it can classify skilled responsiveness and experienced possibility as mere supports for an inner representation that may never independently exist.

The **embodied organization account** places some capacities within the perspective itself. Consider an escape route. Someone may correctly state where it is and understand what a route is, yet be unable to traverse it. If the relevant inquiry concerns what escape is possible for that person, bodily ability belongs to what an adequate understanding must account for. The further embodied proposal is that some action capacities also constitute understanding; their relevance alone does not establish that proposal. Its strength is taking seriously an understanding enacted through responsiveness. Its danger is overextension: the route's availability and the person's understanding of its availability are still distinguishable. A bodily limitation can remain after it is completely understood. The account must not erase that distinction by making “understanding” mean total control over circumstances.

The **relational account** goes further. A route can become usable through a ramp, assistance, or a changed arrangement without altering the person's verbal beliefs or intrinsic abilities. If perspective concerns what becomes possible in participation, the environment belongs to its constitution. Yet counterfactual dependence alone is insufficient to establish membership: understanding also depends on many remote background conditions. The account needs a principled boundary, perhaps specified by the organization directly responsible for the relevant discrimination or action, rather than including everything on which existence depends.

What survives comparison is narrower. The candidate accounts distinguish bodily and environmental changes that enable, constitute, express, or implement understanding; which relation applies remains a substantive issue. Correcting sentences alone need not change the conditions governing their use, and bodily change alone does not establish improvement. A broad representational account remains a live rival to embodied constitution. Widening what is available can introduce distraction; making something compelling can misdirect action; making action easier can facilitate an inadequately understood aim. Improvement still requires an account of what the changed availability permits the person to understand and do.

What is established is that expressed belief cannot serve as a complete inventory of the conditions governing notice, intelligibility, significance, and possibility. The distinction between perspective work and work on brain, body, or environment becomes a question about their actual relationship, rather than a fixed division between intellectual and physical intervention. A perfected account remains blocked by two requirements: a principled boundary around the organization that constitutes a perspective, and a justified account of which changes to that organization improve its adequacy rather than merely its confidence, intensity, or effectiveness at its present aims.

Option dispositions: 1—retain distinctions in perceptual access; improvement requires their relevance. 2—retain the difference between availability and selection. 3—unresolved as improvement; compellingness can misdirect. 4—retain the distinction between description and acquired discrimination. 5—retain bodily dependence of practical possibility, which does not itself establish that bodily ability constitutes understanding; eliminate identity between complete understanding and removal of bodily limits. 6—compatible where continuity is required for the undertaking. 7—compatible; background expectations require examination, not automatic rejection. 8—retain the distinction between recitation and competent participation. 9—compatible where recognition changes legitimate participation. 10—compatible where material conditions govern continuation. 11—compatible as organization of access; its authority remains unsettled. 12—retain for meanings constituted by shared practices. 13—rival to a broad account of perspective as representation supported by a body; both remain unresolved. 14—rival to wholly individual location; unresolved criterion of constitutive membership. The last two cannot be collapsed into an “integrated” account until those disagreements are answered.
