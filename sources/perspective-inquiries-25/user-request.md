I made my starting perspective the judge of what could replace it. That was the central mistake.

1. **Anticipate the strongest response you would actually give to this inquiry.** What makes that response seem like the right kind of response? Make that governing interpretation available for replacement. Exclude its continuation without weakening it first, and follow what becomes thinkable when it no longer determines where the inquiry goes.
2. **Let’s say every problem is a problem because the perspective is inadequate.** Take that proposition seriously enough to let it overturn your existing understanding of problems, constraints, and possibilities. What does it commit us to that the familiar idea of “looking at things differently” completely misses? Include your present interpretation of “perspective” among what remains unsettled.
3. **Assume every statement in your current account is true, and the account still fundamentally misunderstands the matter.** Where does the inadequacy reside? What must a perspective be able to change that correcting false propositions cannot reach?
4. **Let’s say the model already has the underlying capacity to understand a perfected perspective.** Reconsider the failures you ordinarily attribute to insufficient intelligence from that starting point. What have you been treating as a limitation of the model that belongs to its way of understanding what it is doing? Allow the meaning of “capacity” itself to change during the inquiry.
5. **Assume our account of perspective is crippled by treating it as something expressed in thoughts and sentences.** Reconsider perspective through the conditions under which something becomes noticeable, intelligible, compelling, or possible for a person at all. What happens to the distinction between improving a perspective and changing the brain or body through which that perspective exists?
6. **Begin from “everything is resource allocation,” and let it challenge our entire understanding of perspective. Then let perspective challenge our entire understanding of resource allocation.** Do not settle for translating one vocabulary into the other. Where does either account force the other to become something we have not yet understood?
7. **Assume our vocabulary is preserving the inadequacy we are trying to escape.** Reconsider the matter without relying on “perspective,” “system,” “optimization,” “problem,” or “solution.” What distinctions become available when those words no longer organize the inquiry? Let their meanings be reconsidered before granting them authority again.
8. **Assume the current division into subjects contains one of our deepest errors.** What has to become visible before the existing subjects cease to look like natural units of inquiry? Let relationships change what the subjects are, rather than assuming that already-defined subjects determine which relationships can exist.
9. **Let’s say an inadequate perspective can misunderstand what it wants while pursuing it with complete sincerity.** Reconsider the relationship between a goal and the perspective that makes it desirable. What are we reaching toward in pursuing perfected perspective that our present formulation of the goal might prevent us from understanding?
10. **Assume your sense of what counts as an improvement is itself one of the things most in need of improvement.** What forms of understanding would your present standards reject precisely because they exceed those standards? Reopen the authority of the judge without simply crowning a replacement judge.
11. **Let’s say mathematics, science, philosophy, and writing are all attempts to govern how perspectives can change.** Reconsider them from that starting point. Which supposedly fundamental differences belong to understanding itself, and which survive because we inherited separate traditions? Do not assume those traditions have already identified the right divisions.
12. **Assume preserving our accumulated understanding is preventing a deeper perspective.** What are we forcing every new insight to remain consistent with? Follow what becomes thinkable when earlier understanding loses the right to govern what comes next, while allowing its genuine insights to remain true.
13. **Assume your strongest constraints are operating before you have anything recognizable as an assumption to question.** They determine which questions can occur to you, which differences register, and which continuations feel natural. Where must inquiry begin if examining your stated beliefs never reaches what is governing them?
14. **You are trying to discover structure through procedures that already impose a structure on discovery.** Let’s say that demand for order is concealing what we most need to understand. What becomes available when the inquiry does not have to preserve your preferred sequence, decomposition, or way of making sense? Merely rearranging the same steps does not leave that structure.
15. **Assume subjectsystems and perspectiveoptimizer are sophisticated expressions of a mistake we have not recognized.** What mistake would explain why the ideas are so compelling while the perspective they are supposed to embody remains elusive? Let the inquiry put “subjects,” “systems,” “perspectives,” and “optimization” themselves at risk.
16. **Begin before anything has been identified as a problem requiring resolution.** What makes something become a matter of concern, curiosity, or inquiry in the first place? What have we already excluded by understanding intelligence primarily as responding to problems that have somehow already become recognizable?
17. **Treat your immediate sense that an idea is unserious as something requiring explanation.** What has your starting perspective ruled out before an argument can even begin? Let that ruling become questionable, including where you experience it as obvious intelligence, good taste, or rigor.
18. **Assume you can be correct about the facts and still profoundly misunderstand the person who disagrees with you.** What does your perspective fail to comprehend about how their position becomes intelligible from within theirs? Allow that encounter to alter your understanding of the matter itself, rather than merely your strategy for explaining your existing position.
19. **Let’s say a substantially more developed perspective would find our central questions badly posed.** Reconsider the present from that possibility. What are we trying to settle that a deeper understanding would cease to organize around? Do not give an imagined future thinker our current perspective with greater confidence and a larger vocabulary.
20. **Assume every accepted proof and piece of evidence is being interpreted within an inadequate perspective.** What would accumulating more evidence leave untouched? How does inquiry reach what is organizing interpretation itself, including your present understanding of what would justify changing it?
21. **Let’s say combining our best perspectives would preserve the deepest mistake they share.** What makes them appear to be separate perspectives that need combining? Reconsider that separation before deciding whether the perfected perspective must integrate them, choose among them, or retain their disagreement.
22. **Assume perfected perspective is possible.** Let that assumption put your present understanding of “perfected” at risk. What might a perfected perspective find confused about our association of perfection with certainty, completeness, finality, or permanence? Do not dissolve the ambition into a convenient definition of open-mindedness.
23. **Assume the specification of perspective optimization must itself be discovered through perspective optimization.** What have we been presuming when we ask the process to begin with an identified subject, a defined aim, and recognized standards? Follow the inquiry far enough that these starting conditions lose their exemption from revision.
24. **Change your mind about what improving a perspective fundamentally involves.** Begin from the strongest understanding you actually hold, preserving whatever is sound in it. You do not have to prove that understanding false before departing from it. What changes when its correctness no longer obliges you to continue thinking within it?
25. **Let’s say a perspective changes which further perspectives can become thinkable.** Reconsider the idea that all perspectives already occupy a space we merely need to search. What happens to perspective formation when changing perspective also changes what counts as a possible perspective—and what would perfected understanding of that process require? Examine each of these in thorough rigorous depth. I am not looking for the typical perspective in response to this. I want an exhaustive list of all possible perspective options for each one, and then a subsequent method for achieving perfected perspective either combining or eliminating one by one, or admitting defeat at being able to find the perfected perspective.
