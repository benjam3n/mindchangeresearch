**What follows when the inquiries constrain one another**

The following conclusions are arguments about the options already at issue. They do not assume that all 25 questions share one hidden answer.

1. **A catalogue cannot obtain the authority its entries are denied merely by being a catalogue.** Inquiry 7 reopens the language that identifies candidates. Inquiry 8 reopens their units. Inquiry 23 reopens the specification used to construct them. Inquiry 25 reopens the continuations through which they become available. Therefore a catalogue whose language, units, specification, and generation rules remain fixed has not, simply by completing its enumeration, discharged those four inquiries. A coverage argument could still establish that its fixed representation captures every relevant transformation. No such argument is supplied by the number of generated entries. This rejects enumeration-as-certification; it does not reject enumeration or prove that a universal representation is impossible.

2. **Correctness of contents and adequacy of the activity are distinct assessments.** Inquiry 3 stipulates true contents and inadequate understanding. Inquiry 18 supplies a concrete way this can occur: one person treats a promise as a forecast of future conduct, while another treats it as an undertaking that changes what is owed. The forecast's factual claims need not become false for its adequacy to the encounter to fail. The missing distinction concerns what the utterance does between the people. This establishes a possible form of inadequacy that replacing false propositions does not reach. It does not establish that propositions are powerless to describe that inadequacy once identified.

3. **An account of changing the judge remains an account produced under conditions of admission.** Inquiry 10 reopens the evaluation standard. Inquiries 13 and 16 reopen what can first become noticeable or concerning. Suppose a proposed method accepts only objections already expressible as arguments. A learned discrimination that becomes expressible only after participation cannot enter that method on its initial terms. Changing the score given to admitted arguments leaves that exclusion intact. Thus revisable evaluation alone is insufficient for the broader ambition. This conditional countercase identifies a limitation of the method; establishing that a particular unavailable discrimination is valuable requires further grounds.

4. **The origin of a criterion and its current warrant must be separated.** A standard developed inside an earlier account is not disqualified merely by that origin. Otherwise no successor could use a sound inference, observation, or distinction inherited from its predecessor. Conversely, calling a standard external, objective, democratic, embodied, or emergent does not establish its warrant. The relevant question is what licenses its particular exercise of authority and whether that license extends to excluding a proposed continuation. This permits departure from the starting account without an impossible demand to reason from nowhere.

5. **Three senses of elimination cannot be substituted for one another.** A claim can be false; an account can be denied universal jurisdiction; a line of work can receive no further effort. An account may lose universal jurisdiction because its own true claims concern only one of several relevant relations. Discontinuing work does not prove falsity. Keeping an account available does not establish its adequacy. This distinction follows from inquiries 3, 10, 12, and 24 and prevents the requested one-by-one elimination from becoming indiscriminate deletion.

6. **Capacity claims require a specified bearer without making that bearer permanently fixed.** Inquiry 4 permits already-sufficient underlying model capacity. Inquiry 5 reopens the role of body and access. Inquiry 6 reopens resources and their availability. A model that can understand a relation after receiving an otherwise unavailable observation does not thereby show that it could have inferred that observation beforehand. Yet its initial failure also does not show that the relation exceeds its capacity to understand. The distinct claims are comprehension given access, independent discovery, selection, reliable enactment, and acquisition of access. A wider human–model–instrument arrangement changes the bearer of the capacity claim. Both the expansion and the remaining distinctions need to be stated.

7. **Understanding a perfected account and producing it are not equivalent capacities.** An agent may understand each step of a supplied construction without possessing a way to find that construction. It may also generate a construction without recognizing which of several constructions is correct. These possibilities do not contradict inquiry 4's assumed capacity to understand. They reject the inference from that capacity alone to guaranteed self-directed discovery. If “capacity” is instead stipulated to include discovery, access, enactment, and certification, that is a substantially stronger proposition whose consequences can be explored, but it cannot be smuggled into the initial one.

8. **The universal perspective proposition has several strengths that must stay separate.** “Every problem is a problem because the perspective is inadequate” can concern how a condition becomes a problem, why an attempt cannot resolve it, or whether adequate understanding can change every unwanted condition. These are rival readings, not equivalent formulations. Even if inadequate understanding contributes causally to every problem, it does not follow without another premise that changing understanding is a sufficient or available remedy. If adequacy means correctly recognizing an impossible demand, perfection need not make the impossible demand achievable. If it means removing every adverse condition, that stronger ambition remains a different claim. The inquiry must carry both far enough to expose their commitments rather than protect the first by retreating into the second's wording or vice versa.

9. **A changed goal can constitute either improved understanding or abandonment of the concern.** Inquiry 9 puts sincere wanting at risk. Inquiry 22 forbids replacing perfection with an easy ideal. These requirements together exclude a cheap escape: the process cannot declare success merely because it has made itself want whatever it can deliver. Suppose a required distinction is impossible for the current account to represent. Calling that distinction unimportant because it is unrepresentable changes the acceptance condition to fit the account. Before claiming that a changed goal fulfills the original ambition, distinguish fulfillment from replacement or abandonment. A successor need not prove the earlier concern misconceived in order to develop. Claiming warranted replacement requires grounds appropriate to that claim, which need not take the form of a prior verbal account. Those grounds also remain open to challenge. No context-free rule for every such revision has been established here.

10. **Changing the order can change the available operations, not only their outputs.** Inquiry 14 questions imposed sequences. Inquiry 25 questions a fixed space of alternatives. Consider a person who must acquire a notation before a certain distinction can be entertained. “Compare the interpretations, then learn the notation” is not the reverse ordering of two already available operations: the first comparison cannot yet be performed in the required sense. Conversely, an encounter can make the notation's relevance visible. This establishes a class of dependencies in which an operation changes later availability. It does not prove there is no representation capable of describing both orders from a wider standpoint.

11. **Relations can constitute the units being related.** Inquiries 6 and 8 jointly challenge the initial resource inventory and the initial subject inventory. A minute of undisturbed attention is not operationally interchangeable with a minute interrupted at a decisive moment; a written mark becomes a usable reminder only through a relation to later recognition and conduct. These examples identify failures of an inventory that treats countable items as already sufficient descriptions of their availability. Whether the deeper account should retain “resources,” “subjects,” or either term is unresolved. Simply changing a noun does not repair the missing relation.

12. **A combined account can preserve all the truths while preserving the wrong relation among them.** Inquiry 21 prevents integration from being the default. If one account describes a promise's predictive content and another its obligations, attaching both paragraphs does not specify when prediction bears on responsibility or whether it does. A combination earns explanatory value only by specifying an actual relationship, boundary, or transformation. If the accounts assert incompatible relations under identical conditions, preserving both as true is not a combination. Their contradiction remains to be addressed. This yields several possible outcomes—conditional coexistence, dependency, succession, or unresolved rivalry—without installing integration as the final judge.

13. **Newly reachable is not equivalent to newly existent.** Inquiry 25 establishes the relevance of changing what can be thought from a particular starting condition. It does not, by itself, settle whether possibilities are created in an unrestricted metaphysical sense. A distinction may have been expressible elsewhere but unavailable here; it may require new operations for practical use; or its identity may depend on a newly established relationship. These cases require different explanations. A universal fixed possibility space remains a rival account, but its mere assertion provides neither a usable generation procedure nor a proof of coverage.

14. **A method's correct self-description does not establish correct enactment.** Inquiry 3 allows every statement in an account to be true. Inquiry 15 asks why the projects remain compelling without embodying the intended perspective. A document can accurately say that its standards are revisable while never reopening one. It can preserve every known correction while arranging access so that readers repeat the corrected interpretation. It can contain a procedure for discovering subjects while its producer still requires subjects to be given. These are possible gaps between description and conduct. Establishing which currently occurs in the repositories requires fresh inspection; the conceptual criticism does not pretend that inspection occurred in this investigation.

15. **A purportedly deeper account can be worse precisely through what it calls depth.** The instruction to distrust familiar standards can become a criterion favoring unfamiliarity. The instruction to preserve alternatives can privilege indecision. The instruction to reopen foundations can prevent a correct short answer from being given. The instruction to avoid guess-and-check can be misread as forbidding all observations capable of overturning an interpretation. Each is a separate overextension. A critique of one unearned authority does not grant unlimited authority to its opposite. This does not justify retreating to the starting account; it requires assessing the new exercise of authority with its own actual consequences and rationale in view.

16. **Failure to certify perfection is a result with a precise scope.** Inquiry 22 permits perfection as a serious possibility. Inquiry 20 reopens evidential interpretation. Inquiry 23 reopens the specification. Even if a candidate is in fact perfect, an inadequate certification process can fail to recognize it. Conversely, a certificate can establish exactly what follows from its assumptions while failing to establish that those assumptions characterize the whole matter. Therefore neither missing certification nor successful local certification settles unrestricted perfection. This investigation supplies neither a universal coverage proof nor a universally sufficient transition procedure. Its failure to establish the requested perfected account must remain explicit.

The dependencies above are not a compulsory order of discovery. For example, 3 changes what 4 means by understanding; 4 changes the possible account of 25; 25 changes what 10 can demand of alternatives; 10 changes how 3 can judge inadequacy. Similarly, 5 changes 6's inventory, 6 changes 8's units, and 8 changes which relations 5 can treat as inside or outside a person. These are mutual dependencies. Turning them into a single fixed hierarchy would settle part of the inquiry before carrying it out.

The distinction between fixed parameters and context-dependent performance has an empirical precedent in Brown and colleagues' few-shot experiments: tasks were presented through text without gradient updates, with both successes and remaining limitations reported. This supports a restricted distinction between available parameters and elicited performance; it does not establish that a present model possesses the capacity to discover or understand every perfected account. Clark and Chalmers argue that some cognitive processes include active coupling with external objects. That is a philosophical source for treating the boundary of a cognitive bearer as contestable, not settled proof that every bodily or environmental influence is part of a perspective. The present investigation retains causal influence and constitutive participation as distinct candidates. [The Extended Mind](https://consc.net/papers/extended.html).
