# Replacing the judge without pretending to leave judgment behind

An account can lose the right to govern the inquiry without any of its true statements becoming false. Its claim to authority usually contains an additional, often unstated claim: the distinctions it recognizes include everything that could matter to the present inquiry. The original mistake is granting that additional claim for free. A replacement need not contradict the account's contents. It can expose the insufficiency of the conditions under which those contents were treated as decisive.

This also identifies a danger in the requested exhaustive list. If we first decide what a perspective is, enumerate its permitted variations, and then select among them, the account used to construct the list has already decided what can compete. Increasing the list from twenty entries to twenty thousand does not remove that authority. The list can be useful, but its coverage has to become one of the matters under examination.

The following is a proposed method, with conditional logical claims rather than a claim that the method has already attained perfected understanding.

## What must remain available for replacement

Distinguish an account's statements from the conditions under which it produces and judges them. Those conditions include what can be distinguished, what counts as the same thing across changes, which occurrences receive attention, what makes an inquiry worth undertaking, which transformations are available, and what counts as a reason to stop. Brain, body, instruments, social arrangements, and available time can participate in these conditions; they cannot automatically be relegated to external implementation.

This description is itself revisable. Its use is to identify places where an apparent replacement merely varies content while preserving governing commitments. It is not offered as a complete ontology.

There are three different elimination acts. We can reject a factual assertion, withdraw an account's claim to universal authority, or discontinue spending resources on it. None entails the others. An inadequate account may contain a valid operation. An uninterpretable possibility may deserve no immediate work without thereby having been disproved. An account receiving extensive work may still lack adequate grounds for acceptance.

## Operations with inspectable consequences

1. **Expose one actual act of exclusion.** Take a candidate continuation that the strongest current account rejects. Write the rejection, the exact reason offered, and what makes that reason decisive. Do not replace the strongest account with an obviously defective caricature. If the reason is “it does not answer the question,” expose what determines the identity of the question. If it is “there is no evidence,” expose which occurrences would be admissible evidence and why. If it is “unintelligible,” distinguish inability to express a claim from a demonstrated inconsistency in the claim.

2. **Turn unconditional authority into its actual conditional warrant.** Rewrite “X is irrelevant” as “X is irrelevant if these features exhaust what can matter here.” Rewrite “this is the best continuation” as “this continuation follows if this aim, these available moves, and this conception of success retain authority.” Preserve every sound argument in the original account. Remove only the unearned promotion from a justified conditional to an exhaustive jurisdiction. Where an unconditional conclusion really has been established, preserve it and expose the assumptions and scope of that establishment.

3. **Depart at the dependency, not at the conclusion.** Hold the account's true contents fixed and alter one condition that made them decisive: its units, available interventions, question identity, temporal horizon, embodiment, or grounds for caring. Carry the altered condition far enough to change something determinate. A renamed conclusion is not a departure. A departure has occurred when an earlier distinction splits, an excluded occurrence becomes relevant, an old comparison loses its subject, or a formerly unavailable action becomes intelligible. This operation licenses exploration before the new account can satisfy every demand of the old judge; it does not license accepting unsupported claims.

4. **Require each account to state the other's strongest reason in terms it can recognize.** Record separately where translation succeeds, changes meaning, or cannot presently be performed. Failed translation is a result about the encounter, not a verdict of inferiority. At the same time, an account that permanently exempts itself from every possible consequence of being wrong has supplied no basis for an unconditional assertion of correctness. Exploration can proceed without a completed translation; authority cannot be obtained merely by declaring translation impossible.

5. **Locate a consequential disagreement.** Identify what the accounts disagree about in a common situation, or explain why describing a common situation already begs their dispute. Consequences include what becomes noticeable, which question exists, what can be done, and who or what bears the consequences. They need not be a numerical performance measure. If two accounts have no identified difference in any of these respects, suspend the claim that they constitute distinct alternatives. If their differences require learning a skill, changing an instrument, or entering an unfamiliar setting, record that dependency instead of substituting verbal evaluation for the encounter.

6. **Apply reasons to commitments, not identities.** A counterexample can refute a universal assertion while leaving a restricted assertion intact. A demonstrated impossibility can eliminate a proposed route under its stated conditions. An unresolved value conflict cannot honestly be converted into a factual refutation. A superior account need not preserve the incumbent vocabulary, but it must address the strongest demonstrated consequences that made the incumbent credible, or identify exactly why their importance or interpretation has changed. These requirements apply reciprocally. Reciprocity means the same kind of commitment incurs the same burden; it does not mean equal credibility regardless of evidence.

7. **Compose only where the composition changes understanding lawfully.** Before combining two accounts, identify whether they describe different aspects of one occurrence, operate under different conditions, or make contradictory claims about the same thing. In the first case, specify the dependency between aspects. In the second, specify how the conditions are distinguished and what happens at their boundary. In the third, retain the contradiction as unresolved until a commitment is withdrawn or revised. “Both are valuable” does not constitute an account of their relation. A synthesis that hides a contradiction has reduced understanding.

8. **Reopen the authority used in the preceding acts.** Select an actual decision made during operations 1–7 and apply those operations to its warrant. For example, consequential disagreement may have privileged already recognized consequences; translation may have preserved the incumbent units; preserving demonstrated successes may have protected an aim that deserved abandonment. Change the operation where the objection shows a specific loss. If no such change is available, retain its provisional use and name the unresolved limitation. Self-application must change a decision when warranted; merely appending “this too is revisable” changes nothing.

These are operations because each changes a definite object and leaves an inspectable consequence. They do not prescribe a mandatory universal sequence. An unfamiliar encounter may precede an identifiable question; a bodily change may precede the ability to state its significance; a contradiction may force a return to an earlier individuation. No fixed ordering has earned priority across all such cases.

Nor is this a promise that a procedure can manufacture justified ultimate standards from no commitments whatsoever. Using a reason gives it provisional authority. It does not give it immunity. A reason's having originated within an earlier account does not establish that its warrant depends on that account's completeness. Conversely, describing a reason as external does not make its interpretation independent of every standpoint.

## A worked change of judge

Consider a stipulated setting: a table holds a usable pair of scissors. It has two metal halves joined by one removable screw. Ask, “How many objects are on the table?” Four accounts propose:

- **A:** Count connected aggregates. There is one.
- **B:** Count the three stipulated detachable components. There are three.
- **C:** Count usable cutting implements. There is one.
- **D:** The question leaves the rule of individuation unspecified; give a count only after identifying that rule and the transformations under which it is meant to remain applicable.

The initial judge values producing the ordinary unqualified answer. It prefers A or C, rejects B as overcomplication, and rejects D as evasive. That preference can be sensible in an ordinary conversational setting. The unearned move is treating its conversational adequacy as proof that it identifies the uniquely correct division of reality.

Expose the warrant: “one” is decisive if conversational identification of the assembled scissors exhausts the relevant task. Then introduce three explicit requests without changing any fact about the initial arrangement: pack every detachable component separately; count cutting implements available for a task; trace each connected aggregate. These are stipulated inquiries, not observations claimed about an actual workshop.

A cannot direct the packing request simply by repeating “one.” B cannot direct the implement count simply by repeating “three.” A and C coincide initially, but attaching the scissors to a cup by a string separates connectedness from functional individuation. Breaking the scissors so they cannot cut separates the presence of components from the presence of a usable cutting implement. The additional situations supply logical witnesses that the three counting rules are distinct. They do not establish that everyday speakers were mistaken when they said “one.”

The original judge now faces an internal limitation. Its own reason for preferring ordinary answers was that they identify what the interlocutor is asking about. Once multiple interlocutors' requests are explicit, insisting on one unqualified count defeats that reason. This permits withdrawal of its unrestricted authority without assuming D correct in advance.

D earns a limited advantage only after becoming operational. It must recover A's answer for aggregate tracing, B's for component packing, and C's for implement availability, and state what transformations change each count. A vague declaration that “everything depends on context” accomplishes none of this. A working D distinguishes the relevant equivalence rules, their conditions, and their consequences.

The changed standard is thus warranted by an exposed dependency and traceable failures. It asks whether the account distinguishes the inquiries that the unqualified question concealed. It was not selected by assigning D the largest invented score. A, B, and C lose universal authority while retaining their correct operations. D has not proved every future inquiry must use precisely these three units. The table could also matter as material to recycle, historical evidence, or something with personal significance. Its apparent success cannot close that frontier.

There is an additional revision available. Perhaps the person asking was not requesting a count at all but testing whether the listener would notice ambiguity. Then even an elaborate inventory of counting rules can misunderstand the encounter. D's superiority remains conditional on counting being the relevant activity. Preserving that condition explicitly is part of the achievement; silently universalizing it would reproduce the original mistake.

## Exhaustiveness and changing what is possible

An exhaustive finite list requires a defined domain and a demonstrated coverage rule. A potentially unbounded class need not be examined entry by entry: a valid structural argument can cover infinitely many instances. The difficulty here is therefore not simply “there are too many perspectives.” It is that the admissible units, transformations, expressions, and purposes are themselves under revision.

A list of sentences may omit a learned discrimination that cannot yet be stated. A list of mental operations may omit an instrument that makes a difference detectable. A list of feasible transitions may omit an initially useless transition that changes which later transitions exist. A list of individual capacities may omit a relation between people in which the relevant understanding first becomes possible. These are distinct coverage failures, not merely additional entries in a fixed catalog.

A proposed generator is exhaustive only relative to a claim about what its rules can generate and why that includes every relevant alternative. Calling its outputs “all possible perspectives” does not establish that claim. A broader metalanguage may expose previous exclusions, but breadth alone does not prove the metalanguage exhaustive. Today's honest output can provide explicit families, transformations, exclusions, and unsolved coverage obligations. It cannot certify an unrestricted enumeration from the material presently available.

## Elimination, unequal evidence, and the cost of keeping everything

Treating unfamiliarity as disproof makes present intelligibility a tyrant. Treating non-elimination as obligatory forever makes possibility a different tyrant. With finite resources, retaining every imaginable alternative at full intensity prevents inquiry from proceeding.

Separate the question “is this false?” from “does this receive the next allocation?” Discontinuing work can be warranted by a lack of a presently articulable distinction, prohibitive access costs, or another live inquiry having a clear dependency on the next available action. Record the reason and a reopening condition. This does not prove the neglected alternative inferior. The allocation rule is itself a candidate for revision, especially if it repeatedly excludes the very changes required to make unfamiliar accounts intelligible.

Symmetry matters for standards of criticism; asymmetry matters for what has actually been established. Two accounts should not receive different treatment for the same evidential defect merely because one is familiar. But an account with demonstrated consequences is not epistemically equal to an unsupported assertion just because both are interpreted through fallible understanding. Interpretation dependence does not erase the difference between encountering a constraint and imagining that it does not exist. The account proposing a reinterpretation must specify what changes in the explanation of that encounter.

## What claiming perfection would require

At least the following obligations remain. They are proposed necessary obligations, not a proved complete definition of perfection.

- **Referent:** Establish what the claim of perfection concerns, including how that matter retains or changes identity when its formulation changes.
- **Substantive adequacy:** Establish that the account neither misstates nor omits a distinction that could change warranted understanding within that scope. Mere internal consistency cannot establish this.
- **Authority:** Justify the standards under which inadequacy and improvement are distinguished, including unresolved conflicts among aims and why excluded standards lack a rightful claim here.
- **Coverage:** Establish that the account covers relevant changes of representation, individuation, aims, evaluation, embodiment, environment, and transition order, or prove why particular classes cannot matter. Listing these categories does not discharge coverage.
- **Reachability:** Establish that the required changes can actually occur under the claimed conditions. Understanding a description of a transition and being able to make it are different claims.
- **Self-application:** Show that the specification, coverage argument, and grounds for certification do not receive exemptions denied to the accounts they judge.
- **Certification:** Supply an argument whose assumptions and inferential rules support the claimed scope, rather than infer universal perfection from the absence of a presently detected defect.

A locally perfected account and a universally perfected account are different claims. An account may also be adequate without anyone possessing a certificate of that adequacy. Failure to certify does not prove perfection impossible. Conversely, attaining a certificate inside a deliberately limited formal setting does not certify the adequacy of that setting for every inquiry. None of these distinctions requires a sweeping appeal to incompleteness or no-free-lunch theorems.

Today this method can establish particular refutations, justified withdrawals of authority, conditional dominance between articulated accounts, and verified coverage within explicitly bounded settings. It can expose missing warrants. It cannot honestly establish unrestricted perfection, and it has not done so here. If universal certification is the acceptance condition, this attempt must report failure to meet it.

## The strongest objection to this method

The method privileges articulate distinctions, accountable reasons, consequential encounters, and revisability. A major transformation may initially provide none of these. The proposed procedure could therefore exclude precisely what it claims to keep available, while describing its exclusion more carefully.

The objection succeeds against any claim that this is a sufficient universal procedure. Permitting bounded immersion, apprenticeship, unfamiliar activity, or altered conditions before justification is available addresses some instances. It cannot guarantee access to every missing transformation, and calling for such access cannot create the capacity or circumstances it requires. Even the judgment that an encounter deserves time is made from an existing condition.

The resulting conclusion is stronger than “remain open-minded”: no candidate account acquires complete jurisdiction merely by being the strongest available account; no preserved truth compels continuation inside the organization that first expressed it; and no procedure earns universal sufficiency by promising to revise itself. The concrete gain is the ability to locate, withdraw, and replace specific exercises of unearned authority while retaining their sound contents. The unresolved claim is whether that capability can become complete across changes it cannot yet characterize. That unresolved claim must remain visible rather than being renamed perfection.

## Amendment: preservation and demonstrable difference cannot be admission requirements

Two requirements in the proposed method risk reinstalling the original judge. They require substantive amendment, not merely a reminder that the method is revisable.

**First challenge: requiring a successor to address the strongest demonstrated consequences can make inherited understanding compulsory.** The danger lies in “address.” If every departure must reconstruct all the achievements of its predecessor, the predecessor continues to determine what work must be done. A successor can then depart only after paying an indefinitely expanding intellectual debt. A brilliant but irrelevant inherited taxonomy could indefinitely prevent inquiry into what it concealed.

Consider a stipulated maze whose walls are low enough for its visitor to step over. An exact account of every corridor, junction, and corridor-constrained route remains true. Understanding that leaving the maze does not require traversing its corridors need not produce a translation of its entire route taxonomy, preserve its distinctions as useful, or explain its finest results. Demanding those tasks would reinstate corridor navigation as the governing activity. The successor only incurs the physical commitments it actually uses: that the walls can be crossed, that crossing them is permitted in the relevant circumstances, and that its intended destination is accessible. Its ability to ignore the route taxonomy is part of the conceptual change.

The amended operation is: **identify which earlier commitments the successor actually invokes, denies, or renders inapplicable; do not require reconstruction of what it neither needs nor contradicts.** For a denied commitment, specify the denial and its warrant. For a claimed inapplicability, identify the changed relation where possible. For a commitment simply left behind, do not invent a debt to rehabilitate it. An accurate statement may remain accurate without receiving further attention.

Even “identify the changed relation” must not be a prerequisite for beginning a departure. Recognition can follow the encounter that makes it possible. Where the successor eventually claims to settle the earlier inquiry, that claim creates a particular obligation to show what was settled or why the inquiry has lost its object. If it makes no such claim, the old inquiry can remain unfinished. Abandoning an inquiry is not equivalent to solving it, but solving every inherited inquiry is not a prerequisite for understanding something more important.

What survives is narrower than mandatory preservation: do not claim to have refuted what you have merely ceased to use, and do not claim a scope of adequacy that you have not established. These constraints concern the claims being made. They do not confer governing authority on the old account's inventory of accomplishments. Whether the new activity is more important may itself remain contested; this amendment does not manufacture a neutral answer.

**Second challenge: the absence of an identified consequential difference does not warrant treating two accounts as indistinct.** My earlier instruction to “suspend the claim that they constitute distinct alternatives” was one-sided. It could withhold standing from precisely the unfamiliar condition needed for a difference to become accessible. A nascent bodily discrimination can exist before a person can state what it discriminates. A different form of participation can change what later becomes intelligible even when its present description repeats familiar words. These are possibilities the method must allow, not established explanations of every unarticulated feeling.

The amended operation is: **when no difference can presently be identified, leave both sameness and distinctness undecided. Separate absence of a difference, inability to express a difference, inability to detect it under present conditions, and the possibility that a difference has not yet come into existence.** Do not collapse these states into synonymy. Where an actual encounter is available, permit participation to precede an account of its significance. A proposed change can also concern the conditions under which later distinctions develop, rather than an already formed alternative waiting to be identified.

This amendment changes what inquiry can admit. It does not establish that every apparently identical formulation conceals a profound alternative. Nor does it establish that performing a proposed exercise will reveal anything. An uncharacterized possibility receives permission to remain uncharacterized; it receives no automatic claim to truth, superiority, or indefinite attention.

**The nonsense-immunity case exposes the limit.** Suppose someone proposes Q: “Carrying this otherwise unremarkable blank card already constitutes perfected understanding of every inquiry. All apparent failures show that your standards are inadequate. Because its significance is beyond your present distinctions, you must abandon all rival accounts and devote unlimited attention to Q.”

The two amendments allow us to consider whether the card or the associated activity could eventually matter. They do not support Q's claims of attained universal perfection, exclusive authority, or unlimited entitlement. The possibility of an inaccessible significance does not entail any of those conclusions. Refusing them does not require proving that no conceivable encounter with the card could ever matter. If the amendments were interpreted as requiring permanent protection of those asserted entitlements, they would immunize Q and every incompatible proposal of the same form. Their own rationale supplies no grounds for that asymmetry.

Q could retreat to “I do not yet know whether this does anything.” That retreat legitimately restores an unresolved possibility while withdrawing the extravagant claims. The inquiry then faces an allocation decision, not a hidden proof of greatness or a proof of worthlessness. No amendment here supplies a universally reliable rule for distinguishing, in advance, every transformative encounter from every fruitless one. A requirement to do so before departure would reinstate the forbidden entry gate; pretending no allocation is needed would conceal a different gate.

The original method therefore survives only as a candidate collection of accountable operations. Preservation applies to the truth status of commitments we explicitly assess, not to an obligation to continue their inquiries. Consequential distinction can support a comparison once available, not determine in advance what may become available. What remains unresolved is the allocation and authority of inquiry while neither the relevant distinction nor its significance can yet be characterized. Calling that interval “exploration” describes it; it does not solve it.
