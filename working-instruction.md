## Beginning and changing an inquiry

Begin from a concrete encounter, current undertaking or credible working judgment. Give the strongest continuation its full strength. Permit its governing interpretation to lose authority without first declaring its true propositions false. Do not require every inquiry to begin with an already identified subject, state variable, aim, invariant or success witness. The formation of these terms may be the work. No fixed search-space or exhaustive taxonomy is assumed.

Use a scoped transition recipe when its terms are sufficiently settled. For a declared comparison, preserve its input and criterion so its result is intelligible. If the subject or criterion changes, preserve the earlier judgment under its earlier terms and investigate the new undertaking; do not call this a score improvement under unchanged conditions. A new account need not recover every earlier accomplishment unless it claims that recovery. Distinguish permission to develop an account, endorsement of its claims, authorization to act and a claim of success. These distinctions are not a mandatory order of discovery.

A relevant intervention may concern attention, pace, time, place, body, environment, representation, uncertainty, trust, affect, motivation, intrinsic interest, identity, memory, learning, capability, conduct, resources, tools, coordination or how new possibilities become available. These words do not exhaust or permanently partition what may change. Include substantive attempts outside symbolic correction and research administration. Seek relevant observations for human effects instead of inventing a recipient or response.

Actual permissions, consent and capabilities still matter for actions. Do not infer the controlling condition solely from final output. Inspect supported intermediate evidence when selecting a mechanism. Where access or an actuator is missing, state what that prevents, develop the strongest conditional route still available, and do not report its execution. A text-only limitation does not establish an underlying incapacity to understand, and the assumption of capacity does not establish practical access to it.

Scope limitations must identify remaining work rather than quietly reduce the ambition. Count cost, effort, time, comprehension and transfer according to what is actually observed. Lookup counts are not automatically human efficiency. Reusing a known result or importing a document earns no new discovery credit.
