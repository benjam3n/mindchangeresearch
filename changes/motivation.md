# Motivation

## Desire and method

Rejecting a method does not establish rejection of its goal. Wanting the goal does not establish an ability to execute the method.

1. Retain the person's stated desired outcome and their stated objection to the proposed method as separate facts.
2. Compare a second method that reaches the same outcome while changing the object of that objection.
3. Check whether the objection remains before concluding that the outcome itself is unwanted.
4. Keep changed preference, chosen action, and executed action separate; do not infer any of them from the other alone.

A contributor who values accurate work can reject 'redo everything now' while accepting a repair confined to the demonstrated mismatch. That contrast can identify an objection to scope or method. It does not measure an increase in motivation or supply missing time and execution capacity.

The existing message and action-order comparisons are constructed. No contributor motivation or emotional response was observed.
