# Identity and self-understanding

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-436"></a>
## 436. Self-description

changing the traits or capacities attributed to oneself.

**Candidate contributors:** Evidence from one's actions; feedback from relevant others; new roles or capacities; comparison across contexts; revised interpretation of prior events.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A single episode may change self-description without establishing an enduring trait.

<a id="target-437"></a>
## 437. Role identity

taking up, revising, or leaving an understood role.

**Candidate contributors:** Taking on or leaving responsibilities; social recognition; repeated enactment; changed expectations; a new account of what the role requires.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A title alone need not alter operative identity or actual responsibility.

<a id="target-438"></a>
## 438. Group identification

changing which groups are included in one's self-understanding.

**Candidate contributors:** Shared activity and commitments; acceptance or exclusion; perceived similarity; group narratives; changed relations to other affiliations.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Identification can vary by situation and need not imply agreement with every group norm.

<a id="target-439"></a>
## 439. Self-worth

changing one's evaluation of oneself.

**Candidate contributors:** Experienced acceptance or rejection; self-evaluation standards; interpretation of success and failure; separation of specific performance from global worth.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Praise can be unconvincing when it conflicts with experience or rests on an unwanted evaluative standard.

<a id="target-440"></a>
## 440. Possible self

becoming able to imagine oneself living or acting differently.

**Candidate contributors:** A concrete alternative life or action; a comparable example; an attainable path; opportunities to enact a previously untried role.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Imagining a possible self does not establish desire, feasibility, or continuity of commitment.

<a id="target-441"></a>
## 441. Self-continuity

changing the perceived connection among past, present, and future self.

**Candidate contributors:** Recognizing continuing concerns or relationships; linking past choices with current circumstances; representing later consequences as one's own.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Continuity need not mean preserving every old commitment or denying substantial change.

<a id="target-442"></a>
## 442. Self-boundary

changing what is experienced as belonging to oneself.

**Candidate contributors:** Changes in bodily ownership cues; personal responsibility and possession judgments; close identification with roles, relationships, or tools.

**Conditions and distinctions:** These are different senses of boundary; changes in one do not establish changes in another.

<a id="target-443"></a>
## 443. Authenticity

changing the relation between experienced and presented identity.

**Candidate contributors:** Recognizing a mismatch between private commitments and public conduct; room for candid expression; altering a role or presentation.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Feeling authentic does not establish that one's self-interpretation is accurate or that every impulse should be expressed.

<a id="target-444"></a>
## 444. Identity flexibility

becoming more or less able to revise a self-description.

**Candidate contributors:** Evidence that a self-description is context-dependent; examples of change; reduced penalties for revising a label; a stable concern surviving the revision.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Flexibility can coexist with stable commitments; instability is not inherently an improvement.

<a id="target-445"></a>
## 445. Label attachment

changing how strongly a label constrains self-interpretation.

**Candidate contributors:** Repeated use of a label; social reinforcement; interpreting new events through it; contrasting cases outside its predictions.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Removing a label can free interpretation or remove useful recognition; retain consequential distinctions.
