# Communication and social participation

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-507"></a>
## 507. Listening readiness

becoming more or less willing to receive another's contribution.

**Candidate contributors:** Expectation of a relevant contribution; room to attend; evidence the speaker understands the live matter; reduced repetition or adversarial pressure.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Listening readiness can fall when the recipient must repeatedly correct the same ignored instruction.

<a id="target-508"></a>
## 508. Speaking readiness

becoming more or less willing to contribute.

**Candidate contributors:** An actual opening to contribute; a formed thought or usable phrase; appropriate audience attention; credible protection from retaliation.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Permission to speak is ineffective if the turn structure or power relation prevents uptake.

<a id="target-509"></a>
## 509. Questioning

becoming able or willing to ask what was previously unasked.

**Candidate contributors:** A recognized gap; words that identify it; an accessible recipient with relevant knowledge; permission to reveal uncertainty.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Asking can burden the recipient when the answer is already available in context.

<a id="target-510"></a>
## 510. Disagreement

becoming able to express a conflicting judgment.

**Candidate contributors:** A specific conflicting claim; grounds and examples; an expression preserving the actual disagreement; space for a consequential response.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Politeness that hides the conflict can prevent resolution; harshness alone contributes no additional grounds.

<a id="target-511"></a>
## 511. Disclosure

deciding to reveal previously withheld material.

**Candidate contributors:** A chosen purpose for sharing; relevance of the information; trust and control over access; understanding likely consequences.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Disclosure changes another's information; it need not produce understanding or support.

<a id="target-512"></a>
## 512. Clarification

recognizing and repairing an ambiguity.

**Candidate contributors:** A discovered interpretation mismatch; a separating example; corrected reference or qualification; checking the repaired meaning.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Repeating the same ambiguous wording does not resolve the ambiguity [S18](../evidence.md#s18).

<a id="target-513"></a>
## 513. Apology

accepting and expressing responsibility for a wrong.

**Candidate contributors:** Recognition of the specific wrong; accurate responsibility attribution; acknowledgment of consequences; a credible repair commitment.

**Conditions and distinctions:** An apology does not substitute for the repair it promises or guarantee forgiveness.

<a id="target-514"></a>
## 514. Negotiation

becoming willing or able to construct a mutually acceptable arrangement.

**Candidate contributors:** Explicit interests and constraints; alternatives to the proposed arrangement; trade-offs with different values for each party; conditional commitments.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Agreement is not necessarily voluntary or beneficial to all parties; power and available alternatives matter.

<a id="target-515"></a>
## 515. Help seeking

becoming able or willing to request assistance.

**Candidate contributors:** Identification of the missing contribution; knowledge of who can supply it; a specific request; acceptable social and practical cost.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Asking for broad rescue can hide a small resolvable dependency; shame or prior refusal may obstruct access.

<a id="target-516"></a>
## 516. Conversational repair

changing how an interaction proceeds after misunderstanding or conflict.

**Candidate contributors:** Locating the failed interpretation or interaction move; accepting a valid correction; changing the next contribution; restoring shared task context.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Explaining why the misunderstanding happened is useful only if it helps repair the ongoing interaction.
