# Agency and efficacy

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-320"></a>
## 320. Agency experience

changing the sense of originating or directing an action.

**Candidate contributors:** Correspondence between intention, action, and observed consequence; a meaningful choice; feedback identifying one's contribution; control over execution.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Felt authorship and actual causal influence can diverge.

<a id="target-321"></a>
## 321. Perceived control

changing what one experiences as influenceable.

**Candidate contributors:** An action whose effects are visible; information about genuine limits; reliable access to tools, resources, or authority; removal of arbitrary constraints.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Control over one variable does not imply control over the whole outcome.

<a id="target-322"></a>
## 322. Self-efficacy

changing confidence in performing a particular activity.

**Candidate contributors:** Successful performance of the relevant operation; observing a comparable capable actor; specific corrective information; an attainable next challenge.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Mastery-based and vicarious interventions differed in a particular fear-related study [S19](../evidence.md#s19); generic praise does not establish capability.

<a id="target-323"></a>
## 323. Helplessness

changing the expectation or experience that one's action makes no difference.

**Candidate contributors:** Repeated action-outcome independence; obstructed access to effective responses; discovery and use of an action that makes a consequential difference.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Being told one has control is insufficient when the actual environment remains uncontrollable.

<a id="target-324"></a>
## 324. Authorship

changing ownership of a choice or contribution.

**Candidate contributors:** Making one's contribution identifiable; real discretion over a choice; acknowledgment of who created which part; separating assistance from substitution.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Receiving credit and experiencing ownership need not correspond to actual authorship.

<a id="target-325"></a>
## 325. Initiation authority

becoming more or less willing to act without external prompting.

**Candidate contributors:** Clear authority; an unambiguous starting condition; scope for independent choice; prior experience acting without punishment or unnecessary permission.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Some actions require genuine coordination or authorization; initiative must fit that scope.

<a id="target-326"></a>
## 326. Responsibility acceptance

taking up or relinquishing responsibility appropriately or inappropriately.

**Candidate contributors:** A clear duty or voluntarily adopted commitment; understanding consequences; adequate control and resources; correction of misplaced blame.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Taking responsibility for an uncontrollable outcome can reduce rather than increase effective agency.

<a id="target-327"></a>
## 327. Choice recognition

becoming aware of alternatives where none seemed available.

**Candidate contributors:** Exposing overlooked feasible actions; removing a false necessity; distinguishing means from ends; identifying a reversible intermediate step.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A nominal option is not usable if material, social, or cognitive conditions prohibit it.

<a id="target-328"></a>
## 328. Dependency recognition

identifying which assistance is necessary and which has become habitual.

**Candidate contributors:** Tracing which operation assistance actually supplies; attempting independent components; distinguishing missing capability from familiar delegation.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Independence is not always beneficial; necessary support can be part of a capable system.
