# Belief and knowledge

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-155"></a>
## 155. Acquisition

coming to accept a previously unaccepted proposition.

**Candidate contributors:** Direct observation; credible testimony; a valid derivation; an interpretation that organizes evidence; repetition or social influence that increases acceptance.

**Conditions and distinctions:** These routes change acceptance differently; acceptance through familiarity need not add justification.

<a id="target-156"></a>
## 156. Rejection

ceasing to accept a proposition.

**Candidate contributors:** A decisive counterexample; invalidated support; a stronger incompatible account; recognition that prior acceptance exceeded the grounds.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Loss of one argument need not establish the proposition's falsity.

<a id="target-157"></a>
## 157. Suspension

withholding judgment where a commitment was previously made.

**Candidate contributors:** Balanced unresolved alternatives; discovery of unreliable evidence; inability to discriminate at the needed scope; explicit separation of assumption from conclusion.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Suspension should preserve what remains established rather than erase all distinctions.

<a id="target-158"></a>
## 158. Correction

replacing a mistaken proposition with a better-supported one.

**Candidate contributors:** Identification of the exact false claim; reliable replacement information; explanation of the error-producing step; revision of dependent conclusions.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A correction that leaves the original causal account intact may fail to change its later use.

<a id="target-159"></a>
## 159. Elaboration

adding qualified detail to an existing account.

**Candidate contributors:** Newly warranted conditions, mechanisms, cases, or exceptions; integration with compatible existing knowledge.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Adding plausible detail without evidence can make an account less accurate while sounding more complete.

<a id="target-160"></a>
## 160. Scope restriction

narrowing where a belief is taken to hold.

**Candidate contributors:** Counterexamples outside a narrower domain; an identified precondition; evidence that a result depends on population, setting, or task.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Restriction can preserve a useful local claim; it should not conceal an independently false part.

<a id="target-161"></a>
## 161. Scope extension

extending a claim where support warrants it.

**Candidate contributors:** Evidence from additional cases; an invariance argument; shared operative conditions; justified transfer across settings.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Analogy or success in one new case alone may leave broader generalization unresolved.

<a id="target-162"></a>
## 162. Inconsistency recognition

noticing incompatible commitments.

**Candidate contributors:** Making commitments jointly explicit; aligning definitions, times, and scopes; deriving their incompatible consequences.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Apparently conflicting sentences may concern different conditions; establish the actual contradiction.

<a id="target-163"></a>
## 163. Justification

gaining or losing grounds for a belief.

**Candidate contributors:** New or withdrawn evidence; source reliability information; a valid inference; exposure of circularity or an unsupported premise.

**Conditions and distinctions:** More reasons are not necessarily more independent support.

<a id="target-164"></a>
## 164. Knowledge integration

making a known fact relevant to other commitments.

**Candidate contributors:** Tracing implications into another judgment; connecting a fact to an active decision; revising dependent expectations or actions.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A fact can be verbally known while remaining absent from the operation it should govern.
