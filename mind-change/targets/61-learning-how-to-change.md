# Learning how to change

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-572"></a>
## 572. Change-target selection

improving or worsening the choice of what needs to change.

**Candidate contributors:** A precise account of what the next activity lacks; the recipient's actual context; a beneficiality criterion; comparison with other consequential targets.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A target can be easy to influence but irrelevant to what would help now.

<a id="target-573"></a>
## 573. Intervention selection

changing how an appropriate means of change is chosen.

**Candidate contributors:** A target-specific contributor relationship; matching its preconditions to the current case; an available operation able to supply that contributor.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Calling an intervention relevant does not establish its effect; directness is relative to the specified relation and scope.

<a id="target-574"></a>
## 574. Intervention construction

becoming able to create a previously unavailable means.

**Candidate contributors:** Decomposing the required transition; constructing missing inputs or operations; composing compatible methods; making intermediate results available to their consumers.

**Conditions and distinctions:** An impressive system description can conceal an unavailable component or unverified causal link.

<a id="target-575"></a>
## 575. Effect recognition

becoming able to distinguish intended, actual, and merely described changes.

**Candidate contributors:** Evidence about the target's actual change; separation of produced material, recipient response, and later capability; appropriate comparison with the prior state.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A fluent self-description and user agreement can leave the target change undetermined.

<a id="target-576"></a>
## 576. Attribution

changing how the cause of a mental change is identified.

**Candidate contributors:** A causal account of the transition; information about alternative contributors; comparable conditions; intervention or derivation appropriate to the claim.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A change occurring after a message does not establish that the message caused all of it.

<a id="target-577"></a>
## 577. Retention design

changing how a useful transformation is made available later.

**Candidate contributors:** A retained representation of the useful result; provenance and conditions; an accessible storage form; a later-use trigger.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Storing a correction supplies potential availability; it does not guarantee retrieval or faithful application.

<a id="target-578"></a>
## 578. Transfer design

changing how a transformation is reconstructed in other circumstances.

**Candidate contributors:** Identification of the relation that made the original change work; corresponding conditions in the receiving case; adaptation of nonshared components.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Copying surface wording can preserve the artifact while losing the operative contributor.

<a id="target-579"></a>
## 579. Backfire recognition

becoming able to detect unintended harmful changes.

**Candidate contributors:** Attention to other affected targets; conflict with the actual purpose; increased dependence, confusion, miscalibration, burden, or lost capability; delayed or recurrent effects.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A visible immediate gain can coexist with a more consequential loss elsewhere.

<a id="target-580"></a>
## 580. Self-application

applying a developed method to the activity that develops methods.

**Candidate contributors:** Making the method's own target choice, representation, operations, and correction conduct available as objects; applying the relevant operation to those objects.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Self-reference alone does not produce self-improvement; the resulting change must contribute under its actual conditions.

<a id="target-581"></a>
## 581. Criterion revision

improving or worsening what counts as beneficial change.

**Candidate contributors:** A case where the old criterion rewards an unhelpful change; neglected concerns; changed user purpose; comparison of candidate standards and their consequences.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A revised criterion can conceal regression; reconsider dependent improvement claims under the new standard.
