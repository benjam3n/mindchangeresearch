# Goals and intentions

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-339"></a>
## 339. Goal formation

adopting an outcome to pursue.

**Candidate contributors:** A valued possible outcome; an explicit adoption decision; a credible connection to an existing concern; circumstances making pursuit worthwhile.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Desiring an outcome and committing to organize activity around it are distinct.

<a id="target-340"></a>
## 340. Goal abandonment

ceasing to pursue a prior outcome.

**Candidate contributors:** Loss of relevance; infeasibility; excessive opportunity cost; a superior way to serve the underlying concern; changed commitments.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Abandoning a means should not accidentally discard an independently valued end.

<a id="target-341"></a>
## 341. Goal priority

changing the order among competing goals.

**Candidate contributors:** Consequences of delay or conflict; prerequisite relationships; scarce resources; explicit precedence among commitments.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Ranking goals abstractly may fail to resolve the particular next allocation.

<a id="target-342"></a>
## 342. Goal specification

making a vague target more determinate.

**Candidate contributors:** Concrete achievement cases; observable completion conditions; scope, beneficiary, and timeframe; a case that looks successful while missing the purpose.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A measurable substitute can make specification easier while changing the actual goal.

<a id="target-343"></a>
## 343. Goal broadening

recognizing a more encompassing concern.

**Candidate contributors:** Identifying the concern served by several narrower aims; including neglected beneficiaries or downstream consequences.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A broader goal needs a usable relation to decisions; breadth alone can dissolve actionable distinctions.

<a id="target-344"></a>
## 344. Goal substitution

adopting a different way of expressing the concern.

**Candidate contributors:** An alternative aim serving the same concern; a changed means-end relation; discovery that the original formulation excludes a better route.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Preserve which end remains stable and which commitment actually changed.

<a id="target-345"></a>
## 345. Means-end distinction

recognizing that an apparent goal serves another purpose.

**Candidate contributors:** Asking what achieving the apparent goal enables; varying the means while retaining the consequence; a case where the means succeeds but the purpose fails.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A means can also have intrinsic value; the distinction need not eliminate either role.

<a id="target-346"></a>
## 346. Intention formation

resolving to perform an action.

**Candidate contributors:** Selecting a specific act; resolving incompatible choices; accepting its relevant consequences; making the act sufficiently determinate to initiate.

**Conditions and distinctions:** An intention can remain inactive when its start cue or required capability is absent.

<a id="target-347"></a>
## 347. Conditional intention

relating an intended action to a relevant condition.

**Candidate contributors:** Linking a recognizable condition to a feasible action; rehearsing their connection; specifying exceptions or a fallback.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Cue-action planning has scoped support [S15](../evidence.md#s15); vague conditions and impossible actions leave the link unusable.

<a id="target-348"></a>
## 348. Commitment strength

changing how strongly a goal governs subsequent activity.

**Candidate contributors:** Protecting time or resources; explicit precedence; repeated enactment; accountability to an accepted commitment; changed reasons for pursuing it.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Stronger commitment can preserve an obsolete goal; revision conditions still matter.
