# Decision making

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-349"></a>
## 349. Option recognition

becoming aware of an existing alternative.

**Candidate contributors:** Information about an overlooked route; a visible available resource; a contrast exposing a false binary; knowledge of an existing alternative.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Existing in principle and accessible to this actor are different conditions.

<a id="target-350"></a>
## 350. Option construction

creating an alternative that was previously unavailable.

**Candidate contributors:** Combining available actions or resources; negotiating a new arrangement; changing a constraint; creating an intermediate or reversible option.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A described alternative becomes available only when its required construction can actually occur.

<a id="target-351"></a>
## 351. Criterion selection

changing what is used to compare options.

**Candidate contributors:** The purpose of the choice; consequences for affected concerns; a failure case under the old criterion; explicit requirements.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Ease of measurement does not justify choosing the measured property as the governing criterion.

<a id="target-352"></a>
## 352. Criterion weighting

changing how competing considerations affect a choice.

**Candidate contributors:** Concrete trade-offs; consequences at relevant scales; a priority rule; evidence that a weight changes the selected option.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Numerical weights can conceal incomparable concerns; retain conflicts that lack an agreed conversion.

<a id="target-353"></a>
## 353. Dominance recognition

recognizing that one option is no worse on relevant dimensions and better on at least one.

**Candidate contributors:** Aligned dimensions and baselines; comparison of each option on every relevant criterion; justified treatment of uncertainty.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Dominance applies only over included relevant dimensions; an omitted cost can reverse the result.

<a id="target-354"></a>
## 354. Decision threshold

changing what is sufficient to choose.

**Candidate contributors:** Cost of delay; consequences of error; reversibility; adequacy requirements; expected value of further information.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A threshold suitable for a reversible action may be inadequate for a consequential commitment.

<a id="target-355"></a>
## 355. Choice

resolving among currently considered alternatives.

**Candidate contributors:** Sufficient differentiation among options; an operative criterion; resolution of relevant conflicts; authority to commit.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Making a choice does not prove it uniquely best or remove uncertainty about outcomes.

<a id="target-356"></a>
## 356. Deferral

deciding to preserve an unresolved choice.

**Candidate contributors:** Unresolved consequential information; a valuable later observation; an available temporary arrangement; manageable delay cost.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Deferral is itself an allocation with consequences; preserve the condition that will reopen the choice.

<a id="target-357"></a>
## 357. Reversal

replacing a prior choice.

**Candidate contributors:** New evidence; changed circumstances or criteria; a revealed error; an alternative whose gain exceeds switching costs.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Regret alone does not establish that reversal now improves the outcome.

<a id="target-358"></a>
## 358. Contingent choice

making the selected action depend on a later condition.

**Candidate contributors:** An observable future condition; a preselected feasible action for each relevant branch; preservation of the conditional commitment.

**Conditions and distinctions:** The future signal must arrive in time and discriminate the branches that matter.
