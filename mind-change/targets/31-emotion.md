# Emotion

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-277"></a>
## 277. Joy

changes in experienced delight or happiness.

**Candidate contributors:** A welcomed event or encounter; achievement of a valued aim; playful or rewarding experience; attention to an appreciated feature.

**Conditions and distinctions:** The same event can evoke different feelings depending on its personal meaning and current state.

<a id="target-278"></a>
## 278. Sadness and grief

changes in felt loss or sorrow.

**Candidate contributors:** Loss or recognition of absence; reminders of attachment; interpretation of what has ended; opportunities to express and share the experience.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Grief is not simply an erroneous belief to remove; changed expression and changed felt loss may diverge.

<a id="target-279"></a>
## 279. Fear

changes in experienced threat or apprehension.

**Candidate contributors:** A perceived threat; uncertainty about harm; learned warning cues; absence or presence of control, protection, and escape.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Fear can be warranted or disproportionate; reassurance changes it only through a relation the person can use.

<a id="target-280"></a>
## 280. Anger

changes in felt grievance and readiness to oppose.

**Candidate contributors:** Perceived obstruction, injustice, disrespect, or intentional harm; attribution of responsibility; available means of redress.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Changing blame can change anger without resolving the actual obstruction or injustice.

<a id="target-281"></a>
## 281. Disgust

changes in felt revulsion.

**Candidate contributors:** An aversive sensory encounter; contamination cues; learned associations; an interpretation of the object or event.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Reappraisal and suppression had different experiential and physiological effects in a disgust-film experiment [S17](../evidence.md#s17).

<a id="target-282"></a>
## 282. Shame

changes in feeling exposed or diminished before oneself or others.

**Candidate contributors:** Perceived global self-failure; anticipated or actual exposure to devaluation; identification with a condemning judgment; acceptance or rejection by valued others.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Separating a specific act from total self-worth is a candidate contribution, not a guaranteed emotional effect.

<a id="target-283"></a>
## 283. Guilt

changes in distress about one's attributed wrongdoing.

**Candidate contributors:** Attributing a harmful act or omission to oneself; recognizing a violated commitment; new evidence about responsibility; possible repair.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Relieving guilt by changing attribution is appropriate only if the attribution was mistaken or incomplete.

<a id="target-284"></a>
## 284. Pride

changes in satisfaction or esteem concerning oneself or an affiliation.

**Candidate contributors:** A valued achievement; attribution of contribution to oneself or a group; recognition by a respected audience; comparison with a meaningful standard.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Pride can rest on accurate contribution, inflated attribution, or a borrowed group achievement.

<a id="target-285"></a>
## 285. Envy and jealousy

changes in feelings concerning another's advantage or a threatened relationship.

**Candidate contributors:** Comparison with another's advantage; a perceived threat to a valued relationship; uncertainty about inclusion; revised information about loss or exclusivity.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Envy and jealousy have different objects; neither licenses assumptions about another person's intentions.

<a id="target-286"></a>
## 286. Gratitude

changes in felt appreciation for a received benefit.

**Candidate contributors:** Recognizing a received benefit; attributing it to another's contribution or care; making its personal significance available.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Demanding gratitude can add pressure or resentment; benefit and benevolent intent are separate questions.

<a id="target-287"></a>
## 287. Compassion

changes in concern for another's suffering.

**Candidate contributors:** Recognizing suffering; understanding its circumstances; perceived connection to the affected person; an available way to help.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Emotional resonance, concern, and effective assistance can vary independently.

<a id="target-288"></a>
## 288. Amusement

changes in experiencing something as funny or playful.

**Candidate contributors:** An unexpected but interpretable incongruity; playful framing; timing and shared context; recognition of a benign alternative meaning.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A mismatch can be amusing to one person and threatening or tedious to another.

<a id="target-289"></a>
## 289. Awe

changes in response to something experienced as vast or beyond the familiar.

**Candidate contributors:** Encountering perceived vastness, complexity, or extraordinary capability; attention to scale; an experience exceeding the current interpretive frame.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Awe can coexist with fear and need not improve the accuracy of subsequent judgments.

<a id="target-290"></a>
## 290. Relief

changes in feeling that an anticipated burden or threat has eased.

**Candidate contributors:** Evidence that an anticipated threat or burden has diminished; release from an obligation; completion of a difficult requirement.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Relief can arise from mistaken reassurance; distinguish felt release from actual resolution.

<a id="target-291"></a>
## 291. Hope and despair

changes in emotional orientation toward future possibility.

**Candidate contributors:** A credible route toward or away from a valued possibility; perceived control; support; evidence about remaining options.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Hope need not require certainty, and severe pessimism does not prove that no route exists.

<a id="target-292"></a>
## 292. Frustration

changes in felt obstruction of an attempted activity.

**Candidate contributors:** A blocked intended operation; repeated unsuccessful effort; unclear requirements; removal or explanation of the obstruction.

**Conditions and distinctions:** An explanation can reduce uncertainty while leaving the practical blockage intact.

<a id="target-293"></a>
## 293. Regret

changes in feelings concerning an unrealized alternative.

**Candidate contributors:** A salient unrealized alternative; belief that it was available; comparison of outcomes; revised understanding of what could have been known.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Hindsight can create regret for a choice whose better alternative was not accessible then.

<a id="target-294"></a>
## 294. Mixed emotion

changes in the coexistence and balance of multiple emotions.

**Candidate contributors:** Simultaneous consequences for different concerns; different interpretations of one event; attention moving among gains, losses, attachment, and conflict.

**Conditions and distinctions:** One emotional label may conceal meaningful coexistence rather than indecision or confusion.

<a id="target-295"></a>
## 295. Love

changes in felt attachment, care, or devotion.

**Candidate contributors:** Repeated caring interaction; attachment and shared history; responsive attention; valued knowledge of the other; changing trust or separation.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Love includes distinct experiences and commitments; no message or sequence guarantees it.

<a id="target-296"></a>
## 296. Admiration

changes in regard for another's qualities or actions.

**Candidate contributors:** Perception of a valued quality in action; understanding the difficulty or contribution involved; comparison with relevant alternatives.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Prestige or impressive presentation can imitate evidence of an admirable quality.

<a id="target-297"></a>
## 297. Embarrassment

changes in discomfort concerning a socially exposed mishap.

**Candidate contributors:** An exposed mismatch with a social expectation; perceived audience attention; interpretation of the mishap; a response that restores workable participation.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Actual audience judgment may differ from imagined judgment; public reassurance can sometimes prolong exposure.

<a id="target-298"></a>
## 298. Contempt

changes in regarding someone or something as beneath consideration.

**Candidate contributors:** Attributing serious inferiority or norm violation; distancing from the target; dehumanizing or dismissive categorization; revised information about the person or act.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Understanding a cause does not require endorsing contempt; global dismissal can conceal relevant distinctions.

<a id="target-299"></a>
## 299. Boredom

changes in feeling insufficiently engaged by available activity.

**Candidate contributors:** Insufficient challenge or meaning; repetitive stimulation; inability to engage an available activity; mismatch between demand and capacity.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Adding stimulation can leave the missing purpose or inaccessible task unchanged.

<a id="target-300"></a>
## 300. Longing

changes in felt desire for an absent person, condition, or possibility.

**Candidate contributors:** Salient absence of a valued person or condition; memory and imagination of contact; perceived distance from attainment; changes in accessibility.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Fulfillment, acceptance of absence, and replacement of the concern are different possible changes.
