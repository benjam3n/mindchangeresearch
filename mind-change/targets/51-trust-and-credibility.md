# Trust and credibility

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-477"></a>
## 477. Source trust

changing reliance on a source of information.

**Candidate contributors:** Accuracy and provenance of prior claims; transparent access to evidence; incentives and conflicts; willingness to correct specific errors.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Credible presentation and evidence-bearing reliability are different contributors to reliance.

<a id="target-478"></a>
## 478. Competence trust

changing confidence in another's ability in a specified domain.

**Candidate contributors:** Relevant demonstrated performance; limits openly specified; successful handling of difficult cases; evidence of transfer to the required domain.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Competence in one task should not automatically extend to another.

<a id="target-479"></a>
## 479. Benevolence trust

changing expectation that another takes one's interests into account.

**Candidate contributors:** Actions reflecting the person's interests when alternatives were available; responsiveness to stated needs; treatment under conflicting incentives.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Friendly tone is weak evidence when conduct repeatedly disregards those interests.

<a id="target-480"></a>
## 480. Integrity trust

changing expectation that another acts consistently with stated commitments.

**Candidate contributors:** Correspondence between commitments and later conduct; handling of costly exceptions; truthful correction; consistent application of stated standards.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Rigid consistency with a mistaken commitment is not the same as justified integrity.

<a id="target-481"></a>
## 481. Self-trust

changing willingness to rely on one's own judgment.

**Candidate contributors:** Relevant successful judgment; accurate recognition of limits; repair of a specific error; distinction between a local failure and global incapacity.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Self-trust should be scoped; confidence alone does not establish reliable judgment.

<a id="target-482"></a>
## 482. Trust scope

narrowing or extending where reliance is appropriate.

**Candidate contributors:** Domain-specific performance; changing stakes and conditions; failures revealing a boundary; independent corroboration where transfer is uncertain.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Trust can remain warranted locally after a failure elsewhere.

<a id="target-483"></a>
## 483. Trust repair

restoring some reliance after a breach.

**Candidate contributors:** Acknowledgment of the actual breach; credible correction of its cause; repair of its consequences; subsequent conduct bearing on renewed reliance.

**Conditions and distinctions:** An apology's persuasive effect does not itself establish that the breach-producing condition changed.

<a id="target-484"></a>
## 484. Skepticism

becoming more or less inclined to demand supporting grounds.

**Candidate contributors:** A discovered error; awareness of incentives or missing support; a requirement for independent grounds; a history of reliable evidence.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Indiscriminate doubt can reject well-supported claims as readily as weak ones.

<a id="target-485"></a>
## 485. Deference

changing whose judgment governs when judgments conflict.

**Candidate contributors:** Relevant expertise; quality of available reasons; division of responsibility; time or capability limits; an accepted decision procedure.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Deference can be rational without implying that the deferred-to person is infallible.

<a id="target-486"></a>
## 486. Independence

becoming able to evaluate a claim without relying on a favored source.

**Candidate contributors:** Access to underlying evidence; a usable evaluation method; independent comparison; separation of claim content from source affinity.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Independence requires capability and information; rejecting authority reflexively is still source-dependent judgment.
