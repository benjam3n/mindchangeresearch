# Explanation and attribution

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-175"></a>
## 175. Mechanism understanding

recognizing how a process produces its effects.

**Candidate contributors:** Tracing entities, transformations, and intermediate states; a worked causal sequence; comparison with an alternative mechanism.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Mechanism words without intermediate relations leave the explanatory work undone.

<a id="target-176"></a>
## 176. Cause revision

changing the attributed cause of an event.

**Candidate contributors:** An intervention contrast; changed temporal or contextual evidence; a competing account explaining the same occurrence; discovery of confounding.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A better-fitting account may still leave causal identification incomplete.

<a id="target-177"></a>
## 177. Multiple causation

recognizing several contributing conditions.

**Candidate contributors:** Cases with different combinations of conditions; interaction between contributors; separation of triggers, enabling conditions, and maintaining processes.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A list of factors does not specify whether they add, interact, substitute, or jointly suffice.

<a id="target-178"></a>
## 178. Person and situation

changing the relative role assigned to traits and circumstances.

**Candidate contributors:** Comparing the same person across situations and different people in one situation; information about incentives, constraints, and available knowledge.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Neither trait nor situation should become a universal explanation; interactions may matter.

<a id="target-179"></a>
## 179. Intention attribution

changing what an action is understood to have aimed at.

**Candidate contributors:** The actor's account; observed choices among alternatives; knowledge of their available information; consistency between stated aims and conduct.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Intention remains partly uncertain; outcome alone does not reveal what was intended [S12](../evidence.md#s12).

<a id="target-180"></a>
## 180. Controllability attribution

changing what could have been influenced.

**Candidate contributors:** Actual intervention opportunities; available resources and authority; time-sensitive alternatives; distinctions between influence and guarantee.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Hindsight can introduce options unavailable at the time.

<a id="target-181"></a>
## 181. Responsibility attribution

changing who or what is held responsible.

**Candidate contributors:** Causal contribution; knowledge and foreseeability; obligations and authority; available alternatives; control over the relevant action.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Causal involvement, moral blame, and assigned duty are different determinations.

<a id="target-182"></a>
## 182. Chance attribution

changing what is treated as accident rather than regularity.

**Candidate contributors:** A suitable chance model; repeated comparable observations; variation expected under that model; recognition of selection effects.

**Conditions and distinctions:** An unlikely event alone does not establish a systematic cause or eliminate one.

<a id="target-183"></a>
## 183. Explanatory limitation

recognizing what an account leaves unexplained.

**Candidate contributors:** A case the explanation cannot distinguish; an omitted variable or transformation; a circular step; a failed scope extension.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Admitting that an account is incomplete is useful when it identifies the unresolved dependency.
