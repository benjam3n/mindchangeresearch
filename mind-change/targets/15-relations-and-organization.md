# Relations and organization

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-127"></a>
## 127. Connection

relating pieces previously considered separately.

**Candidate contributors:** Juxtaposing relevant elements; identifying a common entity, relation, or dependency; a question requiring joint consideration.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Association is not automatically dependence; specify what the connection permits one to infer or do.

<a id="target-128"></a>
## 128. Disconnection

rejecting a relationship previously assumed.

**Candidate contributors:** A counterexample to the alleged relation; recognition of a common cause; revised scope; independent variation between the elements.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Failure to detect a relation does not prove absence when the observation is insensitive.

<a id="target-129"></a>
## 129. Dependency

recognizing what requires what.

**Candidate contributors:** Tracing required inputs; removing a suspected prerequisite in a model or case; identifying what fails when it is absent.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A habitual predecessor is not necessarily a requirement; alternative routes may satisfy the same need.

<a id="target-130"></a>
## 130. Causality

changing what is understood to produce or influence what.

**Candidate contributors:** Temporal and mechanistic information; intervention contrasts; separation of common causes; comparison of competing causal accounts.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Observation alone may leave direction and confounding unresolved; a causal label does not resolve them.

<a id="target-131"></a>
## 131. Analogy

recognizing a transferable relationship across different cases.

**Candidate contributors:** Aligning relations rather than surface features; mapping corresponding roles; identifying where the mapping fails.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Analogical comparison supported transfer in specific negotiation tasks [S09](../evidence.md#s09); mapped similarity does not prove identical mechanisms.

<a id="target-132"></a>
## 132. Composition

understanding what an arrangement enables together.

**Candidate contributors:** Connecting outputs to required inputs; specifying interfaces; considering interactions, shared constraints, and failure propagation.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Useful parts may interfere when combined; composition needs more than a list of components.

<a id="target-133"></a>
## 133. Part and whole

changing the unit through which a matter is understood.

**Candidate contributors:** Changing the unit represented; relating local behavior to collective organization; exposing part-to-whole dependencies.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A property of parts need not belong to the whole, or conversely.

<a id="target-134"></a>
## 134. Order dependence

recognizing that sequence changes what is possible.

**Candidate contributors:** Comparing alternative sequences; exposing prerequisites; identifying irreversible transitions or intermediate states.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Order matters only through a specified dependence; a preferred sequence is not thereby necessary.

<a id="target-135"></a>
## 135. Level distinction

separating explanations or operations at different scales.

**Candidate contributors:** Separating implementation, function, individual, interaction, and collective descriptions; stating correspondence between levels.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Changing vocabulary across levels can leave the same unanswered question concealed.

<a id="target-136"></a>
## 136. Constraint relation

recognizing what limits, permits, or redirects an operation.

**Candidate contributors:** Identifying a limit or enabling condition; a boundary case; changing a constraint and deriving the resulting option set.

**Conditions and distinctions:** An assumed constraint may be negotiable; a physically binding one cannot be removed by redescription alone.
