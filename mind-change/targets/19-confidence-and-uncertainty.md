# Confidence and uncertainty

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-165"></a>
## 165. Confidence increase

becoming more certain of a proposition or judgment.

**Candidate contributors:** Discriminating evidence; successful relevant performance; convergence of independent sources; repeated familiarity or authoritative presentation.

**Conditions and distinctions:** The last two can feel persuasive without adding independent grounds; confidence increase alone is not improvement.

<a id="target-166"></a>
## 166. Confidence decrease

becoming less certain of a proposition or judgment.

**Candidate contributors:** A failed prediction; credible contradiction; exposed missing knowledge; recognition that evidence is dependent or out of scope.

**Conditions and distinctions:** An irrelevant challenge can lower confidence without improving its correspondence to support.

<a id="target-167"></a>
## 167. Calibration

bringing expressed confidence into better correspondence with support or performance.

**Candidate contributors:** Comparing prior confidence with resolved outcomes; separating domains and conditions; revising overused cues; distinguishing evidence strength from ease of response.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Calibration is a pattern across appropriate cases, not one confident or humble statement.

<a id="target-168"></a>
## 168. Possibility recognition

treating an excluded alternative as possible.

**Candidate contributors:** A coherent witness or construction; removal of an invalid exclusion; a case that satisfies the relevant constraints.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Conceivability alone does not establish physical, practical, or institutional possibility.

<a id="target-169"></a>
## 169. Impossibility recognition

excluding an alternative for an adequate reason.

**Candidate contributors:** Contradictory requirements; a proven limit; exhaustive exclusion within a finite defined space; absence of a necessary condition.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Failure to imagine or find an alternative is not a proof of impossibility.

<a id="target-170"></a>
## 170. Probability revision

changing relative expectations among alternatives.

**Candidate contributors:** New diagnostic evidence; corrected base rates; information about dependence between observations; revised alternatives or likelihoods.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A probability shift needs a defined event and reference conditions; vividness does not supply a frequency.

<a id="target-171"></a>
## 171. Uncertainty localization

identifying exactly what is unknown.

**Candidate contributors:** Decomposing the account into known and unknown parts; identifying competing explanations; naming unavailable measurements or assumptions.

**Conditions and distinctions:** An uncertainty label must specify what remains open and what consequence depends on it.

<a id="target-172"></a>
## 172. Uncertainty tolerance

becoming more or less able to proceed without closure.

**Candidate contributors:** A workable action that tolerates several outcomes; bounded exposure to error; available revision or reversal; permission to retain an unresolved judgment.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Proceeding without closure differs from pretending the uncertainty is gone.

<a id="target-173"></a>
## 173. Confidence differentiation

assigning different certainty to parts of one account.

**Candidate contributors:** Separating observations, interpretations, forecasts, and value judgments; attaching each to its own grounds.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A single overall confidence can hide a weak link that determines the conclusion.

<a id="target-174"></a>
## 174. Reliance threshold

changing how much support is required before acting.

**Candidate contributors:** Consequences of a mistaken action; reversibility; delay cost; alternative information; dependence on the claim.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Changing the threshold is a decision change, not necessarily a change in the proposition's probability.
