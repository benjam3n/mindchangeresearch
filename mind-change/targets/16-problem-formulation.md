# Problem formulation

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-137"></a>
## 137. Problem recognition

something coming to count as requiring a response.

**Candidate contributors:** A consequential mismatch between current and wanted conditions; a previously unseen cost; recognition of preventable failure; a newly available standard.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Discomfort alone does not identify what needs changing; the relevant discrepancy must be specified.

<a id="target-138"></a>
## 138. Problem dissolution

an apparent problem ceasing to require the assumed response.

**Candidate contributors:** Removal of a false premise; discovery that the desired condition already holds; withdrawal of an unnecessary requirement; a representation that eliminates the apparent conflict.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Renaming an unresolved difficulty does not dissolve it; show which demand or contradiction disappears.

<a id="target-139"></a>
## 139. Target revision

changing what is actually under examination.

**Candidate contributors:** A separating case revealing the wrong object of inquiry; a missing downstream result; an explicit correction of the intended contribution.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Preserve work that remains relevant, while reopening conclusions that depended on the old target.

<a id="target-140"></a>
## 140. Scope revision

changing the boundary of the matter.

**Candidate contributors:** A dependency outside the current boundary; a case excluded without justification; removal of irrelevant surrounding issues.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Broader scope can reduce tractability; narrower scope must retain the conditions needed for the intended result.

<a id="target-141"></a>
## 141. Unit revision

changing whether an individual, interaction, or larger system is examined.

**Candidate contributors:** Evidence that the outcome depends on interaction or organization; comparison of individual and collective explanations; changing the measured entity.

**Conditions and distinctions:** An individual-level intervention cannot be assumed to alter a collective dependency.

<a id="target-142"></a>
## 142. Timescale revision

changing the interval over which adequacy is considered.

**Candidate contributors:** Delayed effects; persistence requirements; cumulative costs; recovery or recurrence; a decision whose consequences extend beyond the current interval.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Immediate relief and later benefit may differ; specify whose outcome and which interval matter.

<a id="target-143"></a>
## 143. Constraint revision

changing what is treated as fixed or alterable.

**Candidate contributors:** Evidence that a limit is negotiable; an alternative resource or route; a changed requirement; a physical or institutional restriction previously omitted.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Distinguish genuinely altered conditions from merely imagining their absence.

<a id="target-144"></a>
## 144. Attribution revision

changing where the difficulty is located.

**Candidate contributors:** A failure trace locating the missing input or operation; comparison across people and situations; evidence against the initial explanation.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A new blame target is not a causal explanation; identify what difference its alteration would make.

<a id="target-145"></a>
## 145. Requirement revision

changing what would count as resolving the matter.

**Candidate contributors:** Clarification of what the result must enable; discovery of a missing consumer; removal of an unnecessary completion demand; changed user purpose.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A weaker requirement can make the work complete without making the original objective achieved.

<a id="target-146"></a>
## 146. Problem connection

recognizing that several apparent problems share a dependency.

**Candidate contributors:** Tracing several failures to a shared resource, assumption, interface, or criterion; contrasting independent and common causes.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Common wording does not establish a shared dependency; a joint intervention may leave separate causes intact.
