# Recognition

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-43"></a>
## 43. Identity recognition

recognizing a person, object, place, or event.

**Candidate contributors:** Diagnostic identifying features; exposure to different views of the same entity; retrieval cues; distinguishing the entity from similar alternatives.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Familiarity can occur without correct identity; resemblance is insufficient when impostors or duplicates are possible.

<a id="target-44"></a>
## 44. Pattern recognition

noticing a repeated or governing arrangement.

**Candidate contributors:** Juxtaposing instances; aligning repeated relations; removing irrelevant surface differences; representing the arrangement explicitly.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A visible regularity may be accidental; recognizing it and establishing its reliability are separate changes.

<a id="target-45"></a>
## 45. Familiarity

something coming to feel familiar or unfamiliar.

**Candidate contributors:** Prior encounters; repeated exposure; overlap with known features; easier processing; contextual reminders.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Familiarity is not evidence of truth, safety, prior agreement, or accurate recollection.

<a id="target-46"></a>
## 46. Application recognition

recognizing that a known method applies here.

**Candidate contributors:** Matching current preconditions to a known method; contrasting an applicable and inapplicable case; indexing the method by the situation it resolves.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A shared topic is insufficient; the method's required inputs and exclusions must fit.

<a id="target-47"></a>
## 47. Opportunity recognition

noticing an available beneficial possibility.

**Candidate contributors:** Making an unused resource or opening visible; connecting it to a valued consequence; removing an assumed constraint; identifying a feasible next move.

**Conditions and distinctions:** An attractive imaginable outcome becomes an opportunity only if some route is available under the actual conditions.

<a id="target-48"></a>
## 48. Hazard recognition

noticing a consequential threat or failure possibility.

**Candidate contributors:** Diagnostic warning signs; causal knowledge of a failure path; comparison with a near miss; visibility of consequential dependencies.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Vivid examples can inflate perceived prevalence; detection of possibility and estimation of likelihood are different.

<a id="target-49"></a>
## 49. Incongruity recognition

noticing that elements do not fit together.

**Candidate contributors:** Making incompatible elements simultaneously available; stating the expected relation; comparing the case with that relation.

**Conditions and distinctions:** An apparent mismatch can arise from a mistaken expectation rather than from a defective case.

<a id="target-50"></a>
## 50. Affordance recognition

noticing what an object or situation permits one to do.

**Candidate contributors:** Visible handles, openings, and controls; demonstrations of use; knowledge of object properties; fit between bodily capability and environment.

**Conditions and distinctions:** What an object permits depends on the actor; an apparent affordance may be inaccessible or deceptive.
