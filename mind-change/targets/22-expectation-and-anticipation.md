# Expectation and anticipation

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-194"></a>
## 194. Outcome expectation

changing what is expected to happen.

**Candidate contributors:** Observed outcomes; a causal model; credible advance information; changed resources or constraints; comparison with similar cases.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Expectation should concern a defined event; wanting or fearing it supplies different information.

<a id="target-195"></a>
## 195. Timing expectation

changing when something is expected.

**Candidate contributors:** Event schedules; observed timing distributions; elapsed time and stage indicators; a changed dependency on earlier events.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Average timing can conceal wide variability and conditional delays.

<a id="target-196"></a>
## 196. Action dependence

recognizing how alternatives lead to different possible consequences.

**Candidate contributors:** Tracing consequences of each available action; identifying branching conditions; comparison with inaction; accounting for others' responses.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Simulating consequences does not establish their likelihood or feasibility.

<a id="target-197"></a>
## 197. Response anticipation

representing how another person or system may respond.

**Candidate contributors:** The recipient's actual statements and behavior; response histories under similar conditions; known interface rules; unresolved alternative interpretations.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Imagining a viewpoint can increase confidence without improving accuracy; direct perspective-getting helped in studied human tasks [S12](../evidence.md#s12).

<a id="target-198"></a>
## 198. Preparation recognition

seeing what must be available before a future condition.

**Candidate contributors:** Working backward from a future operation to its required inputs; setup duration; deadlines; an unavailable resource that must be obtained earlier.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Preparation matters only if its result survives and is accessible when required.

<a id="target-199"></a>
## 199. Contingency awareness

recognizing that different outcomes require different continuations.

**Candidate contributors:** Distinct outcome branches; observable triggers; a feasible continuation for each consequential branch; a fallback for unresolved cases.

**Conditions and distinctions:** An enormous scenario list can obscure the few differences that alter the next action.

<a id="target-200"></a>
## 200. Surprise

encountering a mismatch between expectation and occurrence.

**Candidate contributors:** A specific prior expectation; a perceived deviation; novelty or violation of an inferred regularity.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Surprise establishes mismatch with an expectation, not necessarily importance or objective improbability.

<a id="target-201"></a>
## 201. Expectation separation

distinguishing hoped-for, feared, possible, and likely outcomes.

**Candidate contributors:** Separating desire, dread, logical possibility, forecast, and commitment; attaching each to its proper grounds.

**Conditions and distinctions:** An emotionally vivid simulation can migrate into a forecast unless these roles remain distinct.

<a id="target-202"></a>
## 202. Expectation influence

recognizing that expressing an expectation can affect what follows.

**Candidate contributors:** A communicated forecast changing attention, effort, coordination, or treatment; awareness of how others respond to the forecast.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Prediction can be an intervention; a changed outcome may reflect that response rather than the original forecast's accuracy.
