# Mood and emotional dynamics

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-301"></a>
## 301. Background mood

changes in the prevailing affective tone.

**Candidate contributors:** Accumulated daily events; recurring interpretations; sustained bodily state; social environment; sleep and activity patterns.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A prevailing mood may have multiple maintaining conditions; one conversational event need not reset it.

<a id="target-302"></a>
## 302. Valence

becoming generally more pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral in feeling.

**Candidate contributors:** Pleasant or unpleasant sensory input; appraised gains and losses; current bodily comfort; emotionally meaningful interaction.

**Conditions and distinctions:** General pleasantness can change while a specific emotion or unresolved concern persists.

<a id="target-303"></a>
## 303. Irritability

changing readiness to react with annoyance.

**Candidate contributors:** Repeated interruption or obstruction; fatigue; discomfort; perceived disrespect; reduced room to recover between demands.

**Conditions and distinctions:** The contributor may be environmental or bodily rather than a defective attitude.

<a id="target-304"></a>
## 304. Emotional intensity

feelings becoming stronger or weaker.

**Candidate contributors:** Perceived magnitude and immediacy of consequences; attention to evocative details; physiological activation; reinterpretation or changed circumstances.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Stronger expression does not necessarily indicate stronger felt intensity.

<a id="target-305"></a>
## 305. Emotional duration

feelings lasting longer or shorter.

**Candidate contributors:** Continued provoking events; repeated recollection or simulation; unfinished action tendencies; new competing experiences; recognition of an ending.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Duration can change without changing the initial intensity or the underlying concern.

<a id="target-306"></a>
## 306. Emotional variability

feelings changing more or less readily over time.

**Candidate contributors:** Rapidly changing events or appraisals; shifts of attention; competing concerns; bodily state; repeated social feedback.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Variability needs an observation interval; a single transition does not characterize a lasting disposition.

<a id="target-307"></a>
## 307. Recovery from emotion

returning toward a prior state after an event.

**Candidate contributors:** Ending the precipitating condition; obtaining needed resolution or support; disengaging from repeated activation; restoring ordinary activity.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Recovery can involve a changed baseline rather than a return to the exact prior state.

<a id="target-308"></a>
## 308. Emotional differentiation

experiencing feelings as more distinct or undifferentiated.

**Candidate contributors:** Comparing nearby feelings; attending to distinct bodily, evaluative, and action components; a usable emotional vocabulary; context-specific reflection.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Vocabulary can become more differentiated without proving equally differentiated experience.

<a id="target-309"></a>
## 309. Emotional numbness

becoming more or less responsive in felt experience.

**Candidate contributors:** Changes in prolonged overload, disconnection, or bodily state; renewed contact with personally meaningful experiences; changes in attention and appraisal.

**Conditions and distinctions:** This label covers different mechanisms; the inventory does not diagnose its cause or prescribe treatment.
