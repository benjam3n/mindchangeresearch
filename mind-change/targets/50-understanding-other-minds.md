# Understanding other minds

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-468"></a>
## 468. Perspective differentiation

distinguishing another's viewpoint from one's own.

**Candidate contributors:** Separating one's information from the other's; knowledge of their access and aims; direct statements revealing a consequential difference.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Understanding one's own reaction is insufficient evidence of the other's viewpoint [S12](../evidence.md#s12).

<a id="target-469"></a>
## 469. Intention interpretation

changing what another is understood to be trying to do.

**Candidate contributors:** The person's stated aim; available alternatives; observed choices; surrounding constraints; clarification of ambiguous behavior.

**Conditions and distinctions:** The same behavior can serve different intentions; retain unresolved accounts where they fit equally well.

<a id="target-470"></a>
## 470. Knowledge attribution

revising what another is assumed to know.

**Candidate contributors:** The person's actual explanations and questions; prior exposure; demonstrated use of the relevant information; explicit correction of an assumption.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Fluent vocabulary or agreement does not establish knowledge of the needed relation.

<a id="target-471"></a>
## 471. Emotion recognition

changing one's interpretation of another's feelings.

**Candidate contributors:** The person's description; contextual information; verbal and nonverbal signals; changes across the interaction.

**Conditions and distinctions:** No isolated expression uniquely identifies a feeling; ask only when the missing distinction materially matters.

<a id="target-472"></a>
## 472. Empathic understanding

becoming more or less able to understand another's experience.

**Candidate contributors:** Receiving the person's account; understanding the events and concerns that organize it; checking the interpretation against their corrections.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Imagined empathy can misread the person; getting relevant perspective improved accuracy in studied tasks [S12](../evidence.md#s12).

<a id="target-473"></a>
## 473. Dispositional attribution

changing what traits are attributed to another.

**Candidate contributors:** Repeated behavior across relevant settings; distinction between stable patterns and situational constraint; information contradicting an initial impression.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A trait label may compress behavior without explaining its mechanism.

<a id="target-474"></a>
## 474. Situational understanding

recognizing how another's circumstances shape their response.

**Candidate contributors:** Information about actual incentives, limitations, relationships, and available knowledge; comparison with how the same actor behaves elsewhere.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A plausible imagined circumstance is not evidence that it actually applied.

<a id="target-475"></a>
## 475. Stereotype revision

changing a generalization applied to a group member.

**Candidate contributors:** Diagnostic counterinstances; attention to individual information; a more representative range of cases; revision of overbroad group inferences.

**Conditions and distinctions:** One salient exception may be treated as exceptional without revising the generalization.

<a id="target-476"></a>
## 476. Misunderstanding recognition

noticing that one's account of another was inadequate.

**Candidate contributors:** An unexpected response; explicit correction; a prediction contradicted by the person's account; evidence that the inferred state was wrong.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Recognition must update dependent responses; merely acknowledging the correction leaves the misunderstanding operative.
