# Learning

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-406"></a>
## 406. Example learning

retaining something from a particular case.

**Candidate contributors:** Attention to the case's relevant structure; an accurate record; relating its outcome to its conditions; retrieving it when a similar relation matters.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Remembering an anecdote does not establish its representativeness or its causal explanation.

<a id="target-407"></a>
## 407. Rule learning

acquiring a relation that applies across cases.

**Candidate contributors:** Comparison across varied cases; detection of an invariant relation; explicit contrasting nonexamples; use of the rule on a new case.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A rule fitting the presented cases may still depend on an unvaried incidental feature.

<a id="target-408"></a>
## 408. Association learning

changing the relation between a cue and another event.

**Candidate contributors:** Predictive co-occurrence between cue and event; changes in their contingency; repeated violation of an old prediction; distinctive context.

**Conditions and distinctions:** The association's strength and its conscious verbal description may change differently.

<a id="target-409"></a>
## 409. Outcome learning

changing future conduct in light of consequences.

**Candidate contributors:** Consequences linked clearly to a particular action; information about alternatives; an updated expectation that influences the next relevant choice.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Delayed or ambiguous consequences can be attributed to the wrong action.

<a id="target-410"></a>
## 410. Discrimination learning

learning when superficially similar cases require different responses.

**Candidate contributors:** Contrasting cases that look alike but require different responses; a diagnostic feature; corrective experience at the confusion point.

**Conditions and distinctions:** The distinguishing cue must remain available under actual performance conditions.

<a id="target-411"></a>
## 411. Generalization

extending a learned relation to further cases.

**Candidate contributors:** Shared operative structure; varied instances; abstraction of the relevant relationship; a new case that satisfies its conditions.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Generalization can extend an error as readily as a useful relation.

<a id="target-412"></a>
## 412. Generalization restriction

preventing transfer where the relation does not hold.

**Candidate contributors:** Boundary cases; explicit exclusions; contrast between superficial resemblance and a missing precondition; a retrieval cue for the exception.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Listing exceptions separately is insufficient if they are absent when the rule is applied.

<a id="target-413"></a>
## 413. Interference change

one learning experience helping or obstructing another.

**Candidate contributors:** Similarity between competing material; timing and context of acquisition; shared useful structure; separation of incompatible rules.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Earlier learning can facilitate or interfere depending on what carries over.

<a id="target-414"></a>
## 414. Relearning

reacquiring something previously learned.

**Candidate contributors:** Residual cues and components; re-exposure to the governing relation; practice focused on the unavailable step; recovery of original context.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A returning feeling of familiarity does not establish full reacquisition.

<a id="target-415"></a>
## 415. Learning-strategy change

altering how one acquires or retains a capability.

**Candidate contributors:** Evidence about what a method makes available later; identifying the current learning dependency; adopting retrieval, comparison, demonstration, or another fitting operation.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A strategy that improves immediate ease may worsen later retention [S06](../evidence.md#s06).
