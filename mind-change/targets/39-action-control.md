# Action control

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-368"></a>
## 368. Initiation

beginning an intended or triggered activity.

**Candidate contributors:** A detected start condition; an executable first action; available materials and authority; removal of immediate friction; a learned trigger.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Stronger intention cannot initiate an action whose first operation remains unspecified or inaccessible.

<a id="target-369"></a>
## 369. Cessation

ending an ongoing activity.

**Candidate contributors:** A recognized completion or stop condition; a competing priority; an interruption mechanism; release from a sustaining cue.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Stopping behavior and stopping its mental rehearsal are separate effects.

<a id="target-370"></a>
## 370. Switching

changing from one activity to another.

**Candidate contributors:** A selected incoming activity; preservation of outgoing state; an explicit transition; availability of the incoming task's requirements.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Unfinished previous instructions can interfere with the new activity.

<a id="target-371"></a>
## 371. Sequencing

executing components in a different order.

**Candidate contributors:** A learned sequence; external ordering cues; prerequisite information; correction of a mistaken transition.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Execution order must track task dependencies rather than mere familiarity.

<a id="target-372"></a>
## 372. Speed control

accelerating or slowing an operation.

**Candidate contributors:** Time constraints; simpler operations; practiced components; reduced decision overhead; deliberate pacing.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Speed can trade against accuracy or coordination; specify which errors and delays matter.

<a id="target-373"></a>
## 373. Precision control

changing the care or accuracy applied.

**Candidate contributors:** A more discriminating target; better sensory or informational feedback; slower or decomposed execution where useful; correction of a recurrent error.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Extra care cannot repair an inaccurate standard or an unavailable measurement.

<a id="target-374"></a>
## 374. Online correction

adjusting an action while it is underway.

**Candidate contributors:** Timely information about deviation; a valid desired state; an available adjustment; control over the relevant action variable.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Feedback arriving after the irreversible step cannot support correction of that step.

<a id="target-375"></a>
## 375. Resumption

returning to an interrupted activity.

**Candidate contributors:** A preserved task-state record; a distinctive return cue; recovery of the next dependency; reconstruction of the interrupted context.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Remembering the general goal may leave the exact restart operation unavailable.

<a id="target-376"></a>
## 376. Completion

bringing an activity to its appropriate ending.

**Candidate contributors:** A concrete completion condition; recognition that it is met; delivery to the required consumer; closure of necessary loose ends.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Stopping because effort is exhausted does not establish completion; unnecessary polishing can prevent an adequate ending.

<a id="target-377"></a>
## 377. Interruptibility

remaining able to stop or redirect an otherwise ongoing process.

**Candidate contributors:** Accessible stop or redirect signals; periodic points for checking conditions; retained control over execution; absence of blocking commitments.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A stop rule is ineffective if the running process cannot receive or obey it.
