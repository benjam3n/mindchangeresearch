# Imagination and simulation

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-203"></a>
## 203. Image construction

forming a previously unavailable mental scene.

**Candidate contributors:** Recombining remembered sensory elements; spatial instructions; a concrete scene description; an external image serving as a construction aid.

**Conditions and distinctions:** People differ in imagery experience; understanding a scene does not require the same subjective image.

<a id="target-204"></a>
## 204. Vividness

making an imagined scene more or less experientially detailed.

**Candidate contributors:** Adding sensory particulars; specifying viewpoint, movement, and sequence; sustained attention to selected features; reducing or abstracting scene detail.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Vividness can increase apparent plausibility without changing the scenario's support.

<a id="target-205"></a>
## 205. Possibility construction

conceiving an alternative not previously represented.

**Candidate contributors:** Combining available elements differently; identifying an overlooked actor or action; relaxing an assumed constraint; introducing a new resource.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A represented possibility requires a separate feasibility account before reliance.

<a id="target-206"></a>
## 206. Counterfactual construction

representing how things could have been otherwise.

**Candidate contributors:** Selecting a changeable antecedent; varying it while retaining relevant background conditions; tracing the alternative consequences.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Changing everything at once prevents attribution of the imagined difference to the selected change.

<a id="target-207"></a>
## 207. Perspective taking

imagining a situation from another position.

**Candidate contributors:** Information about another position's access, aims, history, and constraints; representing what is visible from that position; checking against the person's account.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Imagination supplies a candidate model of another mind, not direct access to it [S12](../evidence.md#s12).

<a id="target-208"></a>
## 208. Future simulation

constructing a possible later situation.

**Candidate contributors:** A specified future time and situation; present dependencies carried forward; plausible branches; concrete details connecting actions to consequences.

**Conditions and distinctions:** One detailed future can crowd out other plausible futures.

<a id="target-209"></a>
## 209. Recombination

bringing elements together in an unfamiliar arrangement.

**Candidate contributors:** A repertoire of components; alternative ways of joining them; cross-domain examples; a purpose that makes an unusual combination consequential.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Combinations need compatible interfaces; novelty of assembly does not establish usefulness.

<a id="target-210"></a>
## 210. Constraint variation

imagining what changes when a supposed constraint changes.

**Candidate contributors:** Naming the assumed constraint; replacing its value or status; deriving which options and dependencies change.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Relaxing one constraint can reveal another; no inference follows from an unspecified 'anything is possible' premise.

<a id="target-211"></a>
## 211. Provisional adoption

using an interpretation without committing to its truth.

**Candidate contributors:** An explicit temporary assumption; a bounded task in which to use it; preserving the original alternatives; a condition for withdrawal.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Provisional use becomes misleading if later outputs omit its assumed status.

<a id="target-212"></a>
## 212. Simulation control

changing the ability to enter, alter, or leave an imagined scenario.

**Candidate contributors:** Entry and exit cues; control over scenario variables; external scene descriptions; an available shift to current sensory or task information.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Ability to describe control does not establish voluntary control over all imagery or intrusive content.
