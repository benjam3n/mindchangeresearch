# Norms and moral judgment

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-497"></a>
## 497. Permission judgment

changing what is considered permissible.

**Candidate contributors:** Interpretation of a governing norm; a relevant exception; consequences for affected people; consent or authority where it changes permissibility.

**Conditions and distinctions:** What is common, legal, socially approved, and morally permissible are different judgments.

<a id="target-498"></a>
## 498. Obligation judgment

changing what is considered required.

**Candidate contributors:** An accepted commitment; dependence of another on one's action; a justified role requirement; a principle applied to the current case.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Pressure or expectation alone does not establish a justified obligation.

<a id="target-499"></a>
## 499. Prohibition judgment

changing what is considered unacceptable.

**Candidate contributors:** Recognition of harm, violated rights, breached commitments, or incompatible principles; a boundary case exposing an exception.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A prohibition's strength and scope require grounds; disgust alone does not settle them.

<a id="target-500"></a>
## 500. Fairness judgment

revising what counts as fair treatment or distribution.

**Candidate contributors:** Information about need, contribution, opportunity, procedure, and outcome; comparison of relevant cases; a justified distribution rule.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Different fairness criteria can conflict; a numerical equality can conceal unequal conditions.

<a id="target-501"></a>
## 501. Legitimacy judgment

changing whether an authority or rule is accepted as justified.

**Candidate contributors:** Grounds for authority; procedural participation; consistency with accepted constraints; accountability; consequences of the rule.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Compliance, popularity, and justified legitimacy are separate outcomes.

<a id="target-502"></a>
## 502. Blame and praise

changing how responsibility is evaluated.

**Candidate contributors:** Evidence about agency, knowledge, intention, alternatives, and actual contribution; correction of outcome-based or hindsight assumptions.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A good result can arise without praiseworthy conduct, and a bad result without blameworthy choice.

<a id="target-503"></a>
## 503. Concern inclusion

extending or restricting who or what receives moral consideration.

**Candidate contributors:** Recognition of a previously excluded entity's interests or standing; application of an inclusion principle; attention to neglected consequences.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Expanded stated concern does not establish changes in treatment or resource allocation.

<a id="target-504"></a>
## 504. Norm internalization

a rule becoming part of one's own regulation.

**Candidate contributors:** Understanding and endorsing the rule's grounds; repeated enactment; identification with its purpose; self-applied reminders and consequences.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Observed conformity may instead reflect external pressure. Norm messages can also produce unintended behavior shifts [S24](../evidence.md#s24).

<a id="target-505"></a>
## 505. Norm rejection

ceasing to accept a previously governing rule.

**Candidate contributors:** A counterexample to the rule's justification; conflict with a stronger commitment; awareness of its origins or consequences; a viable alternative norm.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Rejecting one norm leaves coordination and substantive requirements to be addressed separately.

<a id="target-506"></a>
## 506. Moral conflict handling

changing how competing obligations are understood and addressed.

**Candidate contributors:** Explicit competing obligations; distinction between genuine conflict and avoidable means; prioritization grounds; an alternative reducing the conflict.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A forced aggregate score can conceal an unresolved moral disagreement.
