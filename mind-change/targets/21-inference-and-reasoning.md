# Inference and reasoning

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-184"></a>
## 184. Inference permission

recognizing that a conclusion does or does not follow.

**Candidate contributors:** Explicit premises and inference rules; a countermodel; distinction between necessary and sufficient conditions; corrected quantifier scope.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A compelling example can illustrate a conclusion without licensing the inference.

<a id="target-185"></a>
## 185. Deductive competence

becoming able to derive a consequence from premises.

**Candidate contributors:** Worked derivations; performance of individual transformations; correction at the invalid step; practice with structurally different premises.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Recognizing a valid proof and constructing one require different operations.

<a id="target-186"></a>
## 186. Inductive judgment

changing how examples support a generalization.

**Candidate contributors:** Representative and contrary examples; sample size and selection information; variability; a specified population of generalization.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Repeated dependent examples do not contribute like independent representative observations.

<a id="target-187"></a>
## 187. Abductive judgment

changing which explanation best accounts for observations.

**Candidate contributors:** Alternative mechanisms; their discriminating predictions; fit to all relevant evidence; scope and unsupported assumptions.

**Conditions and distinctions:** The best available explanation may remain inadequate in absolute terms.

<a id="target-188"></a>
## 188. Probabilistic judgment

changing how uncertain evidence is combined.

**Candidate contributors:** A defined event space; frequencies with consistent denominators; explicit conditional relationships; a diagram or calculation exposing dependence.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A convenient representation must preserve the actual probabilities and sampling process.

<a id="target-189"></a>
## 189. Causal reasoning

separating observation, intervention, and causal inference.

**Candidate contributors:** Distinguishing seeing an event from setting it; mapping common causes; an intervention or justified identification argument.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Prediction from observed associations can succeed even when the proposed intervention fails.

<a id="target-190"></a>
## 190. Counterexample use

recognizing what a contrary case actually defeats.

**Candidate contributors:** A contrary case satisfying the claim's antecedent and scope; identification of the precise universal or necessity claim it contradicts.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A case outside the claim's conditions is not a counterexample to it.

<a id="target-191"></a>
## 191. Assumption tracking

preserving the conditions on which a conclusion depends.

**Candidate contributors:** An explicit dependency record; tagging provisional premises; carrying conditions into derived statements; reopening conclusions after premise revision.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A qualification placed elsewhere is ineffective if the dependent conclusion is reused without it.

<a id="target-192"></a>
## 192. Consistency maintenance

reconciling or separating conflicting commitments.

**Candidate contributors:** Jointly inspecting commitments; distinguishing genuine contradiction from different scopes; retracting or qualifying the unsupported dependency.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Consistency alone does not establish truth; many mutually consistent accounts may remain possible.

<a id="target-193"></a>
## 193. Error correction

identifying and repairing a mistaken reasoning operation.

**Candidate contributors:** Locating the mistaken operation; supplying a valid replacement; recomputing dependent steps; preserving independently supported results.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A corrected final sentence can still be generated by an unchanged faulty method.
