# Curiosity and exploration

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-269"></a>
## 269. Interest emergence

a matter becoming worth investigating.

**Candidate contributors:** An unexplained contrast; a personally relevant consequence; a surprising example; recognition of an accessible but missing relation.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A question must be intelligible enough to engage; total opacity may produce disengagement.

<a id="target-270"></a>
## 270. Question appetite

wanting to resolve a particular uncertainty.

**Candidate contributors:** A defined gap between known and wanted information; a plausible route to an answer; anticipated value or enjoyment of discovery.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Resolving uncertainty can be unhelpful when the information has no relevant use and displaces more important work.

<a id="target-271"></a>
## 271. Exploration breadth

widening or narrowing what one is willing to examine.

**Candidate contributors:** Distinct exploratory dimensions; access to unfamiliar examples; lower cost of sampling; a purpose requiring wider coverage.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Breadth without consequential differentiation can produce repetition in disguise.

<a id="target-272"></a>
## 272. Novelty approach

becoming more willing to encounter unfamiliar material.

**Candidate contributors:** A manageable introduction; optional participation; connections to familiar knowledge; visible potential benefit; credible control over exposure.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Unfamiliarity can remain costly or unwanted; novelty is not an intrinsic improvement criterion.

<a id="target-273"></a>
## 273. Ambiguity engagement

becoming willing to remain with an unresolved matter.

**Candidate contributors:** A meaningful unresolved question; partial structure; permission to defer commitment; a way to preserve progress while uncertainty remains.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Remaining engaged need not mean repeatedly revisiting the same unproductive operation.

<a id="target-274"></a>
## 274. Playfulness

entering an activity with freedom to vary its possibilities.

**Candidate contributors:** Reversible variations; low penalties for imperfect attempts; flexible rules; materials inviting manipulation; separation of exploration from final commitment.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Play can reveal possibilities without establishing that they work outside the play conditions.

<a id="target-275"></a>
## 275. Incidental pursuit

following an unexpected connection.

**Candidate contributors:** Recognition that an unexpected connection could supply a missing contribution; a recoverable record of the original path; available exploration time.

**Conditions and distinctions:** An interesting tangent needs a plausible relation to the activity or a consciously revised purpose.

<a id="target-276"></a>
## 276. Exploration cessation

deciding that further exploration is no longer worthwhile.

**Candidate contributors:** No remaining consequential difference among outcomes; declining additional value; a binding resource cost; sufficient material for the next operation.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Stopping search does not prove exhaustive coverage; retain the scope of what was examined.
