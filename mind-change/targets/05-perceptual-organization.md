# Perceptual organization

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-35"></a>
## 35. Figure and ground

changing which part stands out against a background.

**Candidate contributors:** Contrast in color, motion, depth, or contour; directing attention to a region; changing which shape is treated as the foreground.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A foreground shift changes organization without requiring a change in the physical objects.

<a id="target-36"></a>
## 36. Grouping

experiencing separate elements as a unit or a unit as separate.

**Candidate contributors:** Proximity, similarity, continuity, or shared movement; common labels; enclosing boundaries; a task that treats elements jointly.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Perceptual unity does not establish that the elements form a causal or functional unit.

<a id="target-37"></a>
## 37. Boundary perception

changing where one perceived thing ends and another begins.

**Candidate contributors:** Sharper edges; occlusion cues; discontinuities in color, motion, or texture; explicit segmentation; alternative object interpretations.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A drawn border can impose a division that the underlying process does not have.

<a id="target-38"></a>
## 38. Ambiguity resolution

moving between interpretations of an ambiguous stimulus.

**Candidate contributors:** Context favoring one interpretation; cues diagnostic between alternatives; prior exposure; attention to an interpretation-specific feature.

**Conditions and distinctions:** An interpretation becoming dominant does not prove it uniquely fits an ambiguous stimulus.

<a id="target-39"></a>
## 39. Spatial organization

changing perceived depth, distance, orientation, or layout.

**Candidate contributors:** Changing viewpoint; movement-based depth information; binocular and perspective cues; landmarks; explicit orientation information.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A map can improve layout knowledge without changing every immediate depth percept.

<a id="target-40"></a>
## 40. Temporal organization

experiencing events as simultaneous, sequential, or rhythmic.

**Candidate contributors:** Event spacing; repeated rhythm; onset cues; grouping events into units; expectations about sequence.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Perceived simultaneity and physical simultaneity differ; retrospective grouping may change the experienced sequence.

<a id="target-41"></a>
## 41. Multisensory integration

changing how signals from different senses are combined.

**Candidate contributors:** Cross-sensory agreement in timing and location; reliability of each channel; compatible or conflicting source cues.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Visual speech can alter what is heard [S04](../evidence.md#s04); integration can introduce error when signals have different sources.

<a id="target-42"></a>
## 42. Perceptual constancy

changing what is treated as stable despite changing input.

**Candidate contributors:** Stable relationships across viewpoints; information about illumination, distance, or movement; comparison with a reference object.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Constancy is inferred across changing input; unusual contexts can make a normally useful inference misleading.
