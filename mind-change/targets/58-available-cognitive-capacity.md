# Available cognitive capacity

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-545"></a>
## 545. Usable capacity

changing how much a person can actually manage under current conditions.

**Candidate contributors:** Reducing competing demands; making task structure explicit; externalizing intermediate state; supplying missing knowledge or support; restoring impaired alertness.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Improved supported performance does not by itself establish increased general unaided capacity [S05](../evidence.md#s05), [S10](../evidence.md#s10).

<a id="target-546"></a>
## 546. Processing speed

changing how quickly mental operations can be performed.

**Candidate contributors:** Faster retrieval of practiced components; simpler representations; reduced switching and search; changes in physiological functioning.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Speed is operation-specific; easier inputs or altered accuracy demands can create misleading comparisons.

<a id="target-547"></a>
## 547. Flexibility

changing the ability to shift among representations or methods.

**Candidate contributors:** Available alternative methods; recognition of a failed precondition; preserved ability to release the current rule; practice with consequential variation.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Switching frequently is not the same as selecting an appropriate alternative.

<a id="target-548"></a>
## 548. Interference susceptibility

changing vulnerability to competing demands.

**Candidate contributors:** Similarity and timing of competing demands; distinctive contexts; reduced irrelevant input; stable task cues; physiological state.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Filtering efficiency and general storage capacity are related but distinct [S05](../evidence.md#s05).

<a id="target-549"></a>
## 549. Fatigability

changing how quickly performance degrades with continued activity.

**Candidate contributors:** Sustained workload; task monotony or complexity; sleep and bodily state; rest opportunities; effort required per operation.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A rapid decline in one task need not establish the same decline in another; compare relevant conditions.

<a id="target-550"></a>
## 550. Recovery capacity

changing how readily functioning returns after exertion or disruption.

**Candidate contributors:** Termination of the depleting demand; sufficient restoration time; restored sleep or bodily resources; removal of continuing interruption.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Recovery cannot be inferred solely from feeling better; the required function must again be available [S01](../evidence.md#s01).

<a id="target-551"></a>
## 551. Supported capability

gaining an ability through the coordinated use of an external aid.

**Candidate contributors:** An accessible aid supplying computation, memory, perception, or structure; a usable interface; knowledge of how and when to rely on it.

**Conditions and distinctions:** The capability belongs to the coordinated person-and-aid arrangement under its access conditions [S10](../evidence.md#s10).

<a id="target-552"></a>
## 552. Capability access

making an existing ability available under previously obstructive conditions.

**Candidate contributors:** Removal of irrelevant load, fear, sensory obstruction, or access barriers; appropriate cues; restoration of a needed tool or context.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A previously present ability can become usable without being newly acquired.

<a id="target-553"></a>
## 553. Functional loss

a previously available mental operation becoming unavailable.

**Candidate contributors:** Disrupted sensory or informational input; interference; fatigue; loss of required support; injury, disease, or other changes in brain function.

**Conditions and distinctions:** The same apparent loss can have different causes; temporary inaccessibility is not equivalent to permanent destruction.
