# Creativity and invention

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-213"></a>
## 213. Idea availability

gaining access to an idea not previously considered.

**Candidate contributors:** A cue linked to unused knowledge; a changed representation; exposure to a relevant unfamiliar example; a question exposing a missing relation.

**Conditions and distinctions:** An idea can be new to this moment while already existing in the person's repertoire.

<a id="target-214"></a>
## 214. Fluency

changing how readily candidate ideas can be produced.

**Candidate contributors:** Successive prompts along different dimensions; loosening premature selection; access to examples; changing activity or setting.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Walking increased divergent generation in specific tasks, without uniform gains on convergent tasks [S13](../evidence.md#s13).

<a id="target-215"></a>
## 215. Diversity

producing candidates that differ in consequential ways.

**Candidate contributors:** Varying mechanism, target, resource, sequence, or scope; detecting duplicate assumptions; constructing alternatives with different consequences.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Different wording is insufficient diversity when every candidate preserves the same operative choice.

<a id="target-216"></a>
## 216. Originality

constructing an idea new relative to a specified prior repertoire.

**Candidate contributors:** Comparing with the specified prior repertoire; changing a core relation; combining previously unjoined elements; inventing a new operation.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Originality is relative to a reference set; lack of recognition does not establish historical novelty.

<a id="target-217"></a>
## 217. Constraint transformation

changing the structure within which an invention is sought.

**Candidate contributors:** Reframing the desired function; replacing a fixed means; changing the unit of design; exploiting a limit as a design feature.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A changed problem may produce something inventive that no longer serves the original purpose.

<a id="target-218"></a>
## 218. Remote connection

identifying a useful relation between distant matters.

**Candidate contributors:** A shared relation across distant domains; explicit role mapping; a need in one domain supplied by a mechanism in another.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Distance alone adds no value; the transferred relation must survive consequential differences [S09](../evidence.md#s09).

<a id="target-219"></a>
## 219. Elaboration

developing an initial idea into a more articulated possibility.

**Candidate contributors:** Supplying missing components, interfaces, conditions, examples, and consequences; resolving underspecified choices.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Elaboration can expose infeasibility rather than improve the idea; retain that discovery.

<a id="target-220"></a>
## 220. Creative selection

recognizing which idea deserves further development.

**Candidate contributors:** Comparison against the actual purpose; feasibility constraints; consequences that distinguish candidates; development cost; available evidence.

**Conditions and distinctions:** An attractive presentation can dominate attention while contributing little to the design criterion.

<a id="target-221"></a>
## 221. Creative confidence

changing willingness to construct or express unfamiliar ideas.

**Candidate contributors:** A successful act of original construction; a setting permitting unfinished attempts; specific recognition of a useful contribution; separating proposal from endorsement.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Encouragement without opportunity or skill may raise willingness without improving construction.
