# Arousal and readiness

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-19"></a>
## 19. Alertness

becoming more or less ready to detect events.

**Candidate contributors:** A temporally informative warning; an imminent detection requirement; changing sleep pressure; activating or monotonous input.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Brief alerting and sustained vigilance differ; temporary stimulation does not establish restored performance after sleep loss [S01](../evidence.md#s01).

<a id="target-20"></a>
## 20. Agitation

becoming more or less unsettled or activated.

**Candidate contributors:** Unexpected demands; unresolved threat or conflict; intense stimulation; physiological activation; removal of the provoking condition.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Quiet behavior may conceal continuing agitation; reducing expression alone need not settle the underlying state.

<a id="target-21"></a>
## 21. Composure

gaining or losing the ability to remain settled under pressure.

**Candidate contributors:** A practiced response available under pressure; predictable next steps; perceived control over a consequential action; recovery time; reinterpretation of the demand.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Composure can coexist with strong emotion; a calm-sounding instruction is not evidence that composure occurred.

<a id="target-22"></a>
## 22. Vigilance

changing readiness to detect possible threats or opportunities.

**Candidate contributors:** Expected event frequency and consequence; learned warning cues; instructions specifying what to monitor; recent alarms or misses.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Excessive monitoring can increase false alarms and consume attention needed elsewhere; specify the relevant threat or opportunity.

<a id="target-23"></a>
## 23. Startle readiness

changing susceptibility to sudden interruption.

**Candidate contributors:** Sudden stimulus intensity; uncertainty about onset; prior warning; current defensive activation; repetition that changes the event's novelty.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Preparation for a known event and general sensitization can move startle differently.

<a id="target-24"></a>
## 24. Felt safety

a situation coming to feel safer or more threatening.

**Candidate contributors:** Removal of an actual threat; credible protection or escape options; trustworthy responses from others; new evidence about a previously ambiguous situation.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Felt safety can be inaccurate. Reassurance without changed grounds may alter feeling while leaving danger unchanged.

<a id="target-25"></a>
## 25. Action readiness

becoming poised for approach, withdrawal, speech, or movement.

**Candidate contributors:** A recognized opportunity or threat; a selected response; motor preparation; availability of the required object or opening; learned action cues.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Readiness is specific to an action; general activation can leave the needed response unavailable.

<a id="target-26"></a>
## 26. Recovery

returning more quickly or slowly after activation.

**Candidate contributors:** Termination of the provoking input; recognition that the episode has ended; disengagement from repeated threat simulation; restoration of bodily resources.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Returning to baseline and recovering usable capability are distinct; continuing demands can repeatedly restart activation.
