# Enduring dispositions

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-554"></a>
## 554. Persistent curiosity

longer-term change in readiness to investigate.

**Candidate contributors:** Repeated encounters where inquiry produces valued results; available investigative skills; environments supporting questions; enduring personally meaningful gaps.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Persistent disposition change requires evidence across time and relevant contexts, beyond one curious episode.

<a id="target-555"></a>
## 555. Persistent sociability

longer-term change in readiness for social engagement.

**Candidate contributors:** Repeated rewarding or aversive social encounters; participation opportunities; practiced interaction skills; changing roles and relationships.

**Conditions and distinctions:** More social activity may reflect opportunity or obligation rather than a changed enduring inclination.

<a id="target-556"></a>
## 556. Emotional reactivity

longer-term change in typical responses to events.

**Candidate contributors:** Repeated interpretation and regulation patterns; recurring environments; learning about threat or reward; changes in bodily conditions.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A changed response to one event does not establish a general disposition change.

<a id="target-557"></a>
## 557. Conscientious conduct

longer-term change in patterns of follow-through and organization.

**Candidate contributors:** Stable commitments; workable organization methods; reliable cues and resources; repeated follow-through; environments that support completion.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Organized conduct can depend on external scaffolding; that support need not be mistaken for a fixed trait.

<a id="target-558"></a>
## 558. Openness to experience

longer-term change in receptivity to unfamiliar encounters.

**Candidate contributors:** Meaningful unfamiliar experiences; ability to interpret them; reduced unnecessary threat; repeated opportunities for exploration and revision.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Receptivity varies by domain; liking novelty in one area does not establish general openness.

<a id="target-559"></a>
## 559. Characteristic trust

longer-term change in default expectations of others.

**Candidate contributors:** Accumulated experience of others' reliability and care; social environment; learned interpretation of ambiguous conduct; consequential breaches or repairs.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A general expectation can remain poorly calibrated across different people and institutions.

<a id="target-560"></a>
## 560. Characteristic agency

longer-term change in readiness to originate action.

**Candidate contributors:** Repeated opportunities to make consequential choices; competence development; recognized authorship; reliable action-outcome relationships.

**Conditions and distinctions:** External restrictions can limit agency despite an enduring willingness to initiate.

<a id="target-561"></a>
## 561. Characteristic regulation

longer-term change in how impulses and competing concerns are managed.

**Candidate contributors:** Repeated use of appropriate pause, selection, and correction operations; supportive environments; accessible alternatives to impulsive responses.

**Conditions and distinctions:** One successful act of restraint does not establish broad or durable regulation change.

<a id="target-562"></a>
## 562. Identity stability

longer-term change in how stable or revisable self-understanding remains.

**Candidate contributors:** Continuing roles, relationships, and commitments; a coherent but revisable self-account; recurring social recognition; integration of genuine change.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Stability and revisability can coexist; rigidity and fragmentation are not the only alternatives.
