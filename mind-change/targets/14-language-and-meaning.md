# Language and meaning

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-117"></a>
## 117. Vocabulary acquisition

gaining a word for an available or new distinction.

**Candidate contributors:** Pairing a word with clear examples and counterexamples; retrieval during actual use; correction of a mistaken sense.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Recognizing the word and producing it appropriately are separate abilities.

<a id="target-118"></a>
## 118. Sense selection

changing which meaning of an expression is active.

**Candidate contributors:** Surrounding sentence context; task purpose; speaker usage; a contrast between candidate meanings.

**Conditions and distinctions:** The locally natural sense may differ from the author's intended technical sense.

<a id="target-119"></a>
## 119. Disambiguation

separating interpretations that had been conflated.

**Candidate contributors:** Enumerating consequential interpretations; a separating example; explicit qualification; replacement of an ambiguous term.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A preferred reading does not eliminate another reading still permitted by the expression.

<a id="target-120"></a>
## 120. Reference resolution

identifying what a word or statement refers to.

**Candidate contributors:** Shared context; pointing or naming; unique descriptions; resolution of pronoun and scope dependencies.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A referent must be uniquely recoverable enough for the current use; assumed shared knowledge can fail.

<a id="target-121"></a>
## 121. Implication recognition

noticing what an utterance suggests beyond its literal wording.

**Candidate contributors:** Comparison of literal content with the speaker's situation and purpose; conversational context; plausible alternatives to the chosen wording.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Implied meaning is defeasible; inference about intent requires more than linguistic plausibility.

<a id="target-122"></a>
## 122. Presupposition recognition

noticing what an utterance takes for granted.

**Candidate contributors:** Making the assumed background explicit; considering denial or alternative cases; asking what must already hold for the utterance to function.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Recognizing a presupposition does not establish that the speaker consciously endorsed it.

<a id="target-123"></a>
## 123. Pragmatic interpretation

changing the understood purpose of an utterance.

**Candidate contributors:** The interaction's purpose; preceding moves; relationship and setting; recipient response; explicit clarification of intended use.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Identical words can request, warn, joke, or describe; wording alone may underdetermine the act [S18](../evidence.md#s18).

<a id="target-124"></a>
## 124. Translation

making meaning available across languages or vocabularies.

**Candidate contributors:** A correspondence between concepts; contextual examples; disambiguation before substitution; reconstruction of idiom and pragmatic role.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Word-for-word replacement can preserve terms while changing meaning or interpersonal effect.

<a id="target-125"></a>
## 125. Literal and figurative interpretation

changing the role assigned to an expression.

**Candidate contributors:** Genre and context cues; incompatibility of literal reading; a supported metaphorical mapping; explicit literal clarification.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A vivid analogy may import properties outside the intended mapping.

<a id="target-126"></a>
## 126. Expressive precision

becoming better able to say what one means.

**Candidate contributors:** Identifying the exact distinction; choosing discriminating words; adding required qualifications; replacing vague reference with a concrete case.

**Conditions and distinctions:** More technical terminology is useful only when it preserves something consequential for the recipient.
