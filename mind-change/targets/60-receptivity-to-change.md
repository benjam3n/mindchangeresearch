# Receptivity to change

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-563"></a>
## 563. Receptivity

becoming more or less willing to entertain a contribution.

**Candidate contributors:** A contribution connected to the live concern; usable language and form; credible grounds; room to attend; respect for already stated context.

**Conditions and distinctions:** The same content may be useful at one moment and redundant at another.

<a id="target-564"></a>
## 564. Defensiveness

changing readiness to protect an existing position or identity.

**Candidate contributors:** Perceived threat to status, identity, or a valued commitment; adversarial framing; separation of a specific claim from global self-judgment; actual treatment during disagreement.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Defensiveness may respond to real hostility; relabeling it as irrational can intensify the problem.

<a id="target-565"></a>
## 565. Reactance

changing resistance associated with perceived restriction of freedom.

**Candidate contributors:** Perceived restriction of choice; coercive demands; restoration of meaningful options; a reasoned request whose conditions can be evaluated.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Nominal choice is insufficient when consequences make refusal unavailable; disagreement need not be reactance.

<a id="target-566"></a>
## 566. Trial permission

becoming willing to enter an interpretation without permanent commitment.

**Candidate contributors:** A bounded reversible trial; explicit separation of use from belief; preserved alternatives; a clear exit condition.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Trial permission supplies access to examination, not evidence that the interpretation is correct.

<a id="target-567"></a>
## 567. Revision tolerance

becoming able to revise without treating revision as personal defeat.

**Candidate contributors:** A model of revision as repair of a specific dependency; preservation of valid contributions; reduced humiliation costs; actual experience of useful correction.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Demanding enthusiastic agreement with correction can add an unnecessary social burden.

<a id="target-568"></a>
## 568. Credulity

becoming more or less willing to accept without sufficient grounds.

**Candidate contributors:** Familiarity, source prestige, social consensus, persuasive fluency, or desire for closure; greater demand for discriminating evidence can counter these influences.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Lower credulity is not indiscriminate disbelief; evidence quality remains the reference.

<a id="target-569"></a>
## 569. Change discrimination

becoming better or worse at separating helpful from harmful revisions.

**Candidate contributors:** Comparison with the actual prior state and purpose; effects on action, understanding, feeling, and other relevant targets; scope and delayed consequences.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Novelty, confidence, agreement, and pleasantness can each diverge from beneficial change.

<a id="target-570"></a>
## 570. Change fatigue

becoming more or less exhausted by demands to reconsider.

**Candidate contributors:** Repeated demands to reconsider; re-explanation of already settled material; unstable requirements; insufficient time to integrate changes.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Reducing unnecessary revision can improve available attention without reducing openness to consequential correction.

<a id="target-571"></a>
## 571. Change confidence

changing confidence in one's ability to develop.

**Candidate contributors:** A useful change actually achieved; recognition of the operation that supplied it; manageable next opportunities; access to necessary support.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Confidence based only on promises or polished descriptions leaves future capability unresolved.
