# Emotion regulation

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-310"></a>
## 310. Emotion recognition

noticing what one is feeling.

**Candidate contributors:** Attention to current bodily sensations, appraisals, and action urges; comparison with familiar episodes; a prompt naming an overlooked component.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Recognizing an emotion is an interpretation that can remain uncertain.

<a id="target-311"></a>
## 311. Emotion labeling

becoming able to name or distinguish a feeling.

**Candidate contributors:** Words linked to distinguishable experiences; contrast between nearby emotions; descriptions of the event's meaning and action tendency.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A label can organize experience or constrain it inaccurately; preserve mixed and uncertain cases.

<a id="target-312"></a>
## 312. Acceptance

changing willingness to experience a feeling without immediately escaping it.

**Candidate contributors:** Permission for the feeling to occur; distinction between feeling and required action; reduced evaluation of the feeling as a failure; tolerable contact with it.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Acceptance is neither endorsement of a harmful situation nor a requirement to endure every experience.

<a id="target-313"></a>
## 313. Reappraisal

changing an interpretation in a way that alters emotional response.

**Candidate contributors:** A credible alternative meaning; new contextual information; changed attribution; a different comparison or timescale.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Reappraisal can alter felt response in studied conditions [S17](../evidence.md#s17); an implausible reinterpretation need not be usable.

<a id="target-314"></a>
## 314. Expression control

changing whether and how an emotion is displayed.

**Candidate contributors:** Deliberate changes in facial, verbal, or bodily expression; social display rules; a chosen communication purpose.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Suppressing expression need not reduce feeling and can have different physiological consequences [S17](../evidence.md#s17).

<a id="target-315"></a>
## 315. Attention regulation

changing which emotional material receives continued attention.

**Candidate contributors:** Shifting toward or away from evocative details; limiting repeated cues; returning to the current task; deliberately examining an unresolved emotional issue.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Distraction may provide temporary room without resolving the concern; repeated focus can clarify or prolong it.

<a id="target-316"></a>
## 316. Situation selection

changing encounters to alter likely emotional experience.

**Candidate contributors:** Entering, leaving, or changing an encounter; adjusting audience, timing, or exposure; choosing a setting compatible with current needs.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Avoiding every difficult encounter can also remove valued opportunities; relevance depends on the actual consequence.

<a id="target-317"></a>
## 317. Response interruption

interrupting an established emotional reaction.

**Candidate contributors:** Recognizing the early response cue; pausing the next action; changing posture, location, or conversational move; introducing a previously unavailable alternative.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Interrupting the behavioral sequence does not establish that the emotion has ended.

<a id="target-318"></a>
## 318. Co-regulation

an interaction changing the course of another's emotional state.

**Candidate contributors:** Attentive listening; responsive acknowledgment; practical protection or assistance; pacing that fits the person; shared interpretation of the event.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Being heard had context-dependent effects in intergroup studies [S21](../evidence.md#s21); intended comfort does not establish actual comfort.

<a id="target-319"></a>
## 319. Regulation flexibility

becoming able to use a different response when the familiar one is unhelpful.

**Candidate contributors:** A repertoire of distinct regulation operations; recognition of what the current operation changes; access to another response when conditions differ.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Flexibility requires relevant selection, not continual switching or a claim that one strategy is always best.
