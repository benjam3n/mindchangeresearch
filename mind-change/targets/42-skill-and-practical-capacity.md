# Skill and practical capacity

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-396"></a>
## 396. Skill acquisition

becoming able to perform a previously unavailable operation.

**Candidate contributors:** A demonstrable operation; required prerequisite abilities; performance on a manageable instance; information about the failed component; further use that incorporates the correction.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Instruction can supply a procedure while leaving a sensory, motor, social, or resource dependency unresolved.

<a id="target-397"></a>
## 397. Fluency

performing with less hesitation.

**Candidate contributors:** Familiarity with valid transitions; retrieval of needed components; removal of repeated decision points; coordinated practice.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Reduced hesitation can reflect confidence rather than improved competence.

<a id="target-398"></a>
## 398. Accuracy

making fewer or more consequential errors.

**Candidate contributors:** A correct target standard; discrimination of near errors; precise information about deviations; repair of the error-producing operation.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A higher success rate on easier cases may conceal unchanged accuracy on the required task.

<a id="target-399"></a>
## 399. Speed

performing an operation more quickly or slowly.

**Candidate contributors:** More efficient operations; automatic retrieval of practiced components; reduced unnecessary movement or switching; advance preparation.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A speed gain is scoped to the operation and conditions; preserve consequential accuracy requirements.

<a id="target-400"></a>
## 400. Coordination

combining components into an effective activity.

**Candidate contributors:** Compatible component timing; stable interfaces; practice of transitions; feedback about the combined result.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Competence in each component separately does not establish competence in their joint execution.

<a id="target-401"></a>
## 401. Discrimination skill

recognizing distinctions needed for competent performance.

**Candidate contributors:** Examples differing on the performance-critical feature; attention to that feature; corrective information; practice with near neighbors.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Discrimination learned from superficial cues can fail when those cues change.

<a id="target-402"></a>
## 402. Adaptation

modifying a skill for altered conditions.

**Candidate contributors:** Information about a changed condition; a model of what the skill depends on; altered action parameters; experience of the resulting error.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Adaptation to a particular motor environment does not establish general flexibility; transfer depends on the learned representation [S26](../evidence.md#s26).

<a id="target-403"></a>
## 403. Transfer

using a capability in a different setting.

**Candidate contributors:** Correspondence between old and new task relations; varied examples; explicit application conditions; performance in the receiving setting.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Transfer across negotiation cases benefited from supported comparison [S09](../evidence.md#s09); distant transfer remains a separate question.

<a id="target-404"></a>
## 404. Retention

preserving a capability over time.

**Candidate contributors:** Later retrieval or execution; distributed encounters; preserved application cues; reconstruction after disuse.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Fact-learning experiments link useful spacing to the retention interval [S25](../evidence.md#s25); retention of a particular skill needs its own evidence.

<a id="target-405"></a>
## 405. Skill loss and recovery

losing or recovering a previously available capability.

**Candidate contributors:** Disuse, interference, or changed bodily conditions; recovery of missing cues; relearning a lost component; restoration of required support.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Faster reacquisition does not establish that every component remained available throughout the loss.
