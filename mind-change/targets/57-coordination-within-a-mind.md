# Coordination within a mind

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-536"></a>
## 536. Knowledge-action coupling

known considerations beginning or ceasing to govern action.

**Candidate contributors:** Access to the relevant knowledge at the action point; an action rule using it; removal of incompatible defaults; correction of missed application.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Increasing knowledge alone need not strengthen the relation between knowledge and action.

<a id="target-537"></a>
## 537. Value-action coupling

valued concerns beginning or ceasing to govern allocation.

**Candidate contributors:** A value expressed in allocation rules; visible consequences at choice time; feasible alternatives; reduced conflict with automatic defaults.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Verbal endorsement and actual prioritization require different evidence.

<a id="target-538"></a>
## 538. Emotion-attention coupling

changing how feelings direct attention.

**Candidate contributors:** Learned links between emotion and attention; interpretation of the feeling's relevance; an available deliberate focus; changing evocative cues.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A feeling can signal something important or repeatedly redirect attention away from the current requirement.

<a id="target-539"></a>
## 539. Memory-decision coupling

relevant experience becoming available at a choice.

**Candidate contributors:** Retrieval cues embedded in the choice; records indexed by decision conditions; comparison with a relevant prior outcome.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A vivid retrieved episode can dominate despite weak relevance or representativeness.

<a id="target-540"></a>
## 540. Intention-initiation coupling

an intention beginning to trigger its activity.

**Candidate contributors:** A recognizable start cue linked to the intended first action; staged materials; reduced initiation friction; a conditional plan.

**Conditions and distinctions:** The cue must activate an executable action; merely recalling the goal can leave initiation unchanged [S15](../evidence.md#s15).

<a id="target-541"></a>
## 541. Goal-habit coupling

automatic conduct becoming better or worse aligned with an intended concern.

**Candidate contributors:** Aligning recurring cues and responses with the goal; changing a routine's consequences; a stop condition when the goal no longer applies.

**Conditions and distinctions:** An effective habit can outlast the concern it originally served.

<a id="target-542"></a>
## 542. Understanding-expression coupling

expressed words becoming better or worse aligned with understanding.

**Candidate contributors:** Reconstructing meaning before expression; checking examples and consequences against wording; removing terms that imply more than is understood.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A polished explanation can exceed the speaker's operational understanding.

<a id="target-543"></a>
## 543. Monitoring-correction coupling

a detected problem beginning to change conduct.

**Candidate contributors:** A detected discrepancy mapped to a correction rule; available authority and means to change conduct; propagation into dependent steps.

**Conditions and distinctions:** More monitoring adds little if no corrective operation consumes its result.

<a id="target-544"></a>
## 544. Competition resolution

formerly conflicting processes becoming coordinated or newly conflicting.

**Candidate contributors:** A shared governing requirement; priority between conflicting demands; separated timing; an alternative satisfying both; removal of contradictory instructions.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Suppressing awareness of conflict does not establish that the processes became compatible.
