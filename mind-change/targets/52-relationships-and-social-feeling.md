# Relationships and social feeling

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-487"></a>
## 487. Belonging

feeling more or less included in a group or relationship.

**Candidate contributors:** Actual invitations and inclusion; recognized contribution; shared activity; reliable access to the group; interpretation of ambiguous social events.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Feeling included and receiving fair access may diverge; wording alone cannot supply missing participation.

<a id="target-488"></a>
## 488. Connection

experiencing greater or lesser interpersonal closeness.

**Candidate contributors:** Mutual responsiveness; shared attention and activity; appropriate reciprocal disclosure; repeated meaningful interaction.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Structured disclosure increased short-term closeness relative to small talk in one study; durable closeness is separate [S20](../evidence.md#s20).

<a id="target-489"></a>
## 489. Alienation

feeling more or less estranged.

**Candidate contributors:** Exclusion; unrecognized experience; persistent mismatch with surrounding norms; loss of meaningful participation; renewed opportunity to be heard.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Being present in a group does not establish connection or acknowledgment.

<a id="target-490"></a>
## 490. Respect

changing one's regard for another's standing or contribution.

**Candidate contributors:** Recognition of a person's standing, effort, competence, or contribution; direct encounter with their work; revised evidence about conduct.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Respect for standing and admiration of performance need not rise and fall together.

<a id="target-491"></a>
## 491. Affection

changing warmth or fondness.

**Candidate contributors:** Caring encounters; appreciated qualities; positive shared history; responsive attention; changes in trust or repeated hurt.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Warmth cannot be demanded into existence; outward friendliness may conceal a different feeling.

<a id="target-492"></a>
## 492. Hostility

changing antagonism or readiness to oppose.

**Candidate contributors:** Perceived threat, disrespect, or incompatible aims; attribution of hostile intent; repeated antagonistic exchanges; removal or clarification of the conflict.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A hostile tone may perpetuate the condition it appears merely to express.

<a id="target-493"></a>
## 493. Forgiveness

changing one's relation to a perceived wrong.

**Candidate contributors:** A changed relation to the wrong; acknowledgment and repair; revised attribution; separation of releasing grievance from restoring access.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Forgiveness does not require denial of harm, renewed trust, or reconciliation.

<a id="target-494"></a>
## 494. Resentment

changing the persistence or intensity of a grievance.

**Candidate contributors:** Repeated attention to an unresolved wrong; continuing inequity; lack of acknowledgment; meaningful repair or revised understanding.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Reducing resentment by ignoring an ongoing harm can leave the source unchanged.

<a id="target-495"></a>
## 495. Vulnerability readiness

becoming more or less willing to be exposed to another.

**Candidate contributors:** Evidence of discretion and care; control over what is shared; reciprocal but voluntary openness; ability to stop or maintain boundaries.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Pressure to disclose can reduce readiness even when framed as closeness-building.

<a id="target-496"></a>
## 496. Boundary clarity

changing what forms of access or treatment one accepts.

**Candidate contributors:** Identifying acceptable and unacceptable treatment; a specific boundary statement; authority and practical ability to enforce it; responses to violations.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A clear statement alone cannot guarantee that another person respects the boundary.
