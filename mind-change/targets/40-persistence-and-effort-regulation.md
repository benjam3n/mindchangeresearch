# Persistence and effort regulation

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-378"></a>
## 378. Persistence

continuing despite an obstacle or delay.

**Candidate contributors:** A still-valued attainable outcome; visible progress; an alternative around the obstacle; sufficient resources; a reason the delay is worth bearing.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Persistence is beneficial only while the continuation retains a credible contribution.

<a id="target-379"></a>
## 379. Disengagement

withdrawing effort from an activity.

**Candidate contributors:** Recognition of poor prospects or changed relevance; excessive cost; an available alternative; permission to release the commitment.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Disengaging from an ineffective method can preserve effort for the original purpose.

<a id="target-380"></a>
## 380. Frustration tolerance

remaining able to proceed while frustrated.

**Candidate contributors:** A usable next action despite the obstruction; separating discomfort from impossibility; interpreting the setback accurately; room to pause without losing progress.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Tolerance does not require enduring a preventable obstruction indefinitely.

<a id="target-381"></a>
## 381. Effort allocation

changing how much effort an activity receives.

**Candidate contributors:** Expected contribution of additional effort; remaining difficulty; deadlines; available energy; an explicit allocation decision.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Effort amount is a poor proxy when the selected operation cannot supply the missing result.

<a id="target-382"></a>
## 382. Effort distribution

changing how effort is divided across activities.

**Candidate contributors:** Comparison of activities' marginal contributions; shared bottlenecks; dependency order; protected resources for neglected prerequisites.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Evenly distributing effort can leave a critical dependency under-resourced.

<a id="target-383"></a>
## 383. Recovery pacing

changing the relation between exertion and restoration.

**Candidate contributors:** Recognition of declining function; task-specific restoration needs; breaks at workable boundaries; reduced accumulation of unresolved demands.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Feeling rested and restored performance may differ; recovery requirements vary with the demand [S01](../evidence.md#s01).

<a id="target-384"></a>
## 384. Delay endurance

remaining engaged without immediate reward.

**Candidate contributors:** Credible later value; visible intermediate progress; meaningful activity during waiting; protection against competing immediate demands.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Enduring delay is unhelpful when the promised outcome is no longer plausible or valuable.

<a id="target-385"></a>
## 385. Adaptive stopping

ceasing when further effort no longer serves the concern.

**Candidate contributors:** A completion criterion; evidence that further effort adds little; changed alternatives; costs exceeding the remaining contribution.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Stopping needs the actual purpose as reference, not merely fatigue or an arbitrary iteration count.

<a id="target-386"></a>
## 386. Re-engagement

recovering willingness to return after a setback.

**Candidate contributors:** A revised feasible route; corrected explanation of the setback; preserved prior progress; an attainable restart action.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Repeating encouragement without changing the failed dependency can reproduce the setback.
