# Autobiographical meaning

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-446"></a>
## 446. Event interpretation

changing what a past event is understood to mean.

**Candidate contributors:** New evidence about the event; a changed understanding of participants' knowledge or constraints; connection to later consequences; alternative meanings.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Changed meaning does not establish changed historical facts; preserve observation and reconstruction separately.

<a id="target-447"></a>
## 447. Life narrative

reorganizing how events belong to a personal history.

**Candidate contributors:** Selecting different turning points; linking episodes through shared concerns; correcting chronology or causality; integrating previously omitted periods.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A coherent narrative can misrepresent complexity or uncertainty in the underlying history.

<a id="target-448"></a>
## 448. Past responsibility

revising one's understanding of agency or responsibility in a remembered event.

**Candidate contributors:** Evidence about actual options, knowledge, power, and obligations at the time; correction of hindsight assumptions; distinguishing contribution from total control.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Current capability should not be projected backward as an available past option.

<a id="target-449"></a>
## 449. Past relevance

changing which earlier events are used to understand the present.

**Candidate contributors:** A present situation cueing a prior episode; recognition of a shared relation; differentiation from a superficially similar but irrelevant event.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A memorable past event may dominate despite weak applicability to the present case.

<a id="target-450"></a>
## 450. Continuity recognition

finding or losing a connection across life periods.

**Candidate contributors:** Tracing a concern, relationship, or recurring response across periods; locating genuine discontinuities; recovering missing contextual links.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A constructed connection requires evidence; similarity alone need not imply a continuous cause.

<a id="target-451"></a>
## 451. Reconciliation

becoming able to hold previously conflicting parts of one's history together.

**Candidate contributors:** Distinguishing different contexts and commitments; accepting that multiple interpretations capture different aspects; repairing a factual misunderstanding.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Holding complexity together need not erase conflict, loss, or responsibility.

<a id="target-452"></a>
## 452. Future implication

changing what the past is taken to imply about what can happen next.

**Candidate contributors:** Separating a past constraint from a permanent limit; evidence of changed conditions; identifying retained capacities and new routes.

**Conditions and distinctions:** An encouraging future interpretation must preserve real constraints and uncertainty.
