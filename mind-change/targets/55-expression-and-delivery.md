# Expression and delivery

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-517"></a>
## 517. Expression access

becoming able to express an otherwise inarticulate experience.

**Candidate contributors:** An example, metaphor, word, gesture, or image matching the experience; contrast with nearby meanings; permission to begin imperfectly.

**Conditions and distinctions:** The available expression may be provisional; fluency should not force the experience into a misleading label.

<a id="target-518"></a>
## 518. Fluency

speaking or writing with less disruption.

**Candidate contributors:** Retrieval of relevant words; a clear contribution sequence; practiced transitions; reduced competing editing demands; external notes.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Smooth production can preserve conceptual mistakes or leave the recipient's actual need untouched.

<a id="target-519"></a>
## 519. Concision

expressing a contribution with less unnecessary material.

**Candidate contributors:** Identifying each passage's contribution; deleting repetition and announcements; replacing diffuse wording with a discriminating example; choosing a denser suitable form.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Shortening that removes necessary premises or distinctions can make the contribution less efficient overall.

<a id="target-520"></a>
## 520. Specificity

expressing the distinction that was previously left vague.

**Candidate contributors:** A concrete referent, mechanism, example, criterion, or boundary; replacing an evaluative adjective with the difference it denotes.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Specific invented detail increases apparent substance while reducing accuracy.

<a id="target-521"></a>
## 521. Tone control

changing the emotional or interpersonal character of delivery.

**Candidate contributors:** Word choice, directness, acknowledgment, pacing, and emphasis; a clear relationship between the contribution and the recipient's context.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Intended tone does not establish experienced tone; conduct across turns can outweigh local wording.

<a id="target-522"></a>
## 522. Prosody

changing rhythm, emphasis, pacing, or intonation.

**Candidate contributors:** Placement of stress and pauses; speech rate; pitch contour; rhythmic grouping; vocal demonstration or rehearsal.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Emphasis should track consequential relationships; performance conventions differ by language and setting.

<a id="target-523"></a>
## 523. Expressive inhibition

changing restraint in displaying a response.

**Candidate contributors:** Chosen display constraints; anticipated audience response; an alternative expression; time to select the contribution.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Restraint can protect an interaction or prevent needed communication; specify what it preserves and costs.

<a id="target-524"></a>
## 524. Congruence

changing correspondence between the expressed and operative stance.

**Candidate contributors:** Comparing stated commitments with actual selection and conduct; removing performative claims; making the operative position explicit.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Sincere-sounding language does not establish congruence; the relevant conduct must match.

<a id="target-525"></a>
## 525. Medium selection

expressing through speech, writing, image, movement, or another form.

**Candidate contributors:** The relation to be conveyed; recipient access and capability; persistence and interaction needs; comparison of text, image, demonstration, or action.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A preferred medium can be poorly suited to the missing contribution; representation must serve the use [S10](../evidence.md#s10), [S18](../evidence.md#s18).
