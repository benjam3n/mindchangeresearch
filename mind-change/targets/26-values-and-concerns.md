# Values and concerns

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-231"></a>
## 231. Concern formation

something coming to matter.

**Candidate contributors:** Encountering a consequential need or possibility; an emotionally meaningful experience; recognizing effects on someone valued; adopting a commitment.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Attention to a concern and durable valuation differ; a vivid episode may have temporary influence.

<a id="target-232"></a>
## 232. Concern reduction

something ceasing to matter as much.

**Candidate contributors:** Resolution of a need; changed relevance; revised interpretation of the cost; competing concerns; repeated disappointment or disengagement.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Reduced concern can reflect relief, informed reprioritization, or loss of responsiveness; these require different interpretation.

<a id="target-233"></a>
## 233. Value priority

changing which concern takes precedence.

**Candidate contributors:** A concrete conflict between concerns; changed consequences; reflection on accepted sacrifices; a commitment that orders competing demands.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Stated priority may differ from choices under cost; neither automatically captures a context-free permanent value.

<a id="target-234"></a>
## 234. Intrinsic valuation

changing what is valued for its own sake.

**Candidate contributors:** Direct experience of the activity or object; appreciation of its qualities; identification with its practice; changing its felt meaning.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Removing external rewards does not by itself establish that intrinsic valuation will increase.

<a id="target-235"></a>
## 235. Instrumental valuation

changing what is valued for what it enables.

**Candidate contributors:** A newly visible means-end relation; changed effectiveness or cost; an alternative serving the same end; loss of the original goal.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Instrumental value can disappear when its dependency disappears while other kinds of value remain.

<a id="target-236"></a>
## 236. Concern scope

extending or narrowing who or what counts.

**Candidate contributors:** Attention to previously excluded people, entities, consequences, or future periods; recognition of shared features; revised inclusion criteria.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A widened description does not prove that the included concerns now govern choices.

<a id="target-237"></a>
## 237. Value conflict recognition

noticing incompatible or competing concerns.

**Candidate contributors:** Presenting incompatible requirements together; revealing a shared scarce resource; showing that one action advances one concern while obstructing another.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A conflict may be contingent on the available options rather than inherent in the values.

<a id="target-238"></a>
## 238. Value reconciliation

finding a way to relate concerns without the former conflict.

**Candidate contributors:** Separating means from ends; finding a compatible alternative; allocating by circumstance or time; revising an unnecessary requirement.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Some conflicts remain real; reconciliation cannot be established by renaming a sacrifice.

<a id="target-239"></a>
## 239. Value enactment

changing whether acknowledged values govern actual choices.

**Candidate contributors:** A decision rule expressing the value; point-of-choice access to its consequence; resources and authority to act; removal of conflicting defaults.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Changes in enactment may arise without changes in underlying valuation.
