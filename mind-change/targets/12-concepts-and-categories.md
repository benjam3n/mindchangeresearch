# Concepts and categories

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-97"></a>
## 97. Concept acquisition

gaining a previously unavailable distinction.

**Candidate contributors:** Contrasting cases that require different treatment; diagnostic features; a stable label; using the distinction in classification.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Labels aided learning in a specific category task [S08](../evidence.md#s08); a word alone does not supply every needed distinction.

<a id="target-98"></a>
## 98. Category splitting

separating cases previously treated as equivalent.

**Candidate contributors:** A consequential difference within a class; contrasting outcomes or requirements; explicit subcategory criteria; borderline examples.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Splitting adds value only if the new boundary supports a needed distinction or operation.

<a id="target-99"></a>
## 99. Category merging

recognizing cases as belonging to one class.

**Candidate contributors:** A shared governing relation; abstraction from irrelevant differences; a common rule that handles formerly separate cases.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Merging can destroy exceptions that remain consequential.

<a id="target-100"></a>
## 100. Boundary revision

changing which cases count as members.

**Candidate contributors:** Near-boundary cases; a changed membership criterion; counterexamples; clarification of necessary versus typical features.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Changing a boundary may change the concept, not merely correct its application.

<a id="target-101"></a>
## 101. Prototype revision

changing what counts as a typical example.

**Candidate contributors:** Exposure to a different distribution of examples; emphasizing previously underrepresented members; comparing a stereotype with actual variation.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A salient unusual example can distort typicality rather than improve it.

<a id="target-102"></a>
## 102. Definition revision

changing what a term or concept commits one to.

**Candidate contributors:** Explicit entailments; ambiguous uses; counterexamples to the current definition; a purpose requiring different commitments.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A stipulated revision does not retroactively change what previous speakers meant.

<a id="target-103"></a>
## 103. Hierarchy revision

changing relations among broader and narrower categories.

**Candidate contributors:** Set inclusion or exclusion relationships; shared and distinguishing properties; cases revealing multiple parent categories.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A convenient tree can conceal overlapping membership or independent dimensions.

<a id="target-104"></a>
## 104. Exception recognition

identifying limits to an apparent rule.

**Candidate contributors:** A contrary case; a violated precondition; a scope contrast; examination of the supposed rule's quantifier.

**Conditions and distinctions:** One exception defeats a universal claim but may leave a probabilistic tendency intact.

<a id="target-105"></a>
## 105. Concept removal

ceasing to organize a matter through an unhelpful category.

**Candidate contributors:** Showing the category's misleading consequences; replacing its work with more discriminating dimensions; removing habitual labels from the task.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Deleting a word without replacing its useful distinctions can reduce capability.

<a id="target-106"></a>
## 106. Concept transfer

making a distinction usable in a different domain.

**Candidate contributors:** Aligning relational structure across domains; identifying corresponding roles; constructing a new-domain example and a nonexample.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Transfer needs shared operative relations, not resemblance of vocabulary [S09](../evidence.md#s09).
