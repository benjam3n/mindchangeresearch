# Salience and relevance

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-61"></a>
## 61. Noticeability

something becoming harder or easier to overlook.

**Candidate contributors:** Contrast, placement, repetition, labeling, novelty, or a distinctive comparison; removing competing emphasis.

**Conditions and distinctions:** If everything is highlighted, the intended item may gain no relative noticeability.

<a id="target-62"></a>
## 62. Importance

changing how much something seems to matter.

**Candidate contributors:** Showing consequences for a valued concern; changing the scale or scope represented; making a neglected dependency visible.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Something can feel important because it is vivid even when its consequence is small.

<a id="target-63"></a>
## 63. Urgency

changing how soon something seems to require a response.

**Candidate contributors:** A real deadline; showing how delay changes options; detecting an approaching threshold; distinguishing reversible from expiring opportunities.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Urgency should change with delay cost; artificial pressure can produce haste without improving the choice.

<a id="target-64"></a>
## 64. Personal relevance

connecting or disconnecting a matter from oneself.

**Candidate contributors:** Connecting the matter to an actual goal, experience, role, relationship, or constraint; supplying a self-relevant example.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Invented personalization can make an inaccurate connection feel intimate; use available recipient context.

<a id="target-65"></a>
## 65. Practical relevance

seeing or losing a connection to an actual activity.

**Candidate contributors:** Showing where the information enters a real operation; supplying a needed input; demonstrating a changed decision or action.

**Conditions and distinctions:** An abstract statement of usefulness leaves practical relevance unspecified.

<a id="target-66"></a>
## 66. Emotional prominence

an item occupying more or less affective attention.

**Candidate contributors:** An emotionally charged association; a concrete consequence for someone valued; repeated affective attention; reinterpreting the event's significance.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Emotional prominence can increase without improved understanding or warranted priority.

<a id="target-67"></a>
## 67. Backgrounding

an acknowledged matter ceasing to dominate.

**Candidate contributors:** Resolving the concern; recording it with a return trigger; changing its perceived relevance; reducing repeated cues that reactivate it.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Backgrounding can be useful even while the matter remains unresolved, provided later access is preserved.

<a id="target-68"></a>
## 68. Priority competition

changing which of several concerns comes to the foreground.

**Candidate contributors:** Explicit precedence between concerns; visibility of opportunity costs; current consequences; deadlines; removing irrelevant competition.

**Conditions and distinctions:** The most activating concern need not be the most consequential one.
