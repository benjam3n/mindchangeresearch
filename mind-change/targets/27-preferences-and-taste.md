# Preferences and taste

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-240"></a>
## 240. Preference formation

developing a preference where none was settled.

**Candidate contributors:** Experience with alternatives; comparison on newly noticed qualities; social examples; a choice that reveals a previously unspecified concern.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A selection can reflect availability, framing, or compromise rather than a stable ranking.

<a id="target-241"></a>
## 241. Preference reversal

changing the ranking of alternatives.

**Candidate contributors:** Changed consequences or context; new information; altered reference points; satiation; a different representation of the same options.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Presentation alone can reverse choices in studied framing problems [S11](../evidence.md#s11); reversal need not establish changed underlying interests.

<a id="target-242"></a>
## 242. Attraction

becoming more or less drawn to an object or activity.

**Candidate contributors:** Appealing sensory properties; anticipated reward; familiarity or novelty; meaningful association; an opportunity aligned with current need.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Attraction can change independently of considered endorsement or actual benefit.

<a id="target-243"></a>
## 243. Aversion

becoming more or less repelled by it.

**Candidate contributors:** Unpleasant experience; anticipated harm or effort; learned associations; conflicting values; repeated unwanted exposure.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Avoidance can preserve an aversion by preventing relevant corrective experience, but not every aversion is mistaken.

<a id="target-244"></a>
## 244. Taste discrimination

noticing distinctions that change appreciation.

**Candidate contributors:** Contrasting similar works or experiences; vocabulary for consequential features; directed attention to composition, technique, or texture.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Learning to describe differences does not prescribe which should be liked.

<a id="target-245"></a>
## 245. Aesthetic appreciation

coming to perceive beauty, elegance, ugliness, or expressive force.

**Candidate contributors:** Encountering form, contrast, rhythm, or expressive relation; contextual knowledge; a newly available interpretation; sustained perceptual attention.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Appreciation is recipient- and context-dependent; expertise need not converge on one ranking.

<a id="target-246"></a>
## 246. Familiarity preference

changing attraction associated with familiarity.

**Candidate contributors:** Repeated encounters; easier identification and processing; positive associations; reduced uncertainty about the object.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Familiarity may become boredom or aversion; repeated exposure is not universally attractive.

<a id="target-247"></a>
## 247. Novelty preference

changing attraction associated with unfamiliarity.

**Candidate contributors:** A contrast with a saturated repertoire; uncertainty promising discovery; lower expected risk; access to an unfamiliar but interpretable experience.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Novelty can also impose confusion or threat; its value depends on the current concern and capacity.

<a id="target-248"></a>
## 248. Preference independence

becoming less dependent on another person's stated preference.

**Candidate contributors:** Firsthand examination; forming criteria before seeing others' ratings; separate judgments before discussion; identifying conformity pressure.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Independence need not mean rejecting useful expertise; the question is what grounds the preference.
