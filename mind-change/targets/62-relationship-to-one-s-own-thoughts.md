# Relationship to one's own thoughts

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-582"></a>
## 582. Thought identification

changing how completely a thought governs one's sense of what is real or required.

**Candidate contributors:** Distinguishing a thought from evidence, a command, or identity; alternative interpretations; naming its provisional or intrusive status.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Reduced identification does not establish that the thought is false or irrelevant.

<a id="target-583"></a>
## 583. Mental distance

becoming able to examine a thought without immediately acting from it.

**Candidate contributors:** Observing wording as an object; changing self-reference or viewpoint; writing the thought for examination; separating its occurrence from the next action.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Distanced self-talk had scoped effects under social stress [S22](../evidence.md#s22); mental distance is not universal disengagement.

<a id="target-584"></a>
## 584. Repetition

a thought recurring more or less persistently.

**Candidate contributors:** Repeated cues; unresolved concerns; habitual rehearsal; emotionally salient associations; renewed attention during attempts to suppress the thought.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Suppression produced later rebound in a white-bear experiment; this is not proof that all suppression always backfires [S27](../evidence.md#s27).

<a id="target-585"></a>
## 585. Intrusiveness

a thought becoming more or less disruptive or unwanted.

**Candidate contributors:** Unexpected recurrence; interference with a valued activity; interpretation of the thought as threatening or unacceptable; attempts to monitor its absence.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Frequency, unwantedness, and disruption are separate components; changing one may leave another intact.

<a id="target-586"></a>
## 586. Suppression effort

changing effort directed toward preventing a thought from occurring.

**Candidate contributors:** A demand that the thought must not occur; repeated checking for it; an alternative stance or task that changes the suppression goal.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Thought absence cannot be guaranteed by an instruction; rebound effects are conditional, not universal [S27](../evidence.md#s27).

<a id="target-587"></a>
## 587. Self-talk

changing the language and stance used in addressing oneself.

**Candidate contributors:** Changing the pronoun, name, tone, or question used in self-address; replacing global judgment with a specific operation; a personally usable verbal cue.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Changing self-reference affected responses to social stress in experiments; wording alone does not guarantee durable regulation [S22](../evidence.md#s22).

<a id="target-588"></a>
## 588. Thought flexibility

becoming able to leave, return to, or alter a train of thought.

**Candidate contributors:** A recoverable record of the current thread; an available alternative question or representation; deliberate return and exit cues; reduced repetitive triggers.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Voluntary switching capability varies with the thought and current conditions.

<a id="target-589"></a>
## 589. Observational stance

noticing a mental event as an event that can be examined.

**Candidate contributors:** Directing attention to how a thought appears, changes, and recurs; describing it without immediate endorsement; distinguishing observation from interpretation.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Observation can become another form of evaluative monitoring; its actual role matters.

<a id="target-590"></a>
## 590. Thought-action distinction

recognizing that having a thought does not itself commit one to performing an action.

**Candidate contributors:** Explicit separation of mental occurrence, intention, commitment, and action; examples showing unwanted or hypothetical thoughts without enactment; an available deliberate choice.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Having a thought alone does not establish intention, consent, endorsement, or obligation to act.
