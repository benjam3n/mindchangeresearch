# Shared understanding and coordination

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-526"></a>
## 526. Mutual understanding

participants becoming more accurately aware of each other's interpretations.

**Candidate contributors:** Exchanging interpretations; a concrete joint-use case; feedback revealing mismatch; repair of the specific difference.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Agreement words can conceal different meanings; coordination supplies more specific evidence [S18](../evidence.md#s18).

<a id="target-527"></a>
## 527. Common knowledge

a matter becoming understood as mutually known.

**Candidate contributors:** A shared announcement with visible receipt; public acknowledgment; common records and conventions; mutual recognition of participation.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Everyone privately knowing a fact does not establish that everyone knows the others know it.

<a id="target-528"></a>
## 528. Shared attention

participants attending to the same consequential matter.

**Candidate contributors:** A common referent; pointing or highlighting; synchronized timing; an explicit reason the matter needs joint attention.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Looking at the same object does not establish attention to the same feature or consequence.

<a id="target-529"></a>
## 529. Convention formation

establishing a common way of interpreting or responding.

**Candidate contributors:** Repeated coordinated use of a shared signal; an explicit interpretation rule; resolution of competing usages; accessible examples.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A convention must be mutually usable; private invention or unilateral announcement does not establish it.

<a id="target-530"></a>
## 530. Role understanding

changing who is understood to contribute what.

**Candidate contributors:** Explicit ownership of contributions; required interfaces; authority boundaries; acknowledgment by the participants.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A role list can leave overlaps, omissions, or incompatible expectations unresolved.

<a id="target-531"></a>
## 531. Coordination expectation

changing what participants expect one another to do.

**Candidate contributors:** Credible commitments; visible dependencies; prior coordination history; notification of changes; fallback expectations.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Expecting another to act is insufficient when they have not received or accepted the relevant requirement.

<a id="target-532"></a>
## 532. Collective memory access

changing who knows where needed knowledge can be obtained.

**Candidate contributors:** A directory of who knows what; accessible shared records; provenance and version information; a reliable request route.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Knowing where information resides helps only if it can be obtained in time and interpreted correctly.

<a id="target-533"></a>
## 533. Shared-plan formation

making interdependent intentions mutually available.

**Candidate contributors:** Mutually visible intended actions; dependency and timing information; accepted roles; communication of consequential branches.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Several individually coherent plans may be jointly incompatible.

<a id="target-534"></a>
## 534. Collective disagreement

making incompatible interpretations visible instead of silently conflating them.

**Candidate contributors:** Independent expression of interpretations; a case exposing different predictions or actions; explicit disagreement about premises or criteria.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A common label can hide disagreement; averaging judgments can obscure its substantive source.

<a id="target-535"></a>
## 535. Repair capacity

becoming able to recover coordinated activity after divergence.

**Candidate contributors:** A detectable divergence; an agreed repair route; recoverable shared state; authority to revise commitments; a feasible restart.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Declaring a repair procedure does not establish the participants can execute it under disruption.
