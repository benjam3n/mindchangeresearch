# Reward, cost, and trade-offs

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-249"></a>
## 249. Wanting

changing the pull of an anticipated object or outcome.

**Candidate contributors:** Reward-predicting cues; current deprivation or need; opportunity proximity; imagery of attainment; learned incentive associations.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Wanting can persist without enjoyment; desire intensity is not a measure of benefit.

<a id="target-250"></a>
## 250. Liking

changing enjoyment during an experience.

**Candidate contributors:** The consumed or encountered experience; sensory quality; satiation; current bodily state; attention to pleasant or unpleasant features.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Anticipated pleasure and experienced pleasure can differ; observation of pursuit does not establish liking.

<a id="target-251"></a>
## 251. Anticipated reward

changing the expected benefit of an activity.

**Candidate contributors:** Outcome information; remembered experience; a credible path to attainment; revised probability, magnitude, or timing of the benefit.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A more vivid reward may feel larger without becoming more likely or valuable.

<a id="target-252"></a>
## 252. Satisfaction

changing how fulfilled or disappointed an outcome feels.

**Candidate contributors:** Comparison of outcome with expectations and requirements; attention to what was obtained or missed; satiation; changing comparison standards.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Lowering expectations can alter satisfaction while leaving the actual outcome unchanged.

<a id="target-253"></a>
## 253. Effort valuation

changing how burdensome effort seems relative to its benefit.

**Candidate contributors:** Task complexity and friction; visible progress; expected payoff; available support; whether effort itself is valued or resented.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Reducing effort can improve access, while removing valued challenge can reduce engagement.

<a id="target-254"></a>
## 254. Delay valuation

changing the relative weight of earlier and later consequences.

**Candidate contributors:** Making later consequences concrete; credible delivery expectations; a connection to one's future situation; immediate need; alternative uses of resources.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A vivid future is one possible contributor; laboratory allocation changes do not establish enduring real-world benefit [S23](../evidence.md#s23).

<a id="target-255"></a>
## 255. Risk valuation

changing how uncertain gains and losses are weighed.

**Candidate contributors:** Information about probabilities and stakes; gain/loss framing; reference points; ability to absorb adverse outcomes.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Equivalent descriptions can produce different choices [S11](../evidence.md#s11); a preferred frame is not automatically a better valuation.

<a id="target-256"></a>
## 256. Opportunity cost

recognizing what another commitment displaces.

**Candidate contributors:** Making the displaced alternative explicit; a common resource budget; the time or attention occupied by the current commitment.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Only feasible alternatives belong in the immediate opportunity-cost comparison.

<a id="target-257"></a>
## 257. Trade-off recognition

seeing what must be given up to obtain something else.

**Candidate contributors:** A comparison exposing incompatible gains; identifying a binding resource; varying one feature while tracking the other consequence.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A trade-off may disappear with a new design; do not assume the current option set is final.

<a id="target-258"></a>
## 258. Sufficiency judgment

changing when something counts as enough.

**Candidate contributors:** An explicit requirement or saturation point; diminishing additional benefit; displaced opportunities; completion evidence.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Enough for the stated use and globally optimal are different judgments.
