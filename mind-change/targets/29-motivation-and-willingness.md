# Motivation and willingness

Candidate contributors describe possible functional relationships. Each source marker supports only its attached statement at the [specified scope](../evidence.md). [Construction](../contribution-construction.md) supplies operations for a particular use.

<a id="target-259"></a>
## 259. Desire

developing, strengthening, weakening, or losing a want.

**Candidate contributors:** A newly salient valued outcome; current need; rewarding experience or anticipation; changed associations; loss of appeal through satiation.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A want can strengthen without becoming a considered goal or a feasible action.

<a id="target-260"></a>
## 260. Willingness

becoming more or less prepared to undertake an activity.

**Candidate contributors:** An acceptable expected consequence; reduced initiation cost; available ability and support; meaningful choice; tolerable exposure to failure.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Someone may value an outcome while declining the proposed means for sound reasons.

<a id="target-261"></a>
## 261. Approach

becoming more inclined to move toward a possibility.

**Candidate contributors:** A visible attainable benefit; an available first move; lower perceived threat; prior rewarding contact with the possibility.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Approach tendency does not establish that the option serves longer-term interests.

<a id="target-262"></a>
## 262. Avoidance

becoming more inclined to move away from it.

**Candidate contributors:** Expected harm, loss, humiliation, effort, or incompatibility; learned warning cues; an available exit or alternative.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Avoidance can be protective or unnecessarily restrictive; identify the actual predicted consequence.

<a id="target-263"></a>
## 263. Effort mobilization

becoming ready to allocate more or less effort.

**Candidate contributors:** A worthwhile and attainable demand; explicit effort requirements; immediate resources; visible progress; removal of competing commitments.

**Conditions and distinctions:** More activation cannot compensate for an impossible task or an unavailable prerequisite.

<a id="target-264"></a>
## 264. Initiative

becoming more or less likely to originate an activity.

**Candidate contributors:** Recognition of an unfilled need; authority to act; ownership of a next step; an accessible starting point; expected consequence of inaction.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Initiative can be blocked by unclear permission or resources even when motivation is strong.

<a id="target-265"></a>
## 265. Commitment to effort

changing willingness to work despite difficulty.

**Candidate contributors:** A sustained reason for the work; credible progress; manageable stages; expected value of continued effort; protection from competing demands.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Continuing through difficulty is beneficial only while the activity still serves its purpose.

<a id="target-266"></a>
## 266. Motivational conflict

changing the competition among incompatible inclinations.

**Candidate contributors:** Making competing wants explicit; separating immediate and later consequences; finding a compatible alternative; choosing a governing priority.

**Conditions and distinctions:** A declared priority need not extinguish the competing inclination.

<a id="target-267"></a>
## 267. Interest maintenance

sustaining or losing engagement with an activity.

**Candidate contributors:** Successive meaningful questions or challenges; increasing capability; variation that retains purpose; access to consequential progress.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Constant novelty can disrupt cumulative understanding rather than sustain useful interest.

<a id="target-268"></a>
## 268. Reason uptake

a recognized reason beginning or ceasing to affect motivation.

**Candidate contributors:** Connecting the reason to an actual choice; making its consequence concrete; resolving an incompatible commitment; providing a feasible response.

**Conditions and distinctions:** Agreement with a reason does not ensure it becomes operative under pressure or distraction.
