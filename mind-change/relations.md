# Forms of mind change

[Perspective construction](perspective-construction.md) supplies operative dimensions and sixty candidate perspective patterns from the preceding conversation.

## Operations

1. Introduce a previously absent content, capacity, response, or relationship.
2. Remove or make unavailable a previously present content, capacity, response, or relationship.
3. Strengthen or weaken a response, commitment, association, or disposition.
4. Accelerate or delay onset, access, processing, or action.
5. Extend or shorten duration.
6. Increase or decrease recurrence.
7. Broaden or narrow scope, attention, concern, or applicability.
8. Differentiate previously conflated matters, or integrate previously separated matters.
9. Connect or disconnect contents, processes, or actions.
10. Reorder operations or change their dependencies.
11. Change which condition activates or inhibits a response.
12. Change a response threshold or decision boundary.
13. Make a process more automatic or more deliberately controllable.
14. Stabilize a useful configuration or make a rigid one revisable.
15. Generalize a learned relation or restrict it to appropriate circumstances.
16. Transfer a capacity or prevent an inappropriate transfer.
17. Change the relationship among concurrent processes.
18. Change the representation through which something is understood.
19. Change which operations are available from a given starting condition.
20. Change how further changes are constructed, recognized, evaluated, or retained.

## Dimensions

1. Target: the content, experience, capacity, process, or relationship that changes.
2. Bearer: one person, one AI system, an interaction, or a coordinated group.
3. Starting condition: what was present, absent, active, accessible, or obstructed.
4. Direction: which specific feature increases, decreases, appears, disappears, or reorganizes.
5. Cause: what actually contributes to the change; a temporal sequence alone does not establish causation.
6. Onset: immediate, delayed, anticipatory, or contingent.
7. Duration: momentary, sustained, recurring, or enduring.
8. Depth: a current state, its activation conditions, a learned disposition, or a capacity.
9. Scope: a single content, a task, a class of situations, or a broader pattern.
10. Awareness: noticed, partly noticed, or unrecognized by the bearer.
11. Voluntariness: intended, accepted, resisted, spontaneous, or involuntary.
12. Reversibility: readily reversible, conditionally reversible, or persistent within the relevant conditions.
13. Transfer: confined to the original encounter or available elsewhere.
14. Dependency: requires a cue, another person, a tool, a bodily condition, or no longer requires a former support.
15. Coupling: helps, obstructs, or leaves unchanged another mental process.
16. Value: beneficial, harmful, mixed, or unresolved relative to specified concerns and affected people.
17. Cost: attention, time, effort, resources, and displaced opportunities.
18. Evidence: self-report, observable capability, behavior, task performance, or other relevant observations; none is automatically interchangeable with the others.

## Ways a change can occur

1. Explanation and argument: make relations, reasons, or implications available.
2. Evidence encounter: provide observations that bear on a live matter.
3. Demonstration: make an operation or relationship directly inspectable.
4. Contrast: make a consequential difference available through juxtaposition.
5. Counterexample: expose a limit in a claim or operation.
6. Analogy: use a preserved relationship to make another matter accessible.
7. Narrative: organize events so their relationships can be encountered and remembered.
8. Framing: change the interpretation through which a matter is approached.
9. Questioning: direct or open a determination that the recipient can undertake.
10. Dialogue: let contributions change in response to another's actual uptake.
11. Naming: make a distinction easier to recognize, communicate, or retrieve.
12. Metaphor and imagery: make a relationship accessible through another representation.
13. Humor: invite a shift in interpretation and affect through an experienced incongruity.
14. Rhythm, emphasis, and timing: alter what becomes prominent and when it is encountered.
15. Credibility and relationship: alter whether and how a contribution is received.
16. Modeling: make another's way of perceiving, responding, or acting available.
17. Imitation: acquire or vary a response through reproducing an observed one.
18. Practice: change capability through performing an operation.
19. Feedback: make consequences available to alter subsequent activity.
20. Retrieval and rehearsal: bring prior material into active use.
21. Reward and cost encounters: alter experienced consequences of an activity.
22. Exposure and repeated encounter: change familiarity, responsiveness, or opportunity to learn; the direction is case-dependent.
23. Simulation and role taking: construct a temporary encounter with possible situations or positions.
24. Play and exploration: vary possible responses without requiring immediate settled commitment.
25. Environmental rearrangement: change what can be noticed, accessed, or done.
26. Tool use and external representation: change the demands and possibilities of an activity.
27. Social interaction: change participation, expectations, belonging, trust, or shared understanding.
28. Bodily and physiological change: alter conditions under which mental activity occurs.
29. Sleep, rest, and wake transitions: change current awareness and available functioning.
30. Development and aging: change dispositions and capacities over longer periods.
31. Injury, illness, deprivation, or recovery: alter functioning through conditions that are not simply communicative choices.
32. Spontaneous association and recollection: change what becomes available without an explicit external instruction.
33. Self-directed inquiry: apply a constructed operation to one's own interpretation or conduct.
34. Persistent records and recurrent cues: alter what remains available for future activity.

## Divergent effects

1. Acquiring a fact while losing the confidence needed to use an existing skill.
2. Feeling reassured while missing a condition that still requires attention.
3. Becoming more certain without acquiring better grounds.
4. Recognizing an option while becoming less willing to choose.
5. Learning a vocabulary while retaining the same inadequate distinctions.
6. Understanding a method without recognizing when it applies.
7. Recognizing applicability without being able to initiate the operation.
8. Forming an intention without changing what happens at the relevant moment.
9. Feeling motivated while adopting an infeasible plan.
10. Feeling understood while a consequential misunderstanding remains.
11. Seeing more possibilities while becoming less able to select among them.
12. Correcting one conclusion while preserving the process that generated the error.
13. Becoming more skeptical in a way that indiscriminately rejects well-supported claims.
14. Trusting a source more broadly than its demonstrated competence warrants.
15. Becoming less dependent on one source while becoming uncritically dependent on another.
16. Remembering the wording while forgetting its application conditions.
17. Generalizing a useful correction beyond the circumstances that justify it.
18. Suppressing outward expression while the unwanted felt state persists.
19. Becoming more self-monitoring while disrupting a fluent skill.
20. Becoming more excited while losing attention to constraints.
21. Obtaining short-lived clarity that cannot be reconstructed later.
22. Receiving more correct material while becoming fatigued, distracted, or unwilling to continue.
23. Changing what is reported without changing the relevant experience or capability.
24. Improving one person's state while impairing coordination with others.
25. Optimizing an immediate response in a way that makes later beneficial change harder.

These dimensions, operations, and routes can combine. They are candidate distinctions, not independent validated factors or a compulsory sequence.
