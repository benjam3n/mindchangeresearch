## Self-application that actually changes the method

The opening review rule says to suspend the claim of distinct alternatives when no consequential difference has been identified (`authority_and_completion_review.md:27`). Another says a superior successor must address the predecessor’s strongest demonstrated consequences or explain their changed importance (`:29`). Those rules can recreate the user’s starting mistake: the present account decides which differences must already be expressible and which inherited tasks every successor must continue. Accountability becomes an admission barrier.

The amendment does the work it had required. It supplies a maze with low walls: an exact corridor map remains true, but stepping over a wall does not require translating its complete route taxonomy. The successor owes support for the commitments it invokes—crossability, permission, destination access—not for every irrelevant former achievement. This is a specific counterexample to compulsory inheritance, not just “preservation is revisable” (`review:106–114`).

The second amendment separates absent difference, unexpressed difference, difference presently undetectable, and difference not yet formed. Failure to identify a difference does not distinguish these states. Therefore it licenses neither a confident identity judgment nor automatic superiority. The final actually replaces the old instruction with leaving both sameness and distinctness undecided (`review:116–120`; `final:19–25`). The conclusion is scoped correctly: a distinction must be established to support a claim relying on that distinction, but prior expression of it need not be a permission condition for the encounter through which it could become available.

The blank-card proposal Q then tests the corrected permissiveness. Possible inaccessible significance does not imply that carrying the card already perfects all understanding, grants it exclusive authority, or requires unlimited attention. Those conclusions require their own grounds. Refusing those claims does not require disproving every imaginable use of the card. This performs a boundary argument against both premature exclusion and immunity from criticism (`review:122–128`; `final:86–94`).

These are real counterexamples to performative self-criticism: a named objection changes a concrete rule, the final preserves the change, and the change’s limits are tested. They do not establish an internal model-state transformation, universal sufficiency, or reliable future compliance. The documents generally preserve that distinction.

## Necessary conclusion for the comprehensive philosophy

**Completion should mean discharging the obligations incurred by the claim and task, not mechanically preserving every inherited activity.** If a successor asserts preservation, it owes the relevant mapping. If it asserts that it solved the old problem, it owes that resolution or an argument that the object disappeared. If it merely leaves the old activity behind, the old activity can remain unfinished without the successor becoming false. Calling abandonment solution remains an error; demanding that every new inquiry finish every old one is a different error.

The paired rule is equally necessary: **permission to begin is not a certificate of success**. Requiring a fully justified finished alternative before allowing its development can prohibit the very work needed to understand it. But calling every uncharacterized change potentially profound cannot establish any achievement. The performed amendments provide this distinction rather than substituting generalized open-mindedness for perfection.

The endpoint remains incomplete in a precise way: neither an unrestricted coverage argument nor an adequate rule of allocation when transformations and their significance are not yet characterizable has been constructed. The final explicitly says so. Its source task permits that admitted failure. The exact audit must preserve both conclusions: this is a substantive improvement over describing correction without changing anything, and it is not the perfected universal philosophy it has not established.
