## MRC01-01 — a direct-change instruction receives credit for direct enforcement

In `post-allocation/cycle-01/approach-selection/01-unx-transition-first.md:74–76`, the selected response instructs its recipient to store criterion versions, refuse an incompatible outcome, preserve provenance, and keep an evidence-gated record incomplete. It is a more concrete specification of a merge policy.

Line 78 correctly says the response “selects and specifies” the transition. Line 104 then says its benefit includes preventing “explaining merge discipline instead of changing merge permissibility.” Line 21 also describes the candidate as reaching the artifact state without relying on a person to remember the explanation at every merge.

The delivered imperative does not itself change merge permissions. That requires an implemented and integrated rule, or a demonstrated local operation with the rule's stated scope. The response has made the desired operation explicit; its implementation dependency remains. Selection/specification cannot automatically claim the reliability advantage of enforcement.

The retained result is a completed choice of where a proposed intervention belongs. The withdrawn inference is that this response, merely by specifying a tool intervention, has already removed the human or implementation dependency. The separate COL record reports actual finite candidate decisions; those local outputs do not make this imperative an installed general gate. This finding does not assert that no implementation exists anywhere or that no useful model-side change occurred.
